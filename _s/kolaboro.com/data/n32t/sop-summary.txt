<h1>State of Off-Plan 2026: what developers publish, and what holds up</h1>
<p class="lead">We checked 646 off-plan projects in 123 markets and 62 countries on the same six points a careful buyer needs, then tested 6,419 developer claims against registries, courts, regulators and the press. This is what we found.</p>

<h2 id="h-findings">Key findings</h2>
<ol class="kf"><li>The average project publishes 3.1 of the six things a careful buyer needs. Only 49 of 646 projects (8%) publish all six, and 45 of those also have no problem in news, court or regulator reports.</li><li>Deposit protection is the rarest disclosure: 22% of projects say buyer payments go into an escrow or trust account (139 of 646). Only 28% publish a permit or licence number.</li><li>Of 3,876 check results, 20% are confirmed by an official registry or an independent source. 33% rest on the developer's word alone, 45% are not published at all, and 89 (2%) are contradicted.</li><li>We tested 6,419 individual developer claims. 29% held up against another source, 55% could not be checked, and 17% were contradicted. 526 projects (81%) have at least one contradicted claim.</li><li>The Gulf leads on disclosure: projects there publish 4.3 of six checks on average and 37% of check results are confirmed. In Southeast Asia the figures are 2.7 and 12%.</li><li>Among markets with at least five projects, Dubai publishes the most (5.53 of six on average) and Sharjah the least (1.67).</li><li>80 projects have a reported delay figure. 78 of them are late, by a median of 12.5 months; 12 are more than 24 months behind.</li></ol>
</div></section><section class="s" id="method" aria-labelledby="h-method"><div class="wrap"><h2 id="h-method">Method</h2><div class="cols"><div><h3>Six checks</h3>
<p>Every project is checked on the same six points. A check counts as published when the developer, its sales agents or a listing makes it public.</p>
<ol class="six">
<li><b>Permit or licence number.</b> A building permit, project registration or licence number that a buyer could look up.</li>
<li><b>Deposit protection.</b> A statement that buyer payments go into an escrow or trust account.</li>
<li><b>Construction progress.</b> A progress percentage or dated construction updates.</li>
<li><b>Floor or site plans.</b> Plans for the units or the site.</li>
<li><b>Developer track record.</b> Finished projects a buyer can look at.</li>
<li><b>No guaranteed returns.</b> No promise of a fixed rental return. This is the one check passed by not making a claim.</li>
</ol></div><div><h3>Limits</h3>
<ul class="lim">
<li>Public information only. We record what a buyer could find before paying; we did not receive private documents for this edition.</li>
<li>A public document is not proof that it is genuine. Checking that is the work of a site visit and a registry search.</li>
<li>Not published does not mean it does not exist. It means a buyer cannot see it before asking.</li>
<li>Coverage is uneven: some markets have many projects and some only a few. Market rankings use only markets with at least 5 projects.</li>
<li>This is a fixed edition with data dated 1 November 2026. The live database keeps changing.</li>
</ul></div></div></div></section><section class="s" id="gap" aria-labelledby="h-gap"><div class="wrap"><h2 id="h-gap">The publish gap</h2><p class="lead">Most developers publish the easy things, like plans and a track record. <h2 id="h-ask">What buyers should ask</h2>
<p class="lead">One question for each of the six checks. Ask in writing, and ask for the answer in a form you can check yourself.</p>
<ol class="sop-asks"><li><h3>Permit or licence</h3><p class="q">What is the building permit or project licence number, and can I look it up on the official register myself?</p><p class="why">28% of projects publish a number; 15 check results on permits are contradicted by another source.</p></li><li><h3>Deposit protection</h3><p class="q">Will my payments go into an escrow or trust account in my name, held by a regulated third party? Which one?</p><p class="why">Only 22% of projects say so publicly.</p></li><li><h3>Construction progress</h3><p class="q">What percentage is built today, who certified it, and can I see dated site evidence?</p><p class="why">48% publish progress; 49 progress check results are contradicted, the most of any check.</p></li><li><h3>Plans</h3><p class="q">Can I have the floor plan and site plan that will be attached to my contract, not a brochure version?</p><p class="why">54% publish plans; 3 are confirmed by a registry.</p></li><li><h3>Track record</h3><p class="q">Which projects has this developer finished, when were they promised, and when were they delivered?</p><p class="why">66% publish a track record; 57% of those records are confirmed by a registry or an independent source.</p></li><li><h3>Returns</h3><p class="q">Is any rental return in the contract? If not, what is it based on, and who pays if it is not met?</p><p class="why">94% avoid a guaranteed return, but almost all return figures rest on the developer's word: 76% of these results are developer only.</p></li></ol>
