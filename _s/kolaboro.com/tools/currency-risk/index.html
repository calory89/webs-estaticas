What will your payment plan cost in your own currency?

Off-plan payments are spread over years, and exchange rates move. Enter your price, your schedule and today's rate. See the total you pay in your home currency if the rate moves 10% or 20% either way, and what the plan would have cost with the real rate moves of the past 1, 3 and 5 years.

Real history, not a guess

What this plan would have cost with the rate moves that actually happened

We replay your remaining payments from every trading day in a past window, using the European Central Bank rate published on each payment day, and scale the result to today's rate. This shows the spread of outcomes the past produced. It says nothing about what rates do next.

Method

  • For each start date S in the window and each payment not yet made, we take the rate on S plus that payment's distance from today (the last published rate on or before that day). The payment costs: amount times today's rate times (rate on the payment day divided by the rate on S).
  • Payments dated before today are counted at today's rate, as in the scenarios above.
  • A window of N years means N years of start dates, ending at the latest start date whose whole plan is already in the data. Longer plans therefore reach further back.
  • Percentiles use linear interpolation over all start dates. Worst and best are the single highest and lowest totals, with their start date, so you can check them.
  • The ECB publishes mid-market reference rates. Your bank adds a spread and fees on every payment.
  • Only the two currency codes are sent to our server, to fetch the rates. The calculation runs in your browser.

What you can do

Ways to cut the currency risk

    How it works

    • The rate means: 1 unit of the property currency costs this much in your home currency.
    • A plus move means the property currency gets stronger against yours, so each payment costs you more. A minus move means it costs less.
    • Payments dated before today are counted at today's rate in every scenario, because the money has already gone. For a precise total, enter only what is left to pay.
    • The fetch button asks the free Frankfurter service, which republishes European Central Bank reference rates. It covers about 30 currencies. If your pair is missing, type the rate from your bank.
    • Nothing you enter leaves your browser, except the two currency codes when you fetch a rate.
