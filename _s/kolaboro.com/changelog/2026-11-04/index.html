Morning of 4 November: 19 improvements and 115 new projects20

Built on the morning of 4 November, each live and checked on phone and desktop. The twentieth, copies in the Internet Archive, waits for the owner's approval.

  • 115 new projects in 35 countries: 1,011 tracked in total, each with two sources and a live website.
  • Global company graph: GLEIF matches by exact legal name and country, 20 of 20 correct in a hand check.
  • Insolvency check: The Gazette (UK) and BORME (Spain) live, plus a daily anonymised watch.
  • Is this agent licensed?: official registers for Dubai and five more markets; the Dubai automatic lookup opens after setup.
  • Promise tracker: 992 dated promises, quotes checked in code; 50 broken, 185 moved at least once.
  • Plain-language search alerts: the reading works and the database is ready; emails start when the email key is set.
  • Ask the developer: six questions from your own email, ordered by what is missing.
  • Climate 2030 and 2050: the WRI flood model under two scenarios, five climate models, median and range.
  • Protected land and mangroves within 1 km of your pin, from OpenStreetMap and Global Mangrove Watch.
  • Night lights: NASA Black Marble at projects that should be finished; light is a proxy, not proof of occupancy.
  • Stalled-site alerts for all 168 projects with coordinates: 10 under construction show no visible change for 9 months or more.
  • Six languages: German, French, Russian, Dutch, Arabic and Chinese, 8 pages each, checked by code for identical numbers, links and quotes.
  • Delivery-delay index: 8.2% of dated projects are past their first promised handover, median 10.5 months late; Dubai 11.3%, Bali 14.1%.
  • On-time odds: counted from comparable closed projects; today too few closed projects per market, and the page says so.
  • Monthly press release: five sourced numbers and CC BY 4.0 charts; any AI text is number-checked by code.
  • Price below build cost: 0 of 297 comparable tracked projects are under published build cost.
  • Currency risk history: your plan replayed through real ECB rates over 1, 3 and 5 years.
  • Late handover, contract and law: what the clause and the law say, with the article cited.
  • Court-ready evidence bundle: exhibits, hashes, Bitcoin stamps, custody and an offline checker.
  • Risk feed for banks and insurers: signed and paginated, with a public sandbox key.

Desk review and AI4

The desk review on real websites, and an open benchmark for it.

  • The desk review finds where a site sells: from a home page it looks through the same site for the sales page, in 13 languages, with the sitemap as a fallback. Example: a Bali developer whose offer sits on an "Invest" page, not on its home page. Tested on 19 live developer websites. fix
  • Pages built by JavaScript are now read from the data embedded in them, instead of coming out nearly empty. fix
  • Blocked readers are named: when a website blocks automated readers, the desk review says so instead of reporting on what little it could read.
  • AI benchmark: public test pages, the checks a correct answer must pass, every answer signed. Models forced to answer alone: Haiku 18 of 18 cases (108 of 108 checks), Sonnet 16 of 18 (105 of 108). A script scores your own system.

Verify us6

Ways to check our numbers, our pages and our log without trusting us.

  • Number provenance: click a marked number on the home page, /database, /trust, /investors/dossier, /prices or /status to see its source, read again in your browser with its signature or SHA-256 checked.
  • Verify this page: your browser hashes the page and its scripts and compares them with a signed manifest of 2,090 published files. At launch 175 of 175 HTML pages matched. Now in the site footer.
  • Reproducible worker builds: the code that serves the site is bundled twice in clean folders; the SHA-256 of each build is published and signed.
  • Log witnesses: an open kit lets anyone outside Kolaboro check every day that our public log only grows and cosign it with their own key, free on GitHub Actions. Registering witnesses is live since the morning of 4 Nov; our own witness waits for its signing key. partly open
  • Service level objectives with error budgets, computed in your browser from the live measurements, and a published policy for when a budget runs out. Availability today: 100%.
  • Bug bounty scope and hall of fame. Rewards today are thanks and public credit; no cash rewards yet.

Buyer tools6

Free tools; the document and visit tools run on your own device.

  • Contract check: ten clauses from your purchase agreement, each with the exact words and what the law says in Indonesia, Dubai, Thailand, Spain and Portugal. Optional AI (Qwen2.5 via WebLLM) on your own device. Nothing is uploaded.
  • Law notes: 21 short notes on ownership, escrow and deposit rules, each quoting and linking the official text.
  • Permit QR check: your device reads the QR code on a permit, title or ad, shows the link and says whether it belongs to the official agency.
  • Image check verifies C2PA Content Credentials and lists metadata left by generative AI tools. It does not guess whether a picture was made by AI.
  • Site visit app, works offline: checklist, photos with GPS and time, entries chained on your phone, exported as a signed JSON and PDF. Nothing is uploaded.
  • Signed buyer reports: buyers report delays, payment problems or good experiences, signed with a passkey; proof of purchase stays on their device and a person reads every report first. Live since the morning of 4 Nov, no reports yet.

Data and APIs9

Who is behind a project, natural hazards at a plot, how websites and prices move, and events for partners.

  • Natural hazards for any plot: six open sources read live (height, flood model, earthquakes since 1900, tsunami run-ups, volcanoes, coastline), each with source, query and limits. No score.
  • Hazard figures for 168 tracked projects and 27 watched areas, monthly and rounded so no plot can be located.
  • Zoning guide: how to check a plot on the official Indonesian maps. The official services refuse automated queries, tested hourly, so we never show a zone.
  • Company graph: 17 UK projects matched on Companies House, 56 current directors, 1,253 other companies in their networks (counted per project). Anonymised.
  • Court search by exact company name: UK (Find Case Law) when you ask. US (CourtListener) waits for an access token. The Indonesian court database refuses our servers. opens after setup
  • Mystery shopper: the six questions we will ask every developer and how answers will be measured. 0 enquiries sent, so no numbers yet. not started
  • Website change watch: where a developer's page changed, as shapes only, so projects stay anonymous. First run: 148 baselines of 580 projects with their own website.
  • Asking-price index: same-project advertised prices, 30 markets, recomputed in your browser. All markets 100.03 on 3 Nov against 100 on 28 Oct. A few days of history, thin counts, asking prices only.
  • Signed webhooks: an Ed25519-signed event when a project's public record changes, replay-safe, with retries and verifiers in JavaScript and Python. Saving subscriptions is live since the morning of 4 Nov.

Speed, reliability and privacy10

Fixes and hardening, most found by our own checks.

  • Lighthouse every day on 20 pages. Latest: phone performance 83 on average, desktop 97, accessibility 98, SEO 100, best practices 77.
  • Project pages no longer jump while loading: layout shift 1.5 to 0.001; Lighthouse performance 53 to 82 on phones, 69 to 97 on desktop. fix
  • Headings in order: pages with a heading-order problem went from 36 of 40 tested to 0 of 40; footer column titles are real headings on 155 pages. fix
  • Email protection script removed from the 20 pages that show an email address (it carries no security nonce). fix
  • Session recording masked on pages with keys, logins, agent data, developer replies, signed results, and in the contract and permit checks. For about 30 minutes after launch it recorded the contract check's sample text. fix
  • Developer replies with a document link could not be saved (a database pattern limit). Fix prepared, applied with the next database update. fix
  • Deadlines on slow calls: 15 s for a developer's page, 60 to 120 s for an AI model. The chaos drill gained a slow-model scenario: 9 of 9.
  • Security hardening: 27 inline event handlers removed, server-added scripts carry the nonce, project and market pages kept out of shared caches, open data cached 60 s.
  • 0 serious accessibility issues on the client portal, the sample report and the satellite pages. fix
  • Open-source copy in sync with the live site again; property tests 22 of 22.
