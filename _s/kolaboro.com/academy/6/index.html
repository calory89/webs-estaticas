Lesson 6: Reading construction progress

Telling a finished milestone from a staged one.

What finished looks like

Foundations are poured and cured, structure carries the roof, the roof is closed. Formwork, a few columns or materials on site are not a finished milestone.

Common tricks

Work concentrated where the camera points, photos of a different unit, or a show villa presented as progress on yours.

Stages and what proves them

Land clearing: bare soil, survey pegs. Foundations: footings poured and cured, starter bars standing. Structure: columns and beams that carry the next floor. Roof: closed and waterproofed, not just trusses. Finishes: plaster, tiles, windows, fittings. A milestone is reached when the whole element exists for the buyer's unit, not when work on it has started.

Use the plan-vs-reality check

Upload your photos and the brochure render to the progress checker on /technology. It estimates the stage and lists differences. Treat it as a second pair of eyes: your own observation on site decides.

Checklist

  • Photograph the same corners every visit so progress is comparable.
  • Count workers and machines: no one on site on a weekday is a finding.
  • Look for materials that have been sitting for weeks (rust, weeds).

Exercise

Compare a developer's progress photo with your own visit and list every difference.
