Lesson 1: How property is sold abroad

Who's who and where the incentives point.

The people in a sale

A typical overseas purchase involves the developer, a sales agent (often paid by the developer), a notary who formalises the deal, sometimes a lawyer, and the buyer. Only the buyer's own lawyer and a Kolaboro agent work for the buyer.

Follow the money

Ask who pays each person. Sales agents earn when the buyer signs, so their job is to close. Your job is to make sure the buyer signs only what is true.

The four documents that matter

Whatever the country, a buyer's risk sits in four places: who owns the land (the title), what may be built on it (zoning and permits), what the buyer is promised (the contract) and what actually exists (the site). Every Kolaboro check covers all four, and every finding you write points to one of them.

Why buyers abroad are exposed

They decide from renders and video calls, pay in instalments to a company they have never visited, and rely on people paid by the seller. Your value is simple: you are physically there, you have no reason to please the developer, and you write down what you see.

Checklist

  • Name the developer's company exactly as registered, not the brand.
  • Find out who holds the land title today.
  • Ask who pays the sales agent and how much.
  • Note which notary or lawyer the developer recommends: the buyer should choose their own.

Exercise

For a project in your city, list every person involved in a sale and who pays them.
