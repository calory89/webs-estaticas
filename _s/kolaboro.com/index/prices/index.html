Off-plan asking-price index

How the prices developers advertise move, market by market, measured only on the same projects from one daily snapshot to the next. Every figure below is recomputed in your browser from the published changes.

Read this first. Our history starts on 28 October 2026, so this index covers a few days and rests on thin counts. It measures advertised asking prices, not what buyers pay. It is not a forecast and is never extended past the last snapshot.

Official registered sales, for context

Where a government publishes its sales register openly, we show the monthly median next to the index. It is a different measure: it covers every unit sold, and the mix of units changes from month to month. It is never blended into the index.

How it is computed

  1. Observation. A project counts on a given day only if our research agent re-read its page that day and found a positive advertised "from" price with a currency. A value carried over from an earlier day is not an observation.
  2. Pair. The same project, observed on two consecutive snapshots, in the same currency. Projects that changed currency between the two are left out and counted.
  3. Jumps left out. A step of more than 25 percent up or down is left out and counted. In our data such jumps are nearly always a different unit type, a currency mix-up or a misread page, not a price change. We do not correct them.
  4. Step. The geometric mean of new price divided by old price over the kept pairs (a chained Jevons index). Unchanged prices count as no change. A step needs at least 5 pairs.
  5. Chain. The index starts at 100 on the market's base date and multiplies the steps since then. If a step is too thin, the chain is not bridged across it: the base moves to after the gap.

Limits: an advertised "from" price is the cheapest unit on offer, so it moves when that unit sells out as well as when a developer reprices. Prices are read from web pages by a language model and can be misread. Snapshots are not evenly spaced. Per-project amounts and names are not published, only percentage changes and counts, so projects stay anonymous.
