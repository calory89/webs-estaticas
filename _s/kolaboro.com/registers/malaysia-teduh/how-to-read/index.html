How to read Malaysia’s TEDUH housing register for an off-plan project

TEDUH is the public portal of the National Housing Department (Jabatan Perumahan Negara) at the Ministry of Housing and Local Government (KPKT). Its data comes from the Housing Integrated Management System (HIMS); on 27 November 2026 the pages said it was updated to 26 November 2026.

Step by step

  1. Get the developer’s name, the project code if you can, and the advertising and sales permit number from the sale material. KPKT’s FAQ says a developer must hold a valid development licence and an advertising and sales permit approved by the National Housing Department before selling.
  2. Open teduh.kpkt.gov.my, then Perumahan Swasta, then Semakan Status Kemajuan Projek. Choose Projek or Pemaju, type a name or code in Kata Kunci, and narrow by state if needed.
  3. In the results, read KOD PROJEK & NAMA PROJEK, KOD PEMAJU & NAMA PEMAJU, NO. PERMIT and STATUS PROJEK KESELURUHAN. The page links to a note that explains each overall status.
  4. Open Semakan Lesen & Permit and search the same project. It lists NO. LESEN TERKINI and NO. PERMIT TERKINI, with the earlier licence and permit numbers.
  5. Open Senarai Projek Sakit / Terbengkalai, the list of sick and abandoned projects. It shows the developer, the project, the date of the first sale and purchase agreement (TARIKH SPJB PERTAMA), the date it should have been completed (TARIKH PATUT SIAP) and the category.
  6. Check the developer in Senarai Projek dan Pemaju Berlesen. It shows the developer’s total number of projects and how many are sick or abandoned.

What the fields show

Field (official label)What it tells youUse it for
NO. PERMIT, NO. PERMIT TERKINIThe advertising and sales permit on record for the project.Permit or licence number
NO. LESEN TERKINIThe latest development licence on record.Permit or licence number
STATUS PROJEK KESELURUHANThe department’s overall status for the project.Construction progress
TARIKH PATUT SIAPWhen a project on the sick or abandoned list should have been completed.Construction progress
BILANGAN PROJEK SAKIT, BILANGAN PROJEK TERBENGKALAIHow many of the developer’s projects are sick or abandoned.Developer track record

What it does not prove

  • That the unit and price you were offered are covered by the permit.
  • Where your payments are held: TEDUH does not show the project’s bank account. Ask for the account named in the sale and purchase agreement.
  • Coverage: the state filter lists the states of Peninsular Malaysia and the federal territories of Kuala Lumpur and Putrajaya. Sabah and Sarawak are not in the list.
  • A change made after the stated update date: the data is a copy from HIMS as of that date.

Red flags

  • No permit number is shown, or the number in the brochure is not the latest permit (NO. PERMIT TERKINI).
  • The project or the developer appears on the sick or abandoned list.
  • You are asked for a booking fee before the sale and purchase agreement is signed. KPKT’s FAQ says developers may not collect any payment before then, under regulation 11(2) of the Housing Developers (Control and Licensing) Regulations 1989.
  • The agreement gives a longer building period than the law. KPKT’s FAQ gives 24 months for landed and 36 months for strata projects, unless the Housing Controller approves a change.

How it ties to Kolaboro’s six checks

  • Permit or licence number: NO. LESEN and NO. PERMIT.
  • Construction progress: STATUS PROJEK KESELURUHAN, and the sick and abandoned list with TARIKH PATUT SIAP.
  • Developer track record: the developer’s count of total, sick and abandoned projects.
  • Deposit protection, floor or site plans and no guaranteed returns: not shown in TEDUH. Check the sale and purchase agreement.

If handover is late: KPKT’s FAQ says buyers can take claims for late delivery damages (LAD) of up to RM50,000 to the Homebuyer Claims Tribunal (TTPR); larger claims go to the civil courts.
