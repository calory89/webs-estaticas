How to read Florida DBPR condominium filings for an off-plan project

New condominiums in Florida are regulated by the Division of Florida Condominiums, Timeshares, and Mobile Homes, part of the Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR). This guide uses the division’s public records page and the statute, read on 27 November 2026.

Step by step

  1. Know the rule. Under section 718.502 of the Florida Statutes, the developer of a residential condominium must file the buyer documents with the division. Until it has filed, a contract for a unit is voidable by the buyer before closing.
  2. Before that filing, the developer may only take reservation deposits, and only once an escrow agreement and reservation agreement form are filed with the division. The escrow agreement must give you an immediate, unqualified refund on written request.
  3. Open the DBPR page Condominiums, Timeshares, and Mobile Homes, Public Records, then Condominiums by County, and download the file for your county, for example Dade and Monroe Counties.
  4. Find the project by Condo Name or by Street City State Zip, and note its Project Number and File Number.
  5. Read Primary Status and Secondary Status. In the Dade and Monroe file downloaded on 27 November 2026, Primary Status was Approved or Acknowledged and Secondary Status was Recorded or Delinquent. The page Understanding DBPR Codes explains the codes.
  6. Open the Developer Summary Report under Reports. It lists Developer Address, County, Project Name and Number, and Project Type for approved projects.

What the fields show

Field (official label)What it tells youUse it for
Project Number, File NumberThe division’s identifiers for the condominium.Permit or licence number
UnitsThe number of units on file.Matching the advertised unit count
Primary Status, Secondary StatusThe division’s status codes for the project.Permit or licence number
Managing Entity NameThe association or entity that runs the condominium.Knowing who runs the building after handover
Developer Summary ReportApproved projects by developer and county.Developer track record

What it does not prove

  • The county files include only approved, acknowledged and recorded projects; terminated, rejected or withdrawn projects are left out. A project still in pre-sales may not be in them yet, so its absence alone is not proof of a problem.
  • That your deposit is in escrow. Ask for the escrow agreement and the escrow agent’s name and address, which section 718.502 requires in the reservation escrow agreement.
  • Building permits and construction progress: those sit with the county or city building department, not with DBPR.

Red flags

  • The developer asks for more than a reservation deposit but cannot show that it has filed with the division.
  • The reservation agreement does not state your right to an immediate, unqualified refund.
  • You are told to pay the developer rather than the escrow agent. Section 718.502 says the deposit must be payable to the escrow agent, who must give you a receipt.
  • The unit count or address on file does not match the sales material.

How it ties to Kolaboro’s six checks

  • Permit or licence number: the DBPR Project Number and File Number.
  • Deposit protection: the filed escrow agreement and the escrow agent.
  • Developer track record: the Developer Summary Report.
  • Construction progress, floor or site plans and no guaranteed returns: not in these files. Building permits sit with the county or city.

  • DBPR, Condominiums, Timeshares, and Mobile Homes Public Records (layout, county files, reports) (read 27 November 2026)
  • DBPR, condominiums in Dade and Monroe Counties (CSV) (downloaded 27 November 2026)
  • DBPR, Understanding DBPR Codes (read 27 November 2026)
  • Florida Statutes 2026, section 718.502 (filing prior to sale or lease) (read 27 November 2026)
