How to read a Spanish nota simple for an off-plan home

For an off-plan home the registry entry may still be the plot, or a building declared as under construction. This guide follows the Registradores de España help page and the laws it relies on, read on 27 November 2026.

Step by step

  1. Get the registry details from the developer or the notary: which Registro de la Propiedad, the finca number, or the CRU (formerly IDUFIR). The cadastral reference also works when the plot is coordinated with the Catastro.
  2. Open sede.registradores.org, then Propiedad, then Nota simple. The help page lists the ways to search: owner or company name, CRU, registry finca number, cadastral parcel, ID number, or address.
  3. Search by CRU or finca number where you can. The help page says the indexes are kept by finca number or by owner, so a search by other data may be refused or come back negative.
  4. Request the nota simple and pay the fee shown. A search by owner that finds nothing still produces a negative note, which is also charged.
  5. Read the owner (titular). For an off-plan purchase it should be the company that signs your contract.
  6. Read the description and any declared building. Under article 28 of Real Decreto Legislativo 7/2015, a notary can only record a new building under construction (obra nueva en construcción) once the planning authorisation and a technician’s certificate are produced.
  7. Read the charges (cargas) and any prohibitions or restrictions. The help page says these are always stated.

What it does not prove

  • The help page says it plainly: a nota simple is for information only and does not attest the content of the registry entries (no da fe). For that, the registry issues a certificación.
  • That the building licence is valid today or that the works follow it. Licences are granted by the town hall.
  • That your payments are protected, or that the developer will finish on time.
  • Changes after the day it was issued. Ask for a fresh one before signing; the registry also offers Información continuada for updates.

Red flags

  • The titular is not the company that signs your contract.
  • A mortgage or other charge on the plot that the contract does not mention.
  • Prohibitions or restrictions are listed.
  • The seller only shows an old nota simple, or will not give the finca number or CRU.
  • The developer takes payments but cannot show the insurance or bank guarantee that covers them.

How it ties to Kolaboro’s six checks

  • Deposit protection: Ley 38/1999 (additional provision one) requires developers who take money during construction to guarantee its return with legal interest, by insurance or a joint bank guarantee from the building licence onwards, and to receive it through a credit institution. The nota simple does not show this, so ask for the guarantee.
  • Permit or licence number: a recorded obra nueva en construcción shows the planning authorisation was produced to the notary. The licence itself comes from the town hall.
  • Developer track record: the titular gives you the exact company name to look up in the Registro Mercantil.
  • Construction progress, floor or site plans and no guaranteed returns: not in the nota simple.

Sources

  • Registradores de España, Nota simple help page (read 27 November 2026)
  • BOE, Real Decreto Legislativo 7/2015 (land law), article 28 (read 27 November 2026)
  • BOE, Ley 38/1999 de Ordenación de la Edificación, additional provision one (read 27 November 2026)
