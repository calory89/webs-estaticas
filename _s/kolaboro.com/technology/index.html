Plan vs reality: is the building where the invoice says it is?

Upload up to four site photos, optionally the developer's render, what they claim and how much you've paid. The model estimates how much is actually built and flags what doesn't match. Try the sample: it mixes photos from different building sites, the way some "progress updates" sent to buyers do. See if the model notices.

What the buyer gets back
  • How much is actually built, next to how much has been paid
  • Each element of the claim: matches, differs or not visible
  • Concrete discrepancies to raise with the developer
  • Which photos the agent should retake to be sure

Watched from space

Every week our pipeline builds cloud-free satellite composites of the areas we watch and marks in orange the land that has changed since 2023. On a client's plot, that's how we know whether anything happened between two visits, and whether a "progress update" matches what a satellite saw.

Live · reads property documents in any language

Read a property document

Land certificates, sale contracts, building permits, company deeds and payment requests. Upload photos of the pages or the PDF and paste what you were told. We transcribe it, compare it with the claim, flag the risky clauses and list what to verify. The sample certificate is fictional and has three traps.

What you get back
  • The document typed out: parties, numbers, areas, dates, amounts
  • Every mismatch with what you were told
  • Risky clauses, mortgages and blocks
  • What to verify at the land office, with a notary or on site

Live · runs in your browser

Is the price per m² normal?

Put in the price and the built area you were quoted. We compare the price per m² with published market data for that area, and in Bali with what it costs to build. A price far below construction cost is as much a warning sign as one far above the market.

What you get back
  • Your price per m² and per ft²
  • Where it sits against published data for the area
  • In Bali: against the cost of building it
  • The questions to ask if it is out of range

How accurate is the reader?

We made 12 fictional land certificates with known answers, photographed-looking (tilted, noisy, some blurred), each with a developer claim that hides 0 to 3 traps. Then we ran them through the same reader you use above.

Wrong area, wrong owner and an undisclosed mortgage, compared with what the developer claimed. No false alarms on fields that matched.

Average reading time 8.6 s. Real certificates vary more than our samples (older formats, handwriting, damaged pages), so every reading is confirmed at the land office by an agent before it goes into a report. The test set and the script are rebuilt with every model change.

Live · runs in your browser

Has this render been used somewhere else?

Some projects are sold with renders or photos taken from other projects. Drop an image from a brochure or listing: your browser makes a visual fingerprint and compares it with every image we have collected from 59 project websites. The image never leaves your device.

Matches are found even when the image was resized, recompressed or slightly cropped.

Live · anchored in Bitcoin

Verify a piece of evidence yourself

Drop any photo or document from a Kolaboro report. Your browser computes its SHA-256 fingerprint; the file never leaves your device. We look the fingerprint up in our public timestamp record and give you the OpenTimestamps proof, which you can check with free, independent tools.

How it works: each fingerprint is submitted to independent OpenTimestamps calendars (run by the OpenTimestamps project and Eternity Wall). They aggregate it into a Bitcoin transaction within hours. After that, nobody, including us, can backdate or alter the evidence without it showing.
