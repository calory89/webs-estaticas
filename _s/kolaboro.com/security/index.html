Kolaboro / Security

Your documents, your evidence

What happens to what you send us, who can see it, and what we do if we get something wrong.

Who can see your case

  • You, with your own login to the client portal.
  • The local agent handling your case, only with your agreement.
  • The senior reviewer who checks every report before it reaches you.

Developers never see your documents or your report unless you share them. Agents never take money from developers or sellers; breaking that rule ends their work with Kolaboro.

Where it is stored

The website runs on Cloudflare and your case lives in our database on Supabase. Connections are encrypted (HTTPS). We do not sell your data and we use no advertising cookies. Details are in our privacy policy.

Evidence nobody can change later

Every site photo is stamped on the agent's phone with GPS, accuracy and time, and fingerprinted with SHA-256 the moment it is taken. Only the fingerprint, never the photo, is sent to independent OpenTimestamps calendars and anchored in the Bitcoin blockchain. Anyone can check a piece of evidence without trusting us.

How long we keep it

Enquiries for up to 24 months after our last contact. If you become a client, we keep case records as the law requires. You can ask to see, correct or delete your data at any time: hello@kolaboro.com.

If we get something wrong

  • Every report says what could not be verified instead of guessing.
  • Errors are fixed and logged publicly on our corrections page, with the date and what changed.
  • If we can't take your case, or can't start within 7 days of confirming it, you get a full refund (see our terms).
  • We verify facts and give our opinion on the evidence; we are not a law firm or a notary. For legal advice and signing, use an independent lawyer or notary.
  • To the extent the law allows, our liability is limited to the fees you paid for the service.

Report a security issue

Write to hello@kolaboro.com with "Security" in the subject. We reply within two working days.

Our contact is also published in the standard machine-readable file /.well-known/security.txt (RFC 9116).

In scope: kolaboro.com, its API (/api/open, /api/kb, /api/v1), the client portal, the agent app and the admin. Please don't: access other people's data beyond what you need to show the problem, run denial-of-service tests, or use social engineering. If you follow these rules and give us reasonable time to fix the issue before you publish, we will not pursue or support legal action against you.

Things we already do, so you know what to test against: every open API answer is signed (Ed25519), the public log is a Merkle tree with a signed checkpoint and is witnessed in Sigstore Rekor, AI page reviews strip hidden text and flag instructions aimed at AI, third-party scripts load with Subresource Integrity, and a script allowlist runs in report-only mode. Details: Engineering.

Bug bounty

We welcome reports from anyone. Rewards today are thanks and public credit in the hall of fame below; cash rewards are not offered yet, and if we introduce them the amounts will be published on this page first. The safe harbour above applies to everything in scope.

Scope

In scopeOut of scope
  • kolaboro.com and www.kolaboro.com, every page
  • The APIs: /api/open/*, /api/kb/*, /api/v1/*, /api/x/*, /mcp
  • The client portal, the agent app and the admin
  • Our signatures and proofs: forging an Ed25519 signature, a log checkpoint, an inclusion or consistency proof, a witness cosignature or an evidence receipt that our open verifiers accept
  • The AI desk review: a page that makes it report a claim the page does not make, or hide manipulation (also try the desk review red-team playground)
  • Our open-source verifiers in /open/, with their tests in JavaScript, Python and Go at github.com/calory89/kolaboro-open (MIT)
  • Denial of service, load or rate-limit testing, spam
  • Social engineering of our team, agents or users; physical attacks
  • Third-party services themselves (Cloudflare, Supabase, PayPal, GitHub, Anthropic): report those to them
  • Reports from automated scanners without a working proof
  • Missing headers or best practices with no demonstrated impact
  • The websites of developers we track
