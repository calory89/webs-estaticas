What a satellite can and cannot show

The images come from the European Sentinel-2 satellites, which photograph the whole planet every few days at 10 m per pixel. For each period our pipeline takes every image of the area, drops the cloudy pixels and keeps the median, so one clear picture stands for the whole window. Each yearly period covers the same months, starting with the first period, so the comparison is like with like and is not fooled by the rains.

It can show

  • Land being cleared: fields, scrub or forest turning into bare ground
  • New roads, platforms and large roofs appearing
  • How fast an area is filling in, year on year
  • Whether an area has gone quiet

It cannot show

  • A single villa, its floors or its finish: one pixel is 10 m by 10 m
  • Who owns or builds on a plot, or whether it has a permit
  • Work inside a building, or anything under a roof
  • Change that happened between two periods and was then covered again

    Month by month: is the ground filling in?

    Every month, every Sentinel-2 image of each area with its clouds, cloud shadows and haze masked out. We measure the share of the area that looks built-up or bare and how much of it was not built-up or bare in the first clear month. Cloudy months are shown as gaps, not guessed.

    Built-up or bare, per month

    Bars: share of the clear part of the area. Line: share that was not built-up or bare in the first clear month. Grey ticks: too cloudy to measure. Tap a bar to see that month.

    Read this before trusting a number. At 10 m per pixel a cleared plot, a concrete slab and a new roof can look the same, so "built-up or bare" is one class. Haze, wet ground and the sun angle move the monthly figures by a few points; trust a trend over many months, not one month. Data: satellite-optical.json.
