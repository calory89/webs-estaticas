And Bali?

The question everyone asks. Bali sells clean air and green rice terraces. The reality is scooter exhaust, open burning of household waste and dry-season haze. It is not Zurich. It is also not Jakarta.

Bali's WHO-reported annual mean is about 12 µg/m³ (2020–2021): cleaner than most big cities, still more than twice the WHO guideline. Away from the south-coast traffic readings are likely lower, but there are very few monitoring stations.

How the number is made

cigarettes per day = annual mean PM2.5 (µg/m³) ÷ 22

The rule

Richard and Elizabeth Muller (Berkeley Earth, 2015) estimated that breathing 22 µg/m³ of PM2.5 for a day carries roughly the same mortality risk as smoking one cigarette. It is an approximate, population-level comparison of health effects — not the same substances, and not the same damage for every person.

The data

Every number is the 2024 annual mean PM2.5 at that spot from ACAG SatPM2.5 V6.GL.03 (Washington University): satellite aerosol data + the GEOS-Chem atmospheric model, fused with ground monitors, on a ~1 km grid — the dataset behind the Global Burden of Disease estimates. We then calibrate it locally to recent (2022+) WHO ground monitors within 100 km, so it agrees with what stations actually measure (in desert regions the raw satellite reads high). Cities use the mean within ~5 km of the centre; towns and villages their own ~1–3 km. Where the WHO database has a ground monitor, we show it next to it as a cross-check. WHO guidelines: 5 µg/m³ annual (≈ 0.23 cigarettes a day) and 15 µg/m³ over 24 hours.

What it misses

Tobacco smoke carries carcinogens city air mostly doesn't, and a smoker's dose comes in minutes, not hours. Averages also hide winter spikes: Delhi in November runs several times its yearly mean. Treat this as a sense of scale, not a diagnosis.

Babies and children

Children are more vulnerable: they breathe faster and their lungs are still developing (WHO). Per kilo of body weight, a baby breathes about 3× and a school-age child about 2× as much air as an adult (US EPA Exposure Factors Handbook, 2011), so the baby and child numbers multiply the adult figure by 3 and 2. That scales the dose of particles; it is an approximation, not a validated health-risk equivalence for children.

Every town and village

Beyond the main ranking: 31,534 towns of 15,000+ and 193,442 villages of 500+ (GeoNames), each with its own ~1 km satellite value. We find the place nearest to you; if your location is unknown, you get your country's average.

Not all PM2.5 is equal

The Berkeley Earth rule weighs every microgram the same. Studies suggest particles from burning (traffic, coal, wood, fires) are more harmful than natural desert dust, but there is no agreed factor, so we don't adjust the number. Instead each place shows what its PM2.5 is made of — modelled share by source (McDuffie et al., Nature Communications 2021, GEOS-Chem, 2017) — and, where dust matters, the number without desert dust.

Corrections

Figures are rounded and some cities are represented by their nearest monitored station. If you have better data for a city, send it — the number matters more than the ranking.
