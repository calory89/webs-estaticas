The catch

Can a foreigner actually own it?

The biggest reason cheap beachfront stays cheap is that in many of these countries you cannot own the land under it. Here is the rule in every country we list — and each card carries its own route as a tag.

CountryFromListingsRouteWhat it means in practice
Rule of thumb: an apartment is nearly always easier than a house, because in most restrictive countries the ban is on land, not on buildings. Thailand, Sri Lanka, Cambodia and the Philippines all let a foreigner hold a condo unit far more readily than a beachfront plot — which is why so many of the directly-ownable listings here are apartments.
How we verify

Five rules, and what they cut

Anyone can add a listing. Nothing goes live until it clears all five, and rejections are published with their reason.

1. Beachfront means beachfront. A listing only qualifies if its own page says the property fronts the sea — pé na areia, denize sıfır, fronte mare, prima linie la mare, pied dans l'eau, "94 feet of sugar-sand beach". Portal tags count for nothing: one Thai portal tagged a condo "Beachfront" whose own page placed it 4.8 km inland.
2. A building, not a plot. Bare land is far cheaper and would swamp the marketplace. Every listing has a structure on it.
3. Ownership you actually hold. Freehold or a long lease in your own name or structure, with the route for foreign buyers stated. No timeshare, no fractional weeks, no rental-pool-only schemes, and nothing sold on a receipt with no registered deed.
4. A link that opens, or it does not appear. Every URL is re-requested on a schedule, and a listing stays live only while its link keeps answering. There is no "probably fine" category: in the last sweep, two listings returned a hard 404 and thirteen more could not be confirmed to open across three attempts at different times — all fifteen were pulled, even though some were almost certainly still for sale. A link you click and nothing happens costs more than a listing we lose.
5. Facilities are counted, not estimated. Each number is how many amenities the listing actually names. It rewards thorough sellers and penalises terse ones, so read it as evidence of what is documented.
A marketplace, not a census. What you see here is what owners, agents and developers have submitted and what we have verified — nothing more.
