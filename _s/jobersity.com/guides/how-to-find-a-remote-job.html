How to find a remote job

A “remote” job can mean very different things: from anywhere in the world to “remote, but living in the UK with two office days”. Reading that small print saves you many wasted applications.

The kinds of remote

  • Remote anywhere: the company hires in any country, often as a contractor or through an employer of record (EOR).
  • Remote within a region or country: only from certain countries or time zones, for legal or schedule reasons.
  • Hybrid: some days in the office. Check the city even when the ad says remote.

Where the jobs are

Companies that hire remotely post a lot on their own careers pages, which run on systems like Greenhouse, Lever or Ashby, and on specialist boards. At Jobersity we collect jobs from several of those sources every day on our remote job pages.

Standing out without remote experience

  • Show written communication: a clear CV and message are already proof.
  • Mention projects with teams in other cities or countries and the tools you used.
  • State your time zone and how much you can overlap with the team.
  • Keep LinkedIn consistent with your CV: remotely, it's your first impression.

Before you accept

  • How you'll be hired: local payroll, contractor or EOR. It changes your taxes and benefits.
  • Which currency you're paid in and who covers exchange fees.
  • Equipment and costs: laptop, internet, coworking.
  • Whether your right to work covers the country you'll work from.

FAQ

Can I work remotely from another country?

Only if the company allows it and you follow the rules where you live. Some countries offer digital nomad visas.

Do remote jobs pay less?

It depends: some companies pay the same everywhere, others adjust pay by country. Ask early.

Is remote work only for tech jobs?

No. There are remote roles in customer support, sales, marketing, finance, HR, design and writing.
