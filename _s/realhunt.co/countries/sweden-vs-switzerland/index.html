Can foreigners buy?Yes. There are no citizenship or residence rules for buying a home in Sweden, but you need a Swedish personal identity number or coordination number (samordningsnummer) to register ownership.Under the Lex Koller law, EU/EFTA citizens living in Switzerland and holders of a C permit buy like Swiss citizens, and non-EU holders of a B permit can buy a home they live in. People living abroad need a cantonal permit and can usually only buy one holiday home in a tourist area, within national quotas; a proposed tightening was under consultation in 2026.
Yearly property taxHouses pay a municipal property fee (kommunal fastighetsavgift) of SEK 10,425 in 2026, or 0.75% of the tax value if that is lower.More than half of the cantons charge an annual property tax (Liegenschaftssteuer), usually 0.01-0.3% of the assessed value, and owners also pay cantonal wealth tax on the property value minus the mortgage.
Tax on rental incomeNon-resident individuals pay 30% tax on net rental income as capital income; for a private home you can first deduct SEK 40,000 per property and 20% of the rent (the 20% does not apply to a bostadsrätt).Non-residents pay tax on net Swiss rental income at ordinary cantonal and communal rates plus federal direct tax, and some cantons set the rate using worldwide income.
