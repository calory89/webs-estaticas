Can foreigners buy?Yes. Foreigners can buy property in France without major restrictions, and non-EU buyers do not need a permit, although the paperwork can take longer.Under the Lex Koller law, EU/EFTA citizens living in Switzerland and holders of a C permit buy like Swiss citizens, and non-EU holders of a B permit can buy a home they live in. People living abroad need a cantonal permit and can usually only buy one holiday home in a tourist area, within national quotas; a proposed tightening was under consultation in 2026.
Yearly property taxOwners pay an annual property tax (taxe foncière) set by the local authorities, and owners of a furnished second home also pay housing tax (taxe d'habitation), which some communes raise by a surcharge of 5% to 60%.More than half of the cantons charge an annual property tax (Liegenschaftssteuer), usually 0.01-0.3% of the assessed value, and owners also pay cantonal wealth tax on the property value minus the mortgage.
Tax on rental incomeNon-residents pay French income tax on French rental income at a minimum rate of 20% (30% above about €27,500), plus social charges of 17.2%, or 7.5% for people covered by social security in another EU/EEA country or Switzerland.Non-residents pay tax on net Swiss rental income at ordinary cantonal and communal rates plus federal direct tax, and some cantons set the rate using worldwide income.
