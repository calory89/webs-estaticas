Can foreigners buy?Yes. There are no restrictions on foreigners buying property in Germany, though non-EU buyers usually need a bigger down payment to get a loan.Under the Lex Koller law, EU/EFTA citizens living in Switzerland and holders of a C permit buy like Swiss citizens, and non-EU holders of a B permit can buy a home they live in. People living abroad need a cantonal permit and can usually only buy one holiday home in a tourist area, within national quotas; a proposed tightening was under consultation in 2026.
Yearly property taxOwners pay property tax (Grundsteuer), calculated since 2025 as assessed value x a tax rate x a municipal multiplier (Hebesatz) that each municipality sets.More than half of the cantons charge an annual property tax (Liegenschaftssteuer), usually 0.01-0.3% of the assessed value, and owners also pay cantonal wealth tax on the property value minus the mortgage.
Tax on rental incomeNon-resident owners are taxed in Germany on net rental income at the normal progressive income tax rates, but without the basic tax-free allowance (Grundfreibetrag).Non-residents pay tax on net Swiss rental income at ordinary cantonal and communal rates plus federal direct tax, and some cantons set the rate using worldwide income.
