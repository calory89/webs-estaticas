How RealHunt works

## How we know a home is still for sale

Every night a program opens the original advert of each home and writes down what it finds. A home is marked as no longer for sale only when the advert is found removed on two different days, or when a visitor reports it. When the program cannot read an advert, a person checks it by hand, and a home that nobody could confirm for 30 days shows a notice on its page.

### 1. The nightly check reads every advert

Every night between midnight and 6 am (UTC), in small batches, a program opens the original advert of every home on the site that has one, at most once every 20 hours per home. It writes down one of four results: online (and the price, when the page publishes it in a machine-readable form), removed (the portal answers "not found", sends the visitor to a search or to its front page, or the advert says it is no longer available), blocked (the portal refuses programs: an "are you a robot?" page or a refusal) or no answer. Posts on social networks are not read: they need an account.

### 2. One reading changes nothing

Every morning the last two readings of each home, taken on different days, are compared. Two "removed" readings mark the home as no longer for sale. Two "online" readings after that take the mark off again. When two readings on different days show the same new price in euros, between 1% and 50% away from ours, the price on RealHunt is updated and the change is kept in the home's price history. A reading that says "blocked" or "no answer" never changes a home.

### 3. Visitors can tell us

Each home's page lets a visitor report that it is sold or that it is still available. A home counts as no longer for sale when there is at least one such report and more of them than reports that it is still available. The nightly check files its reports in the same way.

### 4. A person checks what the program cannot read

Some portals block programs, and some homes come from posts on social networks. For those, a person opens the advert by hand. When a person has checked a home, its page says so with the date: "Checked by a person on …".

### 5. A notice after 30 days without confirmation

A home whose advert has not been confirmed online in the last 30 days, and whose last reading was not "online", shows the notice "We could not confirm this home is still for sale", with the last day it was seen online, and goes after the other homes in the lists. Homes on RealHunt for less than 4 days are not marked: they may not have been read yet.

### 6. What the page of a home no longer for sale says

We never write "sold" as a fact: we write that the original advert is no longer online, which is what was seen. The page stays, so the price and the area can still be compared, with similar homes below it. Its structured data stops saying "in stock", and the home leaves the email alerts. Every change is in the [log of homes taken off the site](https://realhunt.co/research/removed-homes).

### What you should still do

Adverts change every day and a check made last night can be out of date this morning. Ask the agent or the seller before you plan a visit or send money.
