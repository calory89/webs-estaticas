## Costs and taxes

- What the buying costs are made of: Closing costs vary by state and typically run 2-5% of the loan amount, covering title search and title insurance, recording fees, attorney fees, appraisal and lender fees; since August 2024 the seller no longer has to pay the buyer's agent, so the buyer may pay it unless the seller agrees to; FIRPTA does not apply when you buy but does when a foreigner later sells, with the buyer generally withholding 15% of the sale price.
- Every year: Expect local property tax, which is often around 1% of value but varies widely by city, plus insurance and upkeep. If you rent it out you also file a US tax return each year when you use the net-income election.
- Tax on rental income: By default a non-resident pays 30% of gross rent (or a lower treaty rate), withheld at source, or can elect to treat the rent as business income and pay graduated rates on net income by filing Form 1040-NR and giving Form W-8ECI to the withholding agent.

## How to buy in the United States, step by step

1. **Check the state rules.** Confirm the state you are buying in does not restrict buyers from your country. Rules differ by state and some only cover farmland or land near military bases.
2. **Sign up with a buyer's agent.** Agents must now have a written agreement with you before showing homes. It sets out how much the agent is paid and who pays it.
3. **Make an offer.** Put your price and conditions in a written offer. You can ask the seller to pay all or part of your agent's commission.
4. **Do the checks.** Get a home inspection and a title search so you know the condition of the house and that the title is clean.
5. **Close and record the deed.** Pay the balance and closing costs at closing. The deed is then recorded with the county.
6. **Set up your tax filing.** If you rent the home out, decide between 30% tax on gross rent or the net-income election, which needs Form W-8ECI and a yearly Form 1040-NR.
