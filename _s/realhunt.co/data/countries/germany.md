## Costs and taxes

- What the buying costs are made of: Real estate transfer tax (Grunderwerbsteuer) of 3.5% to 6.5% depending on the state (3.5% in Bavaria, 6.5% in Brandenburg, North Rhine-Westphalia, Saarland and Schleswig-Holstein), plus notary and land registry fees (Notar- und Grundbuchkosten) of about 1.5-2%; if the seller hired the agent, the buyer pays half the commission by law, which is extra.
- Every year: Expect the municipal property tax, insurance, utilities and upkeep. Rental profit is taxable in Germany, so a non-resident landlord deals with a German tax office.
- Tax on rental income: Non-resident owners are taxed in Germany on net rental income at the normal progressive income tax rates, but without the basic tax-free allowance (Grundfreibetrag).

## How to buy in Germany, step by step

1. **Find a home and arrange money.** Search the portals or use a local agent and get financing in place before signing.
2. **Get independent advice.** The notary is neutral and does not act for you, so consider your own lawyer and a survey.
3. **Sign before a notary.** The notary drafts the purchase contract and both sides sign it in front of the notary; you can sign through a certified power of attorney from abroad.
4. **Secure a priority notice.** The notary registers a priority notice (Vormerkung) in the land register to protect you against other sales.
5. **Pay the transfer tax and the price.** Pay the transfer tax when the tax office sends the bill, and pay the price as the contract sets out, often via the notary.
6. **Get registered as owner.** The land registry (Grundbuch) enters you as owner once the transfer tax is paid, which can take 3 to 4 months.
