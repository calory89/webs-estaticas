## Costs and taxes

- What the buying costs are made of: Registration tax 9% on a second home (resale), or VAT on new builds; notary 1-2% + legal ~1% + agent ~3% (GPG roundtrip 10-18%).
- Every year: IMU (council property tax) on second homes and a local waste tax (TARI). For small rural houses these are often a few hundred euros a year.
- Tax on rental income: Optional flat 'cedolare secca' on short lets: 21% for one property, 26% from the second.

## How to buy in Italy, step by step

1. **Get a codice fiscale.** Your Italian tax code. It is free, from the Agenzia delle Entrate in Italy or an Italian consulate abroad.
2. **Make a written offer.** The proposta d'acquisto usually comes with a small deposit. Once accepted, many buyers sign a preliminary contract (compromesso) with a 10–30% deposit.
3. **Check the house.** Ask for the land registry extract (visura catastale), proof the building matches its plans (conformità urbanistica e catastale), the energy certificate (APE) and any unpaid debts on the property.
4. **Sign at the notary.** The deed (rogito) is signed before a notaio, who checks the title and registers the sale. You can sign by power of attorney if you cannot travel.
5. **Pay the taxes.** Buying from a private seller: registration tax 9% of the cadastral value for a second home, or 2% if it becomes your main home, plus €50 + €50 fixed taxes and the notary's fee.
