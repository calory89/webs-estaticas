## Costs and taxes

- What the buying costs are made of: Registration fee on the deed (tinglysningsafgift) of DKK 1,850 plus 0.6% of the price, about 1% for a buyer's lawyer, and, if you borrow, a mortgage registration fee of DKK 1,825 plus 1.25% of the loan; the seller normally pays the agent.
- Every year: Expect property value tax and municipal land tax, both charged through the Danish tax system, plus insurance, utilities and upkeep. Owners abroad may need to file a Danish tax return if they rent the home out.
- Tax on rental income: A non-resident who rents out a Danish home is taxed in Denmark on the profit under the ordinary Danish rules; staying in Denmark more than 3 months at a time or 180 days a year, or working there, makes you fully tax liable.

## How to buy in Denmark, step by step

1. **Check if you need permission.** Use Civilstyrelsen's rules: EU/EEA citizens living in Denmark sign a declaration, everyone else applies for permission for the specific property.
2. **Get a Danish tax number.** If you have no Danish personal number, request one from the Danish Tax Agency with form 04.063_AP_EN.
3. **Set a budget with a bank.** Danish banks usually lend up to 80% of the value, so plan for at least a 20% down payment.
4. **Make an offer and sign with conditions.** The seller's agent drafts the contract; have your own lawyer review it and add bank and lawyer approval clauses (bank- og advokatforbehold).
5. **Let your lawyer check the documents.** Your lawyer reviews the condition report, title and registered charges before the clauses are lifted.
6. **Register the deed.** The deed (skøde) is registered digitally with the Land Registration Court and the registration fee is paid, which makes you the legal owner.
