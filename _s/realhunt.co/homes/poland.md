### Buying costs and rules for foreigners in Poland

Taxes and fees add about 2.5 to 4% to the price: Tax on civil law transactions (PCC) 2% of the value, paid by the buyer within 14 days of the deed; notary fee (taksa notarialna) capped at PLN 1,010 plus 0.4% of the value above PLN 60,000, plus 23% VAT; court fee of PLN 200 to enter ownership in the land register (plus PLN 100 for a new register); optional lawyer about 1%; the agent fee (about 2-3%) depends on the agreement. On the median home on this page (€75,170) that is €1,879 to €3,007, so €77,049 to €78,177 in all.

At the same percentages: on €40,000, €1,000 to €1,600; on €80,000, €2,000 to €3,200; on €150,000, €3,750 to €6,000.

Yearly property tax: Owners pay a local property tax (podatek od nieruchomości) set by the commune, capped in 2026 at PLN 1.25 per m² of usable floor space for flats and houses.

#### Can foreigners buy a house in Poland?

EU, EEA and Swiss citizens buy on the same terms as Poles. Other foreigners can buy an apartment without a permit, but usually need a permit from the Ministry of the Interior (MSWiA) to buy a house or land.

#### What are the buying costs in Poland?

About 2.5 to 4% on top of the price: Tax on civil law transactions (PCC) 2% of the value, paid by the buyer within 14 days of the deed; notary fee (taksa notarialna) capped at PLN 1,010 plus 0.4% of the value above PLN 60,000, plus 23% VAT; court fee of PLN 200 to enter ownership in the land register (plus PLN 100 for a new register); optional lawyer about 1%; the agent fee (about 2-3%) depends on the agreement.
