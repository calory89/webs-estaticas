Buying costs and rules for foreigners in Germany

Can foreigners buy? Yes. There are no restrictions on foreigners buying property in Germany, though non-EU buyers usually need a bigger down payment to get a loan.

Taxes and fees add about 5 to 8.5% to the price: Real estate transfer tax (Grunderwerbsteuer) of 3.5% to 6.5% depending on the state (3.5% in Bavaria, 6.5% in Brandenburg, North Rhine-Westphalia, Saarland and Schleswig-Holstein), plus notary and land registry fees (Notar- und Grundbuchkosten) of about 1.5-2%; if the seller hired the agent, the buyer pays half the commission by law, which is extra. On the median home on this page (€55,000) that is €2,750 to €4,675, so €57,750 to €59,675 in all.

At the same percentages: on €40,000, €2,000 to €3,400; on €80,000, €4,000 to €6,800; on €150,000, €7,500 to €12,750.

Yearly property tax: Owners pay property tax (Grundsteuer), calculated since 2025 as assessed value x a tax rate x a municipal multiplier (Hebesatz) that each municipality sets.
