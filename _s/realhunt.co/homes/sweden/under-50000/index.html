Buying costs and rules for foreigners in Sweden

Can foreigners buy? Yes. There are no citizenship or residence rules for buying a home in Sweden, but you need a Swedish personal identity number or coordination number (samordningsnummer) to register ownership.

Taxes and fees add about 1.5 to 3.5% to the price: Stamp duty on the title (lagfart) of 1.5% of the price or tax value, whichever is higher, plus a SEK 825 fee; if you borrow and need new mortgage deeds (pantbrev), 2% of their amount plus a SEK 375 fee; optional legal advice; the seller normally pays the agent, and a cooperative flat (bostadsrätt) has no lagfart. On the median home on this page (€25,960) that is €389 to €909, so €26,349 to €26,869 in all.

To keep the whole purchase within €50,000, look at asking prices up to about €48,300: all 3 homes on this page fit.
