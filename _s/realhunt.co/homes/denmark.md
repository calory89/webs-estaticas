### Buying costs and rules for foreigners in Denmark

Taxes and fees add about 1.5 to 3% to the price: Registration fee on the deed (tinglysningsafgift) of DKK 1,850 plus 0.6% of the price, about 1% for a buyer's lawyer, and, if you borrow, a mortgage registration fee of DKK 1,825 plus 1.25% of the loan; the seller normally pays the agent. On the median home on this page (€93,200) that is €1,398 to €2,796, so €94,598 to €95,996 in all.

At the same percentages: on €40,000, €600 to €1,200; on €80,000, €1,200 to €2,400; on €150,000, €2,250 to €4,500.

Yearly property tax: Owners pay property value tax (ejendomsværdiskat) of 0.51% on 80% of the official valuation (1.4% above DKK 9.2 million) plus municipal land tax (grundskyld) at a rate set by each municipality, capped at 30 per mille of the land value.

#### Can foreigners buy a house in Denmark?

Without Danish domicile or 5 years of past residence you need permission from the Department of Civil Affairs (Civilstyrelsen); EU/EEA citizens who live and are registered in Denmark can buy a permanent home without permission by signing a declaration to the Land Registration Court. Holiday homes (sommerhuse) need permission, which is only given to people with particularly strong ties to Denmark, such as many years of holidays there.

#### What are the buying costs in Denmark?

About 1.5 to 3% on top of the price: Registration fee on the deed (tinglysningsafgift) of DKK 1,850 plus 0.6% of the price, about 1% for a buyer's lawyer, and, if you borrow, a mortgage registration fee of DKK 1,825 plus 1.25% of the loan; the seller normally pays the agent. On the median home on this page (€93,200) that is €1,398 to €2,796. General information, not tax advice.
