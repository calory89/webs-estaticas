### Buying costs and rules for foreigners in Japan

Can foreigners buy? Yes. Foreigners can buy freehold land and homes on the same terms as Japanese citizens, even on a tourist visa, though buying does not give a visa or residency.

Taxes and fees add about 6 to 9% to the price: The buyer pays an agent fee capped at 3% plus ¥60,000 plus 10% consumption tax (with a special cap of up to ¥330,000 including tax for cheap vacant homes up to ¥8 million), registration tax of 1.5% on land (until March 2029) and 2% standard on the building, real estate acquisition tax of 3% of assessed value, stamp duty, and a judicial scrivener fee of about ¥100,000 to ¥200,000. On the median home on this page (€44,160) that is €2,650 to €3,974, so €46,810 to €48,134 in all.

At the same percentages: on €40,000, €2,400 to €3,600; on €80,000, €4,800 to €7,200; on €150,000, €9,000 to €13,500.

Yearly property tax: Fixed asset tax is 1.4% of the government's assessed value, plus city planning tax of up to 0.3% in urban areas, and small residential plots get a lower tax base.
