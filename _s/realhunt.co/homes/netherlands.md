### Buying costs and rules for foreigners in the Netherlands

Can foreigners buy? Yes. There are no nationality or residence rules and no surcharge for foreign buyers. Some cities, such as The Hague, restrict renting out cheaper homes after purchase without a permit (opkoopbescherming).

Taxes and fees add about 2 to 9% to the price: Transfer tax (overdrachtsbelasting) of 8% if you will not live in the home, such as a buy-to-let or holiday home, or 2% if it becomes your main home; a notary fee for the transfer deed of around EUR 500; Kadaster registration fees; and mortgage costs if you borrow; the seller pays its own agent. On the median home on this page (€325,000) that is €6,500 to €29,250, so €331,500 to €354,250 in all.

At the same percentages: on €40,000, €800 to €3,600; on €80,000, €1,600 to €7,200; on €150,000, €3,000 to €13,500.

Yearly property tax: Owners pay municipal property tax (onroerendezaakbelasting, OZB) on the official WOZ value, at a rate each municipality sets, which in practice is a per mille figure; tenants of homes do not pay it.
