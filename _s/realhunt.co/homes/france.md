### Buying costs and rules for foreigners in France

Can foreigners buy? Yes. Foreigners can buy property in France without major restrictions, and non-EU buyers do not need a permit, although the paperwork can take longer.

Taxes and fees add about 7 to 8.5% to the price: Transfer taxes (droits de mutation) of about 5.8%, or about 6.3% in departments that raised their rate for 2025 to March 2028; notary fee (émoluments) on a sliding scale from 3.87% down to 0.799% plus 20% VAT, about 1.3% on a €150,000 home; land security contribution (contribution de sécurité immobilière) 0.10%; disbursements (débours) of about €800 to €1,200; the agent fee is usually built into the advertised price (FAI) and paid by the seller, sometimes shared. On the median home on this page (€69,500) that is €4,865 to €5,908, so €74,365 to €75,408 in all.

At the same percentages: on €40,000, €2,800 to €3,400; on €80,000, €5,600 to €6,800; on €150,000, €10,500 to €12,750.

Yearly property tax: Owners pay an annual property tax (taxe foncière) set by the local authorities, and owners of a furnished second home also pay housing tax (taxe d'habitation), which some communes raise by a surcharge of 5% to 60%.
