Buying costs and rules for foreigners in France

Can foreigners buy? Yes. Foreigners can buy property in France without major restrictions, and non-EU buyers do not need a permit, although the paperwork can take longer.

Taxes and fees add about 7 to 8.5% to the price: Transfer taxes (droits de mutation) of about 5.8%, or about 6.3% in departments that raised their rate for 2025 to March 2028; notary fee (émoluments) on a sliding scale from 3.87% down to 0.799% plus 20% VAT, about 1.3% on a €150,000 home; land security contribution (contribution de sécurité immobilière) 0.10%; disbursements (débours) of about €800 to €1,200; the agent fee is usually built into the advertised price (FAI) and paid by the seller, sometimes shared. On the median home on this page (€48,000) that is €3,360 to €4,080, so €51,360 to €52,080 in all.

To keep the whole purchase within €50,000, look at asking prices up to about €46,000: 3 of these 6 homes fit.
