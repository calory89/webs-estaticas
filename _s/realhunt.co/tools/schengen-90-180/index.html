Schengen 90/180 calculator: how many days you can spend at your home

Buying a home in Spain, Italy, France or Greece gives no extra days: a visitor from outside the EU can be in the Schengen area for 90 days in any 180-day period, and every day at the house counts. With the two stays in the example, 81 days are used on 1 August 2026 and 9 are left. Replace them with your own dates.

A worked example

Two stays: 1 March 2026 to 30 April 2026 (61 days) and 1 July 2026 to 20 July 2026 (20 days). On 1 August 2026 the 180 days counted run from 3 February 2026, so both stays are inside: 81 days used, 9 left, and entering that day you could stay 9 days, until 9 August 2026. A month later, on 1 September 2026, the first days of March have dropped out of the period: 76 used, and you could stay 70 days.

How it is worked out

  • The European Commission's wording: you must count back 180 days from each day of your stay and make sure the total does not exceed 90. The period moves forward one day at a time; it is not a calendar half-year.
  • This calculator counts the day of entry and the day of exit as days of stay, and looks at the 180 days that end on the date you choose.
  • "You can stay" adds one day at a time from that date and stops before the count passes 90. It assumes you do not leave in between.
  • Days spent with a residence permit or a long-stay visa of a Schengen country are not counted and should not be entered. Citizens of the EU, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland are not subject to the rule.
  • Up to six stays fit in the form, enough for the last 180 days of most owners. The Commission's own calculator, linked below, is the reference.

Questions people ask

Does owning a home in Spain or Italy let me stay longer than 90 days?

No. Owning property gives no right to stay. To spend more than 90 days in any 180 you need a visa or a residence permit of that country. The rules for each country and nationality are in can I buy there?

Do the 90 days reset when I leave?

No. Leaving does not reset anything: each day only stops counting 180 days after it was spent. That is why two long summers in a row can overlap.

Which countries count?

All the countries of the Schengen area together: days in France and days in Spain add up to the same 90. Ireland and Cyprus are in the EU but outside Schengen and keep their own limits.
