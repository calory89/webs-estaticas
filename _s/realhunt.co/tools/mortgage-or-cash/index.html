Mortgage or cash: what a loan adds to the price of a cheap home

On a home of 80,000 with 30% down, a loan of 56,000 at 4% over 20 years costs 339 a month and 25,444 of interest in total: you end up paying 105,444 instead of 80,000, and in exchange you keep 56,000 in hand on the day you buy. Use the rate a bank has actually offered you.

A worked example

The same loan over 10 years: 567 a month and 12,037 of interest, 13,407 less than over 20 years. The monthly payment rises by 228; the home costs 92,037 in total. In cash it costs 80,000 and no interest, but the 56,000 you would have borrowed are no longer in your account.

How it is worked out

  • Loan = price − down payment. Monthly payment = loan × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n), with r the yearly rate divided by 12 and n the number of months. It is the same formula as the mortgage box on every home page.
  • Interest = monthly payment × months − loan. Extra cost of the loan = interest + the cost of setting it up, if you enter one.
  • The rate is fixed for the whole term and is yours to type: the calculator has no rate of its own and does not guess what rates or prices will do.
  • Taxes and fees of the purchase are the same with or without a loan, so they are left out. Add them with the buying costs calculator.

Questions people ask

Can I get a mortgage on a very cheap home abroad?

Often not. Many banks set a minimum loan, lend a smaller share of the price to non-residents and refuse homes that need work. That is why most cheap homes are bought in cash. Ask a bank of the country before you make an offer that depends on a loan.

Is paying in cash always cheaper?

In money paid for the home, yes: the loan in the example adds 25,444. Whether that is worth it depends on what else you need the cash for, such as repairs, the taxes of the purchase and a reserve. The calculator shows both numbers and leaves the decision to you.

Does a mortgage change the taxes of the purchase?

The transfer tax or VAT is the same. A loan adds its own costs (valuation, bank fees, sometimes a tax on the mortgage deed). See the buying costs calculator for the purchase itself.
