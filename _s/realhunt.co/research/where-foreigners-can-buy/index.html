Where foreigners can and cannot buy a home: the rules in 20 countries (2026)

Of 20 countries, 12 let a buyer of any nationality buy a home on the same terms as locals (Dominican Republic, France, Germany, Italy, Japan, Netherlands, Norway, Portugal, Spain, Sweden, United Kingdom, United States), 5 allow it with conditions such as a permit, a trust or a limit on land, and 3 restrict it for most people who do not live there (Canada, Denmark, Switzerland). EU citizens buy without conditions in 16 of them.

Rules as of November 2026, for 14 nationalities. General information, not legal advice.

Who can buy a home where: the rule in each country

  • Bulgaria

    EU citizens
    ✓ Yes
    Other nationalities
    ! Yes, with conditions

    Yes, buildings and apartments, with rights similar to locals; per GPG, foreigners can't own land directly, so a Bulgarian company is the usual route.

  • Canada

    EU citizens
    ✕ Restricted
    Other nationalities
    ✕ Restricted

    Mostly not for now: a federal ban stops non-Canadians from buying homes in cities and larger towns until January 1, 2027, with exceptions such as work permit holders and spouses of citizens or permanent residents. The ban does not cover rural areas and small towns, and the government has said it does not plan simply to extend it, but a replacement is being studied.

  • Cyprus

    EU citizens
    ✓ Yes
    Other nationalities
    ! Yes, with conditions

    EU citizens buy without restrictions. Non-EU buyers need permission from the Council of Ministers, applied for through the District Officer, and are normally limited to one home or a plot of up to 4,014 m².

    By nationality · How to buy in Cyprus

  • Denmark

    EU citizens
    ! Yes, with conditions
    Other nationalities
    ✕ Restricted

    Without Danish domicile or 5 years of past residence you need permission from the Department of Civil Affairs (Civilstyrelsen); EU/EEA citizens who live and are registered in Denmark can buy a permanent home without permission by signing a declaration to the Land Registration Court. Holiday homes (sommerhuse) need permission, which is only given to people with particularly strong ties to Denmark, such as many years of holidays there.

  • Dominican Republic

    EU citizens
    ✓ Yes
    Other nationalities
    ✓ Yes

    Yes. Foreigners have the same rights as Dominican citizens to buy land and homes, with no restrictions.

  • France

    EU citizens
    ✓ Yes
    Other nationalities
    ✓ Yes

    Yes. Foreigners can buy property in France without major restrictions, and non-EU buyers do not need a permit, although the paperwork can take longer.

  • Germany

    EU citizens
    ✓ Yes
    Other nationalities
    ✓ Yes

    Yes. There are no restrictions on foreigners buying property in Germany, though non-EU buyers usually need a bigger down payment to get a loan.

    By nationality · How to buy in Germany

  • Greece

    EU citizens
    ✓ Yes
    Other nationalities
    ! Yes, with conditions

    Yes. Buyers from outside the EU and EFTA need a permit to buy in designated border areas (parts of northern Greece and eastern Aegean islands such as the Dodecanese and Rhodes). The government has announced a 15% transfer tax for non-EU buyers of homes from 1 January 2027, not yet final in September 2026.

  • Italy

    EU citizens
    ✓ Yes
    Other nationalities
    ✓ Yes

    Yes for EU citizens; non-EU buyers need reciprocity (US, UK, Canada, Australia qualify).

    By nationality · How to buy in Italy

  • Japan

    EU citizens
    ✓ Yes
    Other nationalities
    ✓ Yes

    Yes. Foreigners can buy freehold land and homes on the same terms as Japanese citizens, even on a tourist visa, though buying does not give a visa or residency.

    By nationality · How to buy in Japan

  • Mexico

    EU citizens
    ! Yes, with conditions
    Other nationalities
    ! Yes, with conditions

    Yes, but in the Restricted Zone (within 50 km of the coast or 100 km of borders, which covers Tulum and Playa del Carmen) only through a fideicomiso bank trust or a Mexican company; direct ownership elsewhere.

  • Netherlands

    EU citizens
    ✓ Yes
    Other nationalities
    ✓ Yes

    Yes. There are no nationality or residence rules and no surcharge for foreign buyers. Some cities, such as The Hague, restrict renting out cheaper homes after purchase without a permit (opkoopbescherming).

  • Norway

    EU citizens
    ✓ Yes
    Other nationalities
    ✓ Yes

    Yes. Anyone with the funds can buy property in Norway, and a built plot of up to 2 decares normally needs no concession (konsesjon) or self-declaration; owning property gives no right to live there.

    By nationality · How to buy in Norway

  • Poland

    EU citizens
    ✓ Yes
    Other nationalities
    ! Yes, with conditions

    EU, EEA and Swiss citizens buy on the same terms as Poles. Other foreigners can buy an apartment without a permit, but usually need a permit from the Ministry of the Interior (MSWiA) to buy a house or land.

  • Portugal

    EU citizens
    ✓ Yes
    Other nationalities
    ✓ Yes

    Yes, same rights as Portuguese citizens (IMT increase for foreign second-home buyers announced Sept 2025).

    By nationality · How to buy in Portugal

  • Spain

    EU citizens
    ✓ Yes
    Other nationalities
    ✓ Yes

    Yes, no restrictions at present; the government has proposed a 100% tax on (or ban for) non-EU non-resident buyers, not enacted.

    By nationality · How to buy in Spain

  • Sweden

    EU citizens
    ✓ Yes
    Other nationalities
    ✓ Yes

    Yes. There are no citizenship or residence rules for buying a home in Sweden, but you need a Swedish personal identity number or coordination number (samordningsnummer) to register ownership.

    By nationality · How to buy in Sweden

  • Switzerland

    EU citizens
    ✕ Restricted
    Other nationalities
    ✕ Restricted

    Under the Lex Koller law, EU/EFTA citizens living in Switzerland and holders of a C permit buy like Swiss citizens, and non-EU holders of a B permit can buy a home they live in. People living abroad need a cantonal permit and can usually only buy one holiday home in a tourist area, within national quotas; a proposed tightening was under consultation in 2026.

    By nationality · How to buy in Switzerland

  • United Kingdom

    EU citizens
    ✓ Yes
    Other nationalities
    ✓ Yes

    Yes. There are no nationality restrictions on buying homes anywhere in the UK and no visa is needed, but buying does not give a right to live there.

    By nationality · How to buy in the United Kingdom

  • United States

    EU citizens
    ✓ Yes
    Other nationalities
    ✓ Yes

    Yes. There is no federal ban on foreigners buying homes, but about 30 states have added some limits, and Texas and Florida restrict buyers from certain countries such as China.
