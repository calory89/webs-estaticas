Buying costs by country: taxes and fees on top of the price (2026)

Of 29 countries and destinations, the taxes and fees you pay on top of the price are lowest in Georgia (0.6–1.1%), Albania (1.3–1.9%), Denmark (1.5–3%) and highest in Northern Cyprus (12–22%), Lombok (6.5–18%), Bali (6.5–18%). On a €100,000 home that is from €600 to €22,000.

Transfer tax or VAT, stamp duty, notary, land registry and lawyer, as a share of the price. Compiled from the sources linked for each country; general information, not tax advice.

What the costs are made of, and the yearly tax, country by country

  • Georgia: 0.6–1.1%

    No transfer tax or stamp duty; registry fee 50-200 GEL, notary under 0.1%, legal 0.5-1%; agent 2-3% if buyer-paid.

    Yearly property tax: Only for tax residents: 0.05-1% of value depending on household income; non-residents generally exempt.

    Tax on rental income: 5% of gross rent on residential property (long- or short-term), residents and non-residents alike.

    Work it out for your price · Sources

  • Albania: 1.3–1.9%

    Notary 0.23-0.35% + lawyer 0.1-0.5% + agent 1-2%; transfer tax (0.5-3%) is paid by the seller (GPG, 2021).

    Yearly property tax: Local property tax ~0.05% of cadastral value (about EUR 75/yr on EUR 150k).

    Tax on rental income: Flat 15% on rental income, including short-term, declared via the DIVA platform.

    Work it out for your price · Sources

  • Denmark: 1.5–3%

    Registration fee on the deed (tinglysningsafgift) of DKK 1,850 plus 0.6% of the price, about 1% for a buyer's lawyer, and, if you borrow, a mortgage registration fee of DKK 1,825 plus 1.25% of the loan; the seller normally pays the agent.

    Yearly property tax: Owners pay property value tax (ejendomsværdiskat) of 0.51% on 80% of the official valuation (1.4% above DKK 9.2 million) plus municipal land tax (grundskyld) at a rate set by each municipality, capped at 30 per mille of the land value.

    Tax on rental income: A non-resident who rents out a Danish home is taxed in Denmark on the profit under the ordinary Danish rules; staying in Denmark more than 3 months at a time or 180 days a year, or working there, makes you fully tax liable.

    Work it out for your price · Sources

  • Montenegro: 1–4%

    New build: 21% VAT is normally included in the developer price, buyer pays notary + ~1% legal; resale: 3% transfer tax (progressive on higher values) + notary + ~1% legal (GPG 4.01%).

    Yearly property tax: Municipal, ~0.25-1% of assessed value (higher rates possible for tourist properties).

    Tax on rental income: Around 15% withholding for non-residents.

    Work it out for your price · Sources

  • Sweden: 1.5–3.5%

    Stamp duty on the title (lagfart) of 1.5% of the price or tax value, whichever is higher, plus a SEK 825 fee; if you borrow and need new mortgage deeds (pantbrev), 2% of their amount plus a SEK 375 fee; optional legal advice; the seller normally pays the agent, and a cooperative flat (bostadsrätt) has no lagfart.

    Yearly property tax: Houses pay a municipal property fee (kommunal fastighetsavgift) of SEK 10,425 in 2026, or 0.75% of the tax value if that is lower.

    Tax on rental income: Non-resident individuals pay 30% tax on net rental income as capital income; for a private home you can first deduct SEK 40,000 per property and 20% of the rent (the 20% does not apply to a bostadsrätt).

    Work it out for your price · Sources

  • Canada: 1.5–4%

    Buyers usually pay provincial land transfer tax on a sliding scale (Ontario: 0.5% up to $55,000 and 1% from $55,000 to $250,000), legal fees of about $500 to $1,500, title insurance of about $250 and an inspection of $300 to $1,000, while the seller usually pays the agents; foreign buyers also pay Ontario's 25% non-resident speculation tax anywhere in Ontario and British Columbia's 20% additional tax in Metro Vancouver and four other regions.

    Yearly property tax: Property tax is set by each city as a share of assessed value, from about 0.3% in Vancouver to 2.12% in Saint John in 2026.

    Tax on rental income: Non-residents have 25% of gross rent withheld, but they can file a Section 216 return to pay tax on net rent instead, and Form NR6 lets the agent withhold on net rent.

    Work it out for your price · Sources

  • Norway: 2.5–3%

    Document duty (dokumentavgift) of 2.5% of market value, a registration fee (tinglysingsgebyr) of NOK 545 for the deed and NOK 545 for any mortgage document; a flat in a housing cooperative (borettslag) has no document duty; the seller normally pays the agent.

    Yearly property tax: Some municipalities charge property tax (eiendomsskatt), at most 4 per mille of the value after a reduction of at least 30%, and some also give a basic deduction; many charge nothing.

    Tax on rental income: Rental profit is normally taxed at 22%, and the Tax Administration says its rental rules also apply to owners who are tax resident abroad.

    Work it out for your price · Sources

  • Vietnam: 2.5–3.1%

    New developer apartment: 10% VAT usually already in the price + 0.5% registration fee + 2% maintenance fund + notary/admin; GPG lists 2.55-3.1% buyer costs.

    Yearly property tax: Non-agricultural land-use tax 0.03-0.15% of state land value; negligible for apartments.

    Tax on rental income: 10% of gross rent (5% VAT + 5% PIT) above the exemption threshold; threshold raised to VND 1 billion/yr from 1 Jan 2026 (older guides say VND 100 million).

    Work it out for your price · Sources

  • Cyprus: 2.5–3.5%

    Land Registry transfer fees of 3% up to €85,000, 5% to €170,000 and 8% above, halved for resales without VAT (1.5%, 2.5% and 4%); stamp duty abolished from 1 January 2026; lawyer about 1% plus VAT; small Land Registry charges.

    Yearly property tax: There is no annual national property tax (abolished in 2017); owners pay municipal fees of about €90 to €300 a year, plus sewerage and local authority charges.

    Tax on rental income: Non-residents pay income tax on Cyprus rental income at 20% to 35% after deducting 20% of the gross rent and loan interest, plus a 2.65% health contribution (GHS); the special defence contribution does not apply to them.

    Work it out for your price · Sources

  • ireland: 2.5–3.5%

    Stamp duty of 1% of the price up to €1 million (2% from €1 million to €1.5 million and 6% above), solicitor's fees of about 1-1.5% plus 23% VAT, and registration fees of €425 to €800; the seller pays the estate agent, whose fee is usually built into the asking price.

    Yearly property tax: Local Property Tax (LPT): from 2026 a home valued up to €240,000 pays a basic €95 a year and one valued €240,001 to €315,000 pays €235; each council can raise or lower the basic rate by up to 15%, and most raised it by 15% for 2026.

    Work it out for your price · Sources

  • Switzerland: 1–5%

    Transfer tax (Handänderungssteuer, droits de mutation) 0% to about 3.3% depending on the canton; notary about 0.1-0.5%; land register fee, a small fixed amount or percentage; optional lawyer 1-2%; the agent fee (2-3%) is paid by the seller.

    Yearly property tax: More than half of the cantons charge an annual property tax (Liegenschaftssteuer), usually 0.01-0.3% of the assessed value, and owners also pay cantonal wealth tax on the property value minus the mortgage.

    Tax on rental income: Non-residents pay tax on net Swiss rental income at ordinary cantonal and communal rates plus federal direct tax, and some cantons set the rate using worldwide income.

    Work it out for your price · Sources

  • Poland: 2.5–4%

    Tax on civil law transactions (PCC) 2% of the value, paid by the buyer within 14 days of the deed; notary fee (taksa notarialna) capped at PLN 1,010 plus 0.4% of the value above PLN 60,000, plus 23% VAT; court fee of PLN 200 to enter ownership in the land register (plus PLN 100 for a new register); optional lawyer about 1%; the agent fee (about 2-3%) depends on the agreement.

    Yearly property tax: Owners pay a local property tax (podatek od nieruchomości) set by the commune, capped in 2026 at PLN 1.25 per m² of usable floor space for flats and houses.

    Tax on rental income: Private rental income is taxed as a flat lump sum (ryczałt) of 8.5% of revenue up to PLN 100,000 a year and 12.5% above that.

    Work it out for your price · Sources

  • United States: 2–5%

    Closing costs vary by state and typically run 2-5% of the loan amount, covering title search and title insurance, recording fees, attorney fees, appraisal and lender fees; since August 2024 the seller no longer has to pay the buyer's agent, so the buyer may pay it unless the seller agrees to; FIRPTA does not apply when you buy but does when a foreigner later sells, with the buyer generally withholding 15% of the sale price.

    Yearly property tax: Property tax is set locally and in 2024 averaged about 1.22% of value across the largest city in each state, from 0.30% in Honolulu to 3.02% in Detroit.

    Tax on rental income: By default a non-resident pays 30% of gross rent (or a lower treaty rate), withheld at source, or can elect to treat the rent as business income and pay graduated rates on net income by filing Form 1040-NR and giving Form W-8ECI to the withholding agent.

    Work it out for your price · Sources

  • Netherlands: 2–9%

    Transfer tax (overdrachtsbelasting) of 8% if you will not live in the home, such as a buy-to-let or holiday home, or 2% if it becomes your main home; a notary fee for the transfer deed of around EUR 500; Kadaster registration fees; and mortgage costs if you borrow; the seller pays its own agent.

    Yearly property tax: Owners pay municipal property tax (onroerendezaakbelasting, OZB) on the official WOZ value, at a rate each municipality sets, which in practice is a per mille figure; tenants of homes do not pay it.

    Tax on rental income: Non-residents declare Dutch property in box 3, where tax is 36% on a deemed return of 6% of the property's value in 2026, after a tax-free allowance of EUR 59,357 per person, regardless of actual rent.

    Work it out for your price · Sources

  • Dominican Republic: 4.25–7%

    The buyer pays a 3% transfer tax (0% for qualifying first purchases under CONFOTUR tourism incentives), legal fees of about 1-2% and notary fees of about 0.25-1%, plus registration costs, while the seller pays the 2-3% agent fee.

    Yearly property tax: The yearly IPI tax is 1% of the part of a person's total Dominican property value, as assessed by the tax office DGII, above RD$10,695,494 in 2026, so a single cheap home is often below the threshold.

    Tax on rental income: Rent paid abroad to a non-resident is generally subject to 27% withholding.

    Work it out for your price · Sources

  • Mexico: 3.5–8%

    Acquisition tax 2-5% + notary 0.5-1% + legal 1-1.5% (GPG 3.5-7.5%), plus 0.5-1.5% for fideicomiso/permit setup; TheLatinvestor budgets 5-8%.

    Yearly property tax: Municipal predial, set locally on cadastral value; usually small, often less than HOA fees.

    Tax on rental income: Non-residents: 25% ISR withholding on gross rent; plus 16% IVA on short-term stays (Airbnb remits on its bookings).

    Work it out for your price · Sources

  • Bulgaria: 3.2–8.5%

    Transfer tax 0.1-3% (municipal; Sofia 2.5%) + notary 0.1-1.5% + legal 0.5-1% + agent 2.5-3%.

    Yearly property tax: 0.01-0.45% of the property's tax value plus a municipal garbage fee.

    Tax on rental income: Non-residents: 10% final tax on gross rent, paid quarterly, no deductions.

    Work it out for your price · Sources

  • United Kingdom: 3–9%

    In England and Northern Ireland, Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT) is 0% up to £125,000 and 2% from £125,001 to £250,000, plus a 2% surcharge for non-UK residents and a further 5% if you already own a home anywhere, so a non-resident who owns another home pays 7% up to £125,000 and 9% to £250,000; solicitor (conveyancer) about 0.6-1% with a typical minimum of around £2,100 including VAT; Land Registry fee £20 to £230; searches extra; the seller pays the estate agent.

    Yearly property tax: Owners or occupiers pay council tax to the local council, and many councils in England charge second homes a premium of up to 100% from April 2025.

    Tax on rental income: Non-resident landlords pay UK income tax on rent at 20%, 40% or 45%, with 20% deducted at source by the agent or tenant unless HMRC approves gross payment, and the rates on property income are set to rise to 22%, 42% and 47% from April 2027.

    Work it out for your price · Sources

  • Greece: 5.5–7%

    Transfer tax 3.09% of the price or the taxable value, whichever is higher; notary about 0.8-1% plus VAT; land registry fees about 0.5-0.7%; lawyer 1-2% plus VAT; if an agent is used, the buyer usually pays a commission of 1-3% plus VAT on top.

    Yearly property tax: Owners on 1 January pay the unified property tax (ENFIA), calculated per property from its location, size, age and official zone value, with an extra charge if total Greek property exceeds €500,000.

    Tax on rental income: Non-residents pay Greek tax on Greek rental income at 15% up to €12,000, 25% to €24,000, 35% to €36,000 and 45% above, after a flat 5% deduction for expenses.

    Work it out for your price · Sources

  • uruguay: 5–8%

    Transfer tax (ITP) of 2% for the buyer and 2% for the seller, on the cadastral value (valor real) set by the Dirección Nacional de Catastro; notary fees of about 3%; and an agent's fee of about 3%.

    Work it out for your price · Sources

  • Germany: 5–8.5%

    Real estate transfer tax (Grunderwerbsteuer) of 3.5% to 6.5% depending on the state (3.5% in Bavaria, 6.5% in Brandenburg, North Rhine-Westphalia, Saarland and Schleswig-Holstein), plus notary and land registry fees (Notar- und Grundbuchkosten) of about 1.5-2%; if the seller hired the agent, the buyer pays half the commission by law, which is extra.

    Yearly property tax: Owners pay property tax (Grundsteuer), calculated since 2025 as assessed value x a tax rate x a municipal multiplier (Hebesatz) that each municipality sets.

    Tax on rental income: Non-resident owners are taxed in Germany on net rental income at the normal progressive income tax rates, but without the basic tax-free allowance (Grundfreibetrag).

    Work it out for your price · Sources

  • Japan: 6–9%

    The buyer pays an agent fee capped at 3% plus ¥60,000 plus 10% consumption tax (with a special cap of up to ¥330,000 including tax for cheap vacant homes up to ¥8 million), registration tax of 1.5% on land (until March 2029) and 2% standard on the building, real estate acquisition tax of 3% of assessed value, stamp duty, and a judicial scrivener fee of about ¥100,000 to ¥200,000.

    Yearly property tax: Fixed asset tax is 1.4% of the government's assessed value, plus city planning tax of up to 0.3% in urban areas, and small residential plots get a lower tax base.

    Tax on rental income: Rent paid to a non-resident is subject to 20.42% withholding (except when an individual rents it as their own home), and the owner must file a tax return each year through a tax representative in Japan to settle the final tax.

    Work it out for your price · Sources

  • Portugal: 3–12%

    IMT transfer tax 0-8% (progressive) + stamp duty 0.8% + notary/registry 0.9-1.2% + legal 1.25-2%.

    Yearly property tax: IMI 0.3-0.45% of taxable value (urban), set by municipality.

    Tax on rental income: Long-term: 25% flat for non-residents; short-term (AL) under the simplified regime only 35-50% of gross is taxable.

    Work it out for your price · Sources

  • France: 7–8.5%

    Transfer taxes (droits de mutation) of about 5.8%, or about 6.3% in departments that raised their rate for 2025 to March 2028; notary fee (émoluments) on a sliding scale from 3.87% down to 0.799% plus 20% VAT, about 1.3% on a €150,000 home; land security contribution (contribution de sécurité immobilière) 0.10%; disbursements (débours) of about €800 to €1,200; the agent fee is usually built into the advertised price (FAI) and paid by the seller, sometimes shared.

    Yearly property tax: Owners pay an annual property tax (taxe foncière) set by the local authorities, and owners of a furnished second home also pay housing tax (taxe d'habitation), which some communes raise by a surcharge of 5% to 60%.

    Tax on rental income: Non-residents pay French income tax on French rental income at a minimum rate of 20% (30% above about €27,500), plus social charges of 17.2%, or 7.5% for people covered by social security in another EU/EEA country or Switzerland.

    Work it out for your price · Sources

  • Italy: 4–15%

    Registration tax 9% on a second home (resale), or VAT on new builds; notary 1-2% + legal ~1% + agent ~3% (GPG roundtrip 10-18%).

    Yearly property tax: IMU 0.4-1.06% on second homes (on cadastral value).

    Tax on rental income: Optional flat 'cedolare secca' on short lets: 21% for one property, 26% from the second.

    Work it out for your price · Sources

  • Spain: 7.5–14%

    New build: 10% IVA + 0.5-1.5% AJD stamp duty + notary 0.5-1% + legal 1-2%; resale: ITP transfer tax 6-11% instead of IVA/AJD.

    Yearly property tax: IBI 0.4-1.1% of cadastral value (municipal), plus imputed income tax on unlet homes.

    Tax on rental income: Non-residents: EU/EEA 19% on net income; non-EU 24% on gross with no deductions.

    Work it out for your price · Sources

  • Bali: 6.5–18%

    BPHTB transfer tax 5% + notary 1-2.5% + legal 0.5-1.5% (GPG: 6.5-9%); new developer units add 11% PPN/VAT (stack ~17-18% without the 2026 PPN-DTP incentive, ~6-9% with it). Plain leasehold deals mostly notary/legal.

    Yearly property tax: PBB land & building tax, up to ~0.5% of assessed NJOP-based value (low hundreds USD on a $300k villa).

    Tax on rental income: Non-residents: 20% withholding on gross rent (10% possible under a tax treaty); PT PMA companies 22% corporate tax.

    Work it out for your price · Sources

  • Lombok: 6.5–18%

    BPHTB 5% + notary 1-2.5% + legal 0.5-1.5%; +11% PPN/VAT on new developer units unless the 2026 VAT incentive applies.

    Yearly property tax: PBB, up to ~0.5% of assessed (NJOP-based) value.

    Tax on rental income: Non-residents: 20% withholding on gross rent (10% possible under a treaty); PT PMA 22% corporate tax.

    Work it out for your price · Sources

  • Northern Cyprus: 12–22%

    Title deed transfer fee for foreigners 6-12% (sources disagree: 9% most cited, 12% in one 2026 guide) + 5% VAT on new builds (one source says 19%) + 0.5% stamp duty + legal GBP 1,000-2,500 + transformer fee GBP 1,500-3,000 on new builds.

    Yearly property tax: Per m2, very low: about GBP 10-100/yr for a typical apartment or villa.

    Tax on rental income: 10% of gross rental income (Investra, 2026).

    Work it out for your price · Sources

Method and limits

  • Ranges cover new and resale homes and different regions; where sources disagree, the range is wider. The order uses the middle of each range.
  • Each country page lists the sources that were opened and read, and the year of the figures.
  • Rules and rates change. This is general information, not tax or legal advice: check with a local lawyer or notary before you sign.
  • Agent fees paid by the seller, mortgage costs and currency exchange are not included.
