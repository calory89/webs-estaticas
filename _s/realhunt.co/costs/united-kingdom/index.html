Buying a home in the United Kingdom: what it really costs

Can foreigners buy?
Yes. There are no nationality restrictions on buying homes anywhere in the UK and no visa is needed, but buying does not give a right to live there.
Costs on top of the price
3-9% · In England and Northern Ireland, Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT) is 0% up to £125,000 and 2% from £125,001 to £250,000, plus a 2% surcharge for non-UK residents and a further 5% if you already own a home anywhere, so a non-resident who owns another home pays 7% up to £125,000 and 9% to £250,000; solicitor (conveyancer) about 0.6-1% with a typical minimum of around £2,100 including VAT; Land Registry fee £20 to £230; searches extra; the seller pays the estate agent.
Yearly property tax
Owners or occupiers pay council tax to the local council, and many councils in England charge second homes a premium of up to 100% from April 2025.
Tax on rental income
Non-resident landlords pay UK income tax on rent at 20%, 40% or 45%, with 20% deducted at source by the agent or tenant unless HMRC approves gross payment, and the rates on property income are set to rise to 22%, 42% and 47% from April 2027.
