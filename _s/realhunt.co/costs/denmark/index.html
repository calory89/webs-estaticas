Buying a home in Denmark: what it really costs

Can foreigners buy?
Without Danish domicile or 5 years of past residence you need permission from the Department of Civil Affairs (Civilstyrelsen); EU/EEA citizens who live and are registered in Denmark can buy a permanent home without permission by signing a declaration to the Land Registration Court. Holiday homes (sommerhuse) need permission, which is only given to people with particularly strong ties to Denmark, such as many years of holidays there.
Costs on top of the price
1.5-3% · Registration fee on the deed (tinglysningsafgift) of DKK 1,850 plus 0.6% of the price, about 1% for a buyer's lawyer, and, if you borrow, a mortgage registration fee of DKK 1,825 plus 1.25% of the loan; the seller normally pays the agent.
Yearly property tax
Owners pay property value tax (ejendomsværdiskat) of 0.51% on 80% of the official valuation (1.4% above DKK 9.2 million) plus municipal land tax (grundskyld) at a rate set by each municipality, capped at 30 per mille of the land value.
Tax on rental income
A non-resident who rents out a Danish home is taxed in Denmark on the profit under the ordinary Danish rules; staying in Denmark more than 3 months at a time or 180 days a year, or working there, makes you fully tax liable.
