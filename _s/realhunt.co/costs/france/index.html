Buying a home in France: what it really costs

Can foreigners buy?
Yes. Foreigners can buy property in France without major restrictions, and non-EU buyers do not need a permit, although the paperwork can take longer.
Costs on top of the price
7-8.5% · Transfer taxes (droits de mutation) of about 5.8%, or about 6.3% in departments that raised their rate for 2025 to March 2028; notary fee (émoluments) on a sliding scale from 3.87% down to 0.799% plus 20% VAT, about 1.3% on a €150,000 home; land security contribution (contribution de sécurité immobilière) 0.10%; disbursements (débours) of about €800 to €1,200; the agent fee is usually built into the advertised price (FAI) and paid by the seller, sometimes shared.
Yearly property tax
Owners pay an annual property tax (taxe foncière) set by the local authorities, and owners of a furnished second home also pay housing tax (taxe d'habitation), which some communes raise by a surcharge of 5% to 60%.
Tax on rental income
Non-residents pay French income tax on French rental income at a minimum rate of 20% (30% above about €27,500), plus social charges of 17.2%, or 7.5% for people covered by social security in another EU/EEA country or Switzerland.
