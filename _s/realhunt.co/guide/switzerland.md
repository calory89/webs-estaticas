## How to buy a home in Switzerland as a foreigner

The steps, papers and taxes, in plain words. General information, not legal advice: use a local lawyer before you sign.

### Can foreigners buy?

Under the Lex Koller law, EU/EFTA citizens living in Switzerland and holders of a C permit buy like Swiss citizens, and non-EU holders of a B permit can buy a home they live in. People living abroad need a cantonal permit and can usually only buy one holiday home in a tourist area, within national quotas; a proposed tightening was under consultation in 2026.

### The steps

- Check your Lex Koller status: Find out whether you can buy freely or need a permit. People living abroad must get authorisation from the canton before buying.
- Arrange financing: Banks usually require at least 20% of the price as equity and test affordability at an interest rate of about 5%.
- Find a property and make an offer: Search the main portals and make your offer, usually through the seller's agent.
- Sign the deed at a notary: The notary drafts the purchase contract (Kaufvertrag, acte de vente), reads it aloud and both parties sign it.
- Register the transfer: The notary files the sale with the land register (Grundbuch, registre foncier). Ownership passes once it is registered, which can take several weeks.

### Costs on top of the price

About 1-5%: Transfer tax (Handänderungssteuer, droits de mutation) 0% to about 3.3% depending on the canton; notary about 0.1-0.5%; land register fee, a small fixed amount or percentage; optional lawyer 1-2%; the agent fee (2-3%) is paid by the seller.

### Every year

Yearly costs are wealth tax, cantonal property tax where it exists, and tax on imputed rental value (Eigenmietwert) if you use the home yourself. Imputed rental value is due to end on 1 January 2029, and cantons may then add a new tax on second homes.

Yearly property tax: More than half of the cantons charge an annual property tax (Liegenschaftssteuer), usually 0.01-0.3% of the assessed value, and owners also pay cantonal wealth tax on the property value minus the mortgage.

Tax on rental income: Non-residents pay tax on net Swiss rental income at ordinary cantonal and communal rates plus federal direct tax, and some cantons set the rate using worldwide income.
