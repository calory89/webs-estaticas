How to buy a home in Bulgaria as a foreigner

The steps, papers and taxes, in plain words. General information, not legal advice: use a local lawyer before you sign.

Can foreigners buy?

Yes, buildings and apartments, with rights similar to locals; per GPG, foreigners can't own land directly, so a Bulgarian company is the usual route.

The steps

  1. Check what you can own: Non-EU citizens can buy apartments and buildings in their own name but not land. To buy a house with its plot, a non-EU buyer usually sets up a Bulgarian company.
  2. Hire a lawyer and check the property: A lawyer checks the title and debts, which usually takes 5 to 10 business days.
  3. Sign a preliminary contract: Pay a deposit, usually 10%. If you pull out you lose it, and if the seller pulls out they owe you double.
  4. Get your documents ready: You need your passport and a Bulgarian personal tax number from the National Revenue Agency. Payments of EUR 5,113 or more must go by bank transfer.
  5. Sign the deed before a notary: Sign the notarial deed before a notary in the property's district, with a sworn translator if you do not speak Bulgarian. The notary files it with the Registry Agency, usually in 1 to 3 business days.
  6. Declare the property to the municipality: File a tax declaration with the local municipality within 2 months of buying, and move the utility accounts into your name.

Costs on top of the price

About 3.2-8.5%: Transfer tax 0.1-3% (municipal; Sofia 2.5%) + notary 0.1-1.5% + legal 0.5-1% + agent 2.5-3%.

Every year

Yearly costs are low: municipal property tax is 0.01% to 0.45% of the tax assessment value, with a 5% discount if paid in full by April 30, plus a municipal waste collection fee. Owning through a company adds about EUR 500 to 1,500 a year in running costs.

Yearly property tax: 0.01-0.45% of the property's tax value plus a municipal garbage fee.

Tax on rental income: Non-residents: 10% final tax on gross rent, paid quarterly, no deductions.
