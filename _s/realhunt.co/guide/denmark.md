## How to buy a home in Denmark as a foreigner

The steps, papers and taxes, in plain words. General information, not legal advice: use a local lawyer before you sign.

### Can foreigners buy?

Without Danish domicile or 5 years of past residence you need permission from the Department of Civil Affairs (Civilstyrelsen); EU/EEA citizens who live and are registered in Denmark can buy a permanent home without permission by signing a declaration to the Land Registration Court. Holiday homes (sommerhuse) need permission, which is only given to people with particularly strong ties to Denmark, such as many years of holidays there.

### The steps

- Check if you need permission: Use Civilstyrelsen's rules: EU/EEA citizens living in Denmark sign a declaration, everyone else applies for permission for the specific property.
- Get a Danish tax number: If you have no Danish personal number, request one from the Danish Tax Agency with form 04.063_AP_EN.
- Set a budget with a bank: Danish banks usually lend up to 80% of the value, so plan for at least a 20% down payment.
- Make an offer and sign with conditions: The seller's agent drafts the contract; have your own lawyer review it and add bank and lawyer approval clauses (bank- og advokatforbehold).
- Let your lawyer check the documents: Your lawyer reviews the condition report, title and registered charges before the clauses are lifted.
- Register the deed: The deed (skøde) is registered digitally with the Land Registration Court and the registration fee is paid, which makes you the legal owner.

### Costs on top of the price

About 1.5-3%: Registration fee on the deed (tinglysningsafgift) of DKK 1,850 plus 0.6% of the price, about 1% for a buyer's lawyer, and, if you borrow, a mortgage registration fee of DKK 1,825 plus 1.25% of the loan; the seller normally pays the agent.

### Every year

Expect property value tax and municipal land tax, both charged through the Danish tax system, plus insurance, utilities and upkeep. Owners abroad may need to file a Danish tax return if they rent the home out.

Yearly property tax: Owners pay property value tax (ejendomsværdiskat) of 0.51% on 80% of the official valuation (1.4% above DKK 9.2 million) plus municipal land tax (grundskyld) at a rate set by each municipality, capped at 30 per mille of the land value.

Tax on rental income: A non-resident who rents out a Danish home is taxed in Denmark on the profit under the ordinary Danish rules; staying in Denmark more than 3 months at a time or 180 days a year, or working there, makes you fully tax liable.
