Renting out your home when you are not there: licence and tax, country by country

Before you count on any rent, check two things in the country of the house: whether a holiday let needs a licence or a registration number, and how rent paid to a non-resident is taxed. Spain, Italy and Portugal all require registration for tourist lets, and some cities have stopped giving new licences. We promise no income: the table below is about rules, not returns.

Reviewed on 14 November 2026 · Rules and figures as of November 2026 · General information, not legal or tax advice

Spain, Italy, Portugal and Greece: the licence comes first

  • Spain: a regional tourist licence plus the national single rental registry number, mandatory since 1 July 2025. New tourist lets in apartment blocks need the approval of three fifths of the owners, since 3 April 2025. Licences are regional, so the rule for a village in Granada is not the rule for Valencia: check the region before you buy.
  • Italy: every tourist let needs a national identification code (CIN), shown on the listings and at the property. From the third property the activity is treated as a business.
  • Portugal: registration as local lodging (Alojamento Local) is mandatory. Since November 2024 municipalities can freeze new licences in containment zones, which Lisbon and Porto use, and the owners of a building can object with more than half of the votes.
  • Greece: our file has the tax scale for rent received by non-residents, but no sourced summary of the short-let rules, so we do not describe them.

Someone has to be there

Rent also brings paperwork in the country of the house. In the United States a foreign owner chooses between 30% tax on the gross rent and a net-income election that needs a yearly tax return. In Germany a non-resident landlord deals with a German tax office. In Japan a non-resident owner needs a tax representative with a Japanese address. We have no sourced figures for what a manager or a key holder charges, so we give none: ask for the fee as a share of rent and for what it does not include.
