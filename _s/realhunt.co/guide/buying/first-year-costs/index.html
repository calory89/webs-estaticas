After the purchase: what a home abroad costs every year, in 20 countries

The yearly bill has three parts: the local property tax, the running costs of any house, and a few costs that only an owner from abroad meets, such as a tax representative in Japan, a bank trust fee in Mexico or a company in Bulgaria. The property tax ranges from a fixed fee in Sweden to about 1% of the value, on average, in large US cities.

Yearly property tax and other yearly costs, country by country

From our country files; the sources of each row are on the country's costs page, linked in the first column.

CountryYearly property taxAlso every year
Bulgaria0.01-0.45% of the property's tax value plus a municipal garbage fee.Yearly costs are low: municipal property tax is 0.01% to 0.45% of the tax assessment value, with a 5% discount if paid in full by April 30, plus a municipal waste collection fee. Owning through a company adds about EUR 500 to 1,500 a year in running costs.
CanadaProperty tax is set by each city as a share of assessed value, from about 0.3% in Vancouver to 2.12% in Saint John in 2026.Expect municipal property tax, from about 0.3% to 2.1% of assessed value depending on the city, plus insurance and upkeep. The federal Underused Housing Tax is proposed to end from 2025, but the change was still in Bill C-15 when last checked.
CyprusThere is no annual national property tax (abolished in 2017); owners pay municipal fees of about €90 to €300 a year, plus sewerage and local authority charges.Yearly costs are municipal, sewerage and local authority fees, plus income tax and GHS on any rent.
DenmarkOwners pay property value tax (ejendomsværdiskat) of 0.51% on 80% of the official valuation (1.4% above DKK 9.2 million) plus municipal land tax (grundskyld) at a rate set by each municipality, capped at 30 per mille of the land value.Expect property value tax and municipal land tax, both charged through the Danish tax system, plus insurance, utilities and upkeep. Owners abroad may need to file a Danish tax return if they rent the home out.
Dominican RepublicThe yearly IPI tax is 1% of the part of a person's total Dominican property value, as assessed by the tax office DGII, above RD$10,695,494 in 2026, so a single cheap home is often below the threshold.Most single homes under about $150,000 pay no IPI property tax because they fall under the threshold, but expect condo fees, utilities and upkeep. A 2026 tax law (Law 30-26) sets a 10% tax on gains when an individual sells, though its regulations were still in draft in October 2026.
FranceOwners pay an annual property tax (taxe foncière) set by the local authorities, and owners of a furnished second home also pay housing tax (taxe d'habitation), which some communes raise by a surcharge of 5% to 60%.Expect the annual taxe foncière, plus taxe d'habitation if the home is a furnished second home, and income tax and social charges on any rent.
GermanyOwners pay property tax (Grundsteuer), calculated since 2025 as assessed value x a tax rate x a municipal multiplier (Hebesatz) that each municipality sets.Expect the municipal property tax, insurance, utilities and upkeep. Rental profit is taxable in Germany, so a non-resident landlord deals with a German tax office.
GreeceOwners on 1 January pay the unified property tax (ENFIA), calculated per property from its location, size, age and official zone value, with an extra charge if total Greek property exceeds €500,000.Yearly costs are ENFIA and income tax on any rent.
ItalyIMU 0.4-1.06% on second homes (on cadastral value).IMU (council property tax) on second homes and a local waste tax (TARI). For small rural houses these are often a few hundred euros a year.
JapanFixed asset tax is 1.4% of the government's assessed value, plus city planning tax of up to 0.3% in urban areas, and small residential plots get a lower tax base.Expect fixed asset tax of 1.4% and city planning tax of up to 0.3% of assessed value, which is usually well below market value. Add the cost of a tax representative and, for apartments, monthly management and repair fund fees.
MexicoMunicipal predial, set locally on cadastral value; usually small, often less than HOA fees.Yearly costs include the local property tax (predial), usually 0.05% to 1.2% of the official cadastral value, which is often well below market value. In the restricted zone add the bank trust fee, about US$450 to US$1,000 a year, plus HOA fees, insurance and upkeep.
NetherlandsOwners pay municipal property tax (onroerendezaakbelasting, OZB) on the official WOZ value, at a rate each municipality sets, which in practice is a per mille figure; tenants of homes do not pay it.Expect municipal property tax (OZB), insurance and upkeep, and in a flat the owners' association (VvE) contribution. Non-residents also pay box 3 income tax based on the property's value.
NorwaySome municipalities charge property tax (eiendomsskatt), at most 4 per mille of the value after a reduction of at least 30%, and some also give a basic deduction; many charge nothing.Expect municipal property tax where it applies, municipal fees, insurance and upkeep. Costs such as municipal fees and property tax are deductible against rental income.
PolandOwners pay a local property tax (podatek od nieruchomości) set by the commune, capped in 2026 at PLN 1.25 per m² of usable floor space for flats and houses.Yearly costs are the local property tax, which is low for flats, plus income tax on any rent.
PortugalIMI 0.3-0.45% of taxable value (urban), set by municipality.IMI (council tax), usually 0.3–0.45% of the tax value for urban property.
SpainIBI 0.4-1.1% of cadastral value (municipal), plus imputed income tax on unlet homes.IBI (council tax) and waste collection. Non-residents who do not rent the house out also file a small yearly imputed-income tax.
SwedenHouses pay a municipal property fee (kommunal fastighetsavgift) of SEK 10,425 in 2026, or 0.75% of the tax value if that is lower.House owners pay the municipal property fee and must file a Swedish tax return if they owe it or have rental income, even when living abroad. Add insurance, utilities and upkeep.
SwitzerlandMore than half of the cantons charge an annual property tax (Liegenschaftssteuer), usually 0.01-0.3% of the assessed value, and owners also pay cantonal wealth tax on the property value minus the mortgage.Yearly costs are wealth tax, cantonal property tax where it exists, and tax on imputed rental value (Eigenmietwert) if you use the home yourself. Imputed rental value is due to end on 1 January 2029, and cantons may then add a new tax on second homes.
United KingdomOwners or occupiers pay council tax to the local council, and many councils in England charge second homes a premium of up to 100% from April 2025.Yearly costs are council tax, which may include a second home premium, plus a self assessment tax return and income tax if you let the home.
United StatesProperty tax is set locally and in 2024 averaged about 1.22% of value across the largest city in each state, from 0.30% in Honolulu to 3.02% in Detroit.Expect local property tax, which is often around 1% of value but varies widely by city, plus insurance and upkeep. If you rent it out you also file a US tax return each year when you use the net-income election.

Costs that only an owner from abroad has

  • Japan: a tax representative with a Japanese address to receive and pay the tax bills, and for flats the monthly management and repair fund fees.
  • Mexico: in the restricted zone, the bank trust fee of about US$450 to US$1,000 a year.
  • Bulgaria: owning through a company, as buyers from outside the EU do for a house with land, adds about EUR 500 to 1,500 a year.
  • Spain: a non-resident who does not let the house declares imputed income on it each year.
  • The Netherlands: non-residents pay box 3 income tax based on the value of the property.
  • France: a furnished second home also pays housing tax, which some communes raise by 5% to 60%.

The first twelve months

Some jobs come once, in the first weeks: in Bulgaria you file a tax declaration with the municipality within two months of buying and move the utility accounts into your name; in Japan you report the purchase to the Bank of Japan within 20 days; in Spain bills are expected to go through a Spanish account.

We have no sourced figures for furniture, travel, insurance for a house that stands empty or a neighbour who keeps the keys, so we print none. On each home we list we show the community fees and the yearly tax whenever the listing states them.
