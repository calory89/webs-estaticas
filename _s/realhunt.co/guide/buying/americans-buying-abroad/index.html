For Americans: what to report to the IRS when you own a home or an account abroad

A home abroad that you hold directly is not reportable on the FBAR or on Form 8938, according to the IRS. The foreign bank account you open for it is another matter: the FBAR applies when your foreign accounts together exceed $10,000 at any time in the year. Rent from the house is part of your worldwide income, which US citizens report wherever they live.

Reviewed on 14 November 2026 · Rules and figures as of November 2026 · General information, not legal or tax advice

The house

The IRS comparison table of the two forms has a row for it. Foreign real estate held directly: not reportable on Form 8938, not reportable on the FBAR. Foreign real estate held through a foreign entity: the real estate is still not reported, but the entity itself is a specified foreign financial asset and its maximum value includes the value of the real estate. Buying through a company abroad therefore changes your reporting.

Source: Internal Revenue Service

The bank account that comes with it

  • FBAR: required when the aggregate value of your foreign financial accounts exceeds $10,000 at any time during the calendar year. It is a cumulative balance, so two accounts that together pass $10,000 are both reported. It is filed electronically with FinCEN, not with your tax return.
  • Form 8938, living in the US: total foreign financial assets above $50,000 on the last day of the tax year or $75,000 at any time for an unmarried filer; $100,000 and $150,000 for a married couple filing jointly.
  • Form 8938, living abroad: above $200,000 on the last day or $300,000 at any time for an unmarried filer; $400,000 and $600,000 for a married couple filing jointly.

The moment to watch is the purchase itself: if you move the price through an account in your name abroad, that account passes $10,000 on that day even if it is empty a week later.

Source: Internal Revenue Service

Rent and a later sale

US citizens and resident aliens are taxed on worldwide income from all sources, the IRS says, whether they are in the United States or abroad. Rent from a house in Italy belongs in the US return as well as in the Italian one; the foreign tax credit exists so that the same income is not fully taxed twice, and it has to be claimed. See Do I pay tax in my own country too?.

Source: Internal Revenue Service
