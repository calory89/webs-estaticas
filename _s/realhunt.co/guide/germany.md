## How to buy a home in Germany as a foreigner

The steps, papers and taxes, in plain words. General information, not legal advice: use a local lawyer before you sign.

### Can foreigners buy?

Yes. There are no restrictions on foreigners buying property in Germany, though non-EU buyers usually need a bigger down payment to get a loan.

### The steps

- Find a home and arrange money: Search the portals or use a local agent and get financing in place before signing.
- Get independent advice: The notary is neutral and does not act for you, so consider your own lawyer and a survey.
- Sign before a notary: The notary drafts the purchase contract and both sides sign it in front of the notary; you can sign through a certified power of attorney from abroad.
- Secure a priority notice: The notary registers a priority notice (Vormerkung) in the land register to protect you against other sales.
- Pay the transfer tax and the price: Pay the transfer tax when the tax office sends the bill, and pay the price as the contract sets out, often via the notary.
- Get registered as owner: The land registry (Grundbuch) enters you as owner once the transfer tax is paid, which can take 3 to 4 months.

### Costs on top of the price

About 5-8.5%: Real estate transfer tax (Grunderwerbsteuer) of 3.5% to 6.5% depending on the state (3.5% in Bavaria, 6.5% in Brandenburg, North Rhine-Westphalia, Saarland and Schleswig-Holstein), plus notary and land registry fees (Notar- und Grundbuchkosten) of about 1.5-2%; if the seller hired the agent, the buyer pays half the commission by law, which is extra.

### Every year

Expect the municipal property tax, insurance, utilities and upkeep. Rental profit is taxable in Germany, so a non-resident landlord deals with a German tax office.

Yearly property tax: Owners pay property tax (Grundsteuer), calculated since 2025 as assessed value x a tax rate x a municipal multiplier (Hebesatz) that each municipality sets.

Tax on rental income: Non-resident owners are taxed in Germany on net rental income at the normal progressive income tax rates, but without the basic tax-free allowance (Grundfreibetrag).
