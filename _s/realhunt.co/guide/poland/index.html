How to buy a home in Poland as a foreigner

The steps, papers and taxes, in plain words. General information, not legal advice: use a local lawyer before you sign.

Can foreigners buy?

EU, EEA and Swiss citizens buy on the same terms as Poles. Other foreigners can buy an apartment without a permit, but usually need a permit from the Ministry of the Interior (MSWiA) to buy a house or land.

The steps

  1. Get a PESEL number: Apply for a Polish identification number (PESEL) in Poland, in person or through a lawyer with power of attorney. It is free and usually takes up to seven business days.
  2. Check if you need a permit: Non-EEA buyers of a house or land should apply for an MSWiA permit before buying. Apartments do not need one.
  3. Sign a preliminary agreement: Agree the price and pay a deposit, usually 10%. If you withdraw without cause the seller keeps it; if the seller withdraws they repay double.
  4. Do due diligence: A bilingual lawyer can check the land register (księga wieczysta) for liens, debts, unpaid fees and zoning issues.
  5. Sign the notarial deed: Sign the final deed (akt notarialny) before a notary and pay the fees. The notary files the change with the land register.
  6. Pay the PCC tax: Pay the 2% PCC within 14 days of the deed if the notary has not collected it.

Costs on top of the price

About 2.5-4%: Tax on civil law transactions (PCC) 2% of the value, paid by the buyer within 14 days of the deed; notary fee (taksa notarialna) capped at PLN 1,010 plus 0.4% of the value above PLN 60,000, plus 23% VAT; court fee of PLN 200 to enter ownership in the land register (plus PLN 100 for a new register); optional lawyer about 1%; the agent fee (about 2-3%) depends on the agreement.

Every year

Yearly costs are the local property tax, which is low for flats, plus income tax on any rent.

Yearly property tax: Owners pay a local property tax (podatek od nieruchomości) set by the commune, capped in 2026 at PLN 1.25 per m² of usable floor space for flats and houses.

Tax on rental income: Private rental income is taxed as a flat lump sum (ryczałt) of 8.5% of revenue up to PLN 100,000 a year and 12.5% above that.
