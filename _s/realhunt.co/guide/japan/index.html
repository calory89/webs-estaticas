How to buy a home in Japan as a foreigner

The steps, papers and taxes, in plain words. General information, not legal advice: use a local lawyer before you sign.

Can foreigners buy?

Yes. Foreigners can buy freehold land and homes on the same terms as Japanese citizens, even on a tourist visa, though buying does not give a visa or residency.

The steps

  1. Find a home and agent: Any licensed agent can show you most listings. Many cheap homes are only advertised in Japanese, so an agent who translates helps.
  2. Submit a written offer: Send a non-binding purchase application with your price and conditions. Non-residents usually pay cash, since Japanese banks rarely lend to them.
  3. Hear the property explanation: Before the contract, a licensed specialist must explain the property's legal status. This can be done by video call.
  4. Sign and pay a deposit: Sign the sale contract and pay a deposit of about 5-10%. If you pull out after this, you lose the deposit.
  5. Pay and register: On settlement day you pay the balance and get the keys, and a judicial scrivener registers the transfer at the Legal Affairs Bureau. Since October 5, 2026 all buyers must show proof of nationality when registering.
  6. File reports and name a tax representative: Non-residents must report the purchase to the Bank of Japan within 20 days under the Foreign Exchange Act. You also need a tax representative with a Japanese address to receive and pay tax bills.

Costs on top of the price

About 6-9%: The buyer pays an agent fee capped at 3% plus ¥60,000 plus 10% consumption tax (with a special cap of up to ¥330,000 including tax for cheap vacant homes up to ¥8 million), registration tax of 1.5% on land (until March 2029) and 2% standard on the building, real estate acquisition tax of 3% of assessed value, stamp duty, and a judicial scrivener fee of about ¥100,000 to ¥200,000.

Every year

Expect fixed asset tax of 1.4% and city planning tax of up to 0.3% of assessed value, which is usually well below market value. Add the cost of a tax representative and, for apartments, monthly management and repair fund fees.

Yearly property tax: Fixed asset tax is 1.4% of the government's assessed value, plus city planning tax of up to 0.3% in urban areas, and small residential plots get a lower tax base.

Tax on rental income: Rent paid to a non-resident is subject to 20.42% withholding (except when an individual rents it as their own home), and the owner must file a tax return each year through a tax representative in Japan to settle the final tax.
