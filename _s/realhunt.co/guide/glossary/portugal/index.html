Buying a home in Portugal: the local words, explained

Portuguese paperwork is short on steps and long on acronyms. These are the ones on every purchase: the tax number, the promissory contract, the two property papers and the two taxes.

NIF tax number

Also: número de identificação fiscal

The Portuguese tax number, needed before anything is signed.

You get it at a tax office (Finanças) or through a representative. Buyers who live outside the EU usually need a fiscal representative.

Source: RealHunt country file: Portugal

CPCV promissory contract

Also: contrato-promessa de compra e venda

The promissory contract that fixes the price and the date of the deed.

It comes with a deposit of 10% to 30% of the price, according to our Portugal guide.

Source: RealHunt country file: Portugal

caderneta predial tax record of the property

The tax office's record of the property, with its tax value and whether it is urban or rustic.

Ask for it together with the permanent certificate and the habitation licence (licença de utilização) before you sign.

Source: RealHunt country file: Portugal

certidão permanente permanent land registry certificate

Also: certidão permanente do registo predial

The land registry certificate of a property, kept online and always up to date.

The justice portal says it is valid for six months, is always current and available over the internet, and has to be shown, for example, when you buy or sell a property. It tells you the owner and the debts registered against the house.

Sources: Justiça.gov.pt · RealHunt country file: Portugal

escritura the deed

Also: Casa Pronta

The deed of sale, signed at a notary or at a Casa Pronta desk.

After signing, the sale is registered at the land registry office (Conservatória).

Source: RealHunt country file: Portugal

IMT transfer tax

Also: Imposto Municipal sobre as Transmissões Onerosas de Imóveis

The transfer tax on the purchase, on a sliding scale.

Our Portugal guide puts it at up to about 8% (5% on rustic land), plus stamp duty of 0.8%.

Source: RealHunt country file: Portugal

IMI yearly council property tax

Also: Imposto Municipal sobre Imóveis

The yearly municipal property tax.

Article 112 of the tax code sets the rates: 0.8% of the tax value for rustic property and from 0.3% to 0.45% for urban property, with each municipality choosing inside that band.

Sources: Autoridade Tributária e Aduaneira · RealHunt country file: Portugal
