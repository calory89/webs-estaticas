Buying a home in Italy: the local words, explained

The Italian words on the path from an offer to the keys: the tax code, the two contracts, the notary's deed and the papers that prove the house matches its plans.

codice fiscale Italian tax code

The personal tax code you need for any contract in Italy.

It is free. You ask for it at the tax agency (Agenzia delle Entrate) in Italy or at an Italian consulate abroad, and it is the first step of our Italy guide.

Source: RealHunt country file: Italy

proposta d'acquisto written offer

The written offer to buy, usually with a small deposit.

Once the seller accepts it, many buyers go on to sign the preliminary contract.

Source: RealHunt country file: Italy

compromesso preliminary contract

Also: contratto preliminare

The preliminary contract that binds both sides to sign the deed later.

It normally comes with a deposit of 10% to 30% of the price, according to our Italy guide.

Source: RealHunt country file: Italy

rogito the notary's deed

Also: atto notarile di compravendita

The final deed of sale, signed before a notary (notaio).

The notary checks the title and registers the sale. If you cannot travel you can sign through a power of attorney.

Source: RealHunt country file: Italy

visura catastale cadastral extract

The extract of the cadastre for a property: its identification, class and cadastral income.

The tax agency states that the information in the cadastral and mortgage databases is public and open to everyone; only the floor plans (planimetrie) are reserved for people with rights over the property. Our Italy guide asks buyers to get the extract together with proof that the building matches its plans.

Sources: Agenzia delle Entrate · RealHunt country file: Italy

conformità urbanistica e catastale planning and cadastral conformity

Proof that the building as it stands matches its permits and its cadastral plans.

Our Italy guide lists it among the checks before signing, next to the energy certificate and any unpaid debts on the property.

Source: RealHunt country file: Italy

APE energy certificate

Also: attestato di prestazione energetica

The energy performance certificate of the home.

Ask for it with the other papers before you sign.

Source: RealHunt country file: Italy

imposta di registro registration tax

The tax on buying from a private seller.

Our Italy guide gives 9% of the cadastral value for a second home, or 2% if the house becomes your main home, plus two fixed taxes of €50 each and the notary's fee.

Source: RealHunt country file: Italy

IMU yearly council tax on second homes

Also: imposta municipale propria

The yearly municipal tax on second homes.

Our files give 0.4% to 1.06% of the cadastral value, plus the local waste tax (TARI). For small rural houses the two often add up to a few hundred euros a year.

Source: RealHunt country file: Italy
