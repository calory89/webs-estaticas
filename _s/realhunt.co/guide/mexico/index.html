How to buy a home in Mexico as a foreigner

The steps, papers and taxes, in plain words. General information, not legal advice: use a local lawyer before you sign.

Can foreigners buy?

Yes, but in the Restricted Zone (within 50 km of the coast or 100 km of borders, which covers Tulum and Playa del Carmen) only through a fideicomiso bank trust or a Mexican company; direct ownership elsewhere.

The steps

  1. Check the restricted zone: Foreigners cannot hold title directly within 50 km of the coast or 100 km of a border. Outside that zone you can own in your own name.
  2. Choose how to hold title: In the restricted zone use a bank trust (fideicomiso), where a Mexican bank holds title for you, or a Mexican company. The foreign ministry (SRE) permit cost MXN 21,650 for a trust in 2026.
  3. Check the title: Verify the seller's title, check for liens and disputes, and confirm the land is not ejido land, which cannot be sold normally.
  4. Sign the purchase contract: Sign a contract that sets the price, payment terms and conditions. Do not wire a deposit until the documents and title have been reviewed.
  5. Close with a notary: A public notary prepares and signs the public deed (escritura). A separate buyer's lawyer is recommended for an independent review.
  6. Register the deed: The notary registers the deed in the Public Registry. Keep the final escritura and registration records.

Costs on top of the price

About 3.5-8%: Acquisition tax 2-5% + notary 0.5-1% + legal 1-1.5% (GPG 3.5-7.5%), plus 0.5-1.5% for fideicomiso/permit setup; TheLatinvestor budgets 5-8%.

Every year

Yearly costs include the local property tax (predial), usually 0.05% to 1.2% of the official cadastral value, which is often well below market value. In the restricted zone add the bank trust fee, about US$450 to US$1,000 a year, plus HOA fees, insurance and upkeep.

Yearly property tax: Municipal predial, set locally on cadastral value; usually small, often less than HOA fees.

Tax on rental income: Non-residents: 25% ISR withholding on gross rent; plus 16% IVA on short-term stays (Airbnb remits on its bookings).
