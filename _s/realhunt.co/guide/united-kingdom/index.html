How to buy a home in the United Kingdom as a foreigner

The steps, papers and taxes, in plain words. General information, not legal advice: use a local lawyer before you sign.

Can foreigners buy?

Yes. There are no nationality restrictions on buying homes anywhere in the UK and no visa is needed, but buying does not give a right to live there.

The steps

  1. Make an offer: Make an offer through the seller's estate agent. Acceptance is not binding until contracts are exchanged.
  2. Instruct a solicitor: Hire a solicitor or licensed conveyancer. The whole process can be done remotely.
  3. Run searches and enquiries: Your solicitor runs local searches and raises questions with the seller. This stage often takes 8 to 12 weeks.
  4. Exchange contracts: Sign and exchange contracts and pay the deposit, usually 10%. You are now legally bound to buy.
  5. Complete: Pay the balance on the completion date and get the keys.
  6. Pay SDLT and register: Your solicitor pays the SDLT, files the return with HMRC and registers you as owner at HM Land Registry.

Costs on top of the price

About 3-9%: In England and Northern Ireland, Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT) is 0% up to £125,000 and 2% from £125,001 to £250,000, plus a 2% surcharge for non-UK residents and a further 5% if you already own a home anywhere, so a non-resident who owns another home pays 7% up to £125,000 and 9% to £250,000; solicitor (conveyancer) about 0.6-1% with a typical minimum of around £2,100 including VAT; Land Registry fee £20 to £230; searches extra; the seller pays the estate agent.

Every year

Yearly costs are council tax, which may include a second home premium, plus a self assessment tax return and income tax if you let the home.

Yearly property tax: Owners or occupiers pay council tax to the local council, and many councils in England charge second homes a premium of up to 100% from April 2025.

Tax on rental income: Non-resident landlords pay UK income tax on rent at 20%, 40% or 45%, with 20% deducted at source by the agent or tenant unless HMRC approves gross payment, and the rates on property income are set to rise to 22%, 42% and 47% from April 2027.
