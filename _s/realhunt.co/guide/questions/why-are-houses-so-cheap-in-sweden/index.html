Question

Why are houses so cheap in Sweden?

We have found no single official figure that explains it, so we give what we can show: the price depends on where the house is, and the list below shows where the cheap Swedish houses on RealHunt are. Buying is simple and cheap: anyone can buy, and the stamp duty is 1.5%.

Reviewed on 14 November 2026 · Rules and figures as of November 2026 · General information, not legal or tax advice

What is cheap in Sweden is the process too

There are no citizenship or residence rules for buying a home in Sweden; you need a coordination number to be registered as owner. Lantmäteriet charges a private buyer stamp duty of 1.5% and a fee of SEK 825 for the registration of title. The 2% duty on mortgage deeds only applies if you borrow. The seller normally pays the agent.

One habit works against the buyer: the asking price is a starting point. Bids are not binding and the bidding stays open until the contract is signed, so a house can sell for more than the listing says.

What the price leaves out

Each year a house pays the municipal property fee, SEK 10,425 in 2026 or 0.75% of the tax value if that is lower, plus insurance, utilities and upkeep. A Swedish bank account is normally needed to complete the payment, and foreign buyers may face a larger deposit if a bank lends at all. Buying gives no right to live in Sweden.

Source: RealHunt country file: Sweden

What a low price does not tell you

A low price is a fact about demand in that place, not a verdict on the house. Before you fall for one, read the papers (which ones, by country), learn the local words in the glossary and add the buying costs with the calculator.
