How to buy a home in the Netherlands as a foreigner

The steps, papers and taxes, in plain words. General information, not legal advice: use a local lawyer before you sign.

Can foreigners buy?

Yes. There are no nationality or residence rules and no surcharge for foreign buyers. Some cities, such as The Hague, restrict renting out cheaper homes after purchase without a permit (opkoopbescherming).

The steps

  1. Get a BSN: Register in person at one of the 19 RNI municipalities with a valid ID to get a citizen service number (BSN).
  2. Set up payment and money: Open an account that can pay the notary on time and arrange financing.
  3. Make an offer: A verbal or email acceptance does not bind you; only a signed written purchase agreement (koopovereenkomst) does.
  4. Use the 3-day cooling-off period: A private buyer can cancel within three days of receiving the signed agreement without giving a reason.
  5. Pay the deposit: The standard agreement usually asks for 10% of the price, or a bank guarantee, paid to the notary.
  6. Sign the deed and register: A Dutch civil-law notary signs the transfer deed and you become owner when it is registered with the Kadaster.

Costs on top of the price

About 2-9%: Transfer tax (overdrachtsbelasting) of 8% if you will not live in the home, such as a buy-to-let or holiday home, or 2% if it becomes your main home; a notary fee for the transfer deed of around EUR 500; Kadaster registration fees; and mortgage costs if you borrow; the seller pays its own agent.

Every year

Expect municipal property tax (OZB), insurance and upkeep, and in a flat the owners' association (VvE) contribution. Non-residents also pay box 3 income tax based on the property's value.

Yearly property tax: Owners pay municipal property tax (onroerendezaakbelasting, OZB) on the official WOZ value, at a rate each municipality sets, which in practice is a per mille figure; tenants of homes do not pay it.

Tax on rental income: Non-residents declare Dutch property in box 3, where tax is 36% on a deemed return of 6% of the property's value in 2026, after a tax-free allowance of EUR 59,357 per person, regardless of actual rent.
