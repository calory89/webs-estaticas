### Buying costs and rules for foreigners in Sweden

Can foreigners buy? Yes. There are no citizenship or residence rules for buying a home in Sweden, but you need a Swedish personal identity number or coordination number (samordningsnummer) to register ownership.

Taxes and fees add about 1.5 to 3.5% to the price: Stamp duty on the title (lagfart) of 1.5% of the price or tax value, whichever is higher, plus a SEK 825 fee; if you borrow and need new mortgage deeds (pantbrev), 2% of their amount plus a SEK 375 fee; optional legal advice; the seller normally pays the agent, and a cooperative flat (bostadsrätt) has no lagfart. On the median home on this page (€73,000) that is €1,095 to €2,555, so €74,095 to €75,555 in all.

At the same percentages: on €40,000, €600 to €1,400; on €80,000, €1,200 to €2,800; on €150,000, €2,250 to €5,250.

Yearly property tax: Houses pay a municipal property fee (kommunal fastighetsavgift) of SEK 10,425 in 2026, or 0.75% of the tax value if that is lower.

### How bidding works in Sweden, and why the asking price is only a start

In Sweden a bid binds nobody, not even when it is made in writing. The seller does not have to sell to the highest bidder, or at the price shown in the advert. The sale is binding only when buyer and seller have both signed the purchase contract.

The estate agent must pass every bid on to the seller until the contract is signed and keep a list of the bids with each bidder's name, amount and time, which buyer and seller receive when the sale is completed. So the asking prices on this page are where the bidding may start, not what the homes will sell for.

## How to buy a home in Sweden as a foreigner

The steps, papers and taxes, in plain words. General information, not legal advice: use a local lawyer before you sign.

### Can foreigners buy?

Yes. There are no citizenship or residence rules for buying a home in Sweden, but you need a Swedish personal identity number or coordination number ([samordningsnummer](https://realhunt.co/guide/glossary/sweden-denmark#samordningsnummer)) to register ownership.

### The steps

- Get a coordination number: Book an identity check at a Swedish Tax Agency service centre to get a samordningsnummer, which you need to register as owner.
- Open a Swedish bank account: A Swedish account is normally needed to complete the payment.
- Find a home and arrange money: Listings are on Hemnet and Booli; foreign buyers may face a larger deposit if a bank lends at all.
- Inspect before you commit: Visit the property and consider hiring an inspector before signing.
- Sign the purchase contract: You and the seller sign the contract, usually with the seller's agent handling the paperwork.
- Apply for title at Lantmäteriet: Apply to Lantmäteriet for [lagfart](https://realhunt.co/guide/glossary/sweden-denmark#lagfart) and pay the 1.5% stamp duty to be registered as owner.

### Costs on top of the price

About 1.5-3.5%: Stamp duty on the title (lagfart) of 1.5% of the price or tax value, whichever is higher, plus a SEK 825 fee; if you borrow and need new mortgage deeds ([pantbrev](https://realhunt.co/guide/glossary/sweden-denmark#pantbrev)), 2% of their amount plus a SEK 375 fee; optional legal advice; the seller normally pays the agent, and a cooperative flat (bostadsrätt) has no lagfart.

### Every year

House owners pay the municipal property fee and must file a Swedish tax return if they owe it or have rental income, even when living abroad. Add insurance, utilities and upkeep.

Yearly property tax: Houses pay a municipal property fee ([kommunal fastighetsavgift](https://realhunt.co/guide/glossary/sweden-denmark#fastighetsavgift)) of SEK 10,425 in 2026, or 0.75% of the tax value if that is lower.

Tax on rental income: Non-resident individuals pay 30% tax on net rental income as capital income; for a private home you can first deduct SEK 40,000 per property and 20% of the rent (the 20% does not apply to a bostadsrätt).
