### The yearly property tax in Greece (ENFIA) and the E9 statement

ENFIA is calculated every year on the property held in Greece on 1 January. Owners who live abroad must file the E9 property statement like residents, through the myAADE portal, by 31 March of the year after they buy. The tax can be paid in up to 10 equal monthly instalments of at least €10 each.

## How to buy a home in Greece as a foreigner

The steps, papers and taxes, in plain words. General information, not legal advice: use a local lawyer before you sign.

### Can foreigners buy?

Yes. Buyers from outside the EU and EFTA need a permit to buy in designated border areas (parts of northern Greece and eastern Aegean islands such as the Dodecanese and Rhodes). The government has announced a 15% transfer tax for non-EU buyers of homes from 1 January 2027, not yet final in September 2026.

### The steps

- Get a Greek tax number: Apply for a tax number ([AFM](https://realhunt.co/guide/glossary/greece#afm)) with your passport. It is free and needed to sign contracts and pay taxes.
- Hire a lawyer: A lawyer is not mandatory but is strongly recommended. They can act for you under a power of attorney.
- Check the title: The lawyer checks ownership and searches the land registry for mortgages, liens and claims. Non-EU buyers in border areas apply for the permit at this stage.
- Sign a private agreement: Sign a preliminary purchase agreement and pay a deposit, usually 5-10%, which you normally lose if you withdraw.
- Pay the transfer tax: The notary prepares the transfer tax declaration and you pay the tax before the final deed.
- Sign the deed and register it: Sign the final contract before a notary and pay the balance, usually on the same day. The deed is then registered at the local land registry or cadastre.

### Costs on top of the price

About 5.5-7%: Transfer tax 3.09% of the price or the taxable value, whichever is higher; notary about 0.8-1% plus VAT; land registry fees about 0.5-0.7%; lawyer 1-2% plus VAT; if an agent is used, the buyer usually pays a commission of 1-3% plus VAT on top.

### Every year

Yearly costs are [ENFIA](https://realhunt.co/guide/glossary/greece#enfia) and income tax on any rent.

Yearly property tax: Owners on 1 January pay the unified property tax (ENFIA), calculated per property from its location, size, age and official zone value, with an extra charge if total Greek property exceeds €500,000.

Tax on rental income: Non-residents pay Greek tax on Greek rental income at 15% up to €12,000, 25% to €24,000, 35% to €36,000 and 45% above, after a flat 5% deduction for expenses.

- Tax on rental income: Non-residents pay Greek tax on Greek rental income at 15% up to €12,000, 25% to €24,000, 35% to €36,000 and 45% above, after a flat 5% deduction for expenses.

## How to buy in Greece, step by step

1. **Get a Greek tax number.** Apply for a tax number (AFM) with your passport. It is free and needed to sign contracts and pay taxes.
2. **Hire a lawyer.** A lawyer is not mandatory but is strongly recommended. They can act for you under a power of attorney.
3. **Check the title.** The lawyer checks ownership and searches the land registry for mortgages, liens and claims. Non-EU buyers in border areas apply for the permit at this stage.
4. **Sign a private agreement.** Sign a preliminary purchase agreement and pay a deposit, usually 5-10%, which you normally lose if you withdraw.
5. **Pay the transfer tax.** The notary prepares the transfer tax declaration and you pay the tax before the final deed.
6. **Sign the deed and register it.** Sign the final contract before a notary and pay the balance, usually on the same day. The deed is then registered at the local land registry or cadastre.
