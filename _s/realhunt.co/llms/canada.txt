Taxes and fees add about 1.5 to 4% to the price: Buyers usually pay provincial land transfer tax on a sliding scale (Ontario: 0.5% up to $55,000 and 1% from $55,000 to $250,000), legal fees of about $500 to $1,500, title insurance of about $250 and an inspection of $300 to $1,000, while the seller usually pays the agents; foreign buyers also pay Ontario's 25% non-resident speculation tax anywhere in Ontario and British Columbia's 20% additional tax in Metro Vancouver and four other regions. Yearly property tax: Property tax is set by each city as a share of assessed value, from about 0.3% in Vancouver to 2.12% in Saint John in 2026.

## How to buy a home in Canada as a foreigner

The steps, papers and taxes, in plain words. General information, not legal advice: use a local lawyer before you sign.

### Can foreigners buy?

Mostly not for now: a federal ban stops non-Canadians from buying homes in cities and larger towns until January 1, 2027, with exceptions such as work permit holders and spouses of citizens or permanent residents. The ban does not cover rural areas and small towns, and the government has said it does not plan simply to extend it, but a replacement is being studied.

### The steps

- Check if you can buy: Until January 1, 2027, non-Canadians cannot buy homes in census metropolitan areas or census agglomerations unless an exception applies. Check the location and your status first.
- Check provincial foreign buyer taxes: Ontario charges foreign buyers 25% and British Columbia charges 20% in some regions. These taxes can cost more than the rest of the purchase costs combined.
- Hire a real estate lawyer: The lawyer checks the title, handles the funds and registers the transfer. Ask for a written fee estimate first.
- Make an offer: Sign a purchase agreement with your price and conditions, such as a home inspection.
- Close the sale: On closing day you pay the balance plus land transfer tax and fees through your lawyer, and the home is registered in your name.
- Arrange rental tax filings: If you rent it out, name a Canadian agent to withhold tax, consider filing Form NR6, and file a Section 216 return each year.

### Costs on top of the price

About 1.5-4%: Buyers usually pay provincial land transfer tax on a sliding scale (Ontario: 0.5% up to $55,000 and 1% from $55,000 to $250,000), legal fees of about $500 to $1,500, title insurance of about $250 and an inspection of $300 to $1,000, while the seller usually pays the agents; foreign buyers also pay Ontario's 25% non-resident speculation tax anywhere in Ontario and British Columbia's 20% additional tax in Metro Vancouver and four other regions.

### Every year

Expect municipal property tax, from about 0.3% to 2.1% of assessed value depending on the city, plus insurance and upkeep. The federal Underused Housing Tax is proposed to end from 2025, but the change was still in Bill C-15 when last checked.

Yearly property tax: Property tax is set by each city as a share of assessed value, from about 0.3% in Vancouver to 2.12% in Saint John in 2026.

Tax on rental income: Non-residents have 25% of gross rent withheld, but they can file a Section 216 return to pay tax on net rent instead, and Form NR6 lets the agent withhold on net rent.

## Buying a home in Canada: what it really costs

Can foreigners buy?
Mostly not for now: a federal ban stops non-Canadians from buying homes in cities and larger towns until January 1, 2027, with exceptions such as work permit holders and spouses of citizens or permanent residents. The ban does not cover rural areas and small towns, and the government has said it does not plan simply to extend it, but a replacement is being studied.

Costs on top of the price
1.5-4% · Buyers usually pay provincial land transfer tax on a sliding scale (Ontario: 0.5% up to $55,000 and 1% from $55,000 to $250,000), legal fees of about $500 to $1,500, title insurance of about $250 and an inspection of $300 to $1,000, while the seller usually pays the agents; foreign buyers also pay Ontario's 25% non-resident speculation tax anywhere in Ontario and British Columbia's 20% additional tax in Metro Vancouver and four other regions.

Yearly property tax
Property tax is set by each city as a share of assessed value, from about 0.3% in Vancouver to 2.12% in Saint John in 2026.

Tax on rental income
Non-residents have 25% of gross rent withheld, but they can file a Section 216 return to pay tax on net rent instead, and Form NR6 lets the agent withhold on net rent.

## How to buy in Canada, step by step

1. **Check if you can buy.** Until January 1, 2027, non-Canadians cannot buy homes in census metropolitan areas or census agglomerations unless an exception applies. Check the location and your status first.
2. **Check provincial foreign buyer taxes.** Ontario charges foreign buyers 25% and British Columbia charges 20% in some regions. These taxes can cost more than the rest of the purchase costs combined.
3. **Hire a real estate lawyer.** The lawyer checks the title, handles the funds and registers the transfer. Ask for a written fee estimate first.
4. **Make an offer.** Sign a purchase agreement with your price and conditions, such as a home inspection.
5. **Close the sale.** On closing day you pay the balance plus land transfer tax and fees through your lawyer, and the home is registered in your name.
6. **Arrange rental tax filings.** If you rent it out, name a Canadian agent to withhold tax, consider filing Form NR6, and file a Section 216 return each year.
