### Buying costs and rules for foreigners in the Dominican Republic

Can foreigners buy? Yes. Foreigners have the same rights as Dominican citizens to buy land and homes, with no restrictions.

Taxes and fees add about 4.25 to 7% to the price: The buyer pays a 3% transfer tax (0% for qualifying first purchases under CONFOTUR tourism incentives), legal fees of about 1-2% and notary fees of about 0.25-1%, plus registration costs, while the seller pays the 2-3% agent fee. On the median home on this page (€201,200) that is €8,551 to €14,084, so €209,751 to €215,284 in all.

At the same percentages: on €40,000, €1,700 to €2,800; on €80,000, €3,400 to €5,600; on €150,000, €6,375 to €10,500.

Yearly property tax: The yearly IPI tax is 1% of the part of a person's total Dominican property value, as assessed by the tax office DGII, above RD$10,695,494 in 2026, so a single cheap home is often below the threshold.

## How to buy a home in the Dominican Republic as a foreigner

The steps, papers and taxes, in plain words. General information, not legal advice: use a local lawyer before you sign.

### Can foreigners buy?

Yes. Foreigners have the same rights as Dominican citizens to buy land and homes, with no restrictions.

### The steps

- Get a tax ID (RNC): Get your RNC at a DGII office with your passport, or through a lawyer with a notarized power of attorney. You need it to sign and to register the title.
- Check the title: Have a Dominican lawyer confirm a clean title at the Title Registry, with no liens or lawsuits, and that IPI tax, utilities and condo fees are paid.
- Sign a promise of sale: Sign the promise of sale contract and pay a deposit, usually 10-20%. It usually sets a 30 to 60 day window to complete.
- Sign the deed before a notary: Pay the balance and sign the deed of sale. The notary legalizes the document.
- Pay the transfer tax: Pay the 3% transfer tax to DGII. The title cannot be registered until it is paid.
- Register the title: Your lawyer files the deed and tax receipt at the Title Registry. The new title certificate in your name usually takes 30 to 90 days.

### Costs on top of the price

About 4.25-7%: The buyer pays a 3% transfer tax (0% for qualifying first purchases under CONFOTUR tourism incentives), legal fees of about 1-2% and notary fees of about 0.25-1%, plus registration costs, while the seller pays the 2-3% agent fee.

### Every year

Most single homes under about $150,000 pay no IPI property tax because they fall under the threshold, but expect condo fees, utilities and upkeep. A 2026 tax law (Law 30-26) sets a 10% tax on gains when an individual sells, though its regulations were still in draft in October 2026.

Yearly property tax: The yearly IPI tax is 1% of the part of a person's total Dominican property value, as assessed by the tax office DGII, above RD$10,695,494 in 2026, so a single cheap home is often below the threshold.

Tax on rental income: Rent paid abroad to a non-resident is generally subject to 27% withholding.
