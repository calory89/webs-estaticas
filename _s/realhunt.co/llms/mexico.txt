## How to buy a home in Mexico as a foreigner

The steps, papers and taxes, in plain words. General information, not legal advice: use a local lawyer before you sign.

### Can foreigners buy?

Yes, but in the Restricted Zone (within 50 km of the coast or 100 km of borders, which covers Tulum and Playa del Carmen) only through a [fideicomiso](https://realhunt.co/guide/glossary/mexico-united-states#fideicomiso) bank trust or a Mexican company; direct ownership elsewhere.

### The steps

- Check the restricted zone: Foreigners cannot hold title directly within 50 km of the coast or 100 km of a border. Outside that zone you can own in your own name.
- Choose how to hold title: In the restricted zone use a bank trust (fideicomiso), where a Mexican bank holds title for you, or a Mexican company. The foreign ministry (SRE) permit cost MXN 21,650 for a trust in 2026.
- Check the title: Verify the seller's title, check for liens and disputes, and confirm the land is not [ejido](https://realhunt.co/guide/glossary/mexico-united-states#ejido) land, which cannot be sold normally.
- Sign the purchase contract: Sign a contract that sets the price, payment terms and conditions. Do not wire a deposit until the documents and title have been reviewed.
- Close with a notary: A public notary prepares and signs the public deed (escritura). A separate buyer's lawyer is recommended for an independent review.
- Register the deed: The notary registers the deed in the Public Registry. Keep the final escritura and registration records.

### Costs on top of the price

About 3.5-8%: Acquisition tax 2-5% + notary 0.5-1% + legal 1-1.5% (GPG 3.5-7.5%), plus 0.5-1.5% for fideicomiso/permit setup; TheLatinvestor budgets 5-8%.

### Every year

Yearly costs include the local property tax ([predial](https://realhunt.co/guide/glossary/mexico-united-states#predial)), usually 0.05% to 1.2% of the official cadastral value, which is often well below market value. In the restricted zone add the bank trust fee, about US$450 to US$1,000 a year, plus HOA fees, insurance and upkeep.

Yearly property tax: Municipal predial, set locally on cadastral value; usually small, often less than HOA fees.

Tax on rental income: Non-residents: 25% ISR withholding on gross rent; plus 16% IVA on short-term stays (Airbnb remits on its bookings).

## Costs and taxes

- What the buying costs are made of: Acquisition tax 2-5% + notary 0.5-1% + legal 1-1.5% (GPG 3.5-7.5%), plus 0.5-1.5% for fideicomiso/permit setup; TheLatinvestor budgets 5-8%.
- Every year: Yearly costs include the local property tax (predial), usually 0.05% to 1.2% of the official cadastral value, which is often well below market value. In the restricted zone add the bank trust fee, about US$450 to US$1,000 a year, plus HOA fees, insurance and upkeep.
- Tax on rental income: Non-residents: 25% ISR withholding on gross rent; plus 16% IVA on short-term stays (Airbnb remits on its bookings).

## How to buy in Mexico, step by step

1. **Check the restricted zone.** Foreigners cannot hold title directly within 50 km of the coast or 100 km of a border. Outside that zone you can own in your own name.
2. **Choose how to hold title.** In the restricted zone use a bank trust (fideicomiso), where a Mexican bank holds title for you, or a Mexican company. The foreign ministry (SRE) permit cost MXN 21,650 for a trust in 2026.
3. **Check the title.** Verify the seller's title, check for liens and disputes, and confirm the land is not ejido land, which cannot be sold normally.
4. **Sign the purchase contract.** Sign a contract that sets the price, payment terms and conditions. Do not wire a deposit until the documents and title have been reviewed.
5. **Close with a notary.** A public notary prepares and signs the public deed (escritura). A separate buyer's lawyer is recommended for an independent review.
6. **Register the deed.** The notary registers the deed in the Public Registry. Keep the final escritura and registration records.
