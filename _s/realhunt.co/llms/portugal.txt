### Buying costs and rules for foreigners in Portugal

Can foreigners buy? Yes, same rights as Portuguese citizens (IMT increase for foreign second-home buyers announced Sept 2025).

Taxes and fees add about 3 to 12% to the price: IMT transfer tax 0-8% (progressive) + stamp duty 0.8% + notary/registry 0.9-1.2% + legal 1.25-2%. On the median home on this page (€160,000) that is €4,800 to €19,200, so €164,800 to €179,200 in all.

At the same percentages: on €40,000, €1,200 to €4,800; on €80,000, €2,400 to €9,600; on €150,000, €4,500 to €18,000.

Yearly property tax: IMI 0.3-0.45% of taxable value (urban), set by municipality.

## How to buy a home in Portugal as a foreigner

The steps, papers and taxes, in plain words. General information, not legal advice: use a local lawyer before you sign.

### Can foreigners buy?

Yes, same rights as Portuguese citizens ([IMT](https://realhunt.co/guide/glossary/portugal#imt) increase for foreign second-home buyers announced Sept 2025).

### The steps

- Get a [NIF](https://realhunt.co/guide/glossary/portugal#nif): Your tax number, at a Finanças office or through a representative. Non-EU residents usually need a fiscal representative.
- Sign a promissory contract: The [CPCV](https://realhunt.co/guide/glossary/portugal#cpcv) fixes price and date, with a deposit of 10–30%.
- Check the house: Ask for the [caderneta predial](https://realhunt.co/guide/glossary/portugal#caderneta-predial) and the [certidão permanente](https://realhunt.co/guide/glossary/portugal#certidao-permanente) (owner, debts, urban or rustic), and the habitation licence (licença de utilização).
- Sign the deed: At a notary or a Casa Pronta office, then the sale is registered at the Conservatória.
- Pay the taxes: IMT on a sliding scale up to about 8% (5% on rustic land) and stamp duty of 0.8%.

### Costs on top of the price

About 3-12%: IMT transfer tax 0-8% (progressive) + stamp duty 0.8% + notary/registry 0.9-1.2% + legal 1.25-2%.

### Every year

[IMI](https://realhunt.co/guide/glossary/portugal#imi) (council tax), usually 0.3–0.45% of the tax value for urban property.

Yearly property tax: IMI 0.3-0.45% of taxable value (urban), set by municipality.

Tax on rental income: Long-term: 25% flat for non-residents; short-term (AL) under the simplified regime only 35-50% of gross is taxable.

## How to buy in Portugal, step by step

1. **Get a NIF.** Your tax number, at a Finanças office or through a representative. Non-EU residents usually need a fiscal representative.
2. **Sign a promissory contract.** The CPCV fixes price and date, with a deposit of 10–30%.
3. **Check the house.** Ask for the caderneta predial and the certidão permanente (owner, debts, urban or rustic), and the habitation licence (licença de utilização).
4. **Sign the deed.** At a notary or a Casa Pronta office, then the sale is registered at the Conservatória.
5. **Pay the taxes.** IMT on a sliding scale up to about 8% (5% on rustic land) and stamp duty of 0.8%.
