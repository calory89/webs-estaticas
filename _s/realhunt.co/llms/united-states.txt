### Buying costs and rules for foreigners in the United States

Can foreigners buy? Yes. There is no federal ban on foreigners buying homes, but about 30 states have added some limits, and Texas and Florida restrict buyers from certain countries such as China.

Taxes and fees add about 2 to 5% to the price: Closing costs vary by state and typically run 2-5% of the loan amount, covering title search and title insurance, recording fees, attorney fees, appraisal and lender fees; since August 2024 the seller no longer has to pay the buyer's agent, so the buyer may pay it unless the seller agrees to; FIRPTA does not apply when you buy but does when a foreigner later sells, with the buyer generally withholding 15% of the sale price. ### Selling later as a foreign owner: the 15% withholding

When a foreign person sells a home in the United States, the buyer is in most cases required to withhold 15% of the amount realized on the sale and send it to the IRS, the federal tax office (FIRPTA). There is an exemption when the buyer acquires the home as a residence and the amount realized is $300,000 or less. It applies on the day you sell, so it is worth knowing on the day you buy.

## How to buy a home in the United States as a foreigner

The steps, papers and taxes, in plain words. General information, not legal advice: use a local lawyer before you sign.

### Can foreigners buy?

Yes. There is no federal ban on foreigners buying homes, but about 30 states have added some limits, and Texas and Florida restrict buyers from certain countries such as China.

### The steps

- Check the state rules: Confirm the state you are buying in does not restrict buyers from your country. Rules differ by state and some only cover farmland or land near military bases.
- Sign up with a buyer's agent: Agents must now have a written agreement with you before showing homes. It sets out how much the agent is paid and who pays it.
- Make an offer: Put your price and conditions in a written offer. You can ask the seller to pay all or part of your agent's commission.
- Do the checks: Get a [home inspection](https://realhunt.co/guide/glossary/mexico-united-states#home-inspection) and a title search so you know the condition of the house and that the title is clean.
- Close and record the deed: Pay the balance and [closing](https://realhunt.co/guide/glossary/mexico-united-states#closing) costs at closing. The deed is then recorded with the county.
- Set up your tax filing: If you rent the home out, decide between 30% tax on gross rent or the net-income election, which needs Form W-8ECI and a yearly Form 1040-NR.

### Costs on top of the price

About 2-5%: Closing costs vary by state and typically run 2-5% of the loan amount, covering title search and [title insurance](https://realhunt.co/guide/glossary/mexico-united-states#title-insurance), recording fees, attorney fees, appraisal and lender fees; since August 2024 the seller no longer has to pay the buyer's agent, so the buyer may pay it unless the seller agrees to; [FIRPTA](https://realhunt.co/guide/glossary/mexico-united-states#firpta) does not apply when you buy but does when a foreigner later sells, with the buyer generally withholding 15% of the sale price.

### Every year

Expect local property tax, which is often around 1% of value but varies widely by city, plus insurance and upkeep. If you rent it out you also file a US tax return each year when you use the net-income election.

Yearly property tax: Property tax is set locally and in 2024 averaged about 1.22% of value across the largest city in each state, from 0.30% in Honolulu to 3.02% in Detroit.

Tax on rental income: By default a non-resident pays 30% of gross rent (or a lower treaty rate), withheld at source, or can elect to treat the rent as business income and pay graduated rates on net income by filing Form 1040-NR and giving Form W-8ECI to the withholding agent.

## Costs and taxes

- What the buying costs are made of: Closing costs vary by state and typically run 2-5% of the loan amount, covering title search and title insurance, recording fees, attorney fees, appraisal and lender fees; since August 2024 the seller no longer has to pay the buyer's agent, so the buyer may pay it unless the seller agrees to; FIRPTA does not apply when you buy but does when a foreigner later sells, with the buyer generally withholding 15% of the sale price.
- Every year: Expect local property tax, which is often around 1% of value but varies widely by city, plus insurance and upkeep. If you rent it out you also file a US tax return each year when you use the net-income election.
- Tax on rental income: By default a non-resident pays 30% of gross rent (or a lower treaty rate), withheld at source, or can elect to treat the rent as business income and pay graduated rates on net income by filing Form 1040-NR and giving Form W-8ECI to the withholding agent.

## How to buy in the United States, step by step

1. **Check the state rules.** Confirm the state you are buying in does not restrict buyers from your country. Rules differ by state and some only cover farmland or land near military bases.
2. **Sign up with a buyer's agent.** Agents must now have a written agreement with you before showing homes. It sets out how much the agent is paid and who pays it.
3. **Make an offer.** Put your price and conditions in a written offer. You can ask the seller to pay all or part of your agent's commission.
4. **Do the checks.** Get a home inspection and a title search so you know the condition of the house and that the title is clean.
5. **Close and record the deed.** Pay the balance and closing costs at closing. The deed is then recorded with the county.
6. **Set up your tax filing.** If you rent the home out, decide between 30% tax on gross rent or the net-income election, which needs Form W-8ECI and a yearly Form 1040-NR.
