### Buying costs and rules for foreigners in Spain

Can foreigners buy? Yes, no restrictions at present; the government has proposed a 100% tax on (or ban for) non-EU non-resident buyers, not enacted.

Taxes and fees add about 7.5 to 14% to the price: New build: 10% IVA + 0.5-1.5% AJD stamp duty + notary 0.5-1% + legal 1-2%; resale: ITP transfer tax 6-11% instead of IVA/AJD. On the median home on this page (€120,000) that is €9,000 to €16,800, so €129,000 to €136,800 in all.

At the same percentages: on €40,000, €3,000 to €5,600; on €80,000, €6,000 to €11,200; on €150,000, €11,250 to €21,000.

Yearly property tax: IBI 0.4-1.1% of cadastral value (municipal), plus imputed income tax on unlet homes.

### Before you buy in Spain: the NIE, and no residence permit for buying

A foreign buyer needs a NIE, the personal number Spain gives to foreigners who have economic, professional or social interests in the country. You can ask for it in Spain or at the Spanish consulate where you live, in person or through an accredited representative, with form EX-15 and your passport. The NIE is not a residence permit and does not prove residence.

Buying a home no longer gives a residence permit: Organic Law 1/2025 ended the investor visas on 3 April 2025, including the one for property purchases above €500,000.

## How to buy a home in Spain as a foreigner

The steps, papers and taxes, in plain words. General information, not legal advice: use a local lawyer before you sign.

### Can foreigners buy?

Yes, no restrictions at present; the government has proposed a 100% tax on (or ban for) non-EU non-resident buyers, not enacted.

### The steps

- Get an [NIE](https://realhunt.co/guide/glossary/spain#nie): Your foreigner identity number, from a Spanish consulate or a police station in Spain. You need it to sign and to pay taxes.
- Open a Spanish bank account: Most notaries and councils expect payments and bills to go through a Spanish account.
- Reserve with an [arras](https://realhunt.co/guide/glossary/spain#arras) contract: A private contract with a deposit, usually 10%. If the seller backs out, they normally return it twice.
- Check the house: Order a [nota simple](https://realhunt.co/guide/glossary/spain#nota-simple) from the Land Registry (owner and debts). In the countryside, check the land status ([suelo rústico](https://realhunt.co/guide/glossary/spain#suelo-rustico)) and that the buildings are legal. Ask for the last [IBI](https://realhunt.co/guide/glossary/spain#ibi) receipts.
- Sign at the notary and register: The [escritura](https://realhunt.co/guide/glossary/spain#escritura) is signed before a notario and then registered at the Registro de la Propiedad.
- Pay the transfer tax: Resale homes pay [ITP](https://realhunt.co/guide/glossary/spain#itp) of 6–10% depending on the region, plus notary and registry of roughly €1,000–2,000.

### Costs on top of the price

About 7.5-14%: New build: 10% IVA + 0.5-1.5% AJD stamp duty + notary 0.5-1% + legal 1-2%; resale: ITP transfer tax 6-11% instead of IVA/AJD.

### Every year

IBI (council tax) and waste collection. Non-residents who do not rent the house out also file a small yearly imputed-income tax.

Yearly property tax: IBI 0.4-1.1% of cadastral value (municipal), plus imputed income tax on unlet homes.

Tax on rental income: Non-residents: EU/EEA 19% on net income; non-EU 24% on gross with no deductions.
