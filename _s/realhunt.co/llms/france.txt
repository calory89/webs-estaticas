## How to buy a home in France as a foreigner

The steps, papers and taxes, in plain words. General information, not legal advice: use a local lawyer before you sign.

### Can foreigners buy?

Yes. Foreigners can buy property in France without major restrictions, and non-EU buyers do not need a permit, although the paperwork can take longer.

### The steps

- Make an offer: Agree the price with the seller, usually through the agent. Budget for notary fees and other costs first.
- Sign the preliminary contract: Sign the [compromis de vente](https://realhunt.co/guide/glossary/france#compromis-de-vente) with a notary (notaire). Add conditions (clauses suspensives) such as getting a mortgage.
- Use the cooling-off period: You have 10 days after receiving the signed compromis to withdraw without penalty. The seller cannot withdraw.
- Pay the deposit: After the cooling-off period, pay a deposit, usually 10% of the price. You may lose it if you pull out without a valid condition.
- Let the notary run checks: The notary checks ownership, boundaries and rights of way and collects the compulsory diagnostic surveys (termites, lead, asbestos).
- Sign the final deed: Send the balance and fees to the notary's account before the date, then sign the [acte de vente](https://realhunt.co/guide/glossary/france#acte-de-vente) in person or by power of attorney. The notary registers the sale.

### Costs on top of the price

About 7-8.5%: Transfer taxes (droits de mutation) of about 5.8%, or about 6.3% in departments that raised their rate for 2025 to March 2028; notary fee (émoluments) on a sliding scale from 3.87% down to 0.799% plus 20% VAT, about 1.3% on a €150,000 home; land security contribution (contribution de sécurité immobilière) 0.10%; disbursements (débours) of about €800 to €1,200; the agent fee is usually built into the advertised price (FAI) and paid by the seller, sometimes shared.

### Every year

Expect the annual [taxe foncière](https://realhunt.co/guide/glossary/france#taxe-fonciere), plus taxe d'habitation if the home is a furnished second home, and income tax and social charges on any rent.

Yearly property tax: Owners pay an annual property tax (taxe foncière) set by the local authorities, and owners of a furnished second home also pay housing tax (taxe d'habitation), which some communes raise by a surcharge of 5% to 60%.

Tax on rental income: Non-residents pay French income tax on French rental income at a minimum rate of 20% (30% above about €27,500), plus social charges of 17.2%, or 7.5% for people covered by social security in another EU/EEA country or Switzerland.

## Costs and taxes

- What the buying costs are made of: Transfer taxes (droits de mutation) of about 5.8%, or about 6.3% in departments that raised their rate for 2025 to March 2028; notary fee (émoluments) on a sliding scale from 3.87% down to 0.799% plus 20% VAT, about 1.3% on a €150,000 home; land security contribution (contribution de sécurité immobilière) 0.10%; disbursements (débours) of about €800 to €1,200; the agent fee is usually built into the advertised price (FAI) and paid by the seller, sometimes shared.
- Every year: Expect the annual taxe foncière, plus taxe d'habitation if the home is a furnished second home, and income tax and social charges on any rent.
- Tax on rental income: Non-residents pay French income tax on French rental income at a minimum rate of 20% (30% above about €27,500), plus social charges of 17.2%, or 7.5% for people covered by social security in another EU/EEA country or Switzerland.

## How to buy in France, step by step

1. **Make an offer.** Agree the price with the seller, usually through the agent. Budget for notary fees and other costs first.
2. **Sign the preliminary contract.** Sign the compromis de vente with a notary (notaire). Add conditions (clauses suspensives) such as getting a mortgage.
3. **Use the cooling-off period.** You have 10 days after receiving the signed compromis to withdraw without penalty. The seller cannot withdraw.
4. **Pay the deposit.** After the cooling-off period, pay a deposit, usually 10% of the price. You may lose it if you pull out without a valid condition.
5. **Let the notary run checks.** The notary checks ownership, boundaries and rights of way and collects the compulsory diagnostic surveys (termites, lead, asbestos).
6. **Sign the final deed.** Send the balance and fees to the notary's account before the date, then sign the acte de vente in person or by power of attorney. The notary registers the sale.
