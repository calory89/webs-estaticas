Why it made the list

A stone house of 160 m2 on two levels in the hill village of Karydi, renovated in two stages, 2010 and 2024, and sold fully furnished. The ground floor has an open kitchen with a sitting corner by the fireplace, a large living and dining room, a double bedroom and a big bathroom with laundry area opening to the yard. Upstairs is a second bedroom with two balconies. Front and back yards, a store room, solar water heating and air conditioning in the living room and both bedrooms. About 20 to 25 minutes by car from Elounda and Neapoli, and an hour from Heraklion airport.

What it costs to buy this home

Buying in Greece adds about 5.5-7% in taxes and fees on top of the price: roughly €9,625 to €12,250 here, so plan for about €184,625 to €187,250 in total.

Transfer tax 3.09% of the price or the taxable value, whichever is higher; notary about 0.8-1% plus VAT; land registry fees about 0.5-0.7%; lawyer 1-2% plus VAT; if an agent is used, the buyer usually pays a commission of 1-3% plus VAT on top.

Every year: Owners on 1 January pay the unified property tax (ENFIA), calculated per property from its location, size, age and official zone value, with an extra charge if total Greek property exceeds €500,000.

General information, not tax advice: ask a local lawyer or notary for the exact figure.

Can foreigners buy a home in Greece?

Yes. Buyers from outside the EU and EFTA need a permit to buy in designated border areas (parts of northern Greece and eastern Aegean islands such as the Dodecanese and Rhodes). The government has announced a 15% transfer tax for non-EU buyers of homes from 1 January 2027, not yet final in September 2026.
