## Which homes we reject and why

A person looks at every home before it goes up. A home is turned down when it is not a whole home for sale, when its price is not good for its area, when it needs more than light work, when its photos are poor, when it sits next to noise or industry, when the advert leaves out the basic facts, or when its running costs are too high for the price. All the rules below must hold.

Updated 22 November 2026

### A whole home, for sale, at a stated price

- Turned down: homes for rent, auctions, shared ownership, plots of land, ruins, single rooms, timeshare, leaseholds with few years left and adverts with "price on request".
- Resale homes only. Projects sold off plan are not in the main list; new builds have their own [Top 100 rankings](https://realhunt.co/top).

### A price that is good for the area

- The price must make a foreign buyer stop: clearly good value for that place. As a guide, most homes cost between €30,000 and €200,000; up to about €350,000 only where that is cheap for the place, such as large cities in the United States or the Netherlands, Switzerland, or a sea view in Cyprus.
- Prices in another currency are converted to euros, and the original price is given in the description.

### Ready to live in, or with light work

- Turned down: homes that need structural work or a new roof, homes with damp, cracks or fire damage, homes with squatters or tenants who cannot be moved, homes with legal problems, and adverts that say "investors only", "cash only" or "as is".

### Real photos, inside and out

- At least 6 real, sharp, well-lit photos that show the outside and the inside, with the kitchen or a bathroom.
- Turned down: renders, images made or heavily retouched by a computer, photos with large watermarks, and adverts with a single photo.

### No noise, no industry

- Turned down: homes next to a motorway, a railway, an industrial plant or an airport runway, and homes in a flood plain when the advert says so. Sea, lakes, mountains, historic centres and pleasant villages with services are preferred.
- After a home goes up, its flood and noise indicators are shown on its page with their sources ([flood indicator by country](https://realhunt.co/research/flood-indicator)).

### The basic facts in the advert

- The advert must state the size, the number of bedrooms, the exact town and the price. A home whose advert leaves one of them out is not published.

### Running costs that fit the price

- When the advert states community or owners' association fees, ground rent or the yearly tax, they are written at the end of the description. Homes with very high fees for their price are turned down, and so are communities restricted by age (55 and over).

### An advert we can keep checking

- Since 19 November 2026, a new home must come from a portal that our nightly check can read, so we can keep confirming that it is still for sale ([how we check](https://realhunt.co/how-we-check-homes)).

### Variety

- Each round of new homes takes at most 2 homes from the same town, and mixes towns and types of home.

### How the description is written

- In our own words, never copied from the advert, and only with facts stated in the advert or visible in the photos.
- No promises about rent or returns, no "guaranteed", no distances we did not measure, no phone numbers.
- No words like "bargain", "investment" or "must see" in the title.

### What we do not publish

We do not keep a count of the homes we turn down, so we give none. What we do publish is what happens to a home after it goes up: the homes taken off the site and the homes whose advert went offline, day by day. [Homes taken off the site](https://realhunt.co/research/removed-homes).
