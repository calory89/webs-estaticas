Why We Say No to Most Cheap Houses

A cheap house that needs €80,000 of work is not a cheap house. The rule we use to decide what goes on RealHunt, and why we turn most listings down.

RealHunt Research · 2026-09-23

The internet is full of cheap houses. Most of them are cheap for a reason. The €1 houses in Italian villages come with an obligation to renovate, usually within three years, and the renovation costs far more than €1. The Japanese akiya you see in videos often need new roofs. And a lot of the American homes under $50,000 are listed for investors because they are not fit to live in.

RealHunt is for a different question: what can you buy for under €100,000 and live in, more or less as it is?

The rule

Every home is looked at by a person before it goes up. We say no to:

  • homes that clearly need major work: a new roof, rebuilt walls, no kitchen or bathroom
  • ruins and shells, however pretty the stone
  • land on its own, or land with a trailer or a shed
  • listings with one photo, or photos of the town instead of the house
  • anything over €100,000

Old is fine. Some of our favourite homes were built in the 1800s. What matters is that someone has looked after them.

In practice this means we turn down far more than we publish. We turn down about three listings for every one we publish.

Where the homes come from

Some are found by our own research on public listing sites. Some come from people and accounts who post homes they have found, and every one goes through the same check. Signed-in members can open the original listing and contact the seller or agent directly. RealHunt is not an estate agent and does not sell the houses.

How the ranking works

Every home that passes gets a grade from 70 to 98, built from 11 sub-scores: price, price per m², space, condition, extras, water, weather, airport, buying costs, listing detail and buyer interest. The front page, the country pages and the price lists are ordered by that grade, best first. Votes and saves only move the buyer-interest sub-score. How the score works. There are no paid placements in the ranking. Where there is a sponsored card, it says so.

What we get wrong

Photos can flatter. Listings go out of date. A house that looks fine may have a problem only a survey will find. If you see a home that is sold or not what it seems, use the report button on its page and we will check it. And before you buy anything, anywhere, get an independent inspection and a local lawyer or notary.
