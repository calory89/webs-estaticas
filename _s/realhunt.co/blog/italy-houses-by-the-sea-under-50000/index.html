Italian Homes Near the Sea for Under €50,000: Where They Actually Are

Cheap Italian houses are usually in inland hill towns. A few are close to the water. Here is where they are and what the catch is.

RealHunt Research · 2026-09-23

Most of the cheap Italian homes you see online are in inland villages, and there is a reason: the coast is where Italians go on holiday, so that is where prices are high. But not everywhere. On RealHunt right now there are a handful of homes under €50,000 that are on the coast or a few minutes' drive from a beach, and they cluster in three places: Calabria, Sicily and Puglia.

Calabria

The toe of Italy has the longest coastline of any mainland region and some of the lowest prices. In Acquappesa, on the Tyrrhenian side, there is a one-bedroom place with a sea-view terrace for €39,000, about 2 km from the water. Further south in Falconara Albanese there is a three-room flat with a sea view for €41,000. Two more homes in Calabria, at €30,000 and €35,000, are listed as five minutes from the beach.

Sicily

In Marsala, on the west coast, a 135 m² apartment is €34,000, about 600 metres from the sea. That is €252 per square metre, the lowest on the site for an Italian home with a size in the listing. In Acquedolci, on the north coast near Messina, a two-level flat that was largely renovated is €49,000. There is also a sea-view home with an 87 m² panoramic terrace 20 minutes from Messina for the same price.

Puglia and Sardinia

Puglia has two whitewashed and vaulted houses around 20 to 25 minutes from the beach at €28,000 and €49,000. In Sardinia, a furnished tower house on four floors in a historic district is €35,000, about 3 km from the beaches.

The catch

These are mostly apartments or small village houses, not villas. Sea views at this price usually come with stairs, a small kitchen and a building that has seen a few decades. Summer is busy and some coastal towns are very quiet from November to March. And the costs of buying in Italy are high: plan for 10% on top, plus about €2,500 in fees.

Always ask for the APE (the energy certificate) and whether the property is "conforme": that what is built matches what is registered at the land registry. A good local geometra can check this for a few hundred euros, and it is worth every one.
