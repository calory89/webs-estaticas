Can a Canadian buy property in the Dominican Republic?

Short answer: Yes.

Yes. Canadians have the same rights as Dominican citizens to buy land and homes, with no restrictions.

General information, not legal advice. Updated November 2026.

What it costs to buy

About 4.25-7% on top of the price. On a €150,000 home that is €6,375 to €10,500, so €156,375 to €160,500 in total.

The buyer pays a 3% transfer tax (0% for qualifying first purchases under CONFOTUR tourism incentives), legal fees of about 1-2% and notary fees of about 0.25-1%, plus registration costs, while the seller pays the 2-3% agent fee.

Work it out for your price

Every year

Yearly property tax: The yearly IPI tax is 1% of the part of a person's total Dominican property value, as assessed by the tax office DGII, above RD$10,695,494 in 2026, so a single cheap home is often below the threshold.

Most single homes under about $150,000 pay no IPI property tax because they fall under the threshold, but expect condo fees, utilities and upkeep. A 2026 tax law (Law 30-26) sets a 10% tax on gains when an individual sells, though its regulations were still in draft in October 2026.

Tax on rental income: Rent paid abroad to a non-resident is generally subject to 27% withholding.

Living there

Buying a home does not by itself give you residency in the Dominican Republic.

Good to know as a Canadian

  • Canadian residents whose foreign property costs more than CAD 100,000 in total must file form T1135 with the CRA.

The steps to buy in the Dominican Republic

  1. Get a tax ID (RNC)
  2. Check the title
  3. Sign a promise of sale
  4. Sign the deed before a notary
  5. Pay the transfer tax
  6. Register the title
