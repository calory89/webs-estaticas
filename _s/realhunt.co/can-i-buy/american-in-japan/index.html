Can an American buy property in Japan?

Short answer: Yes.

Yes. Americans can buy freehold land and homes on the same terms as Japanese citizens, even on a tourist visa.

General information, not legal advice. Updated November 2026.

What it costs to buy

About 6-9% on top of the price. On a €150,000 home that is €9,000 to €13,500, so €159,000 to €163,500 in total.

The buyer pays an agent fee capped at 3% plus ¥60,000 plus 10% consumption tax (with a special cap of up to ¥330,000 including tax for cheap vacant homes up to ¥8 million), registration tax of 1.5% on land (until March 2029) and 2% standard on the building, real estate acquisition tax of 3% of assessed value, stamp duty, and a judicial scrivener fee of about ¥100,000 to ¥200,000.

Work it out for your price

Every year

Yearly property tax: Fixed asset tax is 1.4% of the government's assessed value, plus city planning tax of up to 0.3% in urban areas, and small residential plots get a lower tax base.

Expect fixed asset tax of 1.4% and city planning tax of up to 0.3% of assessed value, which is usually well below market value. Add the cost of a tax representative and, for apartments, monthly management and repair fund fees.

Tax on rental income: Rent paid to a non-resident is subject to 20.42% withholding (except when an individual rents it as their own home), and the owner must file a tax return each year through a tax representative in Japan to settle the final tax.

Living there

Buying a home gives no visa or residency in Japan.

Good to know as an American

  • The US taxes its citizens on worldwide income, so rent from a home abroad goes on your US return, and foreign bank accounts above US$10,000 in total need an FBAR report.
  • Some banks abroad are slow to open accounts for US citizens because of FATCA reporting, so ask early.
