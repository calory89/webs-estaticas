Can an American buy property in Spain?

Short answer: Yes.

Yes, for now. Americans buy in Spain on the same terms as Spaniards and need an NIE, the foreigner tax number. The government has proposed a 100% tax on (or a ban for) non-EU buyers who do not live in Spain; it is not law as of November 2026, so check before you sign.

General information, not legal advice. Updated November 2026.

What it costs to buy

About 7.5-14% on top of the price. On a €150,000 home that is €11,250 to €21,000, so €161,250 to €171,000 in total.

New build: 10% IVA + 0.5-1.5% AJD stamp duty + notary 0.5-1% + legal 1-2%; resale: ITP transfer tax 6-11% instead of IVA/AJD.

Work it out for your price

Every year

Yearly property tax: IBI 0.4-1.1% of cadastral value (municipal), plus imputed income tax on unlet homes.

IBI (council tax) and waste collection. Non-residents who do not rent the house out also file a small yearly imputed-income tax.

Tax on rental income: Non-residents: EU/EEA 19% on net income; non-EU 24% on gross with no deductions.

Living there

Buying a home gives no right to live in Spain: the golden visa for property buyers closed on 3 April 2025. The non-lucrative visa is the usual route for people with passive income.

Spain is in the Schengen area: with an American passport you can usually stay up to 90 days in any 180 days across the whole area without a visa.

Good to know as an American

  • The US taxes its citizens on worldwide income, so rent from a home abroad goes on your US return, and foreign bank accounts above US$10,000 in total need an FBAR report.
  • Some banks abroad are slow to open accounts for US citizens because of FATCA reporting, so ask early.

The steps to buy in Spain

  1. Get an NIE
  2. Open a Spanish bank account
  3. Reserve with an arras contract
  4. Check the house
  5. Sign at the notary and register
  6. Pay the transfer tax
