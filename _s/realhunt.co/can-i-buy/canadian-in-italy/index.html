Can a Canadian buy property in Italy?

Short answer: Yes.

Yes. Italy lets non-EU citizens buy when their country lets Italians buy (reciprocity), and the US, UK, Canada, Australia and Switzerland all qualify. You need an Italian tax code (codice fiscale).

General information, not legal advice. Updated November 2026.

What it costs to buy

About 4-15% on top of the price. On a €150,000 home that is €6,000 to €22,500, so €156,000 to €172,500 in total.

Registration tax 9% on a second home (resale), or VAT on new builds; notary 1-2% + legal ~1% + agent ~3% (GPG roundtrip 10-18%).

Work it out for your price

Every year

Yearly property tax: IMU 0.4-1.06% on second homes (on cadastral value).

IMU (council property tax) on second homes and a local waste tax (TARI). For small rural houses these are often a few hundred euros a year.

Tax on rental income: Optional flat 'cedolare secca' on short lets: 21% for one property, 26% from the second.

Living there

Buying a home gives no right to live in Italy; stays over 90 days need a visa, such as the elective residence visa.

Italy is in the Schengen area: with a Canadian passport you can usually stay up to 90 days in any 180 days across the whole area without a visa.

Good to know as a Canadian

  • Canadian residents whose foreign property costs more than CAD 100,000 in total must file form T1135 with the CRA.
