Can a Dutch citizen buy property in Denmark?

Short answer: Yes, with conditions.

Only if you live there: Dutch buyers who live and are registered in Denmark can buy a home they live in all year without permission, by signing a declaration. Without Danish residence you need permission from the Department of Civil Affairs (Civilstyrelsen), and holiday homes (sommerhuse) are only allowed for people with strong, long ties to Denmark.

General information, not legal advice. Updated November 2026.

What it costs to buy

About 1.5-3% on top of the price. On a €150,000 home that is €2,250 to €4,500, so €152,250 to €154,500 in total.

Registration fee on the deed (tinglysningsafgift) of DKK 1,850 plus 0.6% of the price, about 1% for a buyer's lawyer, and, if you borrow, a mortgage registration fee of DKK 1,825 plus 1.25% of the loan; the seller normally pays the agent.

Work it out for your price

Every year

Yearly property tax: Owners pay property value tax (ejendomsværdiskat) of 0.51% on 80% of the official valuation (1.4% above DKK 9.2 million) plus municipal land tax (grundskyld) at a rate set by each municipality, capped at 30 per mille of the land value.

Expect property value tax and municipal land tax, both charged through the Danish tax system, plus insurance, utilities and upkeep. Owners abroad may need to file a Danish tax return if they rent the home out.

Tax on rental income: A non-resident who rents out a Danish home is taxed in Denmark on the profit under the ordinary Danish rules; staying in Denmark more than 3 months at a time or 180 days a year, or working there, makes you fully tax liable.

Living there

As an EU citizen you have the right to live in Denmark; stays over three months usually need registration with the local authorities. Buying a home is not needed for that, and does not change it.

Good to know as a Dutch citizen

  • Dutch residents declare a home abroad in box 3 (savings and investments) of their Dutch tax return.
