Can a Canadian buy property in Germany?

Short answer: Yes.

Yes. There are no restrictions on Canadians buying property in Germany, though banks usually ask non-EU buyers for a bigger down payment.

General information, not legal advice. Updated November 2026.

What it costs to buy

About 5-8.5% on top of the price. On a €150,000 home that is €7,500 to €12,750, so €157,500 to €162,750 in total.

Real estate transfer tax (Grunderwerbsteuer) of 3.5% to 6.5% depending on the state (3.5% in Bavaria, 6.5% in Brandenburg, North Rhine-Westphalia, Saarland and Schleswig-Holstein), plus notary and land registry fees (Notar- und Grundbuchkosten) of about 1.5-2%; if the seller hired the agent, the buyer pays half the commission by law, which is extra.

Work it out for your price

Every year

Yearly property tax: Owners pay property tax (Grundsteuer), calculated since 2025 as assessed value x a tax rate x a municipal multiplier (Hebesatz) that each municipality sets.

Expect the municipal property tax, insurance, utilities and upkeep. Rental profit is taxable in Germany, so a non-resident landlord deals with a German tax office.

Tax on rental income: Non-resident owners are taxed in Germany on net rental income at the normal progressive income tax rates, but without the basic tax-free allowance (Grundfreibetrag).

Living there

Buying a home gives no right to live in Germany.

Germany is in the Schengen area: with a Canadian passport you can usually stay up to 90 days in any 180 days across the whole area without a visa.

Good to know as a Canadian

  • Canadian residents whose foreign property costs more than CAD 100,000 in total must file form T1135 with the CRA.

  2. Get independent advice
  3. Sign before a notary
  4. Secure a priority notice
  5. Pay the transfer tax and the price
