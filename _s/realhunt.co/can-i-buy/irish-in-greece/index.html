Can an Irish citizen buy property in Greece?

Short answer: Yes.

Yes. EU citizens buy in Greece on the same terms as Greeks.

General information, not legal advice. Updated November 2026.

What it costs to buy

About 5.5-7% on top of the price. On a €150,000 home that is €8,250 to €10,500, so €158,250 to €160,500 in total.

Transfer tax 3.09% of the price or the taxable value, whichever is higher; notary about 0.8-1% plus VAT; land registry fees about 0.5-0.7%; lawyer 1-2% plus VAT; if an agent is used, the buyer usually pays a commission of 1-3% plus VAT on top.

Work it out for your price

Every year

Yearly property tax: Owners on 1 January pay the unified property tax (ENFIA), calculated per property from its location, size, age and official zone value, with an extra charge if total Greek property exceeds €500,000.

Yearly costs are ENFIA and income tax on any rent.

Tax on rental income: Non-residents pay Greek tax on Greek rental income at 15% up to €12,000, 25% to €24,000, 35% to €36,000 and 45% above, after a flat 5% deduction for expenses.

Living there

As an EU citizen you have the right to live in Greece; stays over three months usually need registration with the local authorities. Buying a home is not needed for that, and does not change it.

The steps to buy in Greece

  1. Get a Greek tax number
  2. Hire a lawyer
  3. Check the title
  4. Sign a private agreement
  5. Pay the transfer tax
  6. Sign the deed and register it
