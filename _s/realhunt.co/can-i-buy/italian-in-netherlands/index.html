Can an Italian buy property in the Netherlands?

Short answer: Yes.

Yes. There are no nationality or residence rules and no surcharge for foreign buyers in the Netherlands. Some cities restrict renting out cheaper homes after you buy (opkoopbescherming).

General information, not legal advice. Updated November 2026.

What it costs to buy

About 2-9% on top of the price. On a €150,000 home that is €3,000 to €13,500, so €153,000 to €163,500 in total.

Transfer tax (overdrachtsbelasting) of 8% if you will not live in the home, such as a buy-to-let or holiday home, or 2% if it becomes your main home; a notary fee for the transfer deed of around EUR 500; Kadaster registration fees; and mortgage costs if you borrow; the seller pays its own agent.

Work it out for your price

Every year

Yearly property tax: Owners pay municipal property tax (onroerendezaakbelasting, OZB) on the official WOZ value, at a rate each municipality sets, which in practice is a per mille figure; tenants of homes do not pay it.

Expect municipal property tax (OZB), insurance and upkeep, and in a flat the owners' association (VvE) contribution. Non-residents also pay box 3 income tax based on the property's value.

Tax on rental income: Non-residents declare Dutch property in box 3, where tax is 36% on a deemed return of 6% of the property's value in 2026, after a tax-free allowance of EUR 59,357 per person, regardless of actual rent.

Living there

As an EU citizen you have the right to live in the Netherlands; stays over three months usually need registration with the local authorities. Buying a home is not needed for that, and does not change it.

Good to know as an Italian

  • Italian residents declare property abroad in the RW section of their tax return and may owe IVIE, the tax on foreign property.
