Can an Australian buy property in Switzerland?

Short answer: Restricted.

Restricted. Under the Lex Koller law, Australians living abroad need a cantonal permit and can usually only buy one holiday home in a tourist area, within national quotas. Living in Switzerland, holders of a C permit buy like Swiss citizens, and holders of a B permit can buy the home they live in.

General information, not legal advice. Updated November 2026.

What it costs to buy

About 1-5% on top of the price. On a €150,000 home that is €1,500 to €7,500, so €151,500 to €157,500 in total.

Transfer tax (Handänderungssteuer, droits de mutation) 0% to about 3.3% depending on the canton; notary about 0.1-0.5%; land register fee, a small fixed amount or percentage; optional lawyer 1-2%; the agent fee (2-3%) is paid by the seller.

Work it out for your price

Every year

Yearly property tax: More than half of the cantons charge an annual property tax (Liegenschaftssteuer), usually 0.01-0.3% of the assessed value, and owners also pay cantonal wealth tax on the property value minus the mortgage.

Yearly costs are wealth tax, cantonal property tax where it exists, and tax on imputed rental value (Eigenmietwert) if you use the home yourself. Imputed rental value is due to end on 1 January 2029, and cantons may then add a new tax on second homes.

Tax on rental income: Non-residents pay tax on net Swiss rental income at ordinary cantonal and communal rates plus federal direct tax, and some cantons set the rate using worldwide income.

Living there

Buying a home gives no right to live in Switzerland.

Switzerland is in the Schengen area: with an Australian passport you can usually stay up to 90 days in any 180 days across the whole area without a visa.

Good to know as an Australian

  • Australian tax residents are taxed on worldwide income, so rent from a home abroad goes on your Australian return, usually with a credit for tax paid abroad.
