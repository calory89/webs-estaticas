Can a Spanish citizen buy property in Sweden?

Short answer: Yes.

Yes. There are no citizenship or residence rules for buying a home in Sweden. You need a Swedish personal identity number or a coordination number (samordningsnummer) to register ownership.

General information, not legal advice. Updated November 2026.

What it costs to buy

About 1.5-3.5% on top of the price. On a €150,000 home that is €2,250 to €5,250, so €152,250 to €155,250 in total.

Stamp duty on the title (lagfart) of 1.5% of the price or tax value, whichever is higher, plus a SEK 825 fee; if you borrow and need new mortgage deeds (pantbrev), 2% of their amount plus a SEK 375 fee; optional legal advice; the seller normally pays the agent, and a cooperative flat (bostadsrätt) has no lagfart.

Work it out for your price

Every year

Yearly property tax: Houses pay a municipal property fee (kommunal fastighetsavgift) of SEK 10,425 in 2026, or 0.75% of the tax value if that is lower.

House owners pay the municipal property fee and must file a Swedish tax return if they owe it or have rental income, even when living abroad. Add insurance, utilities and upkeep.

Tax on rental income: Non-resident individuals pay 30% tax on net rental income as capital income; for a private home you can first deduct SEK 40,000 per property and 20% of the rent (the 20% does not apply to a bostadsrätt).

Living there

As an EU citizen you have the right to live in Sweden; stays over three months usually need registration with the local authorities. Buying a home is not needed for that, and does not change it.

Good to know as a Spanish citizen

  • Spanish residents with assets abroad above €50,000 in a category (such as property) must file form 720.

The steps to buy in Sweden

  1. Get a coordination number
  2. Open a Swedish bank account
  3. Find a home and arrange money
  4. Inspect before you commit
  5. Sign the purchase contract
  6. Apply for title at Lantmäteriet
