Can a Danish citizen buy property in Canada?

Short answer: Restricted.

Mostly not for now: a federal ban stops non-Canadians from buying homes in cities and larger towns until 1 January 2027. It does not cover rural areas and small towns, and there are exceptions, such as some work permit holders. Check the current status before you look: the government has said a replacement is being studied.

General information, not legal advice. Updated November 2026.

What it costs to buy

About 1.5-4% on top of the price. On a €150,000 home that is €2,250 to €6,000, so €152,250 to €156,000 in total.

Buyers usually pay provincial land transfer tax on a sliding scale (Ontario: 0.5% up to $55,000 and 1% from $55,000 to $250,000), legal fees of about $500 to $1,500, title insurance of about $250 and an inspection of $300 to $1,000, while the seller usually pays the agents; foreign buyers also pay Ontario's 25% non-resident speculation tax anywhere in Ontario and British Columbia's 20% additional tax in Metro Vancouver and four other regions.

Work it out for your price

Every year

Yearly property tax: Property tax is set by each city as a share of assessed value, from about 0.3% in Vancouver to 2.12% in Saint John in 2026.

Expect municipal property tax, from about 0.3% to 2.1% of assessed value depending on the city, plus insurance and upkeep. The federal Underused Housing Tax is proposed to end from 2025, but the change was still in Bill C-15 when last checked.

Tax on rental income: Non-residents have 25% of gross rent withheld, but they can file a Section 216 return to pay tax on net rent instead, and Form NR6 lets the agent withhold on net rent.

Living there

Buying a home gives no right to live in Canada.
