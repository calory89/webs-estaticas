Can an Italian buy property in the United States?

Short answer: Yes.

Yes. There is no federal ban on foreigners buying homes in the US. About 30 states have added some limits, mostly on farmland or on buyers from certain countries such as China, which do not affect Italians.

General information, not legal advice. Updated November 2026.

What it costs to buy

About 2-5% on top of the price. On a €150,000 home that is €3,000 to €7,500, so €153,000 to €157,500 in total.

Closing costs vary by state and typically run 2-5% of the loan amount, covering title search and title insurance, recording fees, attorney fees, appraisal and lender fees; since August 2024 the seller no longer has to pay the buyer's agent, so the buyer may pay it unless the seller agrees to; FIRPTA does not apply when you buy but does when a foreigner later sells, with the buyer generally withholding 15% of the sale price.

Work it out for your price

Every year

Yearly property tax: Property tax is set locally and in 2024 averaged about 1.22% of value across the largest city in each state, from 0.30% in Honolulu to 3.02% in Detroit.

Expect local property tax, which is often around 1% of value but varies widely by city, plus insurance and upkeep. If you rent it out you also file a US tax return each year when you use the net-income election.

Tax on rental income: By default a non-resident pays 30% of gross rent (or a lower treaty rate), withheld at source, or can elect to treat the rent as business income and pay graduated rates on net income by filing Form 1040-NR and giving Form W-8ECI to the withholding agent.

Living there

Buying a home gives no visa or right to live in the US; visits follow the normal visitor rules for your passport.

Good to know as an Italian

  • Italian residents declare property abroad in the RW section of their tax return and may owe IVIE, the tax on foreign property.
