Can an Italian buy property in Norway?

Short answer: Yes.

Yes. Anyone with the funds can buy property in Norway, and a built plot of up to 2 decares normally needs no concession (konsesjon). Farms and larger plots need a concession.

General information, not legal advice. Updated November 2026.

What it costs to buy

About 2.5-3% on top of the price. On a €150,000 home that is €3,750 to €4,500, so €153,750 to €154,500 in total.

Document duty (dokumentavgift) of 2.5% of market value, a registration fee (tinglysingsgebyr) of NOK 545 for the deed and NOK 545 for any mortgage document; a flat in a housing cooperative (borettslag) has no document duty; the seller normally pays the agent.

Work it out for your price

Every year

Yearly property tax: Some municipalities charge property tax (eiendomsskatt), at most 4 per mille of the value after a reduction of at least 30%, and some also give a basic deduction; many charge nothing.

Expect municipal property tax where it applies, municipal fees, insurance and upkeep. Costs such as municipal fees and property tax are deductible against rental income.

Tax on rental income: Rental profit is normally taxed at 22%, and the Tax Administration says its rental rules also apply to owners who are tax resident abroad.

Living there

As an EU citizen you have the right to live in Norway; stays over three months usually need registration with the local authorities. Buying a home is not needed for that, and does not change it.

Good to know as an Italian

  • Italian residents declare property abroad in the RW section of their tax return and may owe IVIE, the tax on foreign property.

The steps to buy in Norway

  1. Find a home and get loan approval
  2. Bid through the agent
  3. Sign the contract
  4. Pay at settlement
  5. Get a D-number if needed
  6. Register the deed
