Can a Norwegian buy property in the United Kingdom?

Short answer: Yes.

Yes. There are no nationality restrictions on buying homes anywhere in the UK. In England and Northern Ireland, buyers who do not live in the UK pay a 2% stamp duty surcharge.

General information, not legal advice. Updated November 2026.

What it costs to buy

About 3-9% on top of the price. On a €150,000 home that is €4,500 to €13,500, so €154,500 to €163,500 in total.

In England and Northern Ireland, Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT) is 0% up to £125,000 and 2% from £125,001 to £250,000, plus a 2% surcharge for non-UK residents and a further 5% if you already own a home anywhere, so a non-resident who owns another home pays 7% up to £125,000 and 9% to £250,000; solicitor (conveyancer) about 0.6-1% with a typical minimum of around £2,100 including VAT; Land Registry fee £20 to £230; searches extra; the seller pays the estate agent.

Work it out for your price

Every year

Yearly property tax: Owners or occupiers pay council tax to the local council, and many councils in England charge second homes a premium of up to 100% from April 2025.

Yearly costs are council tax, which may include a second home premium, plus a self assessment tax return and income tax if you let the home.

Tax on rental income: Non-resident landlords pay UK income tax on rent at 20%, 40% or 45%, with 20% deducted at source by the agent or tenant unless HMRC approves gross payment, and the rates on property income are set to rise to 22%, 42% and 47% from April 2027.

Living there

Buying a home gives no right to live in the UK. Visitors can usually stay up to 6 months, and most now need an electronic travel authorisation (ETA) before they travel.

Good to know as a Norwegian

  • Norway is not in the EU, but as part of the EEA, Norwegians buy and move on almost the same terms as EU citizens inside the EU.
