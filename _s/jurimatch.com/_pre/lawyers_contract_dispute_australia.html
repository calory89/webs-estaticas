    How no-win-no-fee works for contract disputes in Australia

    Contract claims are among the easiest to fund on success terms because the loss is usually documented: the contract, invoices and correspondence show what was promised and what was not delivered. Firms mainly weigh the size of the claim against the cost of running it, and whether the other side can actually pay a judgment.

    Fees in Australia: Percentage-based contingency fees are generally prohibited for lawyers, except in Victoria for group proceedings under court-approved orders. Conditional "no win no fee" arrangements with a capped uplift are common.

    Litigation funding: Australia has one of the most active litigation-funding markets, especially for class actions.

    How do no-win-no-fee lawyers charge?

    Contingency / percentage of recovery

    The lawyer takes an agreed share of what you recover and nothing if you lose. Where allowed, percentages and caps are set by local law or professional rules.

    Conditional fee with success uplift

    The lawyer's normal fees are only payable if you win, sometimes with an agreed uplift. Common where percentage fees are restricted.

    Hybrid / reduced fee

    A lower fixed or hourly fee during the case, plus a success component at the end. Shares the risk between you and the firm.

    Third-party litigation funding

    A funder pays legal costs in return for a share of the proceeds, typically on larger claims. You keep the remainder; if you lose, the funder usually bears its outlay.

    Compared in detail: contingency fee vs litigation funding · no win no fee vs hourly fees.

    Which of these a firm can offer depends on local professional rules in Australia. Always get the agreement in writing and ask who pays the other side's costs if you lose.

    How long do I have to bring a contract dispute in Australia?

    Contract claims in Australia: 3–6 years

    6 years in most states; 3 in the Northern Territory.

    Each state and territory has its own Limitation Act.

    Indicative only, the start date, suspensions and exceptions can change the deadline. Read the Australia guide.

    What happens if you lose in Australia?

    The loser is usually ordered to pay a portion of the winner's legal costs, so ask your firm who carries that risk on a no-win-no-fee agreement.

    Ask any firm to put in writing who pays court costs, expert fees and the other side's costs if the case fails. What "adverse costs" means.

    What do lawyers check before taking it on success terms?

    • A signed contract, order confirmation or clear written exchange setting out the terms
    • Proof of your own performance (delivery notes, payments made, work completed)
    • A quantified loss with supporting documents
    • Evidence that the counterparty has assets or revenue to pay
