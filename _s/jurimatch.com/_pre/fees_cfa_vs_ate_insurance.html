    Conditional fee agreement (CFA) vs After-the-event (ATE) insurance

    A CFA and ATE insurance protect against different costs. What each covers if you win or lose, who pays the premium and why many claims in England and Wales use both.

    Short answer

    They cover different risks and are often used together. A CFA means you pay your own solicitor no fee if you lose. ATE insurance pays the other side's costs, and often your own expenses, if you lose. A CFA alone leaves you exposed to the other side's costs.

    How do they compare?

    • What it protects you from

      Conditional fee agreement (CFA): Your own solicitor's fees if you lose

      After-the-event (ATE) insurance: The other side's costs, and often your own expenses, if you lose

    • Who provides it

      Conditional fee agreement (CFA): Your solicitor

      After-the-event (ATE) insurance: An insurer, usually arranged by your solicitor

    • What it costs if you win

      Conditional fee agreement (CFA): A success fee on top of the base fees, capped by law

      After-the-event (ATE) insurance: A premium, normally paid out of your damages; it is usually not recoverable from the other side

    • What it costs if you lose

      Conditional fee agreement (CFA): No fee to your solicitor if the agreement says so

      After-the-event (ATE) insurance: Often nothing: many premiums are deferred and payable only if you win

    • Needed in personal injury claims

      Conditional fee agreement (CFA): Common

      After-the-event (ATE) insurance: Less often, because QOCS usually protects claimants from the other side's costs

    When does Conditional fee agreement fit best?

    • You want your own solicitor's fees to depend on the result.
    • The claim is likely to recover a good share of its costs from the other side if you win.
    • You are protected from adverse costs in another way, for example by QOCS in most personal injury claims.

    When does After-the-event (ATE) insurance fit best?

    • You could be ordered to pay the other side's costs if you lose, as in most commercial and professional negligence claims.
    • You need to pay expert reports and court fees during the case and want cover for them.
    • A funder or the court asks for security for costs.
