    Claims against Iberia: delays, cancellations and refunds

    Iberia is a Spanish airline, so its flights are generally covered by EU Regulation 261/2004, including long-haul flights departing from Spain. Passenger claims are mostly about long delays, cancellations, missed connections, refunds and baggage.

    JuriMatch editorial team·Updated 1 November 2026·Editorial policy

    What customers typically claim about

    A neutral summary of the kinds of claims customers have brought, based on public information. It does not mean any particular claim will succeed.

    Delay and cancellation compensation

    Under EU Regulation 261/2004 (and its UK copy, UK261), passengers can claim fixed compensation for long delays at arrival, late cancellations and denied boarding, unless the airline shows the disruption was caused by extraordinary circumstances such as severe weather or air traffic control restrictions. The amount depends on the flight distance.

    Missed connections

    When connecting flights are booked together and you reach the final destination more than three hours late, compensation is generally calculated on the distance to the final destination.

    Refunds and care

    When a flight is cancelled, passengers are entitled to choose between a refund and re-routing, and to care (meals, and a hotel if an overnight stay is needed) while they wait. Customers have brought complaints where refunds were slow or only vouchers were offered.

    Baggage

    Lost, delayed or damaged luggage is covered by the Montreal Convention, which has short notice periods: report it at the airport and send a written claim to the airline quickly.

    What to do first

    1. 1

      Claim directly with the airline

      Submit a claim through Iberia's customer service channels with your booking reference and a clear request.

    2. 2

      Note the actual arrival time

      Compensation for delay depends on arrival time at the final destination.

    3. 3

      Keep receipts

      Expenses for meals, hotels and transport during the disruption can be claimed in addition to compensation.

    4. 4

      Escalate to AESA or court

      If the airline refuses or does not reply, complain to AESA (its Passenger Rights Division) for incidents in Spain, or go to court. The European Commission's list notes that AESA's email is for information requests only and that claims go through its online complaint form. For flights from another country, the same list gives that country's enforcement body. In Spain, claims up to 2,000 euros can be brought in a juicio verbal without a lawyer.

    5. 5

      Consider help on success terms

      Claim companies and no-win-no-fee lawyers can take over refused claims for a share of the compensation.

    Evidence checklist

    • Booking confirmation and booking reference
    • Boarding passes (or proof of check-in)
    • Emails, texts or app notifications about the delay or cancellation
    • Photos of departure boards showing times
    • Receipts for meals, hotels and transport you paid for
    • The airline's reply to your complaint

    Typical timeline

    1. Day of travel

      Keep boarding passes, ask the airline staff for the reason for the delay in writing, keep receipts.

    2. Weeks 1–6

      Claim directly with the airline through its online form. Many claims are settled at this stage.

    3. Weeks 6 +

      If refused or ignored, escalate to the national enforcement body or an approved alternative dispute resolution scheme, or to court.

    4. Months +

      Court proceedings, often through a simplified small-claims procedure. Duration varies by country.

    Timelines vary widely by country and case complexity.

    Deadlines, in general terms

    There is no single EU deadline for flight compensation: the limitation period is set by the law of the country where you bring the claim, and it varies considerably between countries.

    Baggage claims under the Montreal Convention have much shorter notice periods (days, not years) and a two-year limit for court action.

    Use the limitation checker for an indicative deadline in your country, and act early: evidence such as boarding passes and emails is easier to gather now.

    Indicative period in Spain (contract claims): 5 years. Check the deadline for Spain

    Watch out

    • Claim companies and law firms typically keep a share of the compensation. Compare that with claiming directly, which is free.
    • An airline voucher is not the same as a refund. You can usually insist on money back for a cancelled flight.
