    How no-win-no-fee works for unpaid invoices

    Debt recovery is often handled on a percentage-of-recovery basis, especially for undisputed debts. If the debtor disputes the quality of the work, it becomes a contract dispute and firms will look harder at the evidence before working on success terms.

    How do no-win-no-fee lawyers charge?

    Contingency / percentage of recovery

    The lawyer takes an agreed share of what you recover and nothing if you lose. Where allowed, percentages and caps are set by local law or professional rules.

    Conditional fee with success uplift

    The lawyer's normal fees are only payable if you win, sometimes with an agreed uplift. Common where percentage fees are restricted.

    Hybrid / reduced fee

    A lower fixed or hourly fee during the case, plus a success component at the end. Shares the risk between you and the firm.

    Third-party litigation funding

    A funder pays legal costs in return for a share of the proceeds, typically on larger claims. You keep the remainder; if you lose, the funder usually bears its outlay.

    Compared in detail: contingency fee vs litigation funding · no win no fee vs hourly fees.

    Which of these a firm can offer depends on local professional rules. Always get the agreement in writing and ask who pays the other side's costs if you lose.

    What happens if you lose?

    In most countries outside the United States, a court can order the losing party to pay at least part of the winner's legal costs. A no-win-no-fee agreement with your own lawyer does not cover that by itself, so ask the firm who carries this risk if you lose (some firms, funders or insurers do).

    Ask any firm to put in writing who pays court costs, expert fees and the other side's costs if the case fails. What "adverse costs" means.

    • Proof of delivery or completion
    • Reminders sent and any reply admitting the debt
