    Crypto recovery scams: how to spot and avoid them

    After a crypto or investment fraud, victims are often targeted a second time by fake recovery services. These are the warning signs and the safer steps.

    JuriMatch editorial team·Updated 1 November 2026·Editorial policy

    If you are talking to a "recovery" service right now: do not send more money, do not share your seed phrase or passwords, and do not install any software they suggest.

    What a recovery scam is

    People who have lost money to a crypto or investment fraud are often contacted soon after by someone offering to get the money back. In many cases the "recovery" company is run by the same criminals, or by others who buy lists of victims. They charge fees upfront, then disappear or ask for more.

    Warning signs

    • They contact you first, by message, social media or a comment under a post about the scam you suffered.
    • They promise to get your crypto back, or say they have already traced it and only need a fee to release it.
    • They ask for money upfront: a fee, a tax, a "gas fee", a deposit or a payment to "unlock" a wallet.
    • They claim links to the police, a regulator, an exchange or a government agency, or use their logos.
    • They want payment in crypto, gift cards or by wire transfer to an individual.
    • They ask for your seed phrase, private keys, passwords or remote access to your computer.
    • They put you under time pressure: the money will be lost unless you act today.

    What a genuine lawyer does differently

    • A law firm can be checked: it is registered with a bar or law society, and you can look it up yourself.
    • It explains in writing what it can realistically do, for example tracing funds through an exchange with a court order, and that recovery is uncertain.
    • On no-win-no-fee or contingency terms, its fee depends on the result and is set out in a written agreement before any work starts.
    • It never needs your seed phrase or access to your wallet.

    Questions to ask on a first call are in our first-call checklist.

    Safer steps after a crypto scam

    1. Stop all payments and contact with the scammers. Keep every message, address, transaction hash and screenshot.
    2. Tell the exchange or platform you paid through, quickly: it may be able to freeze funds that are still there.
    3. Report the fraud to the police or your national fraud reporting service. In the United States, the FBI's IC3 also publishes guidance for cryptocurrency scam victims.
    4. Check any firm that contacts you against your regulator's warnings, for example the FCA ScamSmart list in the UK.
    5. Check your deadline: civil claims have time limits. Use the limitation checker.
    6. If the loss is large, speak to a lawyer who handles fraud claims. See crypto fraud lawyers and our crypto scam claim guide.

    Common questions

    Can stolen crypto really be recovered?
    Sometimes, for example when the funds reach an exchange that can be ordered by a court or the police to freeze them. Often it cannot. Nobody can guarantee recovery, and anyone who does is a warning sign.
    A company says it traced my funds and needs a fee to release them. Is it real?
    Treat it as a likely scam. Genuine tracing does not end with a fee to "release" funds. Do not pay, stop contact and report the approach.
    Where do I report a crypto scam?
    To your national police or fraud reporting service, and to the exchange or platform involved. In the United States the FBI takes reports through IC3. In the United Kingdom the FCA keeps a warning list of unauthorised firms.
