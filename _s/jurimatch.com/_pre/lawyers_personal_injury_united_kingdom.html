    How no-win-no-fee works for personal injury claims in the United Kingdom

    Personal injury is the classic no-win-no-fee area: most claims are paid by an insurer, so recoverability is rarely the issue. The key questions are liability (who was at fault) and how well the injury is documented medically.

    Fees in United Kingdom: Conditional fee agreements (CFAs, "no win no fee" with a success fee) and damages-based agreements (DBAs, a percentage of damages) are both permitted in England & Wales, subject to statutory caps, for example tighter caps for personal injury claims. After-the-event insurance is commonly used to cover the other side's costs.

    Litigation funding: London is one of the largest litigation-funding markets. A 2023 Supreme Court decision (PACCAR) affected how many funding agreements are structured and legislative reform has been proposed, so the current position should be confirmed with a solicitor.

    How do no-win-no-fee lawyers charge?

    Contingency / percentage of recovery

    The lawyer takes an agreed share of what you recover and nothing if you lose. Where allowed, percentages and caps are set by local law or professional rules.

    Conditional fee with success uplift

    The lawyer's normal fees are only payable if you win, sometimes with an agreed uplift. Common where percentage fees are restricted.

    Hybrid / reduced fee

    A lower fixed or hourly fee during the case, plus a success component at the end. Shares the risk between you and the firm.

    Third-party litigation funding

    A funder pays legal costs in return for a share of the proceeds, typically on larger claims. You keep the remainder; if you lose, the funder usually bears its outlay.

    Compared in detail: contingency fee vs litigation funding · no win no fee vs hourly fees · CFA vs DBA.

    Which of these a firm can offer depends on local professional rules in the United Kingdom. Always get the agreement in writing and ask who pays the other side's costs if you lose.

    What happens if you lose in the United Kingdom?

    In England & Wales the loser is normally ordered to pay a large part of the winner's costs. In most personal injury claims, qualified one-way costs shifting (QOCS) generally protects a claimant who loses, and employment tribunals rarely order costs. After-the-event (ATE) insurance is often used to cover the risk.

    Ask any firm to put in writing who pays court costs, expert fees and the other side's costs if the case fails. What "adverse costs" means.
