    Suing without a lawyer: small claims by country

    Last updated: October 31, 2026 · Editorial policy

    For smaller claims, the court fees and procedure are often simple enough to manage without a lawyer, and some countries have procedures designed for that. This page summarises when you can go to court yourself, with links to the official sources. Thresholds change, so always check the official source before you file. General information, not legal advice.

    Key points

    • For smaller claims, court fees and procedure are often simple enough to manage without a lawyer.
    • Several countries have procedures designed for this, such as the small claims track in England and Wales for most claims up to £10,000.
    • Thresholds change, so check the official source linked for each country before you file.

    Reviewed on October 31, 2026

    England and WalesScotlandSpainGermanyFranceCross-border claims in the EUUnited States

    England and Wales

    • Most money claims up to GBP 10,000 are allocated to the small claims track, which is designed for people without lawyers. Lower limits apply to some personal injury and housing disrepair claims.
    • You can start a money claim online. Court fees depend on the amount claimed and are listed on gov.uk.
    • On the small claims track the legal costs the winner can recover are very limited, so you are unlikely to face a large bill for the other side's lawyers if you lose, and equally you cannot expect to recover much for your own.
    • Courts may refer the case to free small claims mediation before a hearing.

    Official sources: gov.uk: Make a court claim for money · Civil Procedure Rules, Part 27 (small claims track)

    Scotland

    • The simple procedure in the sheriff court is designed for claims up to GBP 5,000 and for people without a lawyer.

    Official sources: Scottish Courts and Tribunals: simple procedure

    Spain

    • In a juicio verbal for claims up to EUR 2,000 you do not need a lawyer (abogado) or a court representative (procurador). Standard claim forms are available at the courts.
    • The proceso monitorio, for documented debts, can be started without a lawyer or procurador whatever the amount. If the debtor opposes and the claim exceeds EUR 2,000, you then need both.
    • Since April 2025 most civil claims require a prior attempt to settle (MASC). A documented written demand usually serves, but check what applies to your case.

    Official sources: BOE: Ley de Enjuiciamiento Civil (arts. 23, 31 and 812-814) · BOE: Ley Orgánica 1/2025 (MASC)

    Germany

    • There is no obligation to be represented by a lawyer before the local court (Amtsgericht). Representation by a lawyer is mandatory before the regional court (Landgericht) and higher courts.
    • Which court hears a case depends mainly on the amount in dispute. The Amtsgericht threshold, EUR 5,000 for many years, was raised to EUR 10,000 in 2026; some matters go to a particular court whatever the amount. Check the current figure in § 23 of the Courts Constitution Act (GVG).
    • For claims up to EUR 600, the judge may choose a simplified procedure (§ 495a ZPO).
    • Undisputed debts can be pursued through the court payment order procedure (Mahnverfahren), which can be started online.

    Official sources: gesetze-im-internet.de: § 23 GVG · gesetze-im-internet.de: § 78 ZPO (Anwaltsprozess) · gesetze-im-internet.de: § 495a ZPO · Online-Mahnantrag (official payment order portal)

    France

    • Before the tribunal judiciaire, you can represent yourself without an avocat for claims up to EUR 10,000, among other exceptions to mandatory representation.
    • Undisputed debts can be pursued through an injonction de payer.

    Official sources: Légifrance: Code de procédure civile, article 761 · Service-public.fr (official guidance)

    Cross-border claims in the EU

    • The European Small Claims Procedure covers cross-border civil and commercial claims up to EUR 5,000 (excluding interest and costs). It is mostly written, uses standard forms and does not require a lawyer.
    • It applies in every EU Member State except Denmark.

    Official sources: European e-Justice Portal

    United States

    • Every state has small claims courts where people commonly represent themselves. The maximum amount, fees and whether lawyers may appear vary by state, so check your state court's website.

    Before you file

    1. Send a clear written demand. Our complaint letter template is free.
    2. Try a free complaint route such as an ombudsman.
    3. Check your deadline.
    4. Check the other side can pay: winning does not guarantee collecting.

    If the claim is larger or contested, a no-win-no-fee lawyer may cost you nothing upfront.

    Frequently asked questions

    Can I sue a company without a lawyer?
    In many countries, yes, for lower-value claims. England and Wales has the small claims track, Spain allows claims up to EUR 2,000 in a juicio verbal without a lawyer, and German local courts do not require a lawyer. Higher-value and complex cases usually require or benefit from one.
    What if the company hires a lawyer and I lose?
    It depends on the procedure. On the small claims track in England and Wales, recoverable costs are very limited. In Spain and Germany the loser can generally be ordered to pay the other side's costs, although where you do not need a lawyer the recoverable amounts are often modest. Check before you file.
    Should I try a free complaint route first?
    Usually yes. Ombudsmen and conciliation bodies are free and often faster. See our page on free alternatives.
