The island · real footage

This is where
history gets run.

Lagoons, horse beaches, thatched-roof villages, waves. Sumba is one of the last untouched islands in Indonesia — and nobody has ever run 42 km across it.

🐎

An island of horses

Sumba is famous for its horses, from beach rides to the Pasola, the island's traditional horseback ritual.

🪨

Megalithic villages

Some of the last living megalithic cultures on earth: stone tombs and tall thatched roofs in villages like Ratenggaro.

🧵

Ikat weaving

Hand-woven Sumba ikat is one of Indonesia's most prized textiles.

🌊

Indian Ocean coast

Turquoise lagoons, empty white beaches and waves that surfers travel across the world for.

THE FIRST 100 · SUMBA MARATHON

Never run a marathon? Good.

No qualifying time.
Just finish.

Boston makes you qualify. Chicago, London and New York put most people in a ballot. Here the only standard is getting to the finish. If you start training now, you have time.

Phase 1

Build the habit

Run easy 3–4 times a week. Walk breaks are fine. Consistency beats speed.

Phase 2

Go longer

One long run a week, growing slowly. Most plans add a little distance each week and step back every few weeks.

Phase 3

Train for heat

Sumba is tropical. Practise drinking on the run and train some sessions in warm conditions.

Phase 4

Taper and travel

Ease off in the last weeks and arrive early to rest after the flight.

General guidance, not medical advice. Check with a doctor before starting marathon training.

Why 42.195 km? The distance was set at the 1908 London Olympics, and became the official marathon distance in 1921. In Sumba it has never been run. Yet.

Questions

FAQ

Is this really the first marathon on Sumba?

As far as we can find, no marathon has ever been run on the island. Sumba is famous for horses, beaches and its culture — not for road races. That's exactly why the first one matters.

Where is it?

Sumba Island, East Nusa Tenggara, Indonesia — about an hour's flight east of Bali. The finish line is on the beach at Eco Beach City, the organiser and host.

When?

2027. The exact date is announced to the First 100 before it goes public.

Does it cost anything to reserve?

No. Reserving your bib is free. Entry details and price come with the date, and the First 100 get them first.

Do I need to be fast?

No. You need to finish. The First 100 is about being there at the start of history, not about your time. If you want the champion title, though — bring your legs.

What happens after I reserve?

Your number shows on the live wall with your first name and country, and it's locked to your email. When the date, route and entry details are ready, the First 100 hear first, before anything goes public.

How do I get to Sumba?

Fly to Bali, then take a short domestic flight to Sumba (Tambolaka airport, about an hour). We'll share travel tips with the First 100.

Why only 100?

Because the first edition happens once. 100 names fit on one monument. After that, the First 100 is closed for good.

Can I help make it happen?

Yes. Volunteers, local guides, photographers, physios and brands that want to be part of the first edition: write to carlos@ecobeachcity.com.

What do you do with my details?

We store your name, email, optional WhatsApp, country and bib number to run the First 100 and send you Sumba Marathon news. Only your first name and country are shown on the public wall; your "why" stays private. Ask us any time at carlos@ecobeachcity.com to change or delete your data.

Can I give my bib to someone else?

Yes, just email us before the race. Bibs can't be resold.
