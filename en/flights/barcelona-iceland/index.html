Best time to visit Iceland

January–February is winter in Iceland: sub-zero temps, limited daylight but peak aurora borealis season. For hiking, waterfalls and longer days, consider summer (June–August), though prices rise. Shoulder months (April–May, September) offer milder weather and fewer crowds.

What to do when you arrive in Reykjavik

Your flight lands at Keflavík (KEF), 50 km from Reykjavik. Rent a car or take a bus to explore: the Golden Circle (geysers, waterfalls, tectonic plates), the south coast with glaciers and black-sand beaches, or towns up north. Reykjavik itself is compact, walkable and has excellent food.
