Price and when to book

We've found flights from €441 (return), though most hover around €483–€488. There's no magic formula for price drops, but the golden rule is to book well in advance. This is a long journey; lock in your dates early. Compare across different days of the week: sometimes a Tuesday flight beats Friday by a decent margin.

Airline and stopovers

Air China operates this route with 1 stopover. It's not a direct flight, but one break is reasonable for an Europe-Asia journey. You'll have time to stretch your legs and sort your baggage. Always confirm total flight time and the stopover airport before purchasing.

Best time to visit

Bangkok welcomes tourists year-round, but the cool season (November to February) is most pleasant: less rain and bearable temperatures. Steer clear of peak heat (March to May). If you want to dodge crowds, travel shoulder season: October or April can yield tighter prices. Checking the calendar and flexing your dates helps you save.

What to do when you arrive

You'll land at Suvarnabhumi, Bangkok's main airport. The city centre is 25 km away: take the express train (30 mins, cheap and comfortable) or a taxi if you're heavy on luggage. Bangkok offers temples, floating markets, nightlife and street food. From here you can easily reach southern islands and cities like Chiang Mai. Three weeks is ideal to mix city, beaches and nature without rushing.

No, it has 1 stopover. Air China makes an intermediate stop. This lengthens the journey but is standard on intercontinental routes from Europe to Asia, and prices are usually more accessible than direct flights.

Which airline is best for this route?

What's there to see in Bangkok apart from temples?

Night markets like Chatuchak, street food everywhere, the Grand Palace, the Chao Phraya river, intense nightlife. With time, combine the city with trips to Krabi or Phuket islands (frequent coach or train services) or head north to Chiang Mai for nature and elephants.
