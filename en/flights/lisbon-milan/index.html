Fly from Lisbon to Milan from €59 return with Ryanair. The median price sits at €94, so you have room to find deals. If you're looking for an affordable trip to Lombardy's capital, this route is your best bet.

Price and when to book

Prices start at €59 return, though the average fare hovers around €94. We've seen examples up to €124. Book as far in advance as possible: the earlier you purchase, the lower the prices. Ryanair rewards early booking, so don't waste time if you find a rate that works for you.

Airline and stopovers

Ryanair operates this route with direct flights, no stopovers required. Departure from Lisbon (LIS) to Milan (MIL). Direct flights save you time and hassle: boarding, disembarking, and off you go. Bear in mind that Ryanair charges for checked baggage and seat selection if you want to choose your seat.

Best time to travel

There's no guaranteed low season for this route: it depends on demand and availability per date. What matters is your flexibility and how far ahead you book. Search several dates and compare prices. School holidays and peak periods usually drive fares up.

What to do when you arrive in Milan

Milan is the world capital of design and fashion. Visit the Duomo, explore the Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II, check out Castello Sforzesco. If you love Leonardo da Vinci, don't miss the Last Supper at the Convent of Santa Maria delle Grazie. The Navigli bars are perfect for an evening drink by the waterfront.

FAQ

What's the cheapest price for Lisbon-Milan flights?

The lowest price we've recorded is €59 return. The median fare is €94, so that's the most realistic figure to expect. Some trips reach €124 depending on the season.

Does Ryanair fly direct from Lisbon to Milan?

Yes, Ryanair operates direct flights on this route with no stopovers. Departure from Lisbon (LIS) and arrival in Milan (MIL).

When is it cheapest to fly to Milan?

Price depends on your flexibility and when you book. The further ahead you book, the better fares you'll find. Avoid peak holiday periods and try off-season dates, but the key is to reserve as early as possible.

What should I see in Milan over a weekend?

The Duomo, the Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II, Castello Sforzesco, and the Navigli district. If you have time, visit Leonardo's Last Supper at Santa Maria delle Grazie. A weekend gives you time for the city's essentials.
