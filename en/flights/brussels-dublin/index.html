Brussels and Dublin separated by less than two hours of direct flight. Prices start from €44 return, with a median of €48. Ryanair dominates this route with wallet-friendly fares.

Price and when to book

Tickets start from €44 (return), though the median sits at €48. We've seen prices up to €56. Book as soon as possible from now: Ryanair tends to raise fares as departure approaches. Booking 6–8 weeks ahead usually hits the sweet spot between availability and low price.

Airline and direct flights

Ryanair is the sole operator on this route and offers flights with no stops. The journey is short: direct both ways. This eliminates plane changes and connections, saving time and hassle. If you book on a Monday or Tuesday, fares tend to be more competitive.

Best time to travel

Dublin is attractive year-round, but October to November and February to March offer cheaper flights and fewer crowds. Avoid Christmas, Easter and bank holiday dates if you're chasing the lowest fare. Winter (January–February) and early autumn are your best allies for savings.

What to do when you arrive in Dublin

Dublin Airport (DUB) sits 10 km north of the city centre. Bus, taxi or train connect you in 20–30 minutes. Once there: Temple Bar, Guiness Storehouse, Liffey Walk, Trinity College and traditional pubs are must-sees. Spend at least 3–4 days to grasp the essence of Ireland's capital.

FAQ

What is the cheapest price from Brussels to Dublin?

The lowest recorded price is €44 (return). The typical median is €48. Book 6–8 weeks ahead to maximise your chances of landing that fare.

Is it a direct flight?

Yes, Ryanair operates this route non-stop. Flight time is short (under 2 hours) and direct both outbound and return.

What to see in Dublin on a short visit?

With 3–4 days you have enough time for Temple Bar, Trinity College, Guiness Storehouse and a day trip to Howth or Dun Laoghaire. Don't miss local pubs and Irish cuisine.
