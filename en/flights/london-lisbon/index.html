Price and when to book

Flights start from €54 return (checked on 22 October 2026). The median sits at €75 and we've seen fares up to €80. For this route, it pays to search with flexible dates: prices vary a lot by month and day of the week. Book early and pay less (Ryanair tends to push prices up as departure approaches), but keep an eye out for occasional deals if you're patient. Set up alerts on Google Flights or your search tool of choice.

Airline and stops

Ryanair operates this route. Best part: it's a direct flight, no aircraft changes. Depart London, land straight into Lisbon. With Ryanair, watch the baggage: cabin bag is included (but size-controlled), and checked luggage costs extra. They make their money on extras, so calculate what you actually need before paying.

Best time to visit

Lisbon is good year-round, but spring (March–May) and autumn (September–October) offer nice weather without summer crowds or peak prices. If you want bargain flights, low season (November to February) is your friend, though December ticks up a bit. July and August are pricey and rammed. Skip Christmas and New Year if you're after peace and quiet.

What to do in Lisbon

The capital is compact and easy to get around. Stroll through Belém (Tower of Discoveries, Jerónimos Monastery), climb up to São Jorge Castle for 360° views, explore the bohemian Príncipe Real neighbourhood, try Pastéis de Nata at any local bakery. Tram 28 is an iconic ride (though slow). Eat at local tasca restaurants, drink Portuguese wine without spending big, and catch the Tagus at sunset. Five days covers everything comfortably.

FAQ

What's the cheapest price I've seen on this route?

€54 return with Ryanair (data from 22 October 2026). That's the lowest we've recorded. Luck may bring you something similar again tomorrow.

Is the London–Lisbon flight direct?

Yes, it's direct. Ryanair operates it without stops. Depart London, arrive straight into Lisbon. Straightforward and no surprises.

Which is the best airline for this route?

In this case, Ryanair is the operator. It's cheap and performs well on European routes. Watch the baggage: small cabin bag is included, but anything extra costs money. It lands on time most of the time.

What to see in Lisbon over a weekend?

São Jorge Castle, Belém with the Tower of Discoveries, the Alfama neighbourhood (pure atmosphere), Príncipe Real for shopping and nightlife, and a stroll along the Tagus at sunset. Grab Pastéis de Nata at any bakery. Two or three days and you'll see it all without rushing.
