Best time to visit

Estonia shines in summer (June–August) with long daylight and mild temperatures. Spring (May) and autumn (September) offer pleasant weather without crowds. Winter can be a budget option, but pack for intense cold and short days. Don't arrive without a plan: Tallinn deserves at least 2–3 days.

What to do on arrival

Tallinn is Estonia's cultural hub, famed for its medieval Old Town, a UNESCO World Heritage site. Wander cobbled streets, climb the city walls and sample local cuisine. Lennart Meri Tallinn Airport is 4 km from the centre; taxis, buses or even walking are options. Spend time exploring nearby islands, the KUMU art museum or simply discovering design-led cafés.

What should I see in Tallinn over a weekend?

The Old Town is essential: medieval walls, Gothic churches and historic squares. Alexander Nevsky Cathedral is the most iconic Orthodox landmark. Browse Kalamaja (the bohemian quarter), visit KUMU if contemporary art appeals and try Estonian cuisine at local restaurants. The city is compact, so 2–3 days work well.
