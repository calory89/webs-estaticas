Fly from New York to Vienna with Condor from €550 return. The median price sits at €612, so you'll find affordable options if you book with time to spare. One stop awaits you on the way: straightforward to reach Austria's capital.

Price and when to book

Prices start from €550 return, though most travellers pay around €612. We've seen fares up to €701. The earlier you book from today, the better: airlines raise fares as departure approaches. Book at least 6–8 weeks ahead to secure the lowest rates.

Airline and stops

You'll travel with Condor, a German carrier specialised in transatlantic routes. Your outbound flight includes one stop (not direct), which is standard for this route. The journey combines efficiency and value: it's worth the saving against direct flights from other carriers.

Best time to travel

November is a solid choice: low season in European tourism, fewer crowds at landmarks and steadier prices. Autumn in Vienna offers mild weather and lovely scenery. Skip July and August (peak tourism and higher fares) and December (Christmas rush).

What to do when you arrive

Vienna awaits with St Stephen's Cathedral, Schönbrunn Palace and world-class museums. Wander the old town, enjoy Viennese cafés and take in the Danube. It's a compact city and easy to navigate on foot: no car needed. The international airport is around 20 km from the centre.

FAQ

Are there direct flights from New York to Vienna?

This Condor route includes one stop. Direct flights exist with other airlines but usually cost more. The stop adds time but saves you money.

What's the best month to buy these flights?

There's no fixed best month: booking early is what matters. The sooner you purchase from today, the less you'll pay. Check prices weekly and secure your ticket when it hits your budget.

Is Condor reliable for transatlantic flights?

Yes. It's a German airline with decades of experience on long-haul routes. It offers a good balance between price and service for intercontinental travel.

What can I do in Vienna on a tight budget?

Many churches and squares are free to visit. Schönbrunn Palace charges entry, but the gardens are accessible. Viennese cafés are affordable. Public transport is cheap with a day ticket. Eat at local markets rather than tourist restaurants.
