Ryanair flies Rome to Malta non-stop from €34 for the full round trip. The median price sits at €36, so you have a real shot at these fares. With such a short and direct route, it's one of the most affordable Mediterranean trips.

Price and when to book

Prices range from €34 to €40 in the sample checked on 23 October 2026. Book as far in advance as possible: Ryanair typically increases fares closer to departure. Check weekly if you're planning to travel soon—price swings can be substantial.

Airline and stops

Ryanair operates this route non-stop. You'll fly direct from Rome to Malta on a short flight. Note: Ryanair charges for checked luggage, so stick to cabin bag dimensions or budget extra before you book.

Best time to visit

Malta enjoys sun year-round, but October to April offers milder temperatures (18–25°C) and fewer crowds. July and August are scorching and packed with tourists. For peaceful beaches and reasonable prices, aim for autumn and winter. Spring is also excellent.

What to do when you arrive

Malta airport (MLA) sits 5 km from the city centre. Valletta is a UNESCO capital with medieval streets, fortifications and museums. Mellieha, Ghain Tuffieha and Blue Lagoon beaches are must-sees. Early morning flights give you the full day; late afternoon arrivals mean you'll want to save long walks for tomorrow.

FAQ

Can you really find Rome–Malta flights at €34?

Yes, data from October 2026 shows fares from €34. These are limited-time offers, so set alerts on Ryanair's site and check regularly. Booking early is your best chance.

Does Ryanair fly direct from Rome to Malta?

Yes, it's a non-stop flight. The route is very convenient—you can leave early morning and arrive in the afternoon, or pick a time that suits your schedule.

What's the cheapest month to fly Rome to Malta?

We can't say one month is cheaper than another without full historical data. What we do know is that booking early is crucial. October to April typically see fewer tourists and pleasant temperatures.

What should I see in Malta over a weekend?

Valletta (UNESCO World Heritage), the Three Cities (Vittoriosa, Senglea, Cospicua), Mellieha Bay or Ghain Tuffieha beaches and the Blue Lagoon are essential. A weekend covers the basics; if you can extend, add Mdina (the silent city) and Popeye Village.
