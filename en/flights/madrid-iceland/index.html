Best time to visit

Iceland is spectacular year-round, but each season offers different experiences. For Northern Lights, travel October to February. For midnight sun and milder weather, choose June–August. Winter is pricier due to aurora tourism demand. Spring and autumn strike a good balance between cost and climate.

What to do when you arrive

You'll land at Keflavík International Airport, about 50 km from Reykjavík. The capital is the launchpad for Iceland's Golden Circle (geysers, waterfalls, tectonic plates), the Blue Lagoon, and southern glaciers. It's also the base for winter aurora tours. Route 1 connects all major sights across the island.

FAQ

Is €170 the final price or are there hidden costs?

€170 is the base fare return that we found. Wizz Air charges extra for luggage, seat selection and other services, so check thoroughly before confirming. Some costs (airport fees) are usually included, but always read the fine print.

Is Wizz Air UK safe and reliable?

Yes, Wizz Air UK is an established, EU-regulated airline. It operates thousands of flights safely. Bear in mind it's a budget carrier, so expect basic services: no free meals or drinks, limited baggage. But it gets you to your destination securely.

Is it a stop-over or a direct flight?

It's a one-stop flight, not direct. Many flights to Iceland from Madrid include a layover. Total journey time is longer, but the price is very competitive.

What can you see in Iceland in a long weekend?

In 3–4 days you can cover the Golden Circle: geysers, Gullfoss waterfall and Þingvellir park in one day. Spend another on the Blue Lagoon and black sand beaches in the south. In winter with luck, you might catch the Northern Lights at night. It's tight, but manageable with a rental car.
