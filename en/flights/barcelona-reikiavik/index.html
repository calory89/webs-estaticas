Airline and stopovers

You'll fly with Ryanair. The outbound flight includes 1 stopover, so it's not direct. This explains the competitive price: flights with a connection always cost less than direct routes. Use the search tool to confirm the stopover city based on your dates.

Best time to visit

Iceland is worth visiting year-round, but each season offers different experiences. Winter (November to February) is prime for northern lights, though conditions are harsh. Summer (June to August) brings endless daylight and green landscapes. Shoulder season (April–May, September–October) often has lower fares.

What to do when you arrive

You'll land at Keflavík (KEF), about 50 km from Reykjavik. Hire a car to explore the famous Ring Road, visit the Blue Lagoon, hike waterfalls like Skógafoss, and discover glaciers and canyons. Reykjavik has museums and nightlife. Five to seven days covers the essentials.

FAQ

Can you fly to Iceland for under €200?

Yes, it's proven. Prices start from €162 return. This requires date flexibility and booking 2–3 months ahead. January, February and November often have lower fares.

Is the Ryanair flight direct?

No. This Barcelona–Iceland route includes 1 stopover. That's why the price is so competitive compared to direct connections.

How many hours does the journey take with a stopover?

It depends on the stopover city, but typically 6–8 hours total (flight time plus ground time).

What should I see in Iceland if I have only a few days?

The Golden Circle (geysers, waterfalls, national park), the Blue Lagoon and the south coast with Skógafoss are must-sees. Reykjavik deserves a day. Five days covers the basics; 7–10 days lets you add glaciers and northern fjords.
