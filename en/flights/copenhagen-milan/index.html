Fly from Copenhagen to Milan with Ryanair from €73 return. The median price stands at €87, with options up to €101 depending on dates. Book well in advance to secure the best prices on this direct route.

Price and when to book

The lowest recorded price is €73 return. The market median sits at €87. You'll find fares rising to €101 depending on availability and chosen dates. Book as far in advance as possible: prices tend to climb as your departure date approaches. Check regularly to catch price drops, especially on weekdays.

Airline and stopovers

Ryanair operates this route as a direct flight, with no stops. You depart from Copenhagen (CPH) and arrive directly in Milan (MIL) with no intermediate stops. The airline is known for competitive fares, though remember that additional charges may apply for baggage, seat selection or extra services.

Best time to visit

Milan is appealing year-round, but spring (April-May) and autumn (September-October) offer mild weather and fewer crowds than summer. Winter months (November-February) typically see more competitive fares, though with fewer daylight hours. Avoid holiday periods and bank holidays if you're chasing the lowest prices.

What to do when you arrive in Milan

The Milan Cathedral (Duomo) is a must-see. Explore the Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II, visit Sforza Castle and wander through the Brera neighbourhood with its art galleries. Don't miss the Church of Santa Maria delle Grazie, home to Leonardo da Vinci's 'The Last Supper' (advance booking required). The centre is compact and easy to navigate on foot or by public transport.

FAQ

The lowest recorded price is €73 return. However, the median price sits at €87, meaning you'll find that price or close to it regularly. Higher fares above €101 also exist depending on dates.

Is the flight from Copenhagen to Milan direct?

Yes, Ryanair operates this route non-stop. You depart from Copenhagen (CPH) and arrive directly in Milan (MIL).

Which airline flies between Copenhagen and Milan?

Ryanair operates this route. It's known for competitive low fares, though it's worth checking what's included in your base ticket.

What's worth seeing in Milan in a weekend?

The Duomo, Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II, Sforza Castle, the Brera neighbourhood and the Church of Santa Maria delle Grazie are the key highlights. With two or three days you can cover the essentials.
