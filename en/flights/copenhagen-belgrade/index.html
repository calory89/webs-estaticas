Airline and flight type

Air Serbia operates this route nonstop, no layovers. That means zero plane changes, less hassle, and more time to relax. Flights depart from Copenhagen (CPH) and land in Belgrade (BEG). A clear win: no airport layovers to deal with.

Best time to visit

Belgrade shines in spring (April–May) and autumn (September–October), when the weather is mild and museums and terraces are at their best. Skip July and August if you hate crowds. Winter offers cheaper fares if you don't mind the cold.

What to do when you arrive

Belgrade has plenty on offer: Kalemegdan fortress with views over the Danube, the Bohemian Quarter (Boemski Kvart) packed with shops and cafés, and intense nightlife. The centre is very walkable, and public transport works well. Don't miss the Nikola Tesla Museum if you're into science.

Direct. Air Serbia flies nonstop from Copenhagen to Belgrade, which simplifies your journey and saves time on the ground.

What to see in Belgrade in 3–4 days?

Kalemegdan fortress, St. Sava Church, the Bohemian Quarter, riverside parks along the Danube, and the Nikola Tesla Museum are must-sees. Local food is cheap and tasty: try ćevapi (meatballs) at any burek shop.
