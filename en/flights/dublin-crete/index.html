Best time to visit

Crete shines April to October: beaches, perfect weather and archaeological sites without crowds if you skip July–August. November to March is cheaper but rainy and some sites cut hours. December has held prices and is quiet, though the sea is cold for swimming. June and September are the sweet spot: good weather without tourist overload.

What to do when you arrive

You'll land in Heraklion (HER), the capital. The city is 15 km away; use the bus or taxi. Visit the Palace of Knossos (essential Minoan ruins), the Venetian Fortress of Rethymno with its narrow streets, and Samaria Gorge beaches. Eat at local tavernas, try dakos (crispy bread with tomato and feta) and Cretan wine. The east is quieter; the west is wilder.

FAQ

Can I find cheaper flights than €180?

€180 is the lowest we've seen on this route. It's possible but rare. Your best bet is searching with flexible dates (Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday are often cheaper) and booking 8–10 weeks ahead. Price alerts will notify you if it drops.

Is the Lufthansa flight direct?

No, it has 1 stop. Not a direct flight, but it's the available option on this route. The connection is manageable and typically at a major European hub.

Which airport do I have in Crete?

Heraklion (HER), the main one on the island. It's 5 km from the centre, well served by bus and taxi. It's modern and easy to get anywhere on Crete from there.

How many days should I spend in Crete?

A minimum of 5–7 days if you want the essentials (Knossos, Rethymno, a beach). With 10 days you can explore the quiet east, hike Samaria Gorge and visit mountain villages without rush. The journey from Dublin eats almost a day, so factor that in.
