Fly Singapore to Milan with Etihad Airways from €521 return. The median price sits around €614, so you have economic options if you're flexible. One stop separates you from Milan's fashion and design scene.

Price and when to book

Prices range from €521 to €723 based on the sample checked on 23 October. The median sits at €614, giving you a solid reference point for typical fares. For this long-haul route, booking well in advance is advisable: the earlier you purchase, the better. International flights of this distance usually rise as the travel date approaches.

Airline and stops

You'll travel with Etihad Airways, which operates this route with one stop. A single intermission to cover over 10,000 kilometres is quite reasonable. The airline is known for its long-haul service, so you'll have time to rest before reaching Milan.

Best time to visit

Milan shines in spring (April–May) and autumn (September–October), with mild weather and less rain than winter brings. These periods also mean fewer crowds at museums and monuments. Skip December–January if you want to avoid the cold and peak winter tourism, though January does offer January sales if shopping is your priority.

What to do when you arrive

The main airport is Milan-Malpensa. Once in the city, don't miss Milan Cathedral (Duomo), Castello Sforzesco and Leonardo da Vinci's The Last Supper at Santa Maria delle Grazie. Milan is the capital of design and fashion: stroll through Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II and the Fashion Quadrilateral. Grab a coffee at Piazza del Duomo and sample Lombard cuisine.

FAQ

The lowest price we've found is €521 return with Etihad Airways. Keep in mind this is a snapshot price; the median hovers around €614, so use that as your realistic baseline for this route.

It's not direct. Etihad Airways operates this route with one stop, which is standard given the distance. A single layover on such a long flight is normal and gives you a chance to stretch your legs.

Which airline flies Singapore to Milan?

What should I see in Milan in 3 or 4 days?

Must-see: Milan Cathedral and Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II (city centre), Castello Sforzesco (art and history), Leonardo's The Last Supper at Santa Maria delle Grazie (book ahead), Pinacoteca di Brera (Italian art), Navigli (charming canals for an evening stroll). Spend a day exploring fashion and design if that appeals to you.
