Ryanair connects Lisbon with Bologna on direct flights from just €57 return. The median price sits around €108, so you have good opportunities if you search with flexibility. Bologna, Italy's culinary capital, awaits.

Price and when to book

The lowest price we've found is €57 return, with mid-range prices at €108 and €127. Book early: budget airline fares climb fast as departure dates approach. Compare prices over the next few days if you have dates locked in.

Airline and stops

Ryanair operates this route as a direct flight, no stops. It's the quickest and most comfortable option. Keep in mind Ryanair charges for large cabin bags and checked luggage, review their conditions before booking to avoid airport surprises.

Best time to visit

Bologna is appealing year-round, though April-May and September-October offer perfect weather and fewer crowds than July-August. Prices tend to be lower in off-season (winter, except Christmas). Travel midweek if possible: early morning and late-night flights are usually cheaper.

What to do when you arrive

You land at Marconi Airport, 6 km from the city centre. The airport bus takes you downtown in 25 minutes. Explore the medieval arcades, try fresh pasta (tagliatelle al ragù is a must), and climb Torre Asinelli. The Pinacoteca di Brera and Santuario di Madonna di San Luca are unmissable.

FAQ

What's the cheapest price for Lisbon to Bologna flights?

The lowest fare we've recorded is €57 return with Ryanair. The median price is €108. Prices vary significantly by date; book in advance for the best deals.

Is Lisbon to Bologna a direct flight?

Yes, it's direct. Ryanair operates this route with no stops, so your journey is quick and straightforward.

Which airline flies Lisbon to Bologna?

Ryanair is the airline connecting these cities. Remember their base fares don't include checked luggage or large cabin bags, check their conditions when booking.

What's worth seeing in Bologna over a weekend?

Don't miss the arcaded city centre, Piazza Maggiore, the Basilica of San Petronio, medieval towers, and local food (mortadella, tortellini, ragù). With more time, the Santuario di Madonna di San Luca is worth a trip out.
