Rome and Copenhagen are connected by direct flights from €110 return. The median price hovers around €114, so you have real budget options for this route. Fly to Denmark's capital with no intermediaries.

Price and when to book

Flights start from €110 return; we've also seen options at €114 and €135. Book as early as possible. Don't wait until the last minute: prices tend to go up as the departure date approaches. If your calendar is flexible, compare several days to find the best fares.

Airline and flight type

Ryanair operates this route directly: no stops, point-to-point. You depart Rome and arrive in Copenhagen without intermediate stops. With a low-cost carrier like Ryanair, expect standard conditions: hand baggage included in the base fare, checked luggage and other services at extra cost.

Best time to visit

Copenhagen is appealing year-round, but summer (June to August) is peak season with higher prices and more crowds. The most affordable months are usually autumn and winter, though the weather is colder. Spring strikes a balance: good weather and moderate prices.

What to do when you arrive

Copenhagen's international airport (Kastrup) is well connected to the city centre. Once in the capital, visit Nyhavn port, stroll through Tivoli Gardens, explore Christiania or browse Danish museums. The city is compact and easy to navigate by bike. Try smorrebrod (open sandwiches) and enjoy the local food scene.

FAQ

What's the cheapest price Rome-Copenhagen?

The lowest price we've recorded is €110 return with Ryanair. The median sits at €114, so prices are very similar across options.

Is Rome-Copenhagen a direct flight?

Yes, it's direct. Ryanair operates this route non-stop: you depart Rome and arrive in Copenhagen with no intermediate stops.

Which airline flies Rome-Copenhagen?

Ryanair is the airline on this route. They offer the direct flight at the most economical prices we've found.

What can you see in Copenhagen?

Copenhagen has plenty to offer: Nyhavn (historic port), Tivoli Gardens, Christiania (alternative neighbourhood), Amalienborg Palace, art museums and world-class Danish cuisine. It's a compact city, perfect for 2–3 days or longer.
