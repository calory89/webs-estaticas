# Things to Do in Cologne: A 2-Day Guide Beyond the Dom

> Cologne travel guide by Happy Low Cost, 2-day itinerary. Original page: https://www.happylowcost.com/en/destinations/cologne/
> Updated: 2026-11-21

Cologne is not a pretty city, and it doesn't pretend to be. About 90% of the centre was flattened in the Second World War and rebuilt quickly and cheaply, so between the Gothic cathedral and the Romanesque churches you get a lot of 1950s concrete. What makes it work is the people: Cologne is loud, friendly, proudly Catholic and proudly liberal. It has one of Germany's biggest LGBTQ+ scenes and a beer culture built around small glasses that a waiter keeps refilling until you tell him to stop.

It suits beer lovers, art fans, anyone on a cheap flight who wants a city break without Munich prices, and people who like nightlife that isn't trying too hard. **Two days is the right amount**: one for the cathedral, old town and Rhine, one for museums and the Belgian Quarter and Ehrenfeld. Add a third if you want a day trip to Bonn or Brühl.

## What to see in Cologne: the places worth your time

### Cologne Cathedral (Kölner Dom)

The cathedral is the reason most people come, and it lives up to the hype. It took over 600 years to finish and survived the wartime bombing with its twin spires intact. Entry to the nave is free; give it 45 minutes to see the Shrine of the Three Kings behind the altar and the pixelated Gerhard Richter window in the south transept. **Our tip:** go first thing in the morning, before the tour groups arrive, and avoid service times when visitors are restricted.

### Climbing the Dom tower

The south tower has 533 steps up a narrow spiral staircase to a viewing platform around 100 metres up. It costs about €6-8 and takes an hour round trip, longer if there's a jam on the stairs. The view is caged and the climb is hard work, but halfway up you pass the bells, including the enormous "Dicker Pitter", which is worth the sweat alone.

### KölnTriangle viewing platform

This is the best view of Cologne, because it's the one that actually includes the cathedral. The KölnTriangle tower in Deutz, across the Hohenzollern Bridge, has a rooftop platform for about €5 and a lift instead of stairs. Go around sunset. Twenty minutes is enough.

### Hohenzollern Bridge

The railway bridge over the Rhine is covered in hundreds of thousands of love padlocks, and it is the easiest way to walk between the Dom and Deutz. Free, 15 minutes. The trains rumble past right next to you, which is half the fun.

### Altstadt and Groß St. Martin

The old town between the Dom and the river is mostly rebuilt, but the colourful houses on Fischmarkt with Groß St. Martin's tower behind them make the classic Cologne photo. The church is free and quieter than the Dom. Allow an hour to wander the lanes and the Rhine promenade, and treat the restaurants with menus in six languages with suspicion.

### Museum Ludwig

Right beside the Dom, this is one of Europe's strongest modern art collections: a huge Picasso holding, Warhol, Lichtenstein and a lot of German Expressionism. Tickets are about €13-15 and you need two hours. It's the best rainy-day option in the city.

### Wallraf-Richartz Museum

The other big art museum covers the Middle Ages to the 19th century, with a strong room of medieval Cologne painting and a good Impressionist section. It's about €8-13 depending on exhibitions. Allow 90 minutes. If you only have time for one, pick Ludwig for modern art and this one if you prefer old masters.

### NS-Dokumentationszentrum (EL-DE-Haus)

This was the Gestapo headquarters for Cologne, and the cells in the basement still carry the inscriptions prisoners scratched into the walls. It's sobering, well curated and only about €5. Give it 90 minutes. Most English material is in the audio guide, so take it.

### Kolumba

The archdiocese's art museum was built by Peter Zumthor on top of the ruins of a bombed Gothic church. It mixes medieval religious objects with contemporary art and has almost no labels. About €8, one hour. It's an acquired taste, but the building alone is worth the ticket for architecture fans.

### Chocolate Museum (Schokoladenmuseum)

Located on a peninsula in the Rheinauhafen, it shows the history of chocolate and has a small working production line and a chocolate fountain where staff hand you a wafer. It costs about €16-18 and takes 90 minutes. Kids love it. Adults may find the ticket steep for what is partly a branded shop.

### Rheinauhafen

The old harbour south of the Altstadt is now offices and flats, dominated by the three Kranhäuser (crane houses), huge upside-down L-shaped buildings. It's free and pleasant for a 30-minute walk along the water from the Chocolate Museum, especially on a sunny evening.

### The Romanesque churches

Cologne has twelve major Romanesque churches, all rebuilt after the war. St. Gereon, with its decagonal dome, and St. Maria im Kapitol are the two to prioritise. They're free, and 20-30 minutes each is enough. You'll have them almost to yourself.

### Belgian Quarter (Belgisches Viertel)

This grid of streets west of the ring road, named after Belgian cities, is where Cologne actually lives: independent shops, cafés, natural wine bars and the leafy Brüsseler Platz, where people drink outside on summer nights. Free to wander; give it a full afternoon.

### Ehrenfeld

The former industrial district west of the centre is the city's street art and nightlife area. Venloer Straße has Turkish bakeries and cheap eats, and the walls around the S-Bahn station are covered in murals. It's two stops from the Hauptbahnhof by S-Bahn and worth an evening.

## Things to do in Cologne

Many of the best things to do in Cologne involve a glass in your hand. A few ideas beyond ticking off sights:

- **Do a Brauhaus crawl.** Kölsch is served in 0.2-litre glasses (about €2.50-3 each) by a waiter called a Köbes. Try Früh am Dom, Päffgen on Friesenstraße, Malzmühle and Peters Brauhaus. Put your coaster on top of your glass when you're done, or he'll keep bringing more.

- **Take a Rhine cruise.** A one-hour panorama trip from the Altstadt costs about €15-18. It's touristy but gives you the skyline from the water.

- **Visit the Farina Fragrance Museum.** This is where Eau de Cologne was invented, across from the town hall. The guided tours cost about €8-10 and take an hour. The 4711 flagship store on Glockengasse has a free cologne fountain.

- **Walk Melaten cemetery.** This huge, overgrown 19th-century cemetery in Lindenthal is full of dramatic statues and is unexpectedly peaceful.

- **Go to a football match.** 1. FC Köln's crowd at the RheinEnergieStadion is passionate. Tickets are hard to get, so check the club's resale platform.

- **Experience Carnival.** The season opens on 11 November at 11:11, and the main street carnival runs from Weiberfastnacht (the Thursday before Lent) to Rosenmontag. The whole city drinks in costume for six days.

- **Explore the Christmas markets** from late November. The one by the Dom is the busiest; the Angels' Market on Neumarkt and the harbour market at the Chocolate Museum are nicer.

## Cologne for free (or almost)

A lot of what to see in Cologne costs nothing. The cathedral nave, Groß St. Martin and all the Romanesque churches are free. So is the Hohenzollern Bridge, the Rhine promenade and the Rheinauhafen walk.

- **Flora and Botanical Garden** in Riehl is free and well kept, with glasshouses and a formal garden.

- **Rheinpark** in Deutz has big lawns with cathedral views across the river. Bring a picnic and a few Kölsch bottles from a supermarket.

- **Ehrenfeld street art**. Do a self-guided walk around Venloer Straße and the side streets.

- **Museums**: the city museums (including Ludwig and Wallraf) usually offer free or discounted entry for Cologne residents on the first Thursday of the month (KölnTag). Check whether it applies to visitors before planning around it.

- **Brüsseler Platz** on a summer evening costs only what you drink.

## What is overrated (and what to do instead)

- **Restaurants on the Domplatte and Heumarkt.** Prices are higher and the food is generic. Walk ten minutes to Friesenstraße or the Belgian Quarter instead.

- **Hohe Straße and Schildergasse.** These are some of Germany's busiest shopping streets, and they're full of the same chains as everywhere else. For independent shops, try the Belgian Quarter and Ehrenfeld.

- **The Dom tower as your only view.** The climb is fun, but the cage blocks photos and you can't see the cathedral itself. The KölnTriangle is cheaper, easier and has the better view.

- **The Chocolate Museum for adults without kids.** It's fine, but €16-18 is a lot. If you just want chocolate, visit the shop downstairs and spend the rest on Museum Ludwig.

## When to visit Cologne

**May to September** is the best time: 18-25°C, long evenings and beer gardens along the Rhine. Summer brings occasional heatwaves and heavy showers. Winter is grey, damp and around 2-6°C, but the Christmas markets from late November to 23 December make up for it, though hotel prices rise.

Carnival in February is a separate experience. Book months ahead and expect to pay double for a room. Prices also jump during big trade fairs at Koelnmesse, especially Gamescom in late August, when rooms can triple. Check the fair calendar before booking. The quietest and cheapest months are **January (after New Year), March and early November**.

## Where to stay in Cologne: best areas

- **Altstadt and around the Dom:** This area is best for a first short visit, with everything walkable and the Hauptbahnhof next door. It's noisy at weekends. Mid-range hotels cost about €110-200 a night.

- **Belgian Quarter and around Rudolfplatz:** These neighbourhoods are best for food, bars and a more local feel. They're 15-20 minutes on foot from the Dom. Rooms cost about €100-170.

- **Ehrenfeld:** This is the cheapest of the interesting areas, with good nightlife and quick S-Bahn links to the centre. It suits younger travellers and repeat visitors. Rooms cost about €70-130.

- **Deutz:** This area across the river has great cathedral views and is next to the trade fair grounds, so it's good value except during fairs. Rooms cost about €80-150 normally, and a lot more in fair weeks.

Hostels in the centre and Ehrenfeld charge about €30-45 for a dorm bed.

## What to eat in Cologne

Cologne food is hearty Rhineland pub cooking, best eaten in a Brauhaus. What to order:

- **Halve Hahn:** Despite the name ("half a chicken"), it's a rye roll with a thick slice of aged Gouda, mustard and raw onion. It's the classic Kölsch snack at about €5-7.

- **Himmel un Ääd:** "Heaven and earth" is fried black pudding (Flönz) with mashed potato, apple sauce and fried onions. It's better than it sounds.

- **Rheinischer Sauerbraten:** This pot roast is marinated for days, served in a sweet-sour raisin gravy with potato dumplings and red cabbage. Traditionally it was horse meat, but nowadays it's usually beef.

- **Reibekuchen:** These potato pancakes come with apple sauce. You'll find them at markets and in Brauhäuser, often only on certain days.

- **Schweinshaxe:** This is a giant roast pork knuckle. Order one between two people unless you're very hungry.

- **Kölsch:** This light, crisp top-fermented beer can legally only be brewed in and around Cologne. Ask for "ein Kölsch".

A Brauhaus lunch with a main and two Kölsch costs about €20-25. Dinner with a starter, main and a few beers is around €35-45 a head. Ehrenfeld döner and Turkish pide cost €6-9, and a bakery breakfast is under €6. Many traditional places are cash-friendly, though cards are now widely accepted. Carry some euros anyway.

## Airport to city centre and getting around

**Cologne Bonn Airport (CGN)** is the main airport, with an underground station. The S19 and regional trains (RE6, RE8) reach Köln Hauptbahnhof, right beside the Dom, in about 15 minutes. A single ticket costs about €3-4. Buy it from the machines or the DB Navigator and KVB apps, and validate it if it's a paper ticket. A taxi costs about €35-45 and isn't faster.

From **Düsseldorf Airport**, regional trains take about 40-60 minutes to Cologne and cost roughly €15-20. Some low-cost deals land there, so it's worth comparing.

Within the city, the centre is walkable. KVB trams and U-Bahn lines cover the rest, and S-Bahn trains run to Ehrenfeld and Deutz. Options include:

- A single short-distance ticket for about €2.50-3.50.

- A 24-hour ticket for about €9-10.

- The **KölnCard** (about €10 for 24 hours), which adds discounts on museums and tours.

- The **Deutschlandticket** (about €63 a month), which covers all local transport in Germany, including day trips to Bonn, Düsseldorf and Aachen on regional trains. It's a subscription you cancel before the next month, so it's worth it if you're staying several days or doing day trips.

## Day trips from Cologne

- **Bonn:** The former West German capital is 20-30 minutes away by regional train or tram line 16/18. You can visit Beethoven's birthplace, the Haus der Geschichte (free, excellent on post-war Germany) and the Museum Mile. Plan for half a day to a full day.

- **Brühl:** Augustusburg Palace and the Falkenlust hunting lodge are a UNESCO-listed rococo site. The palace is about 15-20 minutes by regional train. It's a good half-day trip, and Phantasialand theme park is nearby if you have kids.

- **Düsseldorf:** Cologne's rival is about 25-30 minutes by train. Drink Altbier in the Altstadt, walk the Rhine promenade and see the MedienHafen architecture. Don't mention Kölsch.

- **Aachen:** Charlemagne's cathedral is a UNESCO site and arguably more moving than Cologne's. It's about 35-60 minutes by train. Try the Aachener Printen gingerbread.

- **Königswinter and the Drachenfels:** Ride the train to Königswinter via Bonn (about an hour), then take a rack railway or walk up to the castle ruin for Rhine views.

## Common mistakes and scams

- **Not putting your coaster on your glass.** The Köbes will keep replacing your empty Kölsch and marking your coaster. That's not a scam, just how it works, but it adds up.

- **Riding without a validated ticket.** Plain-clothes inspectors are common, and the fine is €60. Paper tickets often need stamping in the machine on board or on the platform.

- **Getting pickpocketed around the Hauptbahnhof and Domplatte.** Watch your phone, especially at Carnival and the Christmas market.

- **Falling for clipboard "petitions".** People on the Domplatte asking you to sign for a charity will then ask for money. Just walk on.

- **Booking during a trade fair by accident.** Check the Koelnmesse calendar before you lock in flights.

- **Assuming everywhere takes cards.** It's much better than it was, but small bars and market stalls may still prefer cash.

## Cologne FAQ

### How many days do you need in Cologne?

Two days covers the cathedral, the old town, the main museums and the best neighbourhoods. Add a third if you want a day trip to Bonn, Brühl or Aachen.

### Is Cologne Cathedral free to enter?

Yes, the cathedral itself is free. The tower climb costs about €6-8, and the Treasury has a separate ticket.

### Is Cologne worth visiting?

Yes, especially for beer, art and nightlife. It's less picturesque than Bavarian cities, but friendlier and cheaper, and the cathedral is one of Europe's great buildings.

### How do I get from Cologne Bonn Airport to the city centre?

Take the S19 or a regional train to Köln Hauptbahnhof. It takes about 15 minutes and costs €3-4.

### What is Cologne famous for?

The cathedral, Kölsch beer, Eau de Cologne, Carnival and its Christmas markets. It's also known as one of Germany's most open, LGBTQ+-friendly cities.

### Is Cologne expensive?

Not by German standards. Food and beer are cheaper than in Munich or Hamburg, but hotels spike during trade fairs, Carnival and December.

### Where is the best view of Cologne?

The KölnTriangle platform in Deutz, because it looks straight across the Rhine at the cathedral and the Hohenzollern Bridge. The Rheinpark lawns give a similar view for free.

### When is Cologne Carnival?

The street carnival runs from Weiberfastnacht, the Thursday before Lent, to Rosenmontag in February or early March. The season officially opens on 11 November at 11:11.
