# The Algarve in 6 Days: Beaches, Caves and Cheap Eats

> The Algarve travel guide by Happy Low Cost, 6-day itinerary. Original page: https://www.happylowcost.com/en/destinations/algarve/
> Updated: 2026-11-21

The Algarve is Portugal's southern coast: about 150 km of golden cliffs, sea caves and long sandy islands, with whitewashed towns behind them that were here long before the golf courses. Some of it has been flattened by package tourism, Albufeira's "Strip" being the obvious case. Other parts haven't changed much in decades: the wild west coast around Aljezur, the lagoon villages near Tavira, the cork-and-chestnut hills of Monchique. The trick is knowing which is which.

It suits beach people, hikers, surfers, families and anyone who wants grilled fish and cheap wine in the sun. It does not suit travellers without a car who want to see everything in a weekend. Give it **6 days**: three in the west (Lagos and the cliffs) and three in the east (Tavira and the Ria Formosa). Two or three days only works if you pick one end and stay put.

## What to see in The Algarve: the places worth your time

### Ponta da Piedade, Lagos

This is the most photogenic stretch of coast in the region: ochre sea stacks, arches and grottoes just south of Lagos. Walk the clifftop path and the wooden boardwalk from Praia do Camilo (about 40 minutes each way from town), and it costs nothing. A small-boat or kayak tour through the grottoes runs about €20-35. **Our tip:** come at sunset, or at 8am if you want photos without twenty other tripods.

### Benagil Cave (Algar de Benagil)

The domed sea cave with a hole in its roof is the Algarve's postcard. Since 2024 you can't land on the beach inside, so you see it from a boat or a kayak that pulls in briefly. Boats from Benagil, Portimão or Armação de Pêra cost about €20-30 for an hour or so, and guided kayak tours around €35-45. **Our tip:** book a morning kayak tour, because seas are calmer early and the afternoon queue of boats at the entrance is absurd. Don't swim there from Benagil beach. People drown doing it.

### Seven Hanging Valleys Trail (Percurso dos Sete Vales Suspensos)

This is the best short hike on the south coast: about 6 km one way from Praia da Marinha to Praia de Vale Centeanes, past Benagil and the clifftop view down into the cave's skylight. It's free and takes 2-3 hours one way. Start early in summer because there's almost no shade, and arrange a taxi back or simply walk it both ways.

### Praia da Marinha

Often listed among Europe's best beaches, and fair enough: double arches, clear water and steps down a cliff. It's free. Parking is limited and fills by 10am in July and August. Bring snorkel gear and swim along the rocks to the left.

### Lagos old town

Lagos is the most likeable base in the west: walls, cobbled lanes, the gilded Igreja de Santo António and plenty of bars. The Museu de Lagos and the church share a ticket of about €3. Give it half a day, plus evenings. The Mercado Municipal on the waterfront is good for fruit and fish in the morning.

### Sagres and Cabo de São Vicente

This is the windy southwestern tip of Europe. The Fortaleza de Sagres (about €3) sits on a huge headland with a giant stone wind rose, and the lighthouse at Cabo de São Vicente is 6 km further. The cape itself is free. Sunset here is a ritual, so expect crowds and a sausage van selling "the last bratwurst before America". Bring a jacket even in August.

### Tavira

The prettiest town in the Algarve: a Roman-style bridge over the Gilão river, hipped roofs and around twenty churches. Climb to the castle ruins (free) and see the Torre de Tavira camera obscura (about €5) for a live projection of the rooftops. Allow a day including the island.

### Ilha de Tavira and Praia do Barril

These are long sandy barrier islands in the Ria Formosa. A ferry from Tavira or Quatro Águas costs about €2-3 return. For Praia do Barril, cross the footbridge from Pedras d'El Rei and take the little train (about €2) or walk 1.5 km to the dunes with the old tuna fishermen's "anchor cemetery". The sand runs for miles, so there's always space.

### Ria Formosa lagoon

This is a protected lagoon system of saltmarsh, channels and islands between Faro and Cabanas. Boat tours from Faro or Olhão (about €20-30) take in Ilha Deserta and Culatra. Birdwatchers come for flamingos and spoonbills. **Our tip:** the public ferries from Olhão to Culatra and Armona do the same thing for a fraction of the price.

### Faro old town (Cidade Velha)

Most people fly in and leave straight away, which is a mistake for a few hours. Go through the Arco da Vila into the walled town and the cathedral (about €5, climb the tower for lagoon views). Then see the Capela dos Ossos at Igreja do Carmo, a chapel lined with monks' bones (about €2-3). Storks nest on half the rooftops.

### Silves

Silves was the Moorish capital of the region, and its red sandstone castle (about €3) is the best-preserved in the Algarve. It's inland, quiet and gives you a break from sand. An hour or two is enough, and you can combine it with Monchique.

### Monchique and Fóia

Fóia is the Algarve's highest point (about 900 m), with views to both coasts on a clear day. Monchique town below makes medronho (strawberry-tree brandy) and has spa waters at Caldas de Monchique. It's free and needs a car. It's often 8-10°C cooler than the coast, which is welcome in August.

### The west coast: Arrifana, Odeceixe, Bordeira

This is the Costa Vicentina natural park: Atlantic surf, dark cliffs, no high-rises. Arrifana has a fishing harbour under a cliff, Odeceixe a river meeting the sea, and Bordeira enormous dunes. The water is cold and the currents serious, so swim where there are lifeguards.

## Things to do in The Algarve

The coast is the headline, but the best days often involve doing something rather than just looking.

- **Kayak or SUP the caves:** From Benagil or Ferragudo, 2-3 hours for about €35-45. It's the closest you'll get to the grottoes legally.

- **Surf lesson:** Sagres and Arrifana have consistent waves and dozens of schools. Expect €35-50 for a group lesson including board and wetsuit.

- **Walk a stage of the Rota Vicentina:** The Fishermen's Trail along the west coast is sandy and tiring, and spectacular.

- **Loulé Saturday market:** The Moorish-style covered market plus a gypsy market outside. Go mid-morning for honey, cheese, cork goods and pastries.

- **Olhão fish market:** Two red-brick halls on the waterfront. Saturday morning adds a farmers' market outside.

- **Dolphin watching:** Trips from Lagos or Albufeira run about €35-50. Common dolphins are seen most days, but go with operators that keep their distance.

- **Wine tasting:** The Algarve now makes decent wine. Quinta dos Vales near Estômbar and Morgado do Quintão near Silves do tastings, which you should book ahead.

- **A medronho tasting in Monchique:** It's around 45% alcohol, so sip it and let someone else drive.

## The Algarve for free (or almost)

Almost everything that matters here is free. Every beach in Portugal is public, and the clifftop paths cost nothing.

- The Ponta da Piedade boardwalk and the Seven Hanging Valleys trail.

- Sunset at Cabo de São Vicente (only the fortress at Sagres charges).

- Tavira's castle gardens and riverside, and Silves' old streets.

- The Olhão and Loulé markets for browsing, and a €1 coffee with a pastel de nata.

- The public ferry to Culatra or Armona islands for €2-4, a lagoon tour in all but name.

- Praia do Barril's anchor cemetery if you walk instead of taking the train.

## What is overrated (and what to do instead)

**Albufeira's Strip (Avenida Sá Carneiro).** It's stag parties, neon and €10 cocktails. Albufeira's old town and beaches are fine, but if you want nightlife with character, Lagos does it better.

**Standing at Benagil beach waiting to "see the cave".** You can't swim in safely and can't land, so you'll mostly watch boats. Do the kayak tour or view it from the clifftop skylight on the trail.

**Vilamoura.** It's a marina full of yachts and chain restaurants. Nearby Loulé or Olhão show you a real Algarve town.

**Praia da Rocha in August.** It's a huge beach backed by high-rises and packed with sunbeds. Praia dos Três Irmãos in Alvor, a few minutes west, has nicer rocks and fewer people.

## When to visit The Algarve

The best months are **May, June and September**: sea warm enough by June, around 25-28°C on land, and prices well below summer. July and August are hot (often 30°C+), crowded and expensive, and Benagil becomes a traffic jam. October is still good for beaches and hiking.

November to March is mild (15-18°C) and sunny on most days, ideal for walking the Rota Vicentina and very cheap, though some beach restaurants close. The Atlantic stays cool all year, peaking around 20-22°C in late summer. The west coast is always colder and windier than the east. Almond blossom in late January and February is a lovely bonus inland.

## Where to stay in The Algarve: best areas

- **Lagos:** Best all-round base for the west, with walkable beaches, nightlife and buses. Hostels €25-40, mid-range doubles €80-150 in summer.

- **Tavira:** Best for couples and slower travellers, with charm, island beaches and a train station. Doubles €70-140.

- **Carvoeiro or Ferragudo:** Ideal for the cliffs and caves in the middle of the coast, and good for families with a car. Apartments €70-150.

- **Faro:** Cheapest city base, handy for late flights and the Ria Formosa, but no beach in town. Doubles €60-110.

- **Aljezur or Sagres:** For surfers and hikers, with a rural, laid-back feel. Guesthouses €50-120. A car is essential.

## What to eat in The Algarve

Seafood, simply grilled, is the point. Look for places with a charcoal grill outside and a short handwritten menu.

- **Cataplana:** Seafood (clams, prawns, fish, sometimes pork) steamed in a copper clam-shaped pan. It's for two and costs about €35-50.

- **Sardinhas assadas:** Grilled sardines, best June to September, eaten on bread. About €10-14 a plate.

- **Polvo:** Octopus grilled or as a salad, the speciality of Santa Luzia.

- **Frango piri-piri:** Chilli chicken, famous in Guia (near Albufeira) and Monchique. About €10-12 for half a chicken.

- **Amêijoas à Bulhão Pato:** Clams in garlic, coriander and white wine.

- **Arroz de marisco:** A soupy seafood rice. Order it to share.

- **Sweets:** Dom Rodrigo (egg threads and almond) and almond-and-fig marzipan shapes.

Lunch is cheapest: a **prato do dia** (dish of the day) with drink and coffee runs €10-14 in local cafés. Dinner with wine costs €25-40 per person in a decent restaurant. A beer is €2-3 and a coffee €1. Fish sold by weight is priced per kilo, so ask what your piece will cost before they grill it.

## Airport to city centre and getting around

Faro Airport is the gateway, 6 km from Faro town.

- **Bus to Faro centre:** Próximo lines 14 and 16, about 20 minutes, around €2-3.

- **Taxi or Uber to Faro:** 10-15 minutes, €12-20. Uber and Bolt work across the Algarve.

- **To Lagos:** Take a bus to Faro, then a Vamus Algarve express coach or the regional train. Both take about 1h30-2h, €7-10.

- **Airport transfers:** Shared shuttles to Lagos or Albufeira cost €15-25 per person.

The **regional train** runs from Lagos to Vila Real de Santo António via Silves, Albufeira, Faro, Olhão and Tavira, slow but cheap (Faro to Tavira about €3). Note that Albufeira's station is a few km from town. **Vamus Algarve** buses fill the gaps. For cliffs, west-coast beaches and Monchique you really want a **hire car**, from €20 a day in winter to €50+ in August. The A22 motorway uses electronic tolls, so get your rental's transponder.

## Day trips from The Algarve

- **Seville (Spain):** About 2 hours' drive from Tavira, or 2h30-3h by coach from Faro. It's a long day, so staying a night is better.

- **Mértola:** A fortified Alentejo town above the Guadiana river, with a church that was once a mosque. About 1 hour north of Tavira by car.

- **Alcoutim and the Guadiana:** A sleepy river village facing Spain, with a zip line across the border. About 1 hour from Tavira by car.

- **Lisbon:** Around 3 hours by train from Faro. It's better as a separate trip than a day trip, but it can be done.

- **Odeceixe and the Alentejo coast:** About 45 minutes north of Lagos by car, to see how wild Portugal's coast gets.

## Common mistakes and scams

- **The couvert:** Bread, olives and butter put on your table aren't free (€2-6). Send them back if you don't want them.

- **Fish by the kilo:** A "€60/kg" sea bass can become a €45 main. Ask for the weight first.

- **Swimming on red-flag days:** Atlantic rip currents are no joke, especially on the west coast. Obey the lifeguards.

- **Cliff edges:** They crumble. Stay behind the warning lines and don't sit under overhangs on beaches.

- **Car rental extras:** Watch out for hard-sell insurance and toll transponder fees at the desk. Read the terms before you land.

- **Street drug offers in Lagos and Albufeira:** They're common, and the "hashish" is usually bay leaves or stock cubes. Ignore them.

- **Underestimating distances:** Sagres to Tavira is 2 hours. Don't base yourself in one spot and try to day-trip everything.

## The Algarve FAQ

### How many days do you need in the Algarve?

Six days lets you see both the cliffs of the west and the islands of the east without rushing. With three days, stick to one end, Lagos or Tavira.

### Can you go inside Benagil Cave?

You can enter by kayak or boat, but landing on the beach inside has been banned since 2024. Swimming there from Benagil beach is dangerous and not allowed.

### Do you need a car in the Algarve?

Not for the main towns, which the train and buses connect. For the cliffs, west-coast beaches and Monchique, a car makes life far easier.

### Is Lagos or Albufeira better to stay in?

Lagos for most people: more character, better beaches within walking distance and a livelier but less rowdy evening scene. Albufeira suits package holidays and families who want resort facilities.

### Is the sea warm in the Algarve?

It's cool by Mediterranean standards. Expect around 20-22°C in August and September on the south coast, and colder on the west coast.

### What is the best month to visit the Algarve?

June and September give the best balance of warm sea, sun, fewer crowds and lower prices.

### What is the most beautiful beach in the Algarve?

Praia da Marinha usually wins for cliffs and clear water. For space and wildness, Bordeira on the west coast and Praia do Barril in the east are hard to beat.
