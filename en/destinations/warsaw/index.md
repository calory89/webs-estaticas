# Warsaw travel guide: what to see, eat and plan in 3 days

## Why go to Warsaw

Warsaw is not a postcard city, and that is part of the appeal. In 1944 and 1945 most of it was destroyed, and what you walk through today is a place that was rebuilt, argued over and rebuilt again. Cobbled Old Town streets sit a short tram ride from Soviet-era monuments and glass towers. Poles then added a food scene, a café culture and a riverbank that locals actually use.

It is also good value, well connected by low-cost airlines, and easy to handle in a long weekend. If you want history that is told honestly, with no gloss, this is one of the best cities in Europe for it.

## What to see in Warsaw

- **Old Town (Stare Miasto) and Castle Square.** Rebuilt brick by brick after the war, partly from 18th-century paintings by Bellotto, and now a UNESCO site. The Market Square, with its mermaid statue, is lovely at 8 a.m. and crowded by noon. Come early or after dinner.

- **Royal Castle.** The rooms are worth the ticket, especially the Canaletto Room, which holds Bellotto's views of the city before it vanished.

- **Warsaw Rising Museum.** Set in a former tram power station in Wola, it covers the 1944 uprising with sound, objects and a replica bomber. It is intense. Give it at least two hours.

- **POLIN Museum of the History of Polish Jews.** It stands in Muranów, on the site of the former ghetto, opposite the Monument to the Ghetto Heroes. The medieval-to-modern timeline is one of the best museum designs in Europe.

- **Palace of Culture and Science.** Stalin's "gift" is still the city's best-known building. Locals joke that the best view of Warsaw is from the top, because you can't see the palace from there. The 30th-floor terrace is worth it on a clear day.

- **Łazienki Park.** Warsaw's finest park, with peacocks, red squirrels, the Palace on the Isle and the big Chopin monument. From May to September there are free Chopin recitals on Sundays at noon and 4 p.m.

- **Wilanów Palace.** A baroque royal residence in the south with gardens and a poster museum. It is the one big building that escaped destruction. Take bus 180 from the centre.

- **Praga and the Koneser Centre.** Across the Vistula, Praga kept its prewar courtyards and bullet-scarred façades. The Koneser Centre is a former vodka factory turned into restaurants and galleries. Ząbkowska Street is good for bars.

- **Krakowskie Przedmieście and Nowy Świat.** This is the Royal Route, a long walk from Castle Square past churches, the university, the Presidential Palace and cafés. Plan an hour without stops, three with them.

## Things to do

Look for the Chopin benches around town. Press a button and each plays a piece. It is silly and charming. In summer the Multimedia Fountain Park by the Old Town puts on water-and-light shows after dark.

Walk or cycle the Vistula boulevards. The west bank is paved and calm, with a long stretch of bars and sand near the Poniatowski Bridge, while the east side is wilder and quieter. The Copernicus Science Centre is a good rainy-day choice if you travel with kids.

If you have an extra day, take the fast train to Kraków (about two and a half hours) or go to Żelazowa Wola, Chopin's birthplace, about 50 km west. Neither is essential, and Warsaw alone can fill four days.

## When to go

The climate is continental. Summers (June to August) are warm, usually in the low to mid 20s °C, with the odd thunderstorm. May, June and September are the sweet spot: long light, open terraces and fewer crowds.

Winters are cold and grey, often around zero or below, with snow possible from December to February. The one good reason to come then is the Christmas markets on Castle Square and in the Old Town, which are cosy and cheap. July and August are also the best months for the riverside and open-air concerts.

## Where to stay

**Śródmieście (city centre):** the best base for a first visit. Everything is a short tram or metro ride away. Expect hostels from about 15 to 25 euros, solid mid-range hotels from 60 to 110, and top hotels from 150 upwards.

**Old Town:** beautiful, and quiet at night, but pricier and with fewer good restaurants.

**Powiśle:** a relaxed area by the river with cafés, close to the university and the Copernicus Science Centre. It is my pick for couples.

**Praga:** the more affordable and more local option, with plenty of bars and a creative scene. The metro and trams make the commute easy.

**Near Plac Zbawiciela:** best for nightlife and for restaurants, with a lively crowd even on weeknights.

## What to eat and where

Start with **pierogi**, ideally the potato-and-cheese ruskie, or minced meat. Add **żurek**, a sour rye soup served with sausage and egg, sometimes in a bread bowl. **Bigos**, a slow-cooked cabbage and meat stew, and **gołąbki**, cabbage rolls, are the hearty classics. For something cheaper, try a potato pancake (placki ziemniaczane) or a zapiekanka, an open baguette baked with mushrooms and cheese.

The best budget option is the **milk bar** (bar mleczny), a Communist-era canteen that is still cheap and still busy. Bar Mleczny Prasowy on Marszałkowska and Bar Bambino on Krucza are well-known examples. You pay at the counter, queue, and get a full lunch for a few euros. Expect plain décor and no frills, but the food is honest.

For something sweet, try a **pączek** (Polish doughnut), or sit down for hot chocolate at the E. Wedel chocolate lounge on Szpitalna. Wash it all down with a Polish craft beer, or a vodka tasting in Praga.

## Getting from the airport and getting around

Chopin Airport (WAW) is only about 10 km from the centre. The simplest way into town is the SKM or Koleje Mazowieckie train to Warszawa Śródmieście or Centralna, taking about 20 minutes for a couple of euros. Bus 175 also runs to the centre and the Old Town. Taxis and Bolt or Uber are cheap by Western European standards.

Some low-cost flights use Modlin Airport, about 40 km north. A train or shuttle bus links it to the centre in roughly 40 to 60 minutes, so check which airport your ticket uses before booking.

Inside the city, buy a ZTM ticket and use the metro (two lines), trams and buses on the same fare. A single ticket costs about a euro, and day passes are a few euros. The centre is also very walkable, and Veturilo city bikes are an easy way to get along the river.

## Rough daily budget

- **Budget:** about 40 to 55 euros a day, with a hostel, milk-bar lunches, public transport and one museum.

- **Mid-range:** about 80 to 120 euros, with a decent hotel, restaurant dinners and a couple of entrances.

- **Comfortable:** 150 euros or more, with a good hotel, taxis and cocktail bars.

Poland uses the złoty (PLN), not the euro. Museum tickets are usually a few euros, and several museums have a free day each week, so check before you go.

## FAQs

### Is Warsaw worth visiting?

Yes, especially if you like history, food and fair prices. It is less pretty than Kraków, but more real.

### How many days do I need?

Three days covers the main sights comfortably. Two days is the minimum, and four lets you add Wilanów or a day trip.

### Is Warsaw safe?

Generally yes. Normal city precautions apply: watch for pickpockets on crowded trams and around the central station at night.

### Do I need to speak Polish?

No. Younger people and most hotel and restaurant staff speak good English. Learning "dzień dobry" (hello) and "dziękuję" (thank you) goes down well.

### Can I pay by card?

Almost everywhere, including trams and milk bars. Carry a little cash in złoty for small stalls and some markets.
