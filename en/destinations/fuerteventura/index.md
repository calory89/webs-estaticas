# Fuerteventura in 6 Days: Dunes, Wind and Wild Beaches

> Fuerteventura travel guide by Happy Low Cost, 6-day itinerary. Original page: https://www.happylowcost.com/en/destinations/fuerteventura/
> Updated: 2026-11-21

Fuerteventura is the Canary Island for people who like space more than nightlife. It is brown, flat in places, almost treeless, and permanently windy. That sounds like a warning, but it is the point: the beaches are some of the longest and emptiest in Europe, the water is a ridiculous shade of turquoise, and you can drive twenty minutes from a resort and have a volcano to yourself. Lush hiking trails, big-city culture and grand old towns are not on offer here. Tenerife and Gran Canaria do those better.

It suits surfers, kitesurfers, families who want sand and shallow water, couples who want quiet, and anyone happy to rent a car and explore. You need **6 days** to see it properly: the island is about 100 km long, the best spots sit at opposite ends, and the far south (Cofete, Jandía) deserves a full day on its own. With less time, pick north or south and accept you will miss half.

## What to see in Fuerteventura: the places worth your time

### Parque Natural de las Dunas de Corralejo

This is the island's headline sight: around 24 square kilometres of pale sand dunes running straight into the sea, just south of Corralejo. The FV-1 road cuts right through, so you can park in the lay-bys and walk in. Give it two to three hours with a swim at Grandes Playas. It is free. Our tip: come at sunset, when the dunes turn gold and the tour coaches have gone, and bring a windbreaker, because blowing sand stings.

### Isla de Lobos

A small, protected volcanic island 2 km off Corralejo with a lagoon-like beach (Playa de La Concha), a lighthouse walk and a climb up Montaña de la Caldera. The ferry from Corralejo harbour takes 15-20 minutes and costs about €15-20 return. You also need a free online permit from the Canarian government, with a daily cap on visitors, so book it days ahead. Allow a half day. Take the earliest boat, carry water and food (there is one small restaurant and little else), and wear proper shoes for the caldera.

### Cofete beach

Cofete is 12 km of wild, empty beach backed by the jagged Jandía mountains, and the most dramatic landscape on the island. Getting there means a rough 20 km gravel track from Morro Jable, which many rental contracts exclude from insurance. The alternative is the Tiadhe 4x4 bus (line 111) from Morro Jable for a few euros. Plan a full day including the drive south. Do not swim: the currents are deadly and there are no lifeguards. Look out for the mysterious Villa Winter on the hillside, and stop at the Mirador de Cofete on the way down for the best view.

### Sotavento and Risco del Paso

On the Jandía peninsula's east coast, the Sotavento beaches form a vast lagoon at low tide with water so clear it looks photoshopped. Risco del Paso is the best access point. It is free, and you need half a day. Check the tide tables: low tide creates the lagoon and the sandbanks, high tide covers most of it. In summer it hosts kitesurf and windsurf world championships, so expect crowds and parking chaos then.

### El Cotillo

A fishing village on the northwest coast that has stayed small and laid-back. South of the village sit long surf beaches with cliffs; to the north are the calm, shallow lagoons (Los Lagos) that are perfect for kids and snorkelling. Spend a full afternoon here. Eat at one of the fish restaurants by the old harbour, then watch the sunset from the Torre del Tostón, the 18th-century defence tower.

### Faro del Tostón

The lighthouse north of El Cotillo marks the island's northwest tip, with black lava, white sand coves and Lanzarote on the horizon. It is free and an hour is enough, longer if you bring a picnic. The small fishing museum inside the lighthouse building is worth a quick look; check the hours as they change.

### Betancuria

The island's first capital, founded in 1404 in a sheltered valley, is the prettiest village on Fuerteventura: whitewashed houses, palm trees and the Iglesia de Santa María. It is tiny, so an hour or two does it. Entry to the church is a couple of euros. Go first thing, because by late morning the coach tours arrive and the village is overwhelmed. Drive in via the Mirador Morro Velosa, which has the giant bronze statues of the Majorero kings Guise and Ayose and huge views.

### Ajuy caves

Ajuy is a black-sand fishing hamlet on the west coast with a cliff path to large sea caves carved into some of the oldest rock in the Canaries. Around 90 minutes total, and it is free. Wear decent shoes, stay off the rocks in big swell, and have grilled fish in the village afterwards. Combine it with Betancuria, as they are 20 minutes apart.

### Calderón Hondo

An easy walk up an extinct volcano near Lajares, ending at a crater rim where you look straight down into the cone. Count on about 1.5-2 hours return from the car park at Lajares, and it is free. The friendly ground squirrels will beg for food; please do not feed them, they are an invasive species. Morning is best before the heat and wind pick up.

### Montaña de Tindaya

A sacred mountain for the island's original Majorero people, with ancient foot-shaped carvings near the summit. Climbing it requires a permit from the Cabildo, so for most visitors it is a view rather than a hike. Drive past at late afternoon when the rock glows red, and stop for photos from the FV-10 road.

### Barranco de los Enamorados and Las Peñitas

Near Vega de Río Palmas, a short hike leads through a ravine to the dam of Las Peñitas and the small Ermita de la Peña, tucked into the rock. It is free and takes about two hours. Go after rain (rare) and you might even see water. Pair it with Betancuria.

### Salinas del Carmen

Traditional salt pans on the east coast near Caleta de Fuste, with a small museum explaining how salt was harvested and a whale skeleton on display. Entry is about €5 and an hour is enough. A decent stop if the wind makes the beach unbearable.

## Things to do in Fuerteventura

The island is built for outdoor activity. Pick two or three of these rather than trying to do everything.

- **Take a surf lesson.** Corralejo, Lajares and El Cotillo are full of surf schools. A group lesson costs about €40-60 including board and wetsuit. Beginners go to the gentler spots; the north shore reefs are for experienced surfers only.

- **Try kitesurfing or windsurfing.** Sotavento and Flag Beach in Corralejo are the main centres. Courses are not cheap (about €80-120 for a first session) but the conditions are world-class.

- **Snorkel Lobos or El Cotillo's lagoons.** Calm water, rays and lots of small fish. Bring your own mask to save on rental.

- **Visit a goat cheese farm.** Queso Majorero has its own protected designation. Several farms around Antigua and Tefía offer tastings.

- **Go to the Ecomuseo de La Alcogida in Tefía.** A restored rural village showing how islanders lived before tourism. Entry is about €5.

- **Spot dolphins and whales.** Boat trips run from Morro Jable and Corralejo. Choose operators that follow responsible watching guidelines.

- **Stargaze.** Large parts of the island are a Starlight Reserve. Drive away from the resorts on a moonless night and the sky is spectacular.

- **Visit Oasis Park in La Lajita.** A large zoo and botanical garden. It is pricey (around €35-40) and divides opinion, but it works for families on a windy day.

## Fuerteventura for free (or almost)

The best things on the island cost nothing but fuel. All beaches are free and public, including the dunes, Sotavento, Cofete and El Cotillo's lagoons. Hiking Calderón Hondo, the Ajuy caves path and the Barranco de los Enamorados is free. The miradores (Morro Velosa, Sicasumbre, Cofete) are free and arguably the best views on the island.

Betancuria's streets cost nothing to wander, only the church and museums charge. The Lobos permit is free; you only pay for the boat. Corralejo's Friday and Monday markets are free to browse, though the stalls are mostly souvenirs. For a cheap meal, buy Majorero cheese, bread and tomatoes at a supermarket and picnic on the dunes.

## What is overrated (and what to do instead)

- **Caleta de Fuste as a base.** Convenient for the airport, but the beach is artificial and the town is generic resort. Stay in Corralejo or El Cotillo instead.

- **Puerto del Rosario.** The capital is a working town with some street art but little reason to spend more than an hour. Pass through on the way to Betancuria.

- **Corralejo's main strip at night.** The bars along the main tourist drag are expensive and formulaic. Head to the backstreets near the harbour or eat in Lajares.

- **Jeep "safari" tours.** They charge €70-100 for what you can do yourself with a rental car and a map. Book the 4x4 bus to Cofete for a few euros instead.

## When to visit Fuerteventura

Fuerteventura is warm all year, with daytime highs of about 20-22°C in winter and 26-30°C in summer, and rain on only a handful of days. The wind is the real variable: July and August are the windiest months, which is great for kitesurfers and miserable for sunbathers.

The best balance is **April-June and September-October**: warm sea, calmer wind, fewer crowds and better prices. Winter (November-March) is high season for northern Europeans escaping the cold, so hotels cost more, especially around Christmas. The sea is coolest in February and March (about 18-19°C). The cheapest flights tend to be in May, early June and November.

## Where to stay in Fuerteventura: best areas

- **Corralejo:** the best all-round base, with restaurants, bars, the dunes on the doorstep and ferries to Lobos and Lanzarote. Expect €70-140 per night for a mid-range apartment or hotel.

- **El Cotillo:** quieter, more local, great for surfers and couples. Apartments from about €60-120 per night.

- **Lajares:** an inland village popular with surfers and digital nomads, with good cafés. Small apartments from about €55-100.

- **Costa Calma:** a quieter southern resort near Sotavento, suited to families and kitesurfers. Around €70-150.

- **Morro Jable:** the southern tip, with a long beach, a real town centre and the best base for Cofete. Around €65-140 per night.

What we would do: split the stay, three nights in the north and three in the south. It saves hours of driving.

## What to eat in Fuerteventura

The local food is simple, goaty and fishy. Do not leave without trying these:

- **Queso Majorero:** the island's protected goat cheese, often rubbed with paprika or gofio. Order a mixed plate of fresh, semi-cured and cured.

- **Papas arrugadas con mojo:** wrinkly salted potatoes with red mojo picón and green mojo verde.

- **Cabrito or baifo:** slow-stewed goat or kid, the island's classic meat dish.

- **Pescado del día:** grilled vieja (parrotfish), cherne or sama, best eaten in Ajuy, El Cotillo or Morro Jable.

- **Gofio:** toasted grain flour, mixed into stews or desserts like gofio mousse.

- **Sancocho canario:** salted fish with potatoes, sweet potato and mojo.

A menu del día in a local restaurant costs about €12-16. A dinner with fish, wine and dessert runs about €25-40 per person. Avoid restaurants with photo menus and touts on the main promenades; they are pricier and the fish is often frozen. Coffee is cheap: try a barraquito, a layered coffee with condensed milk, liqueur and cinnamon.

## Airport to city centre and getting around

Fuerteventura Airport (FUE) sits about 5 km south of Puerto del Rosario. The Tiadhe bus line 3 runs to Puerto del Rosario in about 15 minutes, and line 10 goes south to Morro Jable via Caleta de Fuste and Costa Calma in about 1.5-2 hours. To Corralejo you usually change in Puerto del Rosario to line 6, around one hour total. Single fares are cheap, from roughly €1.50 to €10 depending on distance. A taxi from the airport costs about €15-20 to Caleta de Fuste, €40-50 to Corralejo and €100+ to Morro Jable.

Honestly, a **rental car** is the way to see the island. Buses connect the main towns but not Ajuy, the miradores or the best beaches. Rental costs about €25-45 per day outside peak season. Book in advance, check fuel and insurance policies, and note that most contracts do not cover gravel tracks like the road to Cofete. Tiadhe sells a rechargeable card (bono) that discounts fares if you rely on buses.

**Rough daily budget**

| Level | Per person per day | What it covers |

| Shoestring | €60-90 per day | Hostel or shared apartment, supermarket picnics, buses, free beaches and hikes. |
| Mid-range | €130-200 per day | Private apartment, a rental car, one restaurant meal a day and an activity such as a surf lesson or the Lobos trip. |

Fuel and car hire are the hidden costs. Groceries are cheaper than in mainland Spain thanks to lower taxes, so self-catering saves a lot.

## Day trips from Fuerteventura

- **Lanzarote:** ferries from Corralejo to Playa Blanca take about 25-35 minutes (Fred. Olsen and Naviera Armas), roughly €25-35 each way. Rent a car there to see Timanfaya National Park and the César Manrique sites.

- **Isla de Lobos:** 15-20 minutes by boat from Corralejo, a half day.

- **Gran Canaria:** ferries from Morro Jable to Las Palmas take about 2-3 hours. A long day, better as an overnight.

- **Jandía peninsula:** technically on the island, but from the north it feels like a separate trip, with Cofete and Punta de Jandía lighthouse taking a full day.

## Common mistakes and scams

- **Driving to Cofete in a standard rental.** If you damage the car or a tyre, insurance often will not pay. Take the 4x4 bus.

- **Swimming at the west coast beaches.** Cofete, parts of El Cotillo and Playa de Garcey have strong currents. Respect the flags.

- **Underestimating the sun and wind.** The wind feels cool while you are getting badly burned. Use high SPF and bring a hat.

- **Forgetting the Lobos permit.** No permit means no landing, even with a ferry ticket.

- **Timeshare touts.** In Corralejo and Caleta de Fuste, people with scratchcards and "free prizes" are selling timeshare. Walk on.

- **Car rental add-ons.** Watch for full-to-empty fuel policies and pushy insurance upsells at the counter.

- **Leaving valuables in the car.** Break-ins happen at remote beach car parks. Keep the car empty.

## Fuerteventura FAQ

### How many days do you need in Fuerteventura?

Six days is ideal to cover the north, centre and south without rushing. With three or four days, focus on either Corralejo and the north or the Jandía peninsula.

### Is Fuerteventura worth visiting?

Yes, if you love beaches, water sports and wide open landscapes. If you want green hiking or lively cities, Tenerife or Gran Canaria will suit you better.

### Do I need a car in Fuerteventura?

Not strictly, but it makes a huge difference. Buses link the main towns, while the best viewpoints, beaches and villages are much easier to reach by car.

### Which is the best area to stay in Fuerteventura?

Corralejo is the best all-round base. El Cotillo suits those wanting quiet, and Morro Jable or Costa Calma are best for the southern beaches.

### Is Fuerteventura very windy?

Yes, especially in summer. Its name is often linked to strong winds, and the east coast beaches are generally more sheltered than the west.

### Can you swim at Cofete beach?

It is not recommended. Strong currents and no lifeguards make it dangerous, so enjoy it on foot.

### Do you need a permit to visit Isla de Lobos?

Yes, a free online permit with a daily visitor limit. Book it before buying your ferry ticket.

### Is Fuerteventura expensive?

It is mid-priced for Europe. Self-catering and buses keep costs low, while car hire and peak-season hotels push them up.

### What to see in Fuerteventura in one day?

If you only have one day, follow day 1 of this itinerary: Corralejo and the dunes. Morning: settle into Corralejo and walk the seafront along Avenida Marítima.

### What to see in Fuerteventura in two days?

With two days, follow days 1 and 2 of this itinerary. Day 1: Corralejo and the dunes. Morning: settle into Corralejo and walk the seafront along Avenida Marítima. Day 2: Isla de Lobos. Morning: early ferry to Lobos, walk to the lighthouse and climb the caldera.
