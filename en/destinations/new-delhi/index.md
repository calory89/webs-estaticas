# Things to Do in New Delhi: A 4-Day Guide Without the Scams

> New Delhi travel guide by Happy Low Cost, 4-day itinerary. Original page: https://www.happylowcost.com/en/destinations/new-delhi/
> Updated: 2026-11-21

New Delhi is loud, polluted, chaotic and occasionally exhausting, and it is also one of the best cities in Asia for history and food. Within a single metro ride you go from the medieval lanes of Old Delhi to the wide avenues the British laid out, then to 13th-century ruins sitting in the middle of South Delhi parks. The city rewards people who plan their days around the traffic and the heat, and it punishes people who try to see everything at once.

It suits travellers who like history, street food and a bit of friction. If you want calm, come for the gardens and tombs, not the bazaars. You need **4 days**: three to cover Old Delhi, New Delhi and the south properly, and one either for slower neighbourhoods or a day trip to Agra. Prices below use roughly €1 = ₹95.

## What to see in New Delhi: the places worth your time

### Humayun's Tomb

If you only see one monument in Delhi, make it this one. The 16th-century Mughal tomb is the architectural ancestor of the Taj Mahal, set in symmetrical gardens and usually far less crowded than the Red Fort. Give it 1.5 to 2 hours. Foreigners pay about ₹600 (€6). Our tip: go in the late afternoon when the red sandstone glows, then walk next door to Sunder Nursery.

### Sunder Nursery

A restored Mughal-era garden with lotus ponds, smaller tombs and very few touts. It is where Delhi families come for picnics and where you come to recover from Chandni Chowk. Entry is about ₹100-150 for foreigners (around €1-1.50). Allow an hour, more if you bring a book.

### Red Fort (Lal Qila)

The Mughal emperors' main palace, with huge sandstone walls and a UNESCO listing. Honestly, the inside is a bit underwhelming compared with the forts in Agra and Rajasthan because the British stripped and rebuilt much of it. Foreigners pay about ₹600 (€6), it is closed on Mondays, and 1.5 hours is enough. Buy tickets online to skip the cash queue, and pair it with Chandni Chowk, which starts right outside.

### Jama Masjid

India's largest mosque, built by Shah Jahan, with a courtyard that holds tens of thousands. Entry is free, but expect to pay a camera fee of around ₹300 (€3) and a small fee to climb the southern minaret, which gives the best view over Old Delhi's rooftops. Tourists cannot enter during prayer times, so check before you go. Dress modestly; robes are lent at the gate if needed.

### Chandni Chowk and Old Delhi

The old Mughal market street and the tangle of lanes around it: wedding saris, silver, paper, spices, all sold in alleys barely wider than a cycle rickshaw. It is crowded, dirty and fascinating. Spend at least half a day. Our tip: walk to **Khari Baoli**, Asia's biggest spice market, and look for the stairs up to the rooftop of the Gadodia Market building for a view over the spice sacks (locals may ask a small tip).

### Gurudwara Bangla Sahib

Delhi's most important Sikh temple, a short walk from Connaught Place. It is free, open to everyone, and genuinely moving: a golden dome, a sacred pool and a community kitchen (langar) that feeds thousands daily. Cover your head (scarves are provided), leave shoes at the counter, and stay for a simple meal or volunteer to chop vegetables. One hour is fine.

### Qutub Minar and Mehrauli

The 73-metre minaret dates from the late 12th and early 13th centuries and is surrounded by the ruins of India's first mosque, with an iron pillar that hasn't rusted in 1,600 years. Foreigners pay about ₹600 (€6). Allow 1.5 hours. Afterwards, walk into the free **Mehrauli Archaeological Park** next door, which has around 100 tombs, step wells and mosques and almost nobody in it.

### India Gate and Kartavya Path

The war memorial arch at the end of the grand ceremonial avenue (formerly Rajpath) that runs to Rashtrapati Bhavan, the presidential palace. Free. Go at dusk when families come out, ice cream vendors set up and the arch is lit. The **National War Memorial** just behind it is also free and worth 30 minutes.

### Lodhi Garden

A large park in central Delhi with 15th-century tombs of the Sayyid and Lodi dynasties scattered among lawns and joggers. Free, open early, and at its best first thing in the morning. Combine it with the street murals of **Lodhi Colony** a few minutes' walk away.

### Hauz Khas Village and Deer Park

Medieval madrasa ruins overlooking a lake, with a strip of cafés, bars and boutiques squeezed in beside them. The ruins and Deer Park are free. Come late afternoon to see the lake at sunset, then stay for dinner. The bars are fine but pricey by Delhi standards.

### Akshardham

An enormous modern Hindu temple complex in hand-carved pink stone, opened in 2005. Entry is free, but phones, cameras and bags must be left in the cloakroom, and security is airport-level. Closed on Mondays. Allow 2-3 hours. The evening water show costs extra (about ₹100-200). Love it or find it kitsch, it is impressive in scale.

### Nizamuddin Dargah

The shrine of the Sufi saint Nizamuddin Auliya, reached through narrow lanes in Nizamuddin Basti. On Thursday evenings, and often other evenings too, qawwali singers perform in the courtyard. Free, though you will be asked for donations (give modestly, directly at the shrine). Cover your head and expect crowds. It is one of the most atmospheric experiences in the city.

### Agrasen ki Baoli

A 14th-century step well with 100-odd steps descending between stone walls, hidden behind office blocks near Connaught Place. Free, quick (30 minutes) and very photogenic in the morning.

### Lotus Temple

The Bahá'í House of Worship, shaped like a white lotus. Free and closed on Mondays. Inside is a silent prayer hall, and the queue can be long on weekends. It looks best from the outside, so do not bend your day around it.

## Things to do in New Delhi

The monuments are only half the reason to come. These are the experiences we would prioritise:

- **Take an Old Delhi food walk.** A guided walk (around €15-30) gets you to the right stalls for parathas, jalebi and kebabs, and helps you eat safely.

- **Eat at the langar in Bangla Sahib.** Free, filling and a lesson in how a kitchen feeds thousands.

- **Hear qawwali at Nizamuddin.** Thursday after sunset is the classic time.

- **Shop at Dilli Haat.** An open-air crafts market (small entry fee) with stalls from every Indian state and regional food counters. Prices are fixed-ish and the quality is decent.

- **Browse Khan Market.** Bookshops, cafés and upscale shops; where Delhi's diplomats and expats shop.

- **Visit Rashtrapati Bhavan.** The presidential palace offers guided visits on certain days, booked online in advance. The Mughal Gardens (Amrit Udyan) open seasonally, usually in early spring.

- **Catch a cricket match or Bollywood film.** A cinema in Saket or Connaught Place costs €3-6 and the audience is part of the show.

- **Ride the metro at rush hour once.** Only once. Use the women-only front carriage if you can.

## New Delhi for free (or almost)

A surprising amount of Delhi costs nothing. Our free list:

- Jama Masjid (camera fee aside), Gurudwara Bangla Sahib and its langar, Nizamuddin Dargah

- Lodhi Garden and the Lodhi Colony murals

- Mehrauli Archaeological Park

- India Gate, Kartavya Path and the National War Memorial

- Hauz Khas lake and ruins, Deer Park

- Akshardham and Lotus Temple

- Agrasen ki Baoli

- Wandering Chandni Chowk and Khari Baoli

Street food meals cost €1-3, and a metro ride rarely goes above €0.70, so a cheap day is genuinely cheap.

## What is overrated (and what to do instead)

- **The Red Fort interior.** Big walls, sparse rooms. Agra Fort is better; in Delhi, give Humayun's Tomb more time instead.

- **Paharganj's Main Bazaar as an experience.** It is a backpacker strip of touts and cheap souvenirs. Fine for a bed, not for a day out. Go to Chandni Chowk for the real market.

- **Connaught Place shopping.** The colonnaded circles are handsome but most shops are chain stores. Have a coffee at Indian Coffee House on Janpath, then move on to Khan Market or Dilli Haat.

- **Hauz Khas nightlife hype.** Bars have become expensive and samey. Go for sunset at the ruins and dinner, then call it a night or head to a bar in Gurgaon or Aerocity if you really want a big night.

## When to visit New Delhi

The best time is **October to March**, with February and March our favourite: mild days (20-28°C), clear-ish skies and gardens in flower. December and January are pleasant by day but cold at night, and fog can delay flights and trains badly.

November is the catch. After Diwali and with crop burning in neighbouring states, Delhi's air quality is often among the worst in the world. If you have breathing problems, avoid it and check the AQI before booking. April to June is brutal (40-45°C), and July to September brings monsoon rain and flooded underpasses. Summer and monsoon are the cheapest and quietest months for hotels; peak prices come in December and around the winter holidays.

## Where to stay in New Delhi: best areas

- **Connaught Place and around:** central, on several metro lines, good for first-timers. Mid-range hotels €50-120 a night (₹4,700-11,400).

- **Paharganj:** next to New Delhi railway station and the cheapest base, with hostels from €8-15 a dorm bed and basic rooms €20-40. Noisy and touty, but practical for budget travellers.

- **South Delhi (Hauz Khas, Defence Colony, Greater Kailash):** leafy, safer-feeling, with better restaurants and guesthouses. €40-100 a night. Further from Old Delhi.

- **Karol Bagh:** busy Indian shopping district on the Blue Line, popular with domestic travellers. Good value at €25-60.

- **Aerocity:** international chain hotels by the airport, €80-200. Only for early flights or a layover; it is a bubble.

## What to eat in New Delhi

Delhi is a North Indian food city first: rich, smoky, heavy on wheat and dairy. What to order:

- **Butter chicken and dal makhani:** both linked to Moti Mahal in Daryaganj, which claims to have invented them.

- **Nihari and mutton korma:** slow-cooked stews at Karim's near Jama Masjid, best with khamiri roti.

- **Stuffed parathas:** fried in ghee at Paranthe Wali Gali off Chandni Chowk.

- **Chole bhature:** chickpea curry with puffed fried bread; Sita Ram Diwan Chand in Paharganj is the classic.

- **Chaat:** papdi chaat, aloo tikki and golgappe. Bengali Market is a safer bet than random carts.

- **Jalebi:** hot and syrupy at Old Famous Jalebi Wala in Chandni Chowk.

- **Momos:** Tibetan dumplings, Delhi's favourite snack; try them at Majnu ka Tilla, the Tibetan quarter.

A street-food lunch costs €2-4 (₹200-400). A sit-down dinner at a good mid-range restaurant runs €10-20 per person (₹1,000-2,000) with drinks; alcohol pushes it up fast. Where not to eat: the generic "multi-cuisine" rooftops in Paharganj serving pancakes, pizza and lukewarm curry.

## Airport to city centre and getting around

The fastest route from Indira Gandhi International Airport is the **Airport Express metro (Orange Line)** from Terminal 3 (and Terminal 1 via Aerocity) to New Delhi station in about 20 minutes, for roughly ₹60-75 (under €1). From there, change to the Yellow Line or take a short auto ride.

- **Prepaid taxi or Uber/Ola:** about ₹500-900 (€5-10) to Connaught Place, 40-75 minutes depending on traffic. Use the official prepaid booth inside the terminal, not drivers who approach you.

- **Delhi Metro:** clean, cheap and air-conditioned, the best way to beat traffic. Single trips ₹10-60. A **tourist card** costs about ₹200 for one day or ₹500 for three days of unlimited travel (plus a refundable deposit), or just tap a contactless bank card or use a mobile QR ticket.

- **Auto-rickshaws:** fine for short hops. Book through Uber or Ola to avoid haggling; on the street, agree the fare before you get in.

- **Cycle rickshaws:** the only sensible transport inside Old Delhi lanes, about ₹50-150 per ride.

## Day trips from New Delhi

- **Agra (Taj Mahal):** the obvious one. The Gatimaan Express or Vande Bharat from Hazrat Nizamuddin or New Delhi takes about 1h40-2h. Go on the earliest train for the Taj at sunrise-ish light. Closed on Fridays.

- **Jaipur:** about 4-5 hours by train or road. Possible as a long day, but much better as an overnight trip.

- **Mathura and Vrindavan:** Krishna's birthplace and temple town, about 2-3 hours by train or road. Spectacular during Holi, intense at any time.

- **Sultanpur National Park:** a bird sanctuary in Haryana, about 1.5 hours by car, worth it in winter when migratory birds arrive.

## Common mistakes and scams

Delhi's scams are old and predictable, which makes them easy to dodge once you know them.

- **The fake tourist office at New Delhi station.** Men will tell you the foreigners' booking office has moved, your ticket is invalid or the platform is closed, then steer you to a "government" travel agency. The real International Tourist Bureau is upstairs inside the station. Ignore everyone in between.

- **"Your hotel is closed / full / burned down."** A driver line to take you to a hotel paying commission. Call your hotel.

- **Shopping detours.** Cheap auto tours that stop at "emporiums." Say no to any rickshaw offering a tour for ₹50.

- **Overpacking your days.** Traffic turns a 10 km ride into an hour. Group sights by area, as in the itinerary above.

- **Drinking tap water or ice from carts.** Stick to sealed bottles and busy stalls where food is cooked hot in front of you.

- **Ignoring Monday closures.** Red Fort, Akshardham and Lotus Temple shut on Mondays; plan Monday for tombs and gardens.

- **Dress.** Cover shoulders and knees at religious sites, and women will get less hassle in loose, covered clothing in Old Delhi.

## New Delhi FAQ

### How many days do you need in New Delhi?

Four days is right: three for Old Delhi, central New Delhi and South Delhi, plus one for a day trip to Agra or a slower day. With only two days, focus on Old Delhi and Humayun's Tomb.

### Is New Delhi safe for tourists?

Generally yes in central and South Delhi, with petty scams the main nuisance. Women travelling alone should use app-based taxis at night, dress conservatively and expect staring.

### What is the difference between Delhi and New Delhi?

New Delhi is the district designed by the British in the early 20th century, home to the government. Delhi is the wider city, including Old Delhi and the sprawling suburbs.

### Do I need a visa for India?

Most nationalities need one, and the e-Visa is the easiest route. Apply only on the official Indian government site, not lookalike agencies charging extra.

### Can you visit the Taj Mahal as a day trip from Delhi?

Yes, comfortably by fast train in under two hours each way. Avoid Fridays, when the Taj is closed.

### Is the Delhi Metro easy for tourists?

Very. Signs are in English, stations have security checks, and contactless or QR tickets work. Avoid 8-10am and 5-8pm if you can.

### What should I wear in New Delhi?

Light, loose clothing covering shoulders and knees, plus a scarf for temples and mosques. In December and January, bring a warm layer for evenings.

### How bad is the pollution in New Delhi?

From late October to January it can be severe, especially in November. Check the AQI daily, carry an N95 mask and plan indoor time on bad days.

### Is New Delhi worth visiting?

For the traveller who wrote this guide, yes: New Delhi is loud, polluted, chaotic and occasionally exhausting, and it is also one of the best cities in Asia for history and food. 4 days are enough for the essentials. Budget: Hostel dorm €8-15, street food €5, metro €2, one or two paid monuments €6-12.

### Is New Delhi expensive?

It depends on how you travel. This guide puts the "shoestring" level at about €25-35 per person per day and the "mid-range" level at about €70-120. Flights are extra: this page shows real fares checked by Happy Low Cost.
