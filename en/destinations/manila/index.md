# Things to Do in Manila: An Honest 3-Day City Guide

> Manila travel guide by Happy Low Cost, 3-day itinerary. Original page: https://www.happylowcost.com/en/destinations/manila/
> Updated: 2026-11-21

Manila is not a pretty city at first sight. It is hot, loud and jammed with traffic, and the airport drop-off will not change your mind. Give it a couple of days, though, and the layers show up: Spanish walls in Intramuros, the world's oldest Chinatown in Binondo, free national museums that would charge €20 in Europe, and a food scene that runs from €1 street sisig to some of the best restaurants in Southeast Asia.

It suits travellers who like cities with rough edges, history buffs and anyone who eats their way through a place. It is less fun if you want beaches (those are a flight away) or easy walkability. Our advice: **3 days is right**. Two for old Manila (Intramuros, Binondo, the museums) and one for Makati and BGC, then fly on to Palawan, Siargao or Bohol.

## What to see in Manila: the places worth your time

### Intramuros

Intramuros is the walled Spanish city and the reason most people come to Manila. Heavily bombed in 1945 and partly rebuilt, it is a grid of cobbled streets, churches and old stone gates. Walk the walls themselves for free and give it at least half a day. Our tip: go first thing in the morning, because by 11am the heat on the stone is brutal.

### Fort Santiago

Fort Santiago is the old citadel at the northern tip of Intramuros, where national hero José Rizal was imprisoned before his execution in 1896. Entry is about PHP 75 (€1.20), and you need around 90 minutes. The Rizal Shrine inside is worth the time; the brass footsteps tracing his last walk are oddly moving.

### San Agustin Church and Museum

San Agustin is the oldest stone church in the Philippines and a UNESCO site, the only major building in Intramuros that survived the war. The trompe-l'oeil ceiling is the highlight. The church is free outside of services, while the museum costs around PHP 200 (€3) and takes an hour. Enter through the museum: the cloisters and the choir loft view down into the nave are the best part.

### Casa Manila

Casa Manila is a reconstructed 19th-century colonial mansion across from San Agustin, furnished the way a wealthy family would have lived. Count on 45 minutes and about PHP 75 (€1.20). It is a replica, so do not expect authenticity, but it is the clearest picture you will get of the bahay na bato style.

### Manila Cathedral

The cathedral on Plaza Roma has been destroyed and rebuilt several times; the current version dates from the 1950s. It is free and takes 20 minutes. Look up at the dome and the stained glass, then move on.

### National Museum of the Philippines

Three museums sit next to Rizal Park: Fine Arts, Anthropology and Natural History, and all of them are **free**. In the Fine Arts building, Juan Luna's huge "Spoliarium" is the star. Anthropology has the Manunggul Jar and finds from the San Diego galleon wreck. Natural History has the best building, with a central "tree of life" structure. Allow a full afternoon for two of them, and check whether you need to book an online slot, as the system has changed over the years.

### Rizal Park (Luneta)

Rizal Park is the big green space between Intramuros and the bay, home to the Rizal Monument. It is free and takes 30 minutes to cross. Honestly, it is more a connector between sights than a destination. The changing of the guard at the monument is the one moment worth timing.

### Binondo

Binondo, founded in 1594, is generally called the oldest Chinatown in the world, and it is where you should eat in Manila. Start at Binondo Church, then work your way along Ongpin Street and Carvajal Street, a narrow food alley. Plan 3 hours and €10-15 to eat yourself silly. Go hungry around 10am and skip breakfast.

### Quiapo Church and the Quiapo market streets

Quiapo Church houses the Black Nazarene, the focus of the giant January procession. The streets around it sell everything from herbal remedies to amulets and pirated DVDs. It is free, and an hour is plenty. Keep your phone in a front pocket: this is pickpocket territory, especially on Fridays when it gets packed.

### Escolta Street

Escolta was Manila's grand commercial street before the war, and a handful of art deco and beaux-arts buildings remain. The First United Building hosts a small Saturday market for local makers on some weekends. It is free, short and pairs well with Binondo next door.

### Manila Bay sunset

The sunsets over Manila Bay are famous, and they are earned: the pollution makes the colours ridiculous. The Baywalk along Roxas Boulevard is free, as is the seaside area behind SM Mall of Asia. Our pick is the Mall of Asia end, which feels safer and has places to sit down for a drink afterwards.

### Ayala Museum

The Ayala Museum in Makati tells Philippine history through 60 dioramas and has a striking gold gallery with pre-colonial jewellery. Entry is about PHP 650 (€10), and you need 2 hours. If you only see one paid museum in Manila, make it this one.

### Manila American Cemetery

The cemetery in Taguig, next to BGC, is the largest American military cemetery from World War II, with more than 17,000 graves and huge mosaic maps of the Pacific campaigns. It is free, and you need an hour. Bring ID and go in the morning. It is quiet, beautifully kept and a strange contrast with the city around it.

### Manila Chinese Cemetery

In Santa Cruz, the Chinese Cemetery has mausoleums with kitchens, bathrooms and air conditioning. It is free to enter, but going with a local guide makes it much more interesting. Plan for 90 minutes and do not wander the back sections alone.

## Things to do in Manila

Sightseeing only gets you so far here. These are the things that make Manila make sense.

- **Do a Binondo food walk.** A guided food tour runs about €25-40 and takes you to places you would never pick yourself. Doing it alone is fine too: start at Binondo Church and follow the queues.

- **Cycle Intramuros on a bamboo bike.** Bambike runs tours on bikes made of bamboo, around €25-35. It is the easiest way to cover the walled city in the heat.

- **Ride a jeepney once.** Fares start at around PHP 13-15 (€0.20). Pass your coins forward to the driver and say "bayad po". Use Grab for anything complicated.

- **Hit the Salcedo or Legazpi weekend markets.** The Salcedo Saturday Market and Legazpi Sunday Market in Makati are the best place to try regional food in one go. Go before 10am.

- **Spend an evening in Poblacion.** This Makati neighbourhood is Manila's best bar district: rooftops, speakeasies, cheap beer and Filipino-Asian kitchens.

- **Walk the BGC street art.** Bonifacio Global City has dozens of large murals around Bonifacio High Street. It is the most walkable part of Manila.

- **Sing karaoke.** Videoke is a national sport. A private room in Makati costs around €15-30 per hour, split between a group.

- **Watch a basketball game.** A PBA game at the Smart Araneta Coliseum or the Mall of Asia Arena is loud and cheap, with tickets from €3-10.

## Manila for free (or almost)

Manila is cheap to see, and the best museums cost nothing.

- The three National Museum buildings are free.

- The Intramuros walls, Manila Cathedral and the San Agustin church are free.

- Rizal Park, the Baywalk and the Mall of Asia seafront are free, and sunset costs nothing.

- Binondo Church and Quiapo Church are free, and a walk through Binondo can cost under €5 if you snack instead of eating sit-down meals.

- The Manila American Cemetery is free.

- The BGC street art is free and the walk through it takes about an hour.

- The LRT-1 from Makati-side stations to Central Terminal for Intramuros costs under €1.

## What is overrated (and what to do instead)

**The kalesa (horse carriage) rides in Intramuros.** Drivers quote one price and charge another, and the horses look tired. Rent a Bambike or simply walk.

**The malls as attractions.** SM Mall of Asia is huge, and that is about it. Use malls for air conditioning and food courts, not as a sight. Go to the weekend markets for local flavour instead.

**Manila Ocean Park.** It is pricey for what it is, and the animal welfare is questionable. Spend the money on the Ayala Museum.

**Rizal Park as a half-day trip.** It is a nice walk between the museums and Intramuros, not more. Pass through on the way and keep moving.

## When to visit Manila

The best time to visit Manila is **December to February**, when it is dry and relatively cool (around 25-30°C). March to May is still dry, but heat often goes above 34°C and walking becomes a chore. June to October is the rainy season, with typhoons most likely from July to September: streets flood and flights get cancelled.

The cheapest months are the rainy months, when hotel prices drop by 20-30%. Christmas and New Year are crowded and expensive, and the city empties out during Holy Week as locals leave, which makes the traffic lighter but closes some businesses. Mid-January is very busy around Quiapo for the Black Nazarene procession.

## Where to stay in Manila: best areas

- **Makati (Poblacion and Legazpi Village):** the best base for most visitors. Safe, walkable by Manila standards, full of restaurants and bars. €35-60 for mid-range, €100+ for upscale, hostels from €12.

- **BGC (Bonifacio Global City):** clean, modern and the most walkable area, but far from old Manila and a little bland. €60-120.

- **Ermita and Malate:** close to Intramuros and the museums, and cheap. Rougher around the edges, especially at night. €20-45.

- **Intramuros:** atmospheric, with a small number of hotels, and quiet after dark. Good if history is your priority. €50-90.

- **Pasay and Entertainment City:** near the airport and Mall of Asia, with large casino-resorts. Useful for a short stopover. €45-150.

## What to eat in Manila

Filipino food is salty, sour and sweet, often all at once, and it is badly underrated abroad. These are the dishes to order.

- **Adobo:** chicken or pork braised in vinegar, soy and garlic. The national dish.

- **Sinigang:** a sour tamarind soup with pork or shrimp. It is comforting even in the heat.

- **Sisig:** chopped pork face and ears on a sizzling plate with egg and calamansi. It is perfect with beer.

- **Lechon:** spit-roasted pig. Ask for the crackling skin.

- **Kare-kare:** oxtail in peanut sauce, eaten with bagoong (shrimp paste).

- **Halo-halo:** shaved ice, ube, beans, jelly and leche flan. The best dessert for the heat.

- **In Binondo:** dumplings, fried siopao, kiamoy and hopia from Eng Bee Tin.

- **Jollibee:** try the Chickenjoy and the sweet spaghetti once. It is a cultural experience more than a meal.

A carinderia (canteen) lunch of rice plus two dishes costs PHP 100-180 (€1.50-3). A mid-range Filipino dinner with a drink is around PHP 600-1,200 (€9-18). A good restaurant in Makati or BGC runs €25-45 a person. A San Miguel beer costs around PHP 60-150 (€1-2.50).

## Airport to city centre and getting around

Ninoy Aquino International Airport (NAIA) has several terminals, so check which one your flight uses. The best option to reach the city is **Grab**, the local Uber: about PHP 250-600 (€4-9) to Makati, taking 30 minutes at night and 90 minutes or more at rush hour. The yellow airport taxis cost more but are metered. Coupon taxis are fixed-price and expensive. There are also airport bus services to Makati and other areas for roughly €1-5; check the current routes on arrival.

Getting around, Grab is the default. Three rail lines help: **LRT-1** runs north-south along the old city (Central Terminal for Intramuros, Carriedo for Quiapo), **LRT-2** runs east-west, and **MRT-3** runs along EDSA through Makati. Fares are €0.25-0.50. A Beep card works on the trains. Avoid the MRT at rush hour (7-9am and 5-8pm): it is crushing. Never plan more than two areas in one day, because traffic eats everything.

## Day trips from Manila

**Tagaytay and Taal Volcano:** about 2 hours south by car or bus from Pasay. Views over Taal Lake from the ridge, cooler air, and bulalo (beef marrow soup). Volcano access depends on activity alerts, so check before going.

**Corregidor Island:** a ferry ride of about an hour from Manila Bay takes you to the island fortress, with WWII ruins and the Malinta Tunnel. Packaged tours with the ferry, lunch and a guide cost around €45-60. Book ahead on weekends.

**Las Casas Filipinas de Acuzar:** in Bagac, Bataan, about 3-4 hours away. Restored colonial houses moved here from around the country. It is contested among heritage people, but beautiful. Better as an overnight trip.

**Pinto Art Museum, Antipolo:** about 1-2 hours east by Grab, depending on traffic. Contemporary Filipino art in white Mediterranean-style buildings and gardens. Entry is around €5-7.

## Common mistakes and scams

- **Taxis without the meter.** Drivers quote a "fixed price". Use Grab, or insist on the meter and get out if refused.

- **The friendly stranger.** Someone says their sister is a nurse moving to your country and invites you home. It ends with a rigged card game. Decline politely.

- **Kalesa price switching.** The quoted price turns out to be "per person" or "per 15 minutes". Agree the total in writing.

- **Short-changing at money changers.** Count the money in front of the counter. ATMs charge a fee of €3-5, so withdraw larger amounts.

- **Overplanning.** Trying to cover Intramuros, BGC and Tagaytay in one day. Traffic will win.

- **Phones in back pockets.** Quiapo, Divisoria and crowded trains are pickpocket zones.

- **Skipping Grab Food.** Using it on a rainy night is not lazy, it is local.

## Manila FAQ

### How many days do you need in Manila?

Three days is enough for Intramuros, Binondo, the museums and Makati or BGC. Add a fourth day for a trip to Tagaytay or Corregidor.

### Is Manila safe for tourists?

Mostly yes, if you use the same sense you would in any big city. Makati and BGC are very safe, while Quiapo and parts of Ermita call for more care at night. Petty theft and scams are the main risks.

### Is Manila worth visiting or should I skip it?

It is worth 2-3 days if you like history and food. If you only have a week in the Philippines and want beaches, a single night here is fine.

### What is the best area to stay in Manila for first-timers?

Makati, specifically Poblacion or Legazpi Village. It is central, safe and full of food, with Grab rides to old Manila taking 30-60 minutes.

### Do I need cash in Manila?

Yes, for jeepneys, carinderias, markets and Binondo stalls. Hotels, malls and restaurants in Makati and BGC take cards, and GCash is widely used locally.

### How do you get from Manila airport to Makati?

Take a Grab car for about €4-9, which takes 30-90 minutes depending on traffic. Metered yellow taxis are the backup.

### What is the best free thing to do in Manila?

The National Museum of the Philippines, which is three free museums next to Rizal Park. Combine it with a sunset on Manila Bay.

### What to see in Manila in one day?

If you only have one day, follow day 1 of this itinerary: Intramuros and the museums. Morning: Be at Fort Santiago when it opens, then walk south through Intramuros to the Manila Cathedral, San Agustin and Casa Manila.
