# Vancouver in 4 Days: Mountains, Seawall and Real Food

> Vancouver travel guide by Happy Low Cost, 4-day itinerary. Original page: https://www.happylowcost.com/en/destinations/vancouver/
> Updated: 2026-11-21

Vancouver is a city that loses to its own scenery. The architecture is mostly glass towers, the nightlife goes to bed early and it is one of the most expensive places in North America. Then you walk out on the seawall at sunset, with snowy peaks behind the skyline and seaplanes landing in the harbour, and you forgive it almost everything. The best things to do in Vancouver happen outdoors: cycling, hiking, kayaking, eating very good Asian food, and drinking craft beer in jeans because nobody dresses up here.

It suits people who like walking, nature and food more than museums and late nights. Our tip is **4 days**: two for the city itself, one for the North Shore mountains and one for a day trip (Squamish, Victoria or Whistler). Three days works if you skip the day trip. Bring a rain jacket in any season except high summer.

## What to see in Vancouver: the places worth your time

### Stanley Park and the Seawall

This is the one place you cannot skip: a 400-hectare forest park on the edge of downtown, wrapped by a 9 km waterfront path. Walking the loop takes 2-3 hours; cycling it takes about an hour with stops. Entry is free, and bike rental on Denman Street costs about CAD 15-20 an hour (€10-13). Ride counter-clockwise (it is one-way for bikes) and stop at Prospect Point and Third Beach. Go in the late afternoon so you finish at English Bay for sunset.

### Granville Island

A former industrial island under the Granville Street Bridge, now a public market, artists' studios, a brewery and a theatre. The Public Market is the highlight: fresh fish, smoked salmon, fruit stands and lunch counters. Allow 2-3 hours. Entry is free but you will spend. Get there by the little Aquabus or False Creek Ferries boats from the downtown side (about CAD 4-6, €3-4), which is half the fun. Go before 11am at weekends or it becomes a slow-moving queue.

### Gastown

The oldest part of the city, with cobblestones, brick warehouses and the famous steam clock on Water Street. It is the most photogenic downtown neighbourhood and the best area for cocktail bars and restaurants. Give it an hour by day and come back in the evening to eat. The steam clock whistles every 15 minutes; see it once and move on.

### Chinatown and Dr. Sun Yat-Sen Classical Chinese Garden

One of the largest Chinatowns in North America, just south-east of Gastown. The garden is a Ming-style scholar's garden with a guided tour included, about CAD 20 (€13), and worth it for the explanation. Next door, the public park part is free. Allow 1-2 hours. Be aware the area borders the Downtown Eastside, where the city's poverty and drug crisis are very visible. It is rarely dangerous for visitors, but it is confronting.

### Museum of Anthropology at UBC

The best museum in Vancouver, and one of the best in Canada: huge Indigenous carvings, totem poles and Bill Reid's sculpture "The Raven and the First Men" in a building designed by Arthur Erickson. Tickets are about CAD 30 (€20). Allow 2-3 hours. It is on the university campus, about 40 minutes by bus from downtown, so combine it with Wreck Beach or the Pacific Spirit forest trails.

### Kitsilano and Kits Beach

Kits is the laid-back beach neighbourhood across False Creek: yoga studios, cafés on West 4th Avenue and a beach with mountain views. In summer there is a long saltwater outdoor pool on the beach (about CAD 7, €5). Allow half a day if the weather is good. Walk there from Granville Island along the waterfront through Vanier Park.

### English Bay and the West End

English Bay is the city's sunset spot, with the giant laughing statues (A-maze-ing Laughter) and the Inukshuk on the sand. The West End behind it is the most walkable residential area in the city, with Davie and Denman streets full of cheap places to eat. Free. Come for the last hour of daylight.

### Grouse Mountain

The ski hill directly above North Vancouver, reached by gondola, with views over the whole city on a clear day. The gondola costs around CAD 75-80 (€50-55), which is steep. Fit people hike the Grouse Grind instead (about 1.5-2 hours straight up, nearly 3,000 steps) and pay only the one-way ride down, about CAD 20 (€13). Check the webcam before you go: in fog you pay full price to see a grey wall.

### Lynn Canyon Park

A free suspension bridge over a canyon in a rainforest, with pools and short trails. It is our pick over the famous paid bridge (more on that below). Allow 2 hours. Take the SeaBus to Lonsdale Quay and a bus from there, about 45-60 minutes total from downtown.

### Queen Elizabeth Park and Bloedel Conservatory

The highest point in the city, with landscaped quarry gardens and a classic view north to the skyline and mountains. The park is free; the domed Bloedel Conservatory with tropical birds is about CAD 10 (€7). Allow 1-2 hours. Take the Canada Line to King Edward station and walk up.

### Canada Place and the Waterfront

The building with white sail-shaped roofs is the cruise terminal, and its promenade gives you close views of seaplanes taking off and the North Shore. Free and quick, 30 minutes. Walk west from here along the Coal Harbour seawall towards Stanley Park.

### Main Street and Commercial Drive

Two neighbourhoods that show local life away from the tower blocks. Main Street (around 20th to 30th Avenue) has vintage shops, independent cafés and breweries. Commercial Drive, "The Drive", is the old Italian strip, with cheap espresso, pubs and a slightly scruffy feel. Free, half a day for both. Buses 3 and 20 run along them.

## Things to do in Vancouver

Vancouver is a city for doing, not looking. These are the activities worth planning around:

- **Kayak or paddleboard in False Creek or English Bay.** Rentals at Granville Island, Kits and English Bay run about CAD 40-60 (€27-40) for two hours. Calm water, skyline views.

- **Ride the SeaBus.** A 12-minute ferry across the harbour on a normal transit fare (about CAD 4, €3). Then eat and wander at Lonsdale Quay and the Shipyards.

- **Hike Quarry Rock in Deep Cove.** About 1-1.5 hours return to a granite viewpoint over the inlet. Reward yourself with a doughnut from Honey's afterwards.

- **Brewery hop in "Yeast Vancouver".** The streets east of Main around Brewery Creek and Strathcona have a dense cluster of taprooms. Tasting flights are about CAD 12-15 (€8-10).

- **Watch a Canucks game.** Ice hockey at Rogers Arena from October to April. Tickets are expensive (often CAD 100+), so a sports bar is the budget version.

- **Richmond Night Market** (summer weekends). Dozens of Asian street-food stalls near the airport. Small entry fee, then CAD 8-15 per dish.

- **Whale watching** from Granville Island or Steveston, roughly April to October, around CAD 170-200 (€115-135). Pricey, but orca and humpback sightings are common.

## Vancouver for free (or almost)

The best of the city costs nothing, which helps balance the restaurant bills. The seawall is the obvious one: you can walk from Canada Place to Kitsilano along the water, around 20 km in all, without paying a cent.

- Stanley Park, English Bay and Kits Beach.

- Lynn Canyon suspension bridge instead of Capilano.

- Pacific Spirit Regional Park trails near UBC, and Wreck Beach (clothing optional, 400+ steps down).

- Queen Elizabeth Park viewpoint.

- Vancouver Art Gallery runs a by-donation evening on Tuesdays; check the current schedule.

- Granville Island and Lonsdale Quay markets for browsing.

- The SeaBus ride, which is the cheapest harbour cruise in town.

## Vancouver itinerary: 4 days

### Day 1: Downtown, Stanley Park and English Bay

**Morning:** start at Canada Place, walk through Gastown and see the steam clock, then Chinatown and the Sun Yat-Sen garden. **Afternoon:** walk the Coal Harbour seawall to Denman Street, rent a bike and loop Stanley Park. **Evening:** sunset at English Bay, then cheap dinner on Denman or Davie Street.

### Day 2: Granville Island, Kits and UBC

**Morning:** Aquabus to Granville Island, early market breakfast. **Afternoon:** walk to Kits Beach, then bus out to the Museum of Anthropology and, if you have energy, down to Wreck Beach. **Evening:** back to Kitsilano for dinner on West 4th, or to Main Street for breweries.

### Day 3: North Shore

**Morning:** SeaBus to Lonsdale Quay, then bus to Lynn Canyon. **Afternoon:** Grouse Mountain if the sky is clear (or Deep Cove and Quarry Rock if it is cloudy). **Evening:** back via the Shipyards for food and a beer with the skyline view, then the SeaBus at dusk.

### Day 4: Day trip

Drive or take a bus up the Sea-to-Sky Highway to Squamish for the Sea to Sky Gondola, or go to Victoria by ferry. Back by evening for dinner in Gastown or a final round of sushi.

## What is overrated (and what to do instead)

- **Capilano Suspension Bridge.** It is well built and the treetop walkways are fun, but entry is around CAD 70-75 (€47-50) and it is packed. Lynn Canyon gives you a similar bridge and a wilder canyon for free.

- **Vancouver Lookout.** Paying around CAD 20 to go up the Harbour Centre tower for a view through glass makes little sense when Queen Elizabeth Park, Cypress Mountain or the SeaBus give better views for less.

- **The Gastown steam clock.** A five-minute stop that tour buses treat as a highlight. Fine in passing, nothing to plan around.

- **Robson Street shopping.** The same global chains you have at home, at Canadian prices plus tax. Main Street is far more interesting.

## When to visit Vancouver

The best time to visit Vancouver is **July to September**: dry, sunny days around 20-25°C and long evenings. It is also the most expensive period, with hotel prices often 30-50% higher. June can be grey ("June gloom") but is cheaper and greener.

From November to March it rains a lot, roughly half the days, with temperatures of 3-8°C and rarely snow in the city. That is when hotels are cheapest and the local mountains (Grouse, Cypress, Seymour) are open for skiing, which is a genuinely unusual city-break combination. April-May and October are the sweet spot for value: mixed weather, fewer crowds, and spot prawn season in May.

## Where to stay in Vancouver: best areas

Hotels in Vancouver are expensive, and BC's strict short-term rental rules have cut the supply of holiday apartments. Book early.

- **Downtown / Coal Harbour:** best for a first visit, walkable to almost everything and close to the Canada Line. About CAD 280-450 (€190-300) a night in summer for a mid-range hotel.

- **West End:** quieter, residential, next to Stanley Park and English Bay, with cheaper food nearby. CAD 220-350 (€150-235).

- **Yaletown:** converted warehouses, restaurants and a Canada Line station. Good for couples. CAD 300-450 (€200-300).

- **North Vancouver (Lonsdale):** cheaper hotels, mountain access, 12 minutes to downtown by SeaBus. CAD 200-300 (€135-200).

- **Budget:** hostel dorms downtown run about CAD 60-90 (€40-60). Avoid cheap hotels on East Hastings, which are cheap for a reason.

## What to eat in Vancouver

Asian food is the reason to come hungry. Vancouver has some of the best sushi, dim sum and Chinese food outside Asia, and it is often better value than Western restaurants. What to order:

- **Sushi:** mid-range sushi here beats expensive sushi in most cities. Try a BC roll (with barbecued salmon skin) and local sockeye. Combos from about CAD 20 (€13).

- **Dim sum:** the best is in Richmond, 20 minutes south on the Canada Line. Go for weekend brunch and order har gow, siu mai and egg tarts.

- **Pacific salmon:** smoked, candied or grilled. Granville Island sells vacuum-packed smoked salmon that travels well.

- **Spot prawns:** sweet local prawns in season around May-June.

- **Japadog:** Japanese-style hot dogs from street carts, a local institution, about CAD 10 (€7).

- **Nanaimo bar:** a no-bake chocolate, custard and coconut slice from BC.

- **Poutine:** it is from Quebec, but you will find decent versions everywhere.

A casual lunch (ramen, pho, sandwich) costs about CAD 20-30 (€13-20). A mid-range dinner with a drink runs CAD 60-90 (€40-60) per person after tax and tip. Menu prices exclude 5% GST, 7% PST on most things and 10% on alcohol, and you are expected to tip 18-20%, so add about a third to what you see.

## Airport to city centre and getting around

The **Canada Line** SkyTrain runs from YVR airport to Waterfront station downtown in about 25 minutes. A one-way fare including the airport surcharge is around CAD 10-11 (€7). Going to the airport there is no surcharge. A taxi or Uber to downtown costs about CAD 40-50 (€27-35) and takes 25-40 minutes.

In town, TransLink runs SkyTrain, buses and the SeaBus on a single system. Tap a contactless bank card or a Compass card; a DayPass is about CAD 12 (€8) and pays off after three rides. Downtown, Gastown, the West End and Yaletown are all walkable, and Mobi bike-share stations are everywhere. You do not need a car in the city, and parking is expensive. Rent one only for day trips.

## How much a trip to Vancouver costs

- **Shoestring:** CAD 120-160 (€80-110) a day. Hostel dorm, food courts and takeaway sushi, transit DayPass, free sights like the seawall and Lynn Canyon.

- **Mid-range:** CAD 350-500 (€235-335) a day per person sharing a double. Mid-range hotel, sit-down dinners, one paid attraction or activity a day.

- **Extras to budget for:** Grouse gondola CAD 75-80, Capilano CAD 70-75, whale watching CAD 170-200, Whistler bus return CAD 60-100.

## Day trips from Vancouver

### Squamish and the Sea to Sky Gondola

About an hour's drive up one of the most beautiful coastal roads in Canada, past Shannon Falls. The gondola is about CAD 80 (€55) and the suspension bridge at the top has a staggering fjord view. Buses run if you do not drive.

### Whistler

Around 2 hours by car or shuttle bus. Ski resort in winter, mountain biking and the Peak 2 Peak gondola in summer. As a day trip it is a long one; staying a night is better.

### Victoria

The provincial capital on Vancouver Island: the Inner Harbour, the Royal BC Museum and Butchart Gardens. BC Ferries from Tsawwassen to Swartz Bay takes about 1.5 hours, plus transfers at each end, so allow a full day. Seaplanes from the downtown harbour are fast but expensive.

### Deep Cove and Bowen Island

Two easy half-days. Deep Cove is about 45 minutes by bus for Quarry Rock and kayaking. Bowen Island is a 20-minute ferry from Horseshoe Bay, with a village, a lagoon and short hikes.

## Common mistakes and scams

- **Budgeting with menu prices.** Tax plus tip adds 25-35% to a restaurant bill. Factor it in from day one.

- **Booking Grouse or the gondolas on a cloudy day.** Check webcams in the morning and switch days if needed.

- **Leaving things in a parked car.** Break-ins at trailheads and downtown car parks are common. Leave nothing visible.

- **Underestimating the North Shore trails.** People get lost on these mountains every year. Start early, carry water and a light, and do not hike in trainers after rain.

- **Wandering East Hastings at night by accident.** Not usually dangerous for tourists, but unpleasant. Stick to Water Street and Pender in Gastown and Chinatown.

- **Illegal holiday rentals.** Listings without a provincial registration number may be cancelled. Check before paying.

- **Overpacking the schedule.** Distances to the North Shore and UBC look short on the map but take 45 minutes or more each way.

## Vancouver FAQ

### How many days do you need in Vancouver?

Four days is ideal: two for the city, one for the North Shore and one for a day trip. Three days covers the city if you skip Squamish or Victoria.

### Is Vancouver expensive for tourists?

Yes, it is one of the priciest cities in North America, mainly for hotels and restaurants. The good news is that the best sights, like the seawall and parks, are free.

### Is Vancouver safe?

It is generally safe for visitors. The main issue is the Downtown Eastside around East Hastings, which is distressing to walk through, plus car break-ins.

### What is the best free thing to do in Vancouver?

Walking or cycling the Stanley Park seawall. Lynn Canyon's suspension bridge is a close second.

### Is the Capilano Suspension Bridge worth it?

Only if you specifically want it and do not mind crowds and the CAD 70+ ticket. Lynn Canyon is free and many people prefer it.

### Do I need a car in Vancouver?

No. SkyTrain, buses and the SeaBus cover the city and North Shore. Rent a car only for Whistler, Squamish or further afield.

### What is the rainiest month in Vancouver?

November is usually the wettest, with December and January close behind. July and August are the driest.

### Can you see Vancouver and Victoria in one trip?

Yes, as a long day trip by ferry, but one night in Victoria makes it much more relaxed.
