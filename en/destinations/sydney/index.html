Sydney is a big, expensive, sun-blasted city built around one of the best harbours on earth. The Opera House and the Bridge are the postcard, but the real draw is how much of daily life happens outdoors: ferry commutes, ocean pools, coastal walks and long brunches. It is not a city of grand old monuments or dense museum quarters. If you want Rome-style history, you will be underwhelmed. If you want to swim before lunch and eat very good Thai food for dinner, you will love it.

It suits beach people, walkers, food lovers and anyone happy to spend a day on a ferry. Families do well here too, because almost everything is safe and spread out. Four days is the right amount: two for the harbour and city, one for the beaches, one for the Blue Mountains or a second beach day. Prices are high, so the tips below lean towards getting the most out of it without paying tourist rates for everything.

What to see in Sydney: the places worth your time

Sydney Opera House

The Opera House is worth seeing up close, and ideally from inside. The one-hour guided tour costs about A$45 (€27) and explains how near-impossible the building was to construct. Walking around the outside is free and takes 30 minutes. Our tip: the best value is a cheap ticket to an actual performance, as some concerts and comedy nights start around A$50-70 (€30-42), which beats paying for a tour.

Sydney Harbour Bridge

Walk across it for free using the eastern footpath from The Rocks (access via Cumberland Street stairs) to Milsons Point, about 30-40 minutes. BridgeClimb costs A$300 or more (€180+) and takes three and a half hours. It's great but steep. The Pylon Lookout at the southern end gives you almost the same view for roughly A$30 (€18).

The Rocks

The Rocks is Sydney's oldest neighbourhood: sandstone lanes, old pubs and colonial-era terraces right by Circular Quay. Give it an hour or two. The weekend Rocks Markets are fine for a browse, but the better move is a beer at the Lord Nelson or the Hero of Waterloo, two of the city's oldest pubs.

Royal Botanic Garden and Mrs Macquarie's Chair

Free, and right next to the Opera House. Allow 1-2 hours and walk the harbour path to Mrs Macquarie's Chair, a sandstone ledge with the classic shot of the Opera House and Bridge lined up together. Go at sunrise or early evening for the light and fewer tour groups.

Bondi Beach

Bondi is famous for good reason, although it is busy and more of a scene than a quiet beach. Swim between the red and yellow flags; the rips are serious. Icebergs, the ocean pool at the south end, costs about A$10 (€6) and is worth it for the photo alone. Take bus 333 from the city or the train to Bondi Junction and then a bus.

Bondi to Coogee coastal walk

This is the single best free thing in Sydney. It is about 6 km of cliffs, coves and ocean pools passing Tamarama, Bronte, Clovelly and Waverley Cemetery, and it takes 2-3 hours with stops. Start in Bondi in the morning so the sun is behind you, and bring swimmers for Bronte's ocean pool or Clovelly's calm inlet.

Manly and the Manly ferry

The F1 ferry from Circular Quay takes about 30 minutes and passes the Opera House, which makes it the cheapest harbour cruise going at around A$9 (€5). Manly has a long surf beach and the Corso, a pedestrian strip. Walk 10 minutes to Shelly Beach for calmer snorkelling. Give it half a day.

Art Gallery of New South Wales

Free general entry, split between the original building and the newer Sydney Modern wing opened in 2022. The Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander collection is the highlight. Plan 2 hours, and don't skip the underground Tank gallery, a former naval fuel tank.

Taronga Zoo

Taronga has the best zoo views in the world, looking straight back at the city skyline. Tickets run about A$50-55 (€30-33) online. Take the ferry from Circular Quay, then the Sky Safari cable car up so you can walk downhill through the zoo. You need half a day.

Watsons Bay

A small harbourside village with a short walk to The Gap, a sheer cliff over the Pacific, and on to Hornby Lighthouse. The ferry takes about 20-25 minutes from Circular Quay. Go late afternoon and have a drink at the Watsons Bay Hotel beer garden as the sun drops behind the city.

Australian Museum

Free general entry, on College Street opposite Hyde Park. It is good for natural history, First Nations culture and a rainy-day backup. One to two hours is enough.

Barangaroo Reserve

A newer headland park on the western edge of the CBD, built from sandstone blocks with native plants. It is free and a quiet spot to sit by the water. Combine it with the walk from The Rocks, about 20 minutes.

Things to do in Sydney

  • Swim in an ocean pool. Bronte, Clovelly, Giles Baths in Coogee and Bondi Icebergs are filled by the sea. Most are free, Icebergs aside.
  • Kayak or paddleboard the harbour. Rentals at Rose Bay or Manly run about A$30-40 (€18-24) an hour.
  • Catch a footy or cricket game. The Sydney Cricket Ground and Allianz Stadium in Moore Park host rugby league, AFL and cricket depending on the season. The atmosphere is relaxed and tickets are often A$30-60 (€18-36).
  • Do a proper brunch. Sydney takes it seriously. Surry Hills, Bondi and Newtown are the places to go for it.
  • Explore Newtown. King Street is the city's alternative strip, with bookshops, vintage clothes, street art and a long line of cheap Thai restaurants.
  • Snorkel at Cabbage Tree Bay. This protected marine area between Manly and Shelly Beach is good for wobbegong sharks (harmless) and giant cuttlefish in winter.
  • See whales. Humpbacks pass the coast roughly May to November. You can often spot them from the Bondi to Coogee walk or North Head.

Sydney for free (or almost)

A surprising amount of the best stuff costs nothing, which matters in a city this pricey.

  • The Bondi to Coogee walk and the Spit to Manly walk (about 10 km through bushland).
  • Walking across the Harbour Bridge.
  • The Royal Botanic Garden and Mrs Macquarie's Chair.
  • The Art Gallery of NSW and the Australian Museum (general entry).
  • Most ocean pools and every beach.
  • The Sunday Opal fare cap: unlimited trains, buses, ferries and light rail all day for under A$10 (€6). This makes Sunday your ferry day.
  • Vivid Sydney, the light festival in late May to June, has free outdoor installations around the harbour.

Sydney itinerary: 4 days

4-day itinerary for Sydney
DayPlan
Day 1Harbour and city. Morning: Start at Mrs Macquarie's Chair early, walk through the Botanic Garden to the Opera House and take the tour. Afternoon: Lunch around Circular Quay, then The Rocks and the walk across the Harbour Bridge to Milsons Point. Ferry back from Milsons Point. Evening: Sunset drink at a Rocks pub, then dinner in Chinatown or the Thai strip on Campbell Street in Haymarket.
Day 2Beaches. Morning: Bus to Bondi, a swim and coffee, then the coastal walk to Coogee. Afternoon: Swim at Clovelly or Giles Baths, then a bus back towards the city. Evening: Dinner and drinks in Surry Hills, where Crown Street has plenty of choice.
Day 3Ferries, Manly and Watsons Bay. Morning: F1 ferry to Manly, walk to Shelly Beach, snorkel or just swim. Afternoon: Back to Circular Quay, then a ferry to Watsons Bay and the walk to The Gap. Swap Watsons Bay for Taronga Zoo if you have kids. Evening: Fish and chips at Watsons Bay, then the ferry back with the city lit up. Do this day on a Sunday if possible to use the fare cap.
Day 4Blue Mountains or Newtown and the gallery. Option A: An early train from Central to Katoomba (about 2 hours), the Three Sisters at Echo Point and a walk along the cliff tops, then the train back for dinner in Newtown. Option B: The Art Gallery of NSW in the morning, Paddington and its terrace streets in the afternoon, and Newtown in the evening for Thai and a bar.

What is overrated (and what to do instead)

  • Darling Harbour. Chain restaurants, inflated prices and attractions (aquarium, wax museum) you can see anywhere. Walk to Barangaroo Reserve instead.
  • Paid harbour cruises. A one-hour cruise for A$40+ shows you less than the Manly ferry for a quarter of the price.
  • Sydney Tower Eye. Fine views, but over A$30 (€18) to look at rooftops. The Pylon Lookout or a drink at a harbour bar is better.
  • Spending your whole beach day at Bondi. It's crowded and flashy. Bronte and Clovelly are calmer, and Manly has more space.

When to visit Sydney

The best time is spring (September to November) and autumn (March to May): warm enough to swim, from 20 to 26°C, without the midsummer crowds. Summer (December to February) is hot, sometimes over 35°C, and beaches are packed. New Year's Eve and school holidays push hotel prices up sharply.

Winter (June to August) is mild at around 17°C during the day and sunny. It is the cheapest and quietest season, and it brings whale watching and Vivid Sydney. Too cold for most people to swim, but perfectly good for walking. Flights from Europe are often cheapest in May-June and again around late October, outside holiday peaks.

Where to stay in Sydney: best areas

  • Circular Quay and The Rocks: best location for first-timers, with ferries on your doorstep. Expensive, from A$300+ (€180+) a night for a decent hotel.
  • CBD and Haymarket: central, well connected, near Central Station and Chinatown. Mid-range around A$200-280 (€120-170); hostels A$45-70 (€27-42) a bed.
  • Surry Hills: best for food and nightlife, walkable to the CBD. Around A$200-300 (€120-180), with fewer big hotels and more boutique and apartment options.
  • Bondi: for beach people who don't mind a 30-40 minute trip into town. A$200-350 (€120-210) in summer, plus some backpacker options.
  • Manly: relaxed, beachy, with a ferry commute that is a pleasure rather than a chore. A$220-350 (€130-210).

What to eat in Sydney

Sydney's food is excellent and very multicultural; the best value is usually Asian. Order a flat white (about A$5.50, €3.30). Coffee standards are high and chain coffee is rarely worth it. Brunch staples are smashed avocado on sourdough and corn fritters, usually A$20-28 (€12-17).

  • Sydney rock oysters: smaller and creamier than Pacific oysters. Try them at the Sydney Fish Market in Pyrmont.
  • Fish and chips: best eaten on a beach or at Watsons Bay. Ask for barramundi or flathead.
  • Meat pie: a pie with peas and mash from Harry's Café de Wheels in Woolloomooloo is a Sydney institution, about A$10-12 (€6-7).
  • Thai and Vietnamese: Campbell Street in Haymarket for Thai; Cabramatta or Marrickville for pho and banh mi.
  • Sweet stuff: lamingtons, pavlova and a packet of Tim Tams from any supermarket.

A cheap lunch (banh mi, noodles, pie) costs A$12-20 (€7-12). A mid-range dinner with a drink is A$50-80 (€30-48) per person. A beer is A$10-14 (€6-8.50) in a pub. Avoid eating along the Circular Quay and Darling Harbour waterfronts unless you're paying for the view.

Airport to city centre and getting around

The fastest route is the Airport Link train to Central or Circular Quay: 15-20 minutes, about A$20 (€12) because of an airport station access fee. A taxi or Uber costs A$45-70 (€27-42) and takes 20-40 minutes depending on traffic. For groups of three or more, a rideshare can work out similar to the train.

Getting around runs on Opal: tap on and off with a contactless bank card or phone (no need to buy a card) on trains, buses, ferries, light rail and the metro. Fares are capped daily (around A$19, €11.50), weekly, and at under A$10 on Sundays. Off-peak train fares are 30% cheaper. Taking the ferry is the best way to see the city; the CBD itself is walkable.

How much a trip to Sydney costs

  • Shoestring: A$110-150 (€65-90) a day. Hostel dorm, supermarket breakfasts, cheap Asian lunches and dinners, free walks and beaches, Opal transport.
  • Mid-range: A$300-450 (€180-270) a day per person. Double room in a 3-4 star hotel (shared), brunch out, one paid attraction a day, restaurant dinners with drinks.
  • Big extras: BridgeClimb A$300+ (€180+), Opera House performances A$70-200 (€42-120), Blue Mountains tours A$150+ (€90+). Doing the Blue Mountains by train costs a fraction of a tour.

Day trips from Sydney

  • Blue Mountains: train from Central to Katoomba, about 2 hours, on regular Opal fares. See the Three Sisters, Echo Point and walks like the Prince Henry Cliff Walk. Leave early and allow a full day.
  • Royal National Park: train to Cronulla then the ferry to Bundeena, or the train to Otford. It has coastal tracks, Wattamolla beach and the Figure Eight Pools (check tides and safety warnings first). A full day.
  • Palm Beach and the Northern Beaches: about 1.5 hours by bus from the city. Climb to Barrenjoey Lighthouse for the view.
  • Hunter Valley: Australia's oldest wine region, about 2-2.5 hours by car. Best as a tour or with a designated driver, since tastings add up.
  • Kiama: train south along the coast, about 2 hours, for the blowhole and coastal walks.

Common mistakes and scams

Sydney is safe, and outright scams are rare. Most mistakes are about sun, sea and money.

  • Underestimating the sun. The UV is brutal even on cloudy days. Use SPF 50 and a hat, and reapply.
  • Swimming outside the flags. Rip currents drown tourists every year. Swim between the red and yellow flags on patrolled beaches.
  • Forgetting surcharges. Many restaurants add 10% on Sundays and 15% on public holidays, plus a card fee. Check the menu.
  • Buying a paid cruise or hop-on bus when ferries and the Sunday cap do the job for less.
  • Tapping with different cards. Use the same card or phone to tap on and off, or you lose the fare caps.
  • Cheap "Aboriginal art" souvenirs. Many are mass-produced imports. Buy from Aboriginal-owned galleries or check for artist information.

Sydney FAQ

How many days do you need in Sydney?

Four days covers the harbour, the beaches and a day trip to the Blue Mountains. With three days, drop the day trip.

Is Sydney expensive for tourists?

Yes, especially for accommodation and eating out. Free walks, beaches, museums and capped public transport help balance it.

What is the best free thing to do in Sydney?

The Bondi to Coogee coastal walk, followed closely by walking across the Harbour Bridge.

Is the Opera House tour worth it?

It's worth it if you're curious about the architecture. Otherwise, a cheap performance ticket gives you the inside and a show.

Can you swim in Sydney in winter?

The water stays around 18-19°C, so it is cold but swimmable for the brave. Ocean pools are the easiest option.

Is Bondi or Manly better?

Bondi has the famous walk and the scene. Manly has the ferry ride and a more relaxed feel. With limited time, do both.

How do you get from Sydney Airport to the city?

The Airport Link train takes 15-20 minutes to Central for about A$20 (€12). A taxi or Uber costs A$45-70.

What to see in Sydney in one day?

If you only have one day, follow day 1 of this itinerary: Harbour and city. Morning: Start at Mrs Macquarie's Chair early, walk through the Botanic Garden to the Opera House and take the tour.

What to see in Sydney in two days?

With two days, follow days 1 and 2 of this itinerary. Day 1: Harbour and city. Morning: Start at Mrs Macquarie's Chair early, walk through the Botanic Garden to the Opera House and take the tour. Day 2: Beaches. Morning: Bus to Bondi, a swim and coffee, then the coastal walk to Coogee.

What to do in Sydney in 4 days?

With four days in Sydney, this is the guide's route: Day 1, Harbour and city; Day 2, Beaches; Day 3, Ferries, Manly and Watsons Bay; Day 4, Blue Mountains or Newtown and the gallery. The table above has the full plan for each day.
