# Vilnius (Vilna) travel guide: how to spend 3 days in Lithuania

## Why go to Vilnius

Vilnius (Vilna, if you read older books or Spanish and Polish sources) is the Baltic capital people tend to skip, and that is its charm. The Old Town is one of the largest medieval centres still standing in Eastern Europe. Yet it feels lived-in, with students, courtyards, and laundry in a backstreet window. Prices are still kinder than in Western Europe, flights from many cities are cheap, and the city is compact enough that you will rarely need a bus.

It is also a city with layers. Baroque churches, a Jewish past that shaped the city for centuries, Soviet-era scars and a small self-declared republic of artists all sit within a short walk of each other.

## What to see in Vilnius

- **Cathedral Square and Gediminas Hill.** The neoclassical cathedral faces a square with a tile marked "Stebuklas", where locals spin on one heel and make a wish. Climb the hill (or take the short funicular) to the Gediminas Tower for the red-roof view. Go late afternoon, when the light turns warm.

- **Gate of Dawn (Aušros Vartai).** The only surviving city gate has a chapel above it with an icon of the Virgin Mary. Locals pray at the window, and passers-by often cross themselves as they walk under. It is quietly moving even if you are not religious.

- **Vilnius University courtyards.** Founded in 1579, with a cluster of linked courtyards, frescoed arcades and a beautiful library. Wander in, but keep your voice down.

- **St. Anne's and the Bernardine Church.** The late Gothic red-brick facade of St. Anne's is delicate enough that Napoleon supposedly wished he could carry it back to Paris. Behind it, the Bernardine Garden is a peaceful place to sit.

- **Užupis.** This is the "republic" across the Vilnelė river, which declared independence on April 1, 1997. Look for the Constitution displayed on mirrored plaques along Paupio street. One article states that everyone has the right to be idle. The Angel of Užupis stands in the main square.

- **Three Crosses Hill.** Three white concrete crosses on a hill in Kalnų Park, with a very good view of the Old Town. The walk up takes about 15 minutes, and it is a good sunset spot.

- **Museum of Occupations and Freedom Fights.** Housed in the former KGB headquarters on Gedimino prospektas. The basement cells and the execution room are heavy going, but they explain a lot about modern Lithuania.

- **Church of Sts Peter and Paul.** A short ride from the centre in the Antakalnis district. The inside is covered in around two thousand white stucco figures. Nothing else in the city looks like it.

- **Jewish Vilna.** Before the war the city was known as the "Jerusalem of the North". The Vilna Gaon Jewish State Museum and the streets around Žydų and Gaono gatvė trace what was lost.

- **TV Tower.** The observation deck and revolving restaurant are on the outskirts. The tower is also a memorial to the 1991 events, when civilians died defending it.

## Things to do

Start with a free or tip-based walking tour. In a city this layered, a good guide saves you hours of reading plaques. Then wander without a plan along **Pilies** and **Literatų** streets, where small works of art are fixed to a wall. Afterwards try the Soviet-era sculptures on the Green Bridge, a short walk across the Neris.

For a change of scale, a hot air balloon flight over Vilnius or Trakai is a popular splurge, weather permitting. A bike ride or a stroll along the Neris river is free and takes you beyond the tourist core. Evenings suit the courtyard bars of the Old Town, or a craft beer in Užupis.

For a half-day trip, go to **Trakai**, about 28 km west. The red-brick island castle sits on Lake Galvė, and you can rent a kayak or rowing boat in summer. Trains and buses make the trip simple. If you want the darker history, a visit to the memorial at **Paneriai** (Ponary) is sobering and takes about two hours.

## Vilnius itinerary: 3 days, day by day

**Two days** covers the highlights. **Three days** is ideal, and it lets you add Trakai without rushing. Four or more makes sense if you like museums or want to base yourself here for the wider Baltic region.

**3-day itinerary for Vilnius**

| Day | Plan |
|---|---|
| Day 1 | Old Town. Gate of Dawn, St. Anne's, the university, Pilies street, Cathedral Square and Gediminas Hill at sunset. Dinner around Stikliai or Vokiečių. |
| Day 2 | Užupis and the modern side. Užupis in the morning, Three Crosses Hill, then the KGB museum in the afternoon. In the evening, the TV tower or a drink in the New Town. |
| Day 3 | Trakai. Take an early train, visit the castle, eat kibinai by the lake and be back in Vilnius for a late dinner. |

## When to go

Vilnius has a continental climate. **Summer** (June to August) is warm, with highs around 22-24°C, long light evenings and some showers. It is busy but never overwhelming. **Late spring and early autumn** are my favourite windows, with fewer visitors, comfortable walking weather and yellow trees along the river. In early March, the Kaziukas fair fills the Old Town with crafts and food stalls.

**Winter** is properly cold, often below freezing, with snow possible from December to February. It is also atmospheric, with a Christmas market on Cathedral Square and cosy bars. Pack warm layers and decent boots, because the cobbles get slippery.

## Where to stay

- **Old Town (Senamiestis).** Best for a first visit. You walk everywhere, but the cobbled streets are noisy on weekend nights. Expect higher prices.

- **New Town (Naujamiestis).** Around Gedimino prospektas, with more modern hotels and good transport links. It is a calmer option, and still a short walk from the Old Town.

- **Užupis.** Smaller guesthouses and apartments, an arty feel and quiet at night. Good for return visitors.

- **Šnipiškės.** Across the Neris, with modern towers, business hotels and shopping. It is practical, but the atmosphere is less charming.

- **Žvėrynas.** Residential, with leafy streets and old wooden houses, for a slower, local stay.

Budget travellers will find hostel dorms from roughly €15-25 a night. Mid-range hotels usually run €70-110. Good boutique or five-star options start higher.

## What to eat and where

Lithuanian food is hearty, built on potatoes, rye, dairy, pork, beetroot and smoked fish. Start with **cepelinai**, large potato dumplings stuffed with minced meat and served with sour cream and bacon bits. In summer, order **šaltibarščiai**, a shockingly pink cold beetroot soup served with hot boiled potatoes. It is much better than it sounds.

With a beer, try **kepta duona**, fried garlic rye bread with a cheese dip. Look for **kibinai**, pastries filled with meat, which are a Karaite speciality from Trakai but sold in Vilnius too. For something sweet, **šakotis** is a tree-shaped cake, and **midus** is a honey mead. Try **gira**, a fermented rye drink, for something cheaper.

For atmosphere, eat in the Old Town streets around Stikliai, Vokiečių and Pilies, although the Užupis side is often a better deal. The **Halės market** near the Gate of Dawn is great for smoked fish, cheese and a quick lunch. Prices are lower than the central restaurants.

## Getting from the airport and getting around

Vilnius International Airport (VNO) is about 5 km south of the centre. The easiest option is the **train**, a trip of around seven minutes to the main station, which is a ten-minute walk from the Old Town. Buses 1 and 2 also reach the centre in about 20 minutes. A taxi or ride-hailing car (Bolt is widely used) takes 10-15 minutes and costs roughly €8-12.

Once there, the Old Town is for walking. For longer hops, use buses, trolleybuses or ride-hailing. In summer, e-scooters are everywhere. The bus and train stations sit next to each other south of the Old Town, with long-distance buses to Riga, Tallinn and Warsaw.

## Rough daily budget

- **Budget:** around €45-65 for a hostel, market food, walking and a museum ticket.

- **Mid-range:** around €90-140, with a decent hotel, restaurant dinners, a few drinks and transport.

- **Comfortable:** €180 and up, covering a boutique hotel, tasting menus, tours and a day trip by taxi.

A main course often costs €10-18, a beer around €3-5, and a short ride-hailing trip a few euros. Prices rise a little in the Old Town and in summer.

## FAQ

### Is Vilnius safe for tourists?

Yes, it is generally very safe. Normal city precautions apply, such as watching your bag in crowded places and avoiding empty streets late at night.

### Do I need to speak Lithuanian?

No. English is widely spoken, especially by younger people and in tourist areas. A simple "ačiū" (thank you) is always appreciated.

### What currency is used?

Lithuania uses the euro. Cards are accepted almost everywhere, though keep a little cash for markets and small cafés.

### Is Vilnius expensive?

Not compared with Western Europe. It is a bit more expensive than it was a decade ago, but food, drinks and accommodation are still good value.

### Is Trakai worth a day trip?

Yes. It is easy to reach, takes about half a day and adds a different landscape. Go early on a weekday in summer to avoid the crowds.
