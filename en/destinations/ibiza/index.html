Ibiza is two islands sharing one coastline. One is the clubbing machine of Playa d'en Bossa and Sant Antoni, with €15 vodka tonics and sunrise taxis. The other is a slow, pine-covered place of whitewashed churches, farm restaurants, salt flats and coves where the loudest thing is the cicadas. Most visitors only see the first. The best trips mix both.

It suits couples, groups of friends, families who stay in the north or east, and anyone who likes beaches more than museums. You need 5 days: one for Ibiza Town, two or three for beaches and villages, and one for Formentera. With fewer days you will spend most of them in transit between coves. A car makes a huge difference, because the buses are fine between towns but useless for the best beaches.

What to see in Ibiza: the places worth your time

Dalt Vila

The walled old town of Ibiza Town is the single best sight on the island, and it is free. The Renaissance walls are UNESCO-listed, and the walk up through the Portal de ses Taules gate to the cathedral at the top takes about 20 minutes if you keep stopping for views, which you will. Allow 2-3 hours. Our tip: go up in the late afternoon, when the stone turns gold and the cruise crowds have gone, and finish at the Baluard de Sant Pere bastion for the view over the port.

Es Vedrà from Cala d'Hort

Es Vedrà is the huge limestone rock rising out of the sea off the southwest coast, and it is the most photographed thing in Ibiza for good reason. Watch it from the beach at Cala d'Hort or, better, walk up to the Torre des Savinar watchtower (about 30-40 minutes from the car park along a rocky path). Free. Bring water, wear proper shoes and leave before full dark, because the path back is rough.

Ses Salines Natural Park

The salt flats at the southern tip are still worked, and the pink-tinged ponds attract flamingos in some seasons. The park includes two long beaches, Ses Salines and Es Cavallet, with clear water and dunes. Entry is free, parking in summer costs a few euros and fills by late morning. Arrive before 10am or come by bus 11 from Ibiza Town.

Cala Comte

A set of small coves near Sant Antoni with some of the clearest turquoise water in the Balearics and views of the islets offshore. It is gorgeous and packed in July and August. Free, with paid sunbeds. Go for sunset rather than midday: there is a famous bar on the rocks, but you can sit on the cliffs with your own drink and get the same view.

Santa Gertrudis

A small inland village in the middle of the island, with a white church, a square full of cafés and a slightly bohemian, slightly expensive feel. One to two hours is enough. Order the ham and cheese bocadillo at Bar Costa, an old-school bar with hanging hams and art on the walls, and pay about €6-8.

Benirràs

A north-coast cove facing a rock shaped like a praying figure (locals call it the Cap Bernat, or "the finger of God"). On Sunday evenings in summer, drummers gather to play the sun down. It is touristy now, but still fun. Free; parking is limited and sometimes closed in peak season, so check before driving and allow a whole afternoon.

Las Dalias and the hippy markets

Las Dalias in Sant Carles holds its market on Saturdays, and the bigger Punta Arabí market at Es Canar runs on Wednesdays in season. Both are relics of the 1960s hippy wave: jewellery, linen, leather, a lot of mass-produced stuff and a few real artisans. Free to browse. Go early before the coach groups, and haggle politely.

Puig des Molins

Just below Dalt Vila, this is one of the best-preserved Phoenician-Punic necropolises in the Mediterranean, with thousands of tombs cut into the hillside and a small museum. Most visitors walk straight past it. Entry costs a few euros, and an hour is plenty. Check the hours before going, as they vary by season.

Cala d'en Serra

A tiny cove in the far north near Portinatx, reached by a steep, winding road, with a concrete ruin of an abandoned hotel above it and snorkelling-perfect water below. Free. There is one beach bar, so bring your own snacks. Go on a weekday.

Sant Miquel and its church

The fortified church of Sant Miquel de Balansat sits on a hill above the village and is one of the prettiest on the island, with frescoes inside. Half an hour is enough. Combine it with the Cova de Can Marçà, a show cave near Port de Sant Miquel once used by smugglers (about €15, 40-minute tour).

Sant Josep and Sant Agustí

Two of the most photogenic inland villages. Sant Josep has a big white church and good cafés on the main road. Tiny Sant Agustí, perched on a hill, has a defence tower and a couple of restaurants with terraces. Combine them with Cala d'Hort in one afternoon drive.

Cala Salada and Saladeta

Two coves north of Sant Antoni, with pine-covered cliffs and boathouses carved into the rock. Saladeta is reached on foot over rocks from Salada. Free. In summer the access road is restricted and a shuttle bus may run, so park in Sant Antoni if needed.

Things to do in Ibiza

Most of the good stuff happens outdoors and outside Ibiza Town. These are the ideas we would pick.

  • Rent a kayak or SUP at Cala Comte, Cala Bassa or Portinatx and paddle to caves and empty rocky inlets. About €15-25 an hour.
  • Snorkel the posidonia meadows. The seagrass around Ibiza and Formentera is UNESCO-listed and the reason the water is so clear. Bring a mask: any rocky cove works.
  • See one big club night, properly. Pacha in Ibiza Town is the classic, Ushuaïa and Hï in Playa d'en Bossa are the big ones, DC10 is for serious techno. Tickets are €40-100 or more, drinks €15-20. Book online, it is cheaper than the door.
  • Take a boat trip around the coast or to Formentera. A shared trip costs €50-80, a private boat with skipper easily €400+.
  • Hike the coast. The walk from Cala d'Hort to Torre des Savinar, or the cliffs around Punta de sa Galera near Cala Salada, are short and spectacular.
  • Eat a long Sunday lunch at a farmhouse restaurant inland. This is what Ibicencos actually do.
  • Visit the Sunday drums at Benirràs or the sunset strip in Sant Antoni (more below on why you should skip the bars).

Ibiza for free (or almost)

Ibiza is expensive, but the best bits cost nothing. Every beach is public, including the ones lined with €50 sunbeds: lay your towel on the sand nearer the water and nobody can stop you.

  • Walking Dalt Vila and the cathedral area.
  • Sunset at Torre des Savinar facing Es Vedrà.
  • Ses Salines and Es Cavallet beaches (get there by €2-4 bus).
  • The white village churches of Sant Miquel, Sant Josep, Santa Gertrudis and Sant Carles.
  • The Sunday drums at Benirràs.
  • Free club opening and closing parties are rare, but some beach bars play great DJs for the price of a drink.
  • Browsing the hippy markets without buying anything.

What is overrated (and what to do instead)

The Sant Antoni sunset strip. Café del Mar and its neighbours were legendary in the 1990s. Today you pay €15 for a cocktail and stand behind a hundred phones. Walk ten minutes north along the coast to the rocks near Caló des Moro, or go to Torre des Savinar.

Playa d'en Bossa by day. A long beach backed by megahotels and bass. Fine if you are staying there, but not worth a trip. Go to Ses Salines instead.

Sunbed clubs. Paying €50-150 for a lounger and a minimum spend is mostly about being seen. A €10 towel and a cooler bag at Cala Comte gets you the same water.

Atlantis (Sa Pedrera). The old quarry near Cala d'Hort is all over Instagram, but the descent is steep, unmarked and has caused serious injuries. If you are not an experienced hiker, skip it.

When to visit Ibiza

The best months are May, June and September: sea warm enough to swim, clubs open, prices below peak and fewer people. July and August are hot (around 30°C), busy and the most expensive time of year, with hotel rates often doubling.

The club season runs roughly from late April or May (opening parties) to early October (closing parties). From November to March, the island goes quiet: clubs closed, many hotels and restaurants shut, but cheap flights, mild weather around 15°C and the real local Ibiza. Almond blossom season in late January and February is lovely for hiking.

Where to stay in Ibiza: best areas

  • Ibiza Town (Eivissa): best base without a car, close to the airport, Dalt Vila, Pacha, the ferry and buses everywhere. Hostals from €80-120, hotels €150-300 in summer.
  • Playa d'en Bossa: for clubbers, steps from Ushuaïa and Hï. Big hotels €150-350, noisy.
  • Sant Antoni: young crowds, cheaper rooms, good access to the west coast coves. €70-180. The West End streets are rowdy, so stay near the bay or Cala de Bou.
  • Santa Eulària: calmer town on the east coast with a seafront promenade, good for families and couples. €100-220.
  • The north (Sant Joan, Portinatx, Sant Miquel): rural houses and boutique stays, best for quiet, but you need a car. €120-400.

What to eat in Ibiza

Ibicenco cooking is farmhouse and fisherman food, and it is much better than the fusion menus in the clubbing areas suggest. Look for menus with dishes in Catalan.

  • Bullit de peix: fish and potato stew, served first, followed by arròs a banda (rice cooked in the broth). Order it in advance at coastal restaurants, it is a full lunch. About €30-45 per person.
  • Sofrit pagès: a hearty meat stew with lamb, chicken and local sausage. Winter food, but found year-round.
  • Frita de polp: octopus fried with potatoes and peppers.
  • Flaó: a mint-and-cheese tart, the island's signature dessert.
  • Greixonera: a bread pudding made from leftover ensaimadas.
  • Hierbas ibicencas: aniseed herbal liqueur, usually offered after dinner.
  • Sobrasada and local cheese from village bars.

A menú del día (two courses, drink, dessert) inland costs €15-20. A beach chiringuito lunch runs €25-40. Dinner at a mid-range place in Ibiza Town or Santa Eulària is €40-60 a head, and at fashionable beach restaurants €80-150. Ca n'Alfredo on Vara de Rey is a long-running classic for local dishes.

Airport to city centre and getting around

Ibiza airport is about 7 km from Ibiza Town. Bus L10 runs to the port in about 20 minutes for around €4, and there are seasonal direct buses to Sant Antoni and Santa Eulària. A taxi to Ibiza Town costs €20-25; to Sant Antoni €30-40. Taxi queues in August can be long, and there is no Uber in the usual sense, though licensed taxi apps exist.

The island buses link the main towns and some beaches (Ses Salines, Cala Comte, Cala Bassa in season), with tickets around €2-4, and a rechargeable card gives cheaper fares. The Discobus runs between Ibiza Town, Playa d'en Bossa and Sant Antoni at night in summer.

To actually see Ibiza, though, rent a car for at least two or three days: €40-80 a day in summer, much less in shoulder season. Book weeks ahead in July and August. A scooter is cheaper but the inland roads are fast and winding.

How much a trip to Ibiza costs

  • Shoestring: €80-120 a day. Hostal or shared room, buses, menú del día, supermarket picnics on the beach, no clubs.
  • Mid-range: €180-300 a day. Hotel double, car rental shared, beach lunches, one dinner out, a couple of drinks.
  • With clubbing: add €100-200 per night for ticket, drinks and taxis.

Prices in July and August can be 50% higher than in May or October.

Day trips from Ibiza

  • Formentera: ferry from Ibiza port to La Savina, about 30-35 minutes, €50-70 return. Rent an e-bike or scooter and head to Ses Illetes. A full day.
  • Tagomago: private islet off the east coast, best seen by boat trip from Santa Eulària or Cala Sant Vicent. Half a day.
  • Sa Conillera and Cala Bassa: boat trips from Sant Antoni circle the islets off the west coast. Two to four hours.
  • Santa Eulària and the river walk: 20 minutes by car or bus from Ibiza Town, with a pretty fortified church on Puig de Missa and the only river in the Balearics. Half a day.
  • Mallorca: ferries run to Palma in about 2-4 hours depending on the boat. Only worth it if you have extra days.

Common mistakes and scams

  • Buying club tickets from street promoters. Some are fake or overpriced. Buy online from the official site.
  • Not booking a car early. In August, last-minute rentals can be €100+ a day or sold out.
  • Paying for a sunbed without asking the price. Some beach clubs quote €100+ at the end. Always ask first.
  • Leaving valuables in cars at beach car parks. Break-ins happen, especially at Cala Comte and Benirràs.
  • Using unlicensed taxis. Illegal "pirate" taxis operate near clubs. They are uninsured and often overcharge.
  • Trying to see everything from one base. Ibiza is small but slow; group sights by area.
  • Underestimating the sun and dehydration on club nights. Free water is not always given; ask.

Ibiza FAQ

How many days do you need in Ibiza?

Five days is ideal: one for Ibiza Town, three for beaches and villages, and one for Formentera. With three days, skip Formentera and focus on the south and west.

Is Ibiza only for partying?

No. The north and the inland villages are quiet, rural and family-friendly. Many visitors never set foot in a club.

What is the best beach in Ibiza?

For clear water, Cala Comte and Ses Salines. For a quieter cove, Cala d'en Serra or Cala Salada outside peak hours.

Do you need a car in Ibiza?

Not if you stay in Ibiza Town or Sant Antoni and stick to the main beaches. For the north, the villages and sunset at Es Vedrà, a car is almost essential.

Is Ibiza expensive?

Yes, especially in July and August and in club areas. You can keep costs down by eating menús del día inland, using buses and travelling in May, June or September.

When does the club season start in Ibiza?

Opening parties are usually in late April or May, and closing parties in late September or early October. Outside that window, the big clubs are closed.

How do you get from Ibiza to Formentera?

Take the ferry from Ibiza port to La Savina, roughly 30-35 minutes and €50-70 return. Book the first morning boat to get a full day.

Is Ibiza Town worth visiting?

Yes. Dalt Vila is the island's best sight, and the town is the easiest base for buses, ferries and the airport.
