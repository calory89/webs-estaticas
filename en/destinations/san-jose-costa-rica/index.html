Costa Rica is not a cheap tropical escape, so don't arrive expecting Nicaraguan or Guatemalan prices. What you get for the money is real, though: smoking volcanoes, cloud forest dripping with moss, sloths you can actually see from the trail, and two coastlines a few hours apart. San José, the capital, is a transit hub rather than a destination, and most people rightly leave it the morning after landing. The country suits nature lovers, active travellers, families and couples who want wildlife without roughing it. Party-first travellers and city-break people will feel short-changed.

We think 7 days is the right amount for a first trip. A week covers the classic loop of Arenal, Monteverde and Manuel Antonio without spending every day in a car. With 10 days or more you can add the Caribbean coast or the Nicoya Peninsula. One thing you should know upfront: almost everything tourist-facing is priced in US dollars, colones are used for local buses and sodas, and the rate is roughly €1 = ₡570.

What to see in Costa Rica: the places worth your time

Arenal Volcano and La Fortuna

Arenal is the perfect cone volcano you have seen on every postcard, and La Fortuna is the town at its feet that lives off adventure tourism. Plan on 2 days here. Arenal Volcano National Park costs about €15 (₡8,500) and its trails cross old lava flows with clear views of the summit, weather permitting. Our tip: the volcano hides in cloud most afternoons, so go first thing for the clearest view.

La Fortuna Waterfall

This is a 70-metre waterfall dropping into a jungle pool, about 10 minutes' drive from town. Entry is about €17 (₡10,000), and you need 1.5-2 hours including the 500 or so steps down and back up. You can swim, but the plunge pool is rough, so stay in the calmer side pool. Arrive early, because tour buses fill it by late morning.

Arenal Hanging Bridges

Here a 3 km loop of suspension bridges takes you through the forest canopy, and it makes an easy half day from La Fortuna. Tickets run about €25-30 for self-guided entry. We would pay extra for a guide here (about €45-50 total): they carry telescopes and find vipers, toucans and sloths that you would walk straight past. Early morning is when the birds are most active.

Monteverde Cloud Forest Reserve

The famous one is cool, misty, high-altitude forest at around 1,500 metres, home to the resplendent quetzal. Entry is about €28 (₡16,000) and a half day is enough. Daily visitor numbers are capped, so book ahead in high season. Quetzals are most likely seen between February and April, ideally with a guide at opening time. Monteverde is also noticeably colder than the rest of the country, so pack a fleece.

Santa Elena Cloud Forest Reserve and Curi-Cancha

Both are smaller alternatives to the main Monteverde reserve, and they get far fewer visitors. Each costs about €20-25 and takes 2-3 hours. Curi-Cancha is a private reserve near Monteverde with excellent birding and a good chance of quetzals and agoutis. If you only do one, we prefer it to the crowded main reserve in peak months.

Manuel Antonio National Park

This is the small park on the central Pacific where the jungle runs right down to white-sand coves. White-faced monkeys, sloths and iguanas are almost guaranteed. Tickets are about €17 and must be bought online in advance through the SINAC system. The park closes one day a week, so check before you go. Allow 4-5 hours including swimming at Playa Manuel Antonio, and hire a guide at the entrance (about €20-30 per person) for the first hour.

Río Celeste and Tenorio Volcano National Park

The river here runs an impossible milky turquoise because of a mineral reaction where two streams meet. The park is about 1.5 hours from La Fortuna. Entry costs about €11 and the hike takes 3 hours, with a steep stairway down to the waterfall. Swimming is not allowed. After heavy rain the colour turns grey, so ask locally before driving out.

Tortuguero National Park

Tortuguero is a roadless village on the Caribbean coast that you reach by boat through canals full of caimans and herons. The main event is sea turtle nesting, July to October for green turtles, on a guided night tour (about €25-35). You need at least 2 nights. Getting there takes most of a day, so it doesn't fit a tight 7-day trip, but it is excellent on a longer one.

Puerto Viejo and Cahuita

The southern Caribbean feels like another country: Afro-Caribbean culture, reggae, rice and beans cooked in coconut milk, and a slower pace. Cahuita National Park runs on donations at the Cahuita town entrance, and you can walk through it on a coastal trail with sloths overhead. Give the region 2-3 days and rent a bike to reach Playa Cocles and Punta Uva. Leave nothing visible in parked cars or on the beach.

Santa Teresa and the Nicoya Peninsula

This is surf country, with long beaches and some of the best sunsets in the country. Santa Teresa is trendy and pricey, while nearby Montezuma is more laid-back with a walkable waterfall. Expect dusty or muddy roads depending on the season. Give it 3+ days, as it is a long trip from anywhere.

Corcovado National Park

National Geographic called Corcovado "the most biologically intense place on earth", and it may be the wildest place you can visit in Central America, with tapirs, scarlet macaws and all four of the country's monkey species. A guide is mandatory. Day trips from Drake Bay or Puerto Jiménez cost about €90-120. This is a trip for travellers with a week or more and some patience with logistics.

Tárcoles Crocodile Bridge

Dozens of huge American crocodiles lounge on the riverbank below a road bridge on Route 34. Seeing them is free and takes 15 minutes, which makes it an easy stop between San José and the Pacific. Park in the guarded lot and use the walkway, not the road edge.

Things to do in Costa Rica

The best things to do in Costa Rica are mostly outdoors, and much of the budget goes on activities rather than entrance fees.

  • Soak in hot springs around Arenal. Tabacón is the luxurious option (about €80-100 with dinner). Baldi and Paradise Hot Springs are cheaper and less polished.
  • Take a night walk. Guided night tours in Monteverde or La Fortuna (about €25-35) show you tarantulas, frogs and sleeping birds. It is one of the best-value tours in the country.
  • Go ziplining in Monteverde. Selvatura and Sky Adventures run long cable courses (about €50-60). Monteverde's are the best in the country.
  • Raft the Sarapiquí or the Pacuare. The Pacuare, out of Turrialba, is world-class class III-IV and costs about €100-120 with lunch.
  • Learn to surf in Santa Teresa, Nosara or Tamarindo. A two-hour lesson with board costs about €45-60.
  • Do a coffee and chocolate tour. Small farms around Monteverde, such as Don Juan or El Trapiche, charge about €30-40 and include tasting.
  • Watch whales at Uvita and Marino Ballena National Park. Humpbacks pass roughly July-October and December-March, and boat trips cost about €70-90.

Costa Rica for free (or almost)

Free things are thinner on the ground here than in Europe, but they exist.

  • Every beach in Costa Rica is public by law, including those in front of luxury hotels. Playa Espadilla, just outside Manuel Antonio park, is free and has the same water.
  • El Chollín, the free hot river under the bridge near Tabacón in La Fortuna, gives you the same thermal water as the resorts at no cost. Go in the late afternoon and keep valuables on you.
  • You can watch for sloths and toucans on public roads around La Fortuna and Manuel Antonio. Look for other people staring up at trees.
  • The Tárcoles crocodile bridge costs nothing.
  • Cahuita National Park runs on donations from the Cahuita entrance.
  • In San José, Mercado Central and the Avenida Central pedestrian street are free to wander. The Pre-Columbian Gold Museum under Plaza de la Cultura is cheap (about €15) and good.

What is overrated (and what to do instead)

  • Tamarindo. It is overbuilt, pricey and more party strip than Costa Rica. For the same surf with more character, go to Nosara or Santa Teresa.
  • Poás Volcano on a cloudy day. You pay for a timed slot and often stare into white fog. Only go on a clear early morning, otherwise skip it.
  • Spending days in San José. One evening is plenty. Go to Barrio Escalante for food if you must stay.
  • Expensive all-inclusive resorts in Guanacaste. You could be in Mexico. Stay in small lodges where the wildlife comes to you.

When to visit Costa Rica

The dry season, December to April, is the best time to visit the Pacific side and the most expensive. Christmas, New Year and Easter (Semana Santa) are the peak: prices jump and locals fill the beaches.

The green season runs May to November and brings sunny mornings and afternoon downpours. It is cheaper, greener and quieter. July often has a short dry spell locally called the "veranillo". September and October are the wettest months on the Pacific and some lodges close, but that is exactly when the Caribbean coast is at its driest.

Our pick is late November, early December or May for a good balance of weather, prices and crowds.

Where to stay in Costa Rica: best areas

Where to stay in Costa Rica depends on your route. For this loop, these are the bases that work.

  • La Fortuna suits first-timers and families. The town is walkable, while lodges outside town have volcano views. Hostels cost €15-25 and mid-range hotels €70-130.
  • Santa Elena (Monteverde) suits nature lovers on a budget. Stay in town for restaurants and buses. Rooms cost €40-100.
  • Manuel Antonio and Quepos split by budget. The hillside road has ocean-view hotels for €100-250+, while Quepos is cheaper at €40-80 and 15 minutes away by bus.
  • Puerto Viejo suits backpackers and couples who want a slower pace, at €20-100.
  • Alajuela is best for your first or last night near SJO, at €40-80.

What to eat in Costa Rica

Eat at sodas, the small family-run local diners. They are the cheapest and often the tastiest places to eat.

  • Casado: rice, beans, salad, plantain and a protein. This is the national lunch, at about €6-9 (₡3,500-5,000).
  • Gallo pinto: rice and beans fried together, served at breakfast with eggs and natilla (sour cream).
  • Chifrijo: a bar snack of rice, beans, pork crackling and pico de gallo. Have it with an Imperial beer.
  • Olla de carne: a hearty beef and vegetable soup, best on rainy days.
  • Rice and beans with Caribbean chicken: cooked in coconut milk on the Caribbean coast.
  • Ceviche: lime-cured white fish, sold at the coast.
  • Batidos: fruit smoothies made with water or milk. Try cas or guanábana.

Put Salsa Lizano on everything, as locals do. A soda lunch costs €6-9, and dinner in a tourist restaurant €20-35 per person. Menus often exclude the 13% tax and 10% service charge, so check the small print.

Airport to city centre and getting around

Most flights land at Juan Santamaría (SJO) in Alajuela, about 20 km from San José. Liberia (LIR) is closer to the Guanacaste beaches.

  • Bus: buses run from outside SJO to central San José in 40-60 minutes for about €1.20 (₡700).
  • Official orange airport taxi: about €30-35, 30-45 minutes.
  • Uber: works and is usually cheaper, though its legal status is murky. Meet the driver outside arrivals.
  • Car rental: the best option for this itinerary, at about €40-80 a day in reality once mandatory insurance is added. Get a 4x4 if you are going to Monteverde in the rainy season, and use Waze.
  • Shared shuttles (Interbus and others): door to door between tourist towns for €50-65 per leg.
  • Public buses: slow but very cheap. San José to La Fortuna costs about €5 and takes around 4.5 hours.
  • Domestic flights: Sansa flies to Quepos, Drake Bay, Tortuguero and other airstrips for about €80-150 one way.

There is no tourist travel pass. Buy national park tickets online through SINAC.

Day trips from Costa Rica

These all work as day trips from the bases on the 7-day route.

  • Río Celeste from La Fortuna: 1.5 hours by car, or about €60-80 on a tour. A full day.
  • Poás Volcano and La Paz Waterfall Gardens from San José: 1-1.5 hours by car. Go early for a clear crater.
  • Pacuare River rafting from San José: about 2 hours to the put-in with pickup included. A full day.
  • Uvita and Marino Ballena from Manuel Antonio: about 1 hour south by car. Walk the "whale tail" sandbar at low tide.
  • Nicaragua (Granada) from Guanacaste: possible on organised tours, but it is a long day with a border crossing. We would only do it with more time.

Common mistakes and scams

  • Rental car insurance shock. The €5-a-day car you booked online becomes €50 at the counter because liability insurance (SLI) is mandatory. Book with a company that shows all-in prices, such as Adobe or Vamos.
  • Flat tyre scam. Someone slashes your tyre near the airport and "helpers" stop to rob the car. Drive to a petrol station before stopping.
  • Theft from cars and beaches. Never leave anything visible. Use guarded lots, which cost about €2-4.
  • Fake guides and parking touts near Manuel Antonio waving you into private lots. Official guides carry ICT licences, and park tickets are only sold online.
  • Unlicensed taxis. Official taxis are red with a yellow triangle (orange at the airport) and should use the meter, called "la maría".
  • Cramming too much. Drives take longer than Google Maps suggests. Never drive after dark on mountain roads.

Costa Rica FAQ

How many days do you need in Costa Rica?

Seven days is enough for Arenal, Monteverde and Manuel Antonio. With 10-14 days you can add the Caribbean coast, Nicoya or Corcovado.

Is Costa Rica expensive?

Yes, it is the most expensive country in Central America, with tourist prices close to those in southern Europe. Eating at sodas and using buses keeps costs down.

Do I need US dollars in Costa Rica?

Dollars are accepted almost everywhere tourist-facing, and cards work widely. Carry some colones for buses, sodas and small shops.

Is Costa Rica safe for tourists?

It is one of the safer countries in Latin America, but petty theft is common, especially from cars and beaches. Avoid walking around central San José at night.

Do I need a 4x4 in Costa Rica?

Not for the main paved routes in dry season. For Monteverde in the rain, Nicoya or remote beaches, a 4x4 is worth it.

What is the best month to visit Costa Rica?

February to April gives the driest Pacific weather. December and May offer a good balance of weather, crowds and price.

Can you drink tap water in Costa Rica?

In most of the country, including San José, La Fortuna and Monteverde, tap water is safe. In some coastal and rural areas, ask locally or stick to filtered water.

Is Costa Rica worth visiting?

What to see in Costa Rica in one day?

If you only have one day, follow day 1 of this itinerary: Airport to La Fortuna. Morning: pick up your car or shuttle and drive about 3 hours north.

What to see in Costa Rica in two days?

With two days, follow days 1 and 2 of this itinerary. Day 1: Airport to La Fortuna. Morning: pick up your car or shuttle and drive about 3 hours north. Day 2: Arenal. Morning: start at La Fortuna Waterfall at opening time, then walk the lava trails in Arenal Volcano National Park.
