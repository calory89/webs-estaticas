# Santorini travel guide: what to see, eat and where to stay

> Santorini travel guide by Happy Low Cost, 3-day itinerary. Original page: https://www.happylowcost.com/en/destinations/santorini/
> Updated: 2026-10-21

## Why go to Santorini

Santorini is what's left of a volcano that blew itself apart in the Bronze Age. What you see today is a flooded crater rim: white villages perched on cliffs, a black-and-rust caldera below, and a sea so deep it looks like ink. The photos are not exaggerated. The crowds are not exaggerated either.

It's worth the trip anyway, as long as you plan around the busiest hours. Go at the right time of day and the right time of year, and the island is spectacular. Go at 7pm in August expecting solitude and you'll be disappointed.

## What to see in Santorini

### Oia

The famous one. The marble lanes, blue domes and sunset crowds are all real. Come at 8am instead, when the streets are almost empty and the light on the whitewash is soft. For sunset, skip the castle ruins scrum and watch from a spot a little further along the rim.

### Fira

The island's capital is louder, more commercial and more practical than Oia. Walk the caldera edge, visit the Museum of Prehistoric Thera for the Akrotiri finds, and then leave for the quieter villages. It is a good base for buses, not for romance.

### Imerovigli and Skaros Rock

Imerovigli sits at the highest point of the caldera rim and feels calmer than its neighbours. The path out to Skaros Rock, where a medieval fortress once stood, gives you some of the best views on the island with far fewer people.

### Akrotiri archaeological site

A Minoan-era town buried by ash around 1600 BC, with multi-storey buildings still standing under a protective roof. It's shaded, which matters in July. Go early or late in the day, and give it an hour and a half.

### Pyrgos and Megalochori

Pyrgos is a hilltop village with a ruined Venetian kastelli and wide views over the whole island. Megalochori, nearby, has shaded squares and old wine houses. Both are the Santorini people imagine before they see Oia.

### Red Beach and the black sand beaches

Red Beach, near Akrotiri, is dramatic: red cliffs falling straight to dark sand. Rockfall risk means access is often restricted, so check locally. Perissa and Perivolos are long black-sand stretches with sunbeds and tavernas. The sand gets very hot at midday, so bring sandals.

### Ancient Thera

Ruins on a ridge above Kamari, reached by a steep road or a hike. It's quiet and the views over both coasts are big. Bring water and a hat.

## Things to do

- **Walk Fira to Oia.** About 10 km along the caldera rim, roughly three to four hours. Start early. There's almost no shade.

- **Boat trip to the volcano.** Nea Kameni is a lava island you can walk across, and nearby hot springs let you swim in warm, rust-coloured water. Most tours also stop at Thirassia.

- **Wine tasting.** Assyrtiko is the grape to try, grown in low, basket-shaped vines to resist wind. Santo Wines has a terrace with caldera views, and Venetsanos and Domaine Sigalas are also well known.

- **Ammoudi Bay.** Below Oia, down a long stairway, with fish tavernas by the water and a rocky swimming spot.

- **Sunset sailing.** Pricey, but out on the water you avoid the crowds on land.

## When to go

May, June, September and early October are the best months. The sea is swimmable, the days are warm and the crowds, though real, are manageable. July and August are hot (around 30°C), packed and expensive, and the meltemi wind can make boat trips choppy. From November to March it's mild but windy and rainy, and many hotels and restaurants close. April can be a good gamble if you don't need the beach.

## Where to stay

### Caldera villages (higher budget)

Oia, Imerovigli and Firostefani have the cliffside rooms with plunge pools and the prices to match. Imerovigli is the best compromise, with the view and less foot traffic.

### Fira (mid-range, practical)

The best transport links, lots of restaurants and a wide range of hotels. It's noisy at night in places, so check which street your room is on.

### Kamari and Perissa (budget)

Beach towns on the east and southeast coast. Rooms are much cheaper, and you get the sea at your doorstep. You lose the caldera view, but not much else. Both are good for families.

### Pyrgos and Megalochori (quiet and local)

Smaller guesthouses in real villages, with a car or bus needed for everything. Good for travellers who want calm after the day.

## What to eat and where

Order **fava**, the local yellow split-pea purée, and **tomatokeftedes**, crisp tomato fritters made with the island's small, intensely flavoured tomatoes. Try white aubergine, caper leaves, and fresh cheese such as chlorotyri. Seafood is excellent at Ammoudi Bay, though not cheap. Grilled octopus and a glass of Assyrtiko is a fine combination.

Caldera-view restaurants charge for the view. For better value, eat in Fira's back streets, in Pyrgos, or at beachfront tavernas in Perissa and Kamari. A gyros or souvlaki wrap is the cheap, quick option at any hour.

## Getting from the airport and getting around

Santorini airport (JTR) is on the east side, near Kamari. KTEL buses run from the airport to Fira, usually every 30 to 60 minutes in season, and it's about 20 minutes. Taxis are available but pricey, and there are queues in summer. Many hotels offer transfers, which are worth booking in advance.

If you arrive by ferry, you'll land at Athinios port, below the cliffs. Buses meet most boats, and the ride to Fira takes about 20 to 30 minutes. Ferries from Piraeus take roughly five to eight hours depending on the type, and there are links from Naxos, Paros, Mykonos and Crete.

Fira is the bus hub, with routes to Oia, Perissa, Kamari and Akrotiri. Buses are cheap but crowded, and services thin out in the evening. A rental car or quad gives freedom, but parking in Oia and Fira is a headache and the roads are narrow. Taxis are limited, so book return trips in advance for far-flung dinners.

## Rough daily budget

- **Budget:** about €80 to €120 per person, with a simple room in Kamari or Perissa, bus travel and casual meals.

- **Mid-range:** about €150 to €250, with a comfortable hotel in Fira or Pyrgos, sit-down dinners and a boat or wine tour.

- **Higher end:** €300 and up, mostly driven by caldera-view accommodation in Oia or Imerovigli.

Prices spike in July and August and drop noticeably in spring and autumn.

## FAQs

### Is Santorini worth it with the crowds?

Yes, if you go in the shoulder season, get up early and spend time outside Oia and Fira.

### How many days do I need?

Three full days covers the main sights without rushing. Two is possible, four is relaxed.

### Do I need to rent a car?

Not necessarily. Buses connect the main villages and beaches. A car helps if you stay in a smaller village or want to visit wineries.

### Where is the best place to watch the sunset?

Oia is the famous spot but it's very crowded. Imerovigli and Pyrgos are quieter, and a sunset boat trip avoids the crowds altogether.

### Are there good beaches?

They're volcanic, not tropical. Perissa, Perivolos and Kamari are black sand and easy to reach. Red Beach is striking but sometimes closed because of rockfall.
