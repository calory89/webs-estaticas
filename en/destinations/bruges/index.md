# Bruges travel guide: what to see, eat and skip in 2 days

> Bruges travel guide by Happy Low Cost, 2-day itinerary. Original page: https://www.happylowcost.com/en/destinations/bruges/
> Updated: 2026-10-21

## Why go to Bruges

Bruges is small enough to walk end to end in under an hour, and that is the point. The medieval centre survived almost intact, and its canals, brick gables and cobbled squares look like a film set. At 7:30 in the morning, with the swans on the water and nobody on the bridges, it is hard to argue with.

The catch is timing. Between about 11:00 and 17:00 in high season, day-trippers fill the Markt and the streets around it. Go early, stay late, and Bruges feels like a different town.

## What to see in Bruges

- **The Markt and the Belfry (Belfort).** The main square is lined with stepped-gable houses, and the 83-metre belfry towers over it. The climb is 366 narrow steps and the view is worth it. Book a timed slot online in summer.

- **The Burg.** A short walk away, this is the older, more elegant square. The Gothic town hall is here, with a richly decorated hall upstairs. Next to it stands the Basilica of the Holy Blood, a small and atmospheric chapel.

- **Rozenhoedkaai.** This is the quay behind the Burg, where the classic postcard shot of canal, bell tower and gables is taken. Come at dawn if you want it without a crowd.

- **Groeninge Museum.** This is the one museum I would not skip. It holds Flemish Primitives, including Jan van Eyck, and it takes about 90 minutes.

- **Church of Our Lady (Onze-Lieve-Vrouwekerk).** Its brick spire is one of the tallest in the world. Inside is Michelangelo's Madonna and Child, one of his few works to leave Italy in his lifetime.

- **Sint-Janshospitaal.** A medieval hospital with a handful of Memling paintings. It is quieter than the Groeninge and easy to pair with the church across the street.

- **The Begijnhof and Minnewater.** The beguinage is a whitewashed courtyard with a lawn that fills with daffodils in spring. The park and lake next to it, the "Lake of Love", are lovely when the day-trippers have gone.

- **The windmills on the Kruisvest.** Walk the grassy ramparts on the north-east edge of town to find a few surviving windmills. Hardly anyone goes there, and the canal views are excellent.

- **Jan van Eyckplein and the Langerei.** The old trading quarter around the former customs house is quiet, with good canal-side light in the late afternoon.

## Things to do

Take a **canal boat tour**. It is touristy, but it is also the best way to see the backs of the houses and the low bridges, and it lasts about 30 minutes. Expect to pay around 12 to 15 euros.

Visit **De Halve Maan brewery**, the last working brewery inside the old walls. The tour ends with a glass of Brugse Zot or Straffe Hendrik on the rooftop terrace.

Rent a **bike** and ride the canal path out to Damme, a tiny village lined with trees, about 5 km from the centre. It is flat, easy and a good break from the cobbles.

Spend an evening drinking beer. **'t Brugs Beertje** is a classic for connoisseurs, with a long Belgian list and patient staff. **Café Vlissinghe**, dating from 1515, is the place for a slow, candlelit pint. Chocolate shops are everywhere. Look for small independent chocolatiers rather than the glossy ones on the Markt.

## Bruges itinerary: 2 days, day by day

One day is enough for the highlights, but two days is the sweet spot. Staying overnight gets you the town before and after the coaches.

**2-day itinerary for Bruges**

| Day | Plan |
|---|---|
| Day 1 | Start at the Markt before the crowds, then climb the Belfry first thing. Walk to the Burg, see the basilica, then pause at Rozenhoedkaai. After lunch, visit the Groeninge Museum and the Church of Our Lady. Finish with the Begijnhof and Minnewater around sunset, then dinner and a beer. |
| Day 2 | Walk the Kruisvest windmills in the morning, then take the boat tour or visit De Halve Maan. After lunch, bike to Damme or visit Sint-Janshospitaal. Wander the Sint-Anna quarter for the last hour, when the streets are almost empty. |

## When to go

Spring, from April to early June, is the best balance: mild weather, daffodils in the Begijnhof and manageable crowds. Summer brings highs of around 20 to 24°C and the largest crowds. Autumn is calmer and moody, with a mix of sun and showers.

Winter is cold, usually 2 to 6°C, grey and damp, but the town is peaceful and the Christmas market on the Markt adds some warmth. Rain is possible all year, so pack a light waterproof and shoes that cope with cobbles.

## Where to stay

**Sint-Anna** is the quiet, residential neighbourhood to the north-east. It has the best atmosphere for sleeping, with small guesthouses and canal views. **Around the Markt and Burg** is the most convenient but the noisiest and the most expensive. **Near 't Zand and the station** is practical and cheaper, and you are still only 10 to 15 minutes' walk from the centre.

- **Budget:** hostel dorms run from roughly 30 to 45 euros a night. Snuffel Hostel is a well-known option.

- **Mid-range:** guesthouses and boutique hotels cost around 110 to 180 euros for a double, and often include a good breakfast.

- **Treat yourself:** canal-view hotels in historic buildings start around 220 euros and climb quickly.

If prices are steep for your dates, consider staying in Ghent, a 25-minute train away, and visiting Bruges as a day trip.

## What to eat and where

Start with **frites**, served in a paper cone with mayonnaise. A proper frituur beats any restaurant for a quick lunch. Next, try **moules-frites** (mussels and fries) in season, which runs roughly from September to April.

For something heartier, order **waterzooi**, a creamy chicken or fish stew from Ghent, or **stoofvlees**, beef slowly stewed in dark beer and served with fries. Many pubs do both honestly and cheaply.

For sweets, a fresh **Brussels or Liège waffle** is good, but eat it from a small stall and not from the shops on the Markt. Buy **speculoos** biscuits and a small bag of pralines to take home.

As a rule, walk two streets away from the Markt and Burg. Prices drop and quality improves.

## Getting from the airport and getting around

The easiest airport is **Brussels Airport (Zaventem)**. Take the train to Brussels (or one of the connecting trains) and change for Bruges. Allow roughly 1.5 to 2 hours in total.

From **Brussels-Midi**, direct trains to Bruges take about an hour and cost around 15 to 20 euros one way. If you fly into **Charleroi**, take the airport shuttle bus to Brussels-Midi, then the train, which takes roughly 2.5 to 3 hours in total.

From Bruges station, the centre is about a 20-minute walk or a short bus ride. Once inside, everything is walkable, as cars are restricted in the old core. Bikes are the other good option, and taxis are rarely needed.

## Rough daily budget

- **Backpacker:** about 70 to 100 euros, with a dorm bed, frites and supermarket lunches, and one museum.

- **Mid-range:** about 160 to 250 euros, with a guesthouse double split between two, restaurant meals, a boat tour and a couple of entry tickets.

- **Comfortable:** 300 euros and up, with a boutique hotel and dinners with wine.

A combined museum pass can save money if you plan on visiting three or more sights. Beer is cheaper than you might expect, usually 4 to 7 euros a glass.

## FAQs

### Is one day enough in Bruges?

Yes, for the main sights. But the town is at its best early and late, so a night there is better.

### Is Bruges walkable?

Very. The old centre is compact, though cobblestones are hard on the feet and on wheeled luggage.

### Is Bruges expensive?

It is pricier than most Belgian cities, particularly for hotels in the centre. Eating away from the Markt and using the train from Ghent or Brussels helps.

### When are the crowds at their worst?

From late morning to late afternoon, especially at weekends and in July and August. Before 9:00 and after 18:00 the town is much calmer.

### Can I visit Bruges as a day trip from Brussels?

Yes. Trains run frequently and take about an hour. Take an early one and you will have the best part of the day before the tour groups arrive.
