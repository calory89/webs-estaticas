# Things to Do in Malaga: 3 Days of Tapas, Forts and Beach

> Malaga travel guide by Happy Low Cost, 3-day itinerary. Original page: https://www.happylowcost.com/en/destinations/malaga/
> Updated: 2026-11-21

Malaga used to be the airport you landed at before heading somewhere else on the Costa del Sol. That changed about fifteen years ago. The city now has a pedestrian old town of polished marble streets, a Moorish fortress on a hill, more museums than you will have time for, and a working city beach a ten-minute walk from the cathedral. It is still a real Andalusian city, not a resort. People live here, eat late and argue about how to order coffee.

It suits people who want culture and a beach on the same day, food lovers on a budget, and anyone flying in cheaply from northern Europe in winter. **Three days is the right amount**: two for the city itself and one for a day trip to Nerja, Ronda, Granada or the Caminito del Rey. With only a weekend you can still cover the essentials, but you will rush the museums.

## What to see in Malaga: the places worth your time

### Alcazaba

The Alcazaba is the 11th-century Moorish palace-fortress climbing the hill above the old town, and it is the best thing in Malaga. Courtyards, fountains, horseshoe arches and orange trees, with views over the port getting better at every level. Allow 1-1.5 hours. Tickets are about €3.50, or about €5.50 combined with Gibralfaro, and entry is free on Sunday afternoons (expect queues). Our tip: take the lift from Calle Guillén Sotelo behind the town hall if your legs are tired, then walk down.

### Castillo de Gibralfaro

Gibralfaro is the castle at the top of the hill, linked to the Alcazaba by walls, and it is mostly about walking the ramparts for the view. The building itself is fairly empty; the panorama of the bullring, the port and the coast is the reason to go. The walk up from the centre takes 20-25 minutes on a steep, exposed path, so in summer take bus 35 up from Paseo del Parque and walk down. Allow an hour. The free **Mirador de Gibralfaro** just below the entrance gives you most of the view if you skip the ticket.

### Roman Theatre

The Roman Theatre sits right at the foot of the Alcazaba, and you can see almost all of it from Calle Alcazabilla without going in. It was only rediscovered in 1951 under a demolished building. Entry to the small visitor centre and the seating is free. Ten minutes is enough by day; come back at night, when it is lit up and the terraces opposite are full.

### Malaga Cathedral

The cathedral is nicknamed **La Manquita** ("the one-armed lady") because its south tower was never finished; the money allegedly went elsewhere. The interior is a huge Renaissance-Baroque space with carved choir stalls worth a look. Entry is about €10, and the separate rooftop tour costs about €10 too. If you only pay for one, do the rooftop: you walk across the vaults with a view over the whole old town. Book a late-afternoon slot for the best light.

### Picasso Museum

Picasso was born in Malaga, and the Museo Picasso in the Buenavista Palace on Calle San Agustín holds a solid collection spanning his whole career. It is not Barcelona's or Paris's, but the building alone is lovely and there are Phoenician ruins in the basement. Allow 1.5 hours; tickets are about €12-14. Entry is free in the last two hours on Sundays, which means crowds, so book a weekday morning slot online instead.

### Picasso's Birthplace (Casa Natal)

The house where Picasso was born is on Plaza de la Merced, a ten-minute walk from the museum. It is small, with family objects, early sketches and ceramics. About €3-4 and 30-45 minutes. Honestly optional unless you are a fan, but the square outside is a nice place to sit with a coffee.

### Mercado Central de Atarazanas

Atarazanas is the main food market, housed in a 19th-century iron hall behind a surviving Moorish gate, with a large stained-glass window at the back. Come for the fish counters, olives, cured meats and the tiny bars at the side serving fried fish and a beer for a few euros. Go mid-morning on a weekday; it is closed on Sundays and winds down after lunch.

### Calle Larios and the old town

Calle Larios is the marble-paved main shopping street, and the lanes either side of it are where most of Malaga's tapas bars are. The street itself is all chain stores, so treat it as a route rather than a destination. Head into Calle Granada, Calle Beatas, Plaza del Obispo and Plaza de la Constitución instead. At Christmas the light show on Larios is the city's big event, and it gets packed.

### Muelle Uno and the Centre Pompidou

Muelle Uno is the redeveloped port promenade with shops, restaurants and the multicoloured glass cube of the **Centre Pompidou Málaga**. The Pompidou branch is small but good, with modern art rotated from Paris; about €9 and an hour. The restaurants on the quay are overpriced, so walk it, see the museum and eat elsewhere. The lighthouse at the end, La Farola, is a good sunset spot.

### Museo Carmen Thyssen

This is the city's best museum after Picasso, focused on 19th-century Spanish and Andalusian painting: bullfighters, flamenco, landscapes, everyday life. It sounds niche and turns out to be the museum that explains the region best. About €10-12, 1-1.5 hours, on Calle Compañía near Plaza de la Constitución.

### Museo de Málaga (Palacio de la Aduana)

The former customs house next to the Alcazaba now holds the fine art and archaeology museum, and it is free for EU citizens (about €1.50 for others). It is large, well laid out and rarely busy. Give it an hour, especially on a hot afternoon or a rainy day.

### La Malagueta and the city beaches

Malagueta is the city beach a 10-minute walk east of the centre: greyish sand, clean water, and lots of locals. It is fine for a swim, not a postcard beach. The famous **Malaga sign** and the beach bars are here. For a better beach day, keep going east to Pedregalejo and El Palo (bus 3 or 11 from Alameda Principal).

### Soho

Soho is the small district between the Alameda and the river, rebranded around large street-art murals and the free CAC Málaga contemporary art centre. Allow 30-60 minutes to wander. It is not beautiful, but the murals are big and the cafés are cheaper than in the old town.

### Jardín Botánico La Concepción

La Concepción is a 19th-century tropical garden in the hills north of the city, with palms, bamboo, a lake and ferns. About €5-6, 2 hours, and a nice escape in summer heat. Bus 2 gets you close, or take the tourist bus; check the hours, since it closes earlier in winter.

## Things to do in Malaga

Once you have ticked off the monuments, these are the things that make Malaga feel like Malaga rather than a museum circuit.

- **Eat espetos on the beach in Pedregalejo.** Sardines skewered and grilled over an open fire in a boat filled with sand. Order a portion and a cold beer at one of the chiringuitos along the promenade.

- **Drink sweet wine at Antigua Casa de Guardia.** The oldest bodega in town (1840), on Alameda Principal. Wine comes straight from the barrels, and the bartender chalks your tab on the wooden counter.

- **Learn the coffee code at Café Central.** On Plaza de la Constitución there is a tiled chart showing nine ways to order coffee, from "solo" to "nube". Order a "mitad" (half coffee, half milk) and you will sound local.

- **Tapas crawl in the old town.** Start around 8.30pm on Calle Granada and work outwards. Two or three tapas per bar, then move on.

- **Catch sunset from Monte Victoria or La Farola.** Monte Victoria is a free hill above La Victoria neighbourhood with fewer people than Gibralfaro.

- **Watch flamenco.** Small venues around the centre run nightly shows for about €20-30. Pick an intimate tablao over a dinner show.

- **Visit during Semana Santa or the August Feria.** Holy Week processions here are among the biggest in Spain; the Feria is a week-long street party. Both are spectacular and both double hotel prices.

- **Take a boat trip from the port.** One-hour sailing trips go for about €15-25; dolphin sightings happen but are not guaranteed.

## Malaga for free (or almost)

Malaga is one of the cheapest cities in Spain to see well. A lot of the best stuff costs nothing:

- The Roman Theatre, seen from the street or from inside.

- The Alcazaba and Picasso Museum on Sunday afternoons (check which hours apply before you go).

- Museo de Málaga, free for EU citizens.

- CAC Málaga, the contemporary art centre in Soho.

- Mirador de Gibralfaro and Monte Victoria for views.

- Paseo del Parque, a shady palm-lined avenue between the port and the old town.

- The beaches, the murals in Soho and the walk along Muelle Uno to La Farola.

- Free tapa culture is limited here (unlike Granada), but a beer and a tapa still runs about €3-5.

## What is overrated (and what to do instead)

- **Eating on Muelle Uno.** Port views, average food, high prices. Walk ten minutes into the old town or out to Pedregalejo.

- **El Pimpi for dinner.** The bodega is a lovely, famous building worth a drink at the bar, but the food is pricey for what it is. Have a glass of wine, then eat elsewhere.

- **Calle Larios as a sight.** It is a pretty high street, nothing more. The side streets are where the life is.

- **Malagueta as your beach day.** It is handy, not memorable. For a proper beach, go to Nerja or at least out to El Palo.

## When to visit Malaga

The best months are **April-June and September-October**: 20-28°C, warm enough for the beach, without peak crowds. Spring flights from northern Europe are often very cheap, apart from Easter week.

July and August are hot (30°C+, sometimes much hotter during "terral" winds from inland), busy and expensive, especially during the August Feria. Winter is Malaga's secret weapon: 15-18°C on sunny days, the lowest hotel prices of the year, and few tourists, though you will not want to swim. November to February is the cheapest time to go, Christmas lights season excepted. Avoid Semana Santa unless you specifically want the processions; prices double and the centre is blocked off.

## Where to stay in Malaga: best areas

- **Centro Histórico (old town):** Best for first-timers who want everything on foot. Noisy at night on weekends. Mid-range hotels about €100-180 a night, hostels €25-40 for a dorm bed.

- **Soho:** Next to the old town and the port, slightly cheaper, younger feel. About €80-140 for a double.

- **La Malagueta:** Between the centre and the beach, with sea-view hotels. Good for couples and beach days. About €110-200.

- **El Perchel / near María Zambrano station:** Less charming, but cheaper and handy for trains, the airport and day trips. About €70-120.

- **Pedregalejo:** A former fishing district by the sea, quieter, with the best espeto bars on your doorstep. 15-20 minutes by bus to the centre. About €80-150, mostly apartments.

## What to eat in Malaga

Malaga eats fish first. The essentials:

- **Espetos de sardinas:** grilled sardines on a skewer, best May to October. About €4-7 a portion.

- **Fritura malagueña:** mixed fried fish, usually boquerones (anchovies), squid, cuttlefish and small red mullet.

- **Boquerones en vinagre:** marinated anchovies with garlic and parsley, a classic tapa.

- **Porra antequerana:** a thicker, local cousin of salmorejo, topped with egg and ham.

- **Ajoblanco:** cold almond and garlic soup, usually served with grapes, great in summer.

- **Campero and pitufo:** a big round sandwich and a small breakfast roll (pitufo with tomato and olive oil is the local breakfast).

- **Malaga sweet wine (vino dulce):** drink it at Casa de Guardia; a glass is a couple of euros.

A menú del día at lunch runs about €12-16 for three courses with a drink. A tapas dinner with wine costs about €20-30 per person; a sit-down fish restaurant dinner is about €35-50. Our tip: eat your main meal at lunch, as locals do, and keep the evening for tapas.

## Airport to city centre and getting around

The easiest way from the airport is the **Cercanías C1 train**: about 12 minutes to María Zambrano and about 15 to Centro-Alameda, for about €2, every 20 minutes or so. The station is a short walk from Terminal 3. The **A Express bus** goes to Paseo del Parque and Alameda in about 20-30 minutes for about €4 (pay by card on board). A taxi costs about €25-30 to the centre, more at night.

In the city you will walk almost everywhere. EMT buses cost about €1.40 a ride; the rechargeable **Tarjeta Transbordo** brings that down and allows changes within an hour. The metro has two lines, useful mainly for the station area and the university, not for sightseeing. Skip the hop-on hop-off bus unless you have mobility issues.

## How much a trip to Malaga costs

- **Shoestring:** about €50-70 a day. Hostel bed (€25-40), menú del día, tapas dinner, free sights and Sunday free entries, buses and walking.

- **Mid-range:** about €120-180 a day per person (sharing a double). Old town hotel, two paid sights, a proper lunch, a tapas dinner with wine, one day trip.

- **Extras:** Caminito del Rey about €10-25 plus transport, Alhambra about €20, flamenco about €25.

## Day trips from Malaga

- **Nerja and Frigiliana:** Nerja has the Balcón de Europa viewpoint, better beaches than Malaga and the huge Nerja Caves. Alsa buses take about 1-1.5 hours (about €5). Frigiliana, a pretty white village, is a short bus ride from Nerja.

- **Caminito del Rey:** A boardwalk pinned to the walls of a gorge, about 3-4 hours on foot. Book tickets weeks ahead; take the train to El Chorro (about 45 minutes) or a tour.

- **Ronda:** The town split by a deep gorge and crossed by the Puente Nuevo. About 2 hours by bus or train; a long but worthwhile day.

- **Granada:** About 1.5-2 hours by bus. Only go if you have pre-booked Alhambra tickets, which sell out well in advance.

- **Córdoba:** About an hour on the high-speed train from María Zambrano, for the Mezquita and the old Jewish quarter.

## Common mistakes and scams

- **Not booking ahead** for the Caminito del Rey, the Alhambra or the Picasso Museum in high season.

- **Taking a taxi from the airport** when the train costs €2 and is faster at rush hour.

- **Accepting rosemary sprigs** near the cathedral; the women offering them will then demand money for a "fortune".

- **Pickpockets** on Calle Larios, at the Christmas lights and during processions. Keep phones out of back pockets.

- **Restaurants with picture menus** and staff touting outside on the main squares: generally poor value.

- **Sunbathing at midday in summer** and walking up to Gibralfaro in the heat. Go early or take the bus.

- **Eating dinner at 7pm** and finding kitchens empty. Lunch is 2-4pm, dinner from about 9pm.

## Malaga FAQ

### How many days do you need in Malaga?

Three days is ideal: two to see the city properly and one for a day trip. Two days covers the main sights if you are short on time.

### Is Malaga worth visiting?

Yes. It combines a Moorish fortress, strong museums, excellent cheap food and a city beach, and it is one of the best-connected airports in Spain for budget flights.

### Can you swim in Malaga in winter?

The water drops to around 15-16°C, so most people do not, but the air is often warm enough to sit on the beach in a T-shirt.

### Is Malaga expensive?

No, by Western European standards it is good value. A menú del día is about €12-16 and a beer with a tapa about €3-5, though hotel prices have risen sharply in summer.

### How do I get from Malaga airport to the city centre?

Take the C1 train for about €2, roughly 15 minutes to Centro-Alameda. The A Express bus (about €4) and taxis (about €25-30) are the alternatives.

### Is the Alcazaba free on Sundays?

Yes, the Alcazaba and Gibralfaro are free on Sunday afternoons. Expect queues and check the current times before you go.

### What is the best beach in Malaga city?

Pedregalejo and El Palo, east of the centre, are nicer and more local than Malagueta. For a proper beach day, head to Nerja.

### What food is Malaga famous for?

Espetos (grilled sardines), fried fish, marinated anchovies, ajoblanco and sweet Malaga wine.

### What to see in Malaga in one day?

If you only have one day, follow day 1 of this itinerary: Moorish Malaga and the old town. Morning: Be at the Alcazaba when it opens, then walk or take bus 35 up to Gibralfaro.
