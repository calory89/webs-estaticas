# Cancún travel guide: beaches, food and how to plan 4 days

> Updated: 2026-10-21

## Why go to Cancún

Cancún gets written off as a spring-break factory, and the Hotel Zone does have its share of mega-resorts and foam parties. But the water really is that colour, the flights are often cheap, and the city is a very good base. Within two hours you can reach Maya ruins, cenotes and a quiet island. Downtown, away from the resorts, is a normal Mexican city with good food.

My advice: treat the beach as the opening act, not the whole show.

## What to see in Cancún

- **Playa Delfines:** the wide public beach near the south end of Boulevard Kukulcán (around Km 18), with the big CANCÚN sign. Surf is rougher than elsewhere, so watch the flags. Go early or at sunset.

- **Playa Tortugas and Playa Langosta:** calm, shallow water on the bay side. Better for kids and for people who don't love waves.

- **El Rey ruins:** a small Maya site in the Hotel Zone, with iguanas everywhere. Not Chichén Itzá, but it takes 45 minutes and costs very little.

- **Museo Maya de Cancún:** a modern museum with a decent collection. The San Miguelito archaeological site shares the grounds, and it is shaded and quiet.

- **Isla Mujeres:** a short ferry ride away. Rent a golf cart, swim at Playa Norte and eat fish somewhere with plastic chairs.

- **MUSA underwater museum:** sculptures sitting on the seabed, reached by snorkelling or diving tours. Visibility depends on the day, so don't expect perfection.

- **Downtown (El Centro):** Parque de las Palapas, Avenida Tulum and Mercado 28. This is where locals eat, shop and hang out.

- **Nichupté Lagoon:** the lagoon behind the Hotel Zone. Sunset boat or kayak trips here are calmer than ocean tours, with plenty of birds.

## Things to do

Snorkelling is the obvious one. Tours to the reef off Punta Nizuc and around Isla Mujeres run daily. Between roughly late spring and September, you can also book whale shark trips from the Isla Mujeres and Holbox area. Pick an operator that limits group sizes and doesn't let people touch the animals.

Cenotes are the best break from the beach. Many are an easy drive south, especially around Puerto Morelos and along the road to Tulum. Go as early as you can, because tour buses arrive by late morning. For nightlife, Coco Bongo is the famous big-production club in the Hotel Zone. It is touristy but fun if you commit. Downtown bars are cheaper and more relaxed.

## Cancun itinerary: 4 days, day by day

Four days is the sweet spot. Three works if you skip the big day trip, and a week lets you add Tulum, Holbox or Bacalar without rushing.

**4-day itinerary for Cancun**

| Day | Plan |
|---|---|
| Day 1 | Arrive, swim at Playa Langosta or Tortugas, dinner downtown near Parque de las Palapas. |
| Day 2 | Ferry to Isla Mujeres early, golf cart, Playa Norte, back in the late afternoon. |
| Day 3 | Day trip to Chichén Itzá (about 2.5 hours each way) or Tulum, ideally with a cenote stop on the way back. |
| Day 4 | Morning at El Rey and Playa Delfines, a slow lunch, then the airport. |

## When to go: weather and seasons

December to April is the best window: dry, sunny and warm, but also the most expensive, especially around Christmas and Easter. May and early June are hot and often cheaper. Hurricane season runs June to November, with the real risk concentrated from August to October. Fares and rooms drop, but check forecasts and buy flexible tickets.

Sargassum, the brown seaweed, can pile up on Caribbean-facing beaches from spring through autumn, and it is usually worst in summer. Bay-side beaches and Isla Mujeres tend to be less affected. Check recent photos before you pick a hotel.

## Where to stay by area and budget

**Hotel Zone:** the classic choice, a 20-odd kilometre strip with beach access, resorts and all-inclusives. Convenient, but you pay for it and eat mostly at hotel prices. Rooms with a sea view start around the mid-range and climb fast.

**Downtown:** hostels, small hotels and apartments, much cheaper, with real restaurants outside your door. You'll need a short bus ride to reach the beach, which is a fair trade. Dorm beds are often in the budget range, and decent private rooms cost far less than in the Hotel Zone.

**Puerto Juárez and the north:** quieter, close to the Isla Mujeres ferry, with a mix of local life and a few resorts. Good if you plan to spend most days on the island.

If you want one beach holiday, an all-inclusive can work out well. If you want to eat and explore, base yourself downtown and visit the beach by day.

## What to eat and where

Skip the tourist menus for a day and eat Yucatecan. **Cochinita pibil** (slow-roasted pork in achiote) is the signature dish, usually in tacos or tortas. **Panuchos** and **salbutes** are fried tortillas with toppings, and **sopa de lima** is a bright chicken and lime soup. Tacos al pastor are everywhere downtown, and the late-night stands are the best. For dessert, find a **marquesita**, a crisp rolled crepe filled with Edam cheese and often Nutella.

Try the food stalls around Parque de las Palapas in the evening, and the small restaurants at Mercado 28 for a casual lunch. Los Almendros is a well-known Yucatecan restaurant downtown, a good place to try poc chuc (grilled marinated pork). La Parrilla is a long-standing Mexican restaurant with a lively crowd, better for dinner. In the Hotel Zone, expect higher prices, so keep your best meals for downtown.

## Getting from the airport and getting around

Cancún International (CUN) is about 20 minutes south of downtown. The ADO bus runs to downtown, to the Hotel Zone and onward to Playa del Carmen, and it is the cheapest sensible option. Official airport taxis are expensive but straightforward. Agree the price before you get in. Ride-hailing at the airport is complicated, so check the current situation locally.

Once in town, public buses on the R-1 and R-2 routes link downtown with the Hotel Zone for a couple of dollars at most, and they run all day. They're crowded but easy. Taxis don't use meters, so agree the fare first. For day trips, rent a car if you're comfortable driving in Mexico, or book a tour. ADO also runs comfortable long-distance buses to Tulum, Valladolid and Chichén Itzá. Ferries to Isla Mujeres leave from Puerto Juárez and Playa Tortugas, and the crossing takes about 20 to 30 minutes.

## Rough daily budget

- **Budget:** around US$40-60 for a hostel bed, street food, buses and one paid activity.

- **Mid-range:** roughly US$100-160 for a downtown hotel, restaurant meals and a tour.

- **Comfortable:** US$250 and up, usually a Hotel Zone stay with several tours.

Tours, ferries and entry fees add up. Bring pesos for small purchases, though many places quote in dollars.

## FAQs

### Is Cancún safe for tourists?

The Hotel Zone and downtown are generally fine with normal care. Use official transport, avoid unlit areas at night and keep valuables out of sight. Check your government's current travel advice before you go.

### Can I swim at every beach?

No. Beaches facing open sea can have strong currents. Follow the flag colours, and choose bay-side beaches if you want calm water.

### Are beaches public in Cancún?

Yes, all beaches in Mexico are public. Resorts can restrict hotel facilities, not the sand itself, though access paths vary.

### Is Cancún worth it if I don't want an all-inclusive?

Definitely. Stay downtown, eat local and use the Hotel Zone just for beaches. It's cheaper and more interesting.

### What's the best day trip?

Isla Mujeres is the easiest. If you want history, go to Chichén Itzá early, then cool off in a cenote on the way back.

### How many days do you need in Cancun?

4 days are enough to see the essentials of Cancun without rushing. The route in this guide is organised day by day.
