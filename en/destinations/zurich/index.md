## Why go to Zurich

Zurich has a reputation for being a bank with a lake attached. That's unfair. It's a compact, clean, slightly smug city where you can swim in a river at lunchtime, climb a hill by train before dinner, and drink from a public fountain on almost every corner. The water is free, which is about the only thing here that is.

It works well as a short break and as a base for the rest of Switzerland. Lucerne, the Rhine Falls and the mountains are all within an hour or so by train. Go for the lake, the old town and the food, and accept early on that you will wince at some menu prices.

## What to see in Zurich

- **Altstadt and Niederdorf:** Cobbled lanes on the east bank of the Limmat, full of small shops, bars and guild houses. Come before 9 am or after 10 pm for the best atmosphere. Midday on Saturday is crowded.

- **Lindenhof:** A small gravel terrace on a hill above the old town, with chessboards and a good view over the river. It's the best free five minutes in the city.

- **Grossmünster:** The twin-towered church that defines the skyline and the place where the Swiss Reformation began. Climb the tower for the rooftop view.

- **Fraumünster:** Go for the stained-glass windows by Marc Chagall. Morning light is best, and the colour is startling.

- **St. Peter church:** Look up at the clock face, which is among the largest in Europe. It takes ten seconds and costs nothing.

- **Bahnhofstrasse:** One of the world's most expensive shopping streets. You don't have to buy anything. Walk it for the watches, the trams and the chocolate shops, and finish at the lake.

- **Lake Zurich and the Seebäder:** The lakeside promenade from Bürkliplatz to Zürichhorn is lovely at dusk. In summer, the wooden lake baths such as Utoquai or Seebad Enge are the real social life of the city.

- **Uetliberg:** A short train from the main station (the S10) takes you up to the city's own mountain, then a ten-minute walk gets you to the summit and a wide view over the lake and, on clear days, the Alps.

- **Zurich West:** Former industrial districts turned into bars, galleries and street food. Look at the railway arches of Im Viadukt and the container-built Freitag tower.

- **Kunsthaus Zürich:** The city's main art museum, strong on Giacometti, Munch and the Impressionists. It's a good rainy-day option.

## Things to do

Swim in the Limmat. In summer the locals float down the river with a waterproof bag and climb out at the Oberer Letten bathing area, which has a bar and a cheerful, slightly alternative crowd. The current is real, so follow the signs and don't try it after heavy rain.

Take a lake boat. The ZVV network includes a few short boat routes, and a basic ride across the water is far cheaper than the long tourist cruises. Spend an evening in a Seebad with a beer, or browse the Saturday market at Bürkliplatz when it's on. If you like chocolate, the Lindt Home of Chocolate in Kilchberg is a short train and bus ride away and has a very good tasting section.

Day trips are easy. The Rhine Falls near Schaffhausen are roughly an hour away, Lucerne is about 45 minutes by train, and Winterthur has some excellent museums and a quieter feel.

## Zurich itinerary: 3 days, day by day

Two days covers the centre. Three is better, because it lets you add Uetliberg or a day trip without rushing. A fourth day only makes sense if you plan a mountain excursion.

## When to go

Late spring and early autumn are the sweet spots. May, June and September give you mild days, long light and fewer crowds than July and August. Summer is the best time for swimming, with temperatures often in the mid-20s °C and occasional hot spells above 30. August brings the Street Parade, a huge techno event that fills hotels and raises prices.

Winter is cold, often between 0 and 5 °C, and the city can sit under grey fog for days. It makes up for that in December, when the Christmas markets appear, including the one inside the main station with its giant crystal-covered tree. April has the Sechseläuten spring festival, with a procession and a burning snowman. Book early for any of these dates.

## Where to stay

**Altstadt and Niederdorf:** The most atmospheric, and the most expensive per square metre. Good if you want to walk everywhere, but noisy on weekend nights.

**Around the Hauptbahnhof and Langstrasse:** Practical and well connected, with plenty of mid-range hotels. The Langstrasse area is livelier and a bit rougher, though generally safe.

**Seefeld and Enge:** Calmer, near the lake, with good restaurants and easy access to the baths. This is my pick for a relaxed stay.

**Zurich West:** Modern, trendy and good for nightlife, with a few design hotels.

On budget, expect dorm beds from around 45 to 70 francs, comfortable mid-range hotels from about 150 to 250, and luxury options such as the Baur au Lac or the Dolder Grand well beyond that. If prices look painful, check hotels near the airport or in Oerlikon, which are a short train from the centre.

## What to eat and where

The signature dish is **Zürcher Geschnetzeltes**: sliced veal in a cream and mushroom sauce, usually served with rösti. Try it at a traditional place like Zeughauskeller near Paradeplatz, where the portions are large and the room is a former arsenal. Cheese fondue and raclette are more of a winter habit, but you'll find them year-round in the old town.

For something cheaper, go for a **bratwurst with a bread roll** at a stand such as Sternen Grill near Bellevue. It's a proper local ritual. Hiltl, on Sihlstrasse, claims to be the oldest vegetarian restaurant in Europe, and its buffet is a good option if you want something lighter. For a splurge in a room full of art, there's Kronenhalle.

Don't leave without a **Luxemburgerli**, the small macaron-like sweet from Confiserie Sprüngli on Paradeplatz. Supermarkets such as Migros and Coop sell decent ready meals and sandwiches, which is how many locals eat lunch on a budget. Tap water is safe everywhere, so carry a bottle and fill it at the fountains.

## Getting from the airport and getting around

Zurich Airport sits in Kloten, about ten to fifteen minutes by train from the Hauptbahnhof. Trains run every few minutes during the day, and a single ticket costs roughly 7 to 9 francs. Buy it at the machine or in the SBB app before boarding. A taxi is much more expensive and is rarely faster.

Inside the city, trams, buses, trains and some boats are run by the ZVV. A day pass for the city zone costs about 9 to 10 francs and is usually better value than buying individual tickets. The Zurich Card bundles transport with museum entry and can pay off if you plan to visit several museums. Most of the central sights are within walking distance, and the tram network handles the rest. Ticket checks are real, so don't ride without one.

## Rough daily budget

Zurich is one of the priciest cities in Europe, so plan accordingly. These are rough figures per person per day, excluding flights.

- **Budget:** about 110 to 150 francs, with a dorm bed, supermarket meals, a street sausage and a day transport pass.

- **Mid-range:** about 250 to 350 francs, with a decent hotel, a sit-down dinner and a couple of attractions.

- **Comfortable:** 500 francs or more, with a top hotel and restaurants.

A coffee costs around 5 to 6 francs and a main course in a normal restaurant often runs 30 to 50. Free pleasures such as the lake, the river, the hills and the fountains help a lot.

## FAQ about Zurich

### Is Zurich worth visiting for a weekend?

Yes. The centre is compact, the lake is close, and you can see the highlights in two days. Add Uetliberg or a day trip if you have a third.

### Is Zurich safe?

Very. Normal city precautions apply, especially around the station and Langstrasse at night, but violent crime is rare.

### Do I need Swiss francs?

Switzerland uses the Swiss franc, not the euro. Cards and contactless are accepted almost everywhere, including trams and small shops, so you need very little cash.

### Can I swim in Lake Zurich and the river?

Yes, in summer, at official bathing spots. The water is clean, but the lake is cold in early season and the Limmat has a strong current, so follow local signs.

### Is Zurich a good base for day trips?

Excellent. Lucerne, the Rhine Falls, Winterthur and several mountain excursions are all reachable by train in about an hour or less.
