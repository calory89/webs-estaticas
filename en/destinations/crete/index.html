Crete in 7 days: beaches, gorges and where to base yourself

🔥 1 deal to Crete🏨 Hotels in Crete

Why go to Crete

Crete is not an island you "do" in a weekend. It is Greece's biggest island, about 260 km from end to end, with snowy mountains down the spine, a Minoan palace that is nearly 4,000 years old, and some of the best food in the country. People who come for the beaches stay for the villages, the raki and the old men playing backgammon outside a kafeneio at eleven in the morning.

It also works for almost everyone: couples, families, hikers, people who just want a sunbed and a grilled fish. The catch is distance. Pick a region or two and resist the urge to cover it all.

What to see in Crete

Knossos

The Minoan palace near Heraklion is the headline act. The reconstruction by Arthur Evans is controversial, with its bright red columns, but it gives you a sense of scale you would not get from ruins alone. Go at opening time or late afternoon, and visit the Heraklion Archaeological Museum first or afterwards to see the frescoes and the Phaistos Disc.

Chania Old Town and Venetian Harbour

The most atmospheric town on the island. Walk the harbour out to the lighthouse, then lose yourself in the lanes of Splantzia and the old Jewish quarter. The quietest hour is before 9 am, when you have the waterfront to yourself.

Rethymno and the Fortezza

Smaller and calmer than Chania, with a tangle of Venetian and Ottoman houses and a large 16th-century fortress on the hill. Good for an overnight stop rather than a long stay.

Samaria Gorge

One of Europe's longest gorges, around 16 km on foot from Omalos to Agia Roumeli, mostly downhill and still tiring. It is generally open from May to October, weather permitting. Start early, wear proper shoes and carry water. From Agia Roumeli a boat takes you to Sougia or Chora Sfakion, then a bus returns you to your base.

Elafonissi and Balos

Elafonissi in the southwest has shallow turquoise water and a pinkish tint to the sand. Balos, on the northwest tip, is the lagoon in every photo. Both are stunning and both get packed from late morning in summer. Arrive early or accept the crowds.

Preveli and the south coast

The palm-fringed beach at Preveli, reached by a steep path, is worth the effort. Nearby, Matala has its famous cave cliffs and a hippie past, while Agia Galini and Plakias make easy bases.

Spinalonga

The island fortress off Elounda was a leper colony until 1957. Boats leave from Elounda and Plaka, and the visit takes about an hour or so. Quiet, odd and moving.

Phaistos and Agia Triada

Less restored than Knossos and set above the Messara plain, Phaistos has a better view and far fewer tour buses. Agia Triada, a short drive away, is a small Minoan site worth adding.

Vai and Zakros in the east

Vai has a natural palm grove behind its beach, while the ruined palace at Zakros sits in a quiet corner of the island. The east is drier, emptier and slower.

Things to do

  • Hike the gorge at Imbros, shorter and easier than Samaria, or the E4 trail along the south coast near Loutro.
  • Take a boat to Gramvousa and Balos from Kissamos, or to Chrissi island from Ierapetra.
  • Visit Arkadi Monastery, a symbol of Cretan resistance to Ottoman rule, in the hills above Rethymno.
  • Drive up to the Lasithi Plateau, with its old windmills and orchards, and stop in a mountain village for lunch.
  • Swim at Seitan Limania, a narrow cove near Chania reached by a short, steep walk.
  • Do a food or cooking morning in a village; many families offer them and they are usually better than touristy ones in town.

How many days and a suggested route

Five days is the minimum for one region. Seven is comfortable, ten lets you see both the west and east. Here is a seven-day route that avoids too much backtracking:

  • Days 1-3: Chania. Old town on day one, Elafonissi or Balos on day two, Samaria or Imbros on day three.
  • Day 4: drive east to Rethymno, stopping at Arkadi, and spend the evening in the old town.
  • Day 5: Preveli and Plakias or Agia Galini on the south coast.
  • Day 6: Phaistos and Matala, then continue to Heraklion for Knossos and the museum the next morning.
  • Day 7: Knossos, museum, then fly out or continue to Agios Nikolaos and Spinalonga if you have more time.

When to go

May, June, September and early October are the sweet spot: warm sea, long days, open tavernas, and fewer people. Samaria Gorge is open and hiking is pleasant. July and August bring heat of 30-35°C or more, strong meltemi winds on the north coast, and packed beaches. April can be lovely, with wildflowers, though the sea is still cold. Winter is mild on the coast but wet, and many seasonal businesses close. The mountains see snow.

Where to stay

Chania is the best all-round base: old-town guesthouses in converted Venetian buildings, with plenty of mid-range choices and easy access to the west. Rethymno suits a quieter stay with a town beach. Heraklion is not pretty, but it is practical for the airport, Knossos and early flights, and hotels there are cheaper. Agios Nikolaos and Elounda are good for the east, with Elounda leaning towards resorts and higher prices. Plakias, Agia Galini and Paleochora are laid-back southern options for those who want a beach and a taverna and not much else.

Roughly, hostels and simple rooms start low, solid mid-range hotels and apartments sit in the middle, and upscale resorts in Elounda or around Agios Nikolaos are at the top end. Book early for July and August.

What to eat and where

Cretan food is built on olive oil, wild greens, local cheese and lamb. Start with dakos, a barley rusk topped with tomato and soft myzithra cheese. Try kalitsounia, small cheese or herb pies, gamopilafo, a rich wedding rice, and antikristo, lamb cooked slowly beside the fire. In Chania, look for boureki, a baked dish of courgette, potato and cheese. Snails with rosemary, chochlioi boubouristi, are a local favourite if you are adventurous.

Finish a meal with raki (tsikoudia), which usually arrives free. In the old towns, tavernas along the waterfront tend to be pricey; walk two streets back for better value. For the best meals, drive into a village such as Anogia or one of the hill villages near Rethymno and eat wherever the locals do.

Getting from the airport and getting around

Crete has two main airports: Heraklion (HER) and Chania (CHQ), plus a small one at Sitia. Both have buses to the nearest town centre, running regularly and cheaply, and taxis at set rates. Heraklion is about 5 km from the centre, Chania roughly 14 km.

For getting around, KTEL buses link the main towns along the north coast, such as Chania, Rethymno, Heraklion and Agios Nikolaos, reliably and cheaply. For beaches, gorges and villages, a rental car is much easier. Roads on the north coast are decent; mountain and southern roads are slower and twisty, so allow more time than the map suggests. Fill the tank before long rural stretches. Ferries run overnight from Piraeus to Heraklion and Chania (Souda), and fast boats connect Heraklion with Santorini in summer.

Rough daily budget

These are general figures per person, and prices rise in July and August.

  • Budget: about €60-80 a day with hostels or simple rooms, bakery breakfasts, gyros or taverna lunches, and buses.
  • Mid-range: about €100-150 a day with a decent hotel or apartment, a rental car shared between two, and dinners in tavernas.
  • Comfortable: €200 and up with boutique stays or resorts, boat trips and long dinners.

FAQs

How many days do you need in Crete?

Five days for one region, seven for a good overview, ten if you want west and east without rushing.

Do I need a car?

For beaches and villages, yes. If you only visit Chania, Rethymno and Heraklion, buses and day tours are enough.

What is the best month to visit?

June and September give the best balance of warm sea, good weather and manageable crowds. May and October also work well.

Is the Samaria Gorge hard?

It is long rather than technical. If you walk regularly and start early, most people manage it. Imbros is a shorter alternative.

Which part of Crete is best for a first visit?

The west, with Chania as a base. It combines a beautiful old town with Elafonissi, Balos and Samaria within reach.
