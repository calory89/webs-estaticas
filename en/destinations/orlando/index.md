# Things to Do in Orlando: A 5-Day Guide Beyond the Parks

> Orlando travel guide by Happy Low Cost, 5-day itinerary. Original page: https://www.happylowcost.com/en/destinations/orlando/
> Updated: 2026-11-21

Orlando is two cities stuck together. One is the theme park machine to the southwest: Disney, Universal, SeaWorld and International Drive, with their hotels, outlet malls and eight-lane roads. The other is a real Florida city to the north, with lakes, oak trees hung with Spanish moss, a good Vietnamese food scene and Winter Park's brick streets. Most visitors never see the second one, and that is a shame, because it is where the trip gets cheaper and calmer.

It suits families, of course, but also roller coaster fans, space nerds and anyone happy to drive to a freshwater spring and float for an afternoon. We think **5 days** is the right amount: two or three park days (more will drain your wallet and your feet), one day for the city and Winter Park, and one day out to Kennedy Space Center or the springs. You need a car or a healthy ride-share budget, because Orlando was built for driving.

## What to see in Orlando: the places worth your time

### Magic Kingdom

If you only do one Disney park, make it this one: Cinderella Castle, Space Mountain, Pirates of the Caribbean and the evening fireworks are the reason most people fly to Orlando. Give it a full day. One-day tickets run about €110-170 ($125-195) depending on the date, plus parking of around €30 ($35). **Our tip:** arrive 45 minutes before opening and head straight to the big rides. The first two hours are worth more than the next six.

### EPCOT

EPCOT is the park adults tend to like most, thanks to the World Showcase: eleven country pavilions around a lake, each with food and drink. Guardians of the Galaxy: Cosmic Rewind is one of the best rides in Orlando, and Test Track and Soarin' hold up well. You need a full day, at similar prices to Magic Kingdom. Come during one of the festivals (Food and Wine in autumn, Flower and Garden in spring) and budget for snacking around the world.

### Universal Studios Florida and Islands of Adventure

These two parks sit side by side, and the Hogwarts Express train between them (Diagon Alley on one side, Hogsmeade on the other) only works with a park-to-park ticket. Rides are more intense than Disney's: VelociCoaster is the best coaster in town and Hagrid's Magical Creatures Motorbike Adventure is close behind. Allow one long day for both. A two-park day costs roughly €150-210 ($175-240). Skip Express Pass on quiet days, but on holiday weekends it can save you hours.

### Epic Universe

Universal's newest park opened in 2025, with lands themed on Harry Potter's Ministry of Magic, Super Nintendo World, How to Train Your Dragon and classic monsters. It is impressive and very popular, so expect long queues and tickets of around €130-190 ($150-220) for a single day, often sold separately from the other two parks. Book early and get there before opening.

### Disney's Animal Kingdom and Hollywood Studios

Animal Kingdom has Pandora (Avatar Flight of Passage is excellent) and a safari ride that's best in the morning when animals are active. Hollywood Studios has Star Wars: Galaxy's Edge and Tower of Terror. Both are fine, but if you are short on time or money, pick one and skip the other. Animal Kingdom closes earlier than the other parks, so half a day plus the afternoon is often enough.

### Disney Springs

Disney's free shopping and restaurant district, no park ticket needed and parking is free. It's a decent evening out with live music on the waterfront and a lot of overpriced shops. Spend two or three hours. Go for dinner or drinks rather than shopping.

### Lake Eola Park

Downtown Orlando's centrepiece is a lake with a walking loop of under a mile, a fountain that lights up at night, swans and swan-shaped pedal boats (around €15-20, $18-22, for half an hour). It's free and takes an hour. The farmers market on Sunday mornings is the best time to come.

### Winter Park

Ten minutes north of downtown, Winter Park is the prettiest part of Orlando: Park Avenue with its independent shops and cafés, Central Park with its oaks, and old lakeside mansions. Take the Scenic Boat Tour (about €17, $20, for an hour) through the chain of lakes and narrow canals. Then visit the **Charles Hosmer Morse Museum of American Art**, which holds the world's largest collection of Louis Comfort Tiffany's work, including his chapel interior. The entry fee is low, usually under €10. Half a day is perfect.

### Kennedy Space Center Visitor Complex

Technically an hour east, but too good to leave off this list. Saturn V is enormous and the Space Shuttle Atlantis display is a real surprise. Tickets are around €65-80 ($75-90), and you need a full day. Check the launch schedule: seeing a rocket go up from the coast is free and unforgettable.

### Gatorland

Gatorland has been on South Orange Blossom Trail since 1949, a cheerful, slightly old-fashioned park full of alligators, the gator-jumping show and a zip line over the ponds. Tickets cost about €30-35 ($35-40), and half a day is plenty. It's the best value animal attraction near Orlando and far less crowded than the big parks.

### Wekiwa Springs State Park

Thirty minutes north in Apopka, this state park has a spring-fed swimming area that stays around 22°C (72°F) all year, plus kayak and canoe rentals on the river. Entry is about €5-6 ($6) per car. On summer weekends it fills up and closes the gate by mid-morning, so arrive early or go on a weekday.

### Harry P. Leu Gardens

Fifty acres of gardens on Lake Rowena, just northeast of downtown, with camellias, roses, palms and a historic house. Entry is about €13 ($15) and it takes 1-2 hours. Go in the morning before it gets hot.

## Things to do in Orlando

Beyond the parks, the best Orlando experiences are about water, food and space.

- **Kayak a spring run:** Wekiwa, Rock Springs or the Wekiva River are clear and full of turtles and the occasional alligator (they leave you alone).

- **Eat your way through Mills 50:** the Vietnamese and Asian district along Mills Avenue and Colonial Drive is the best cheap food in town.

- **Watch a rocket launch:** launches from Cape Canaveral can be seen from Orlando on clear nights, and from the coast it's spectacular.

- **Catch an Orlando City or Orlando Pride match** at Inter&Co Stadium downtown; the atmosphere is good and tickets are cheaper than most US sports.

- **Airboat ride** on the lakes south of the city around Kissimmee: loud, touristy, fun. Expect about €40-55 ($45-65).

- **Drinks at the parks' resorts:** you can visit Disney's monorail hotels or the Polynesian beach at night without a ticket and watch the fireworks from across the lagoon.

- **Thursday farmers markets and Sunday Lake Eola market** are the local side of Orlando.

## Orlando for free (or almost)

Orlando is expensive, but there's more free stuff than you'd guess.

- **Disney Springs**: free entry and parking, with live music.

- **Universal CityWalk**: free entry, but parking costs; it's free after 6pm on some days, so check.

- **Magic Kingdom fireworks** from the beach at the Polynesian Village Resort, free.

- **Lake Eola** and its Sunday market.

- **Winter Park's Park Avenue and Central Park**, plus the Saturday farmers market.

- **Rocket launches** from public beaches on the Space Coast.

- **State parks** for €5 a car, the cheapest full day out in Florida.

## What is overrated (and what to do instead)

- **Doing all four Disney parks in four days.** By day three everyone is tired and arguing. Do two Disney parks well and use the money elsewhere.

- **International Drive for dinner.** It's chain restaurant after chain restaurant at tourist prices. Drive 20 minutes to Mills 50 or Winter Park instead.

- **Character dining.** Disney character meals cost around €50-70 ($60-80) per adult for average buffet food. If the kids aren't specifically desperate for it, skip it.

- **ICON Park and The Wheel.** The view is flat suburbs and car parks. Spend the money on a springs day.

## When to visit Orlando

The best time to visit Orlando is **late January to early March and November**, when it's warm but not punishing (highs around 22-27°C) and the parks are less crowded outside school holidays. Summer is hot and humid at 33°C or more, with a thunderstorm almost every afternoon, and hurricane season runs June to November.

The busiest and most expensive times are Christmas to New Year, Easter and spring break (March), and July. The cheapest and quietest windows are usually late August to September (hot and stormy) and the first two weeks of December. Disney prices tickets by date, so a quiet Tuesday can be noticeably cheaper than a Saturday.

## Where to stay in Orlando: best areas

- **Disney resort area and Lake Buena Vista:** best for Disney-focused trips. Disney's own Value Resorts start around €110-170 ($125-195); hotels on Lake Buena Vista's Hotel Plaza Boulevard are often cheaper with shuttles.

- **Universal area:** Universal's hotels like Cabana Bay start around €130-200 ($150-230), with walking or shuttle access and early park entry. Ideal if Universal is your priority.

- **International Drive:** central between both resorts, lots of choice, from €70-150 ($80-170). Watch for resort fees.

- **Downtown and Winter Park:** best for adults wanting restaurants and bars; €120-220 ($140-250). You'll drive 25-40 minutes to the parks.

- **Kissimmee:** cheapest, with vacation rental houses with pools from €90-180 ($100-210) for a group. Good for big families with a car.

## What to eat in Orlando

Orlando doesn't have one signature dish, but Florida and its immigrant communities eat well. In **Mills 50**, order pho and bánh mì; Pho 88 is the famous one and a bowl costs about €12-15 ($14-17). The Cuban sandwich (roast pork, ham, Swiss cheese, pickles, mustard, pressed bread) is everywhere and is a cheap lunch at €9-13 ($10-15).

Try **gator bites** once (fried alligator, like chewy chicken), **key lime pie** for dessert and fresh grouper sandwiches if you go to the coast. In the parks, Disney's pineapple Dole Whip and Butterbeer at Universal are tourist clichés that everyone ends up buying anyway.

Prices: a quick-service lunch in the parks is about €18-25 ($20-28) per person. Outside, a casual lunch is €13-20 ($15-23). A sit-down dinner with a drink runs €35-60 ($40-70) per person, before tip. Add 18-20% tip and around 6.5-7.5% sales tax to every menu price.

## Airport to city centre and getting around

Orlando International Airport (MCO) is about 25 minutes from downtown and 30 minutes from the Disney and Universal areas. Your options:

- **Rental car:** the most practical choice for most visitors, from €35-60 ($40-70) a day, plus tolls (get a SunPass or the rental company's toll pass) and hotel parking of €15-35 ($17-40) a night.

- **Uber/Lyft:** about €35-55 ($40-65) to Disney or Universal, 30-40 minutes.

- **Mears Connect shuttle:** shared shuttle to the Disney resorts, about €15-20 ($17-23) each way per adult.

- **LYNX bus:** about €2 ($2) a ride, cheap but slow (often over an hour).

Around the parks, Disney runs free buses, monorail, boats and the Skyliner gondola for its hotel guests. The I-Ride Trolley serves International Drive. SunRail commuter trains run north to south through downtown and Winter Park on weekdays, but it won't take you to the parks. If you plan to visit the coast or springs, rent a car for those days at least.

## How much a trip to Orlando costs

Per person per day, not counting flights:

**Rough daily budget**

| Level | Per person per day | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| Shoestring | €110-160 / $125-185 on non-park days | Shared motel or rental in Kissimmee, cheap food in Mills 50 or the supermarket, state parks and free attractions. Add €130-190 for each theme park day. |
| Mid-range | €220-350 / $250-400 on park days | 3-star hotel near the parks, park ticket, one sit-down meal, car rental split among the group. |

The big costs are tickets, parking and food inside the parks. Multi-day tickets get cheaper per day, so a 4-day Disney ticket costs much less per day than a single day.

## Day trips from Orlando

- **Kennedy Space Center and Cocoa Beach:** about an hour east by car on the SR 528 toll road. Full day.

- **Blue Spring State Park:** about 45 minutes to an hour north in Orange City. From November to March, hundreds of manatees shelter in the spring run. Arrive early, since the park closes when full.

- **St. Augustine:** about 2 hours northeast. The oldest city in the US, with a Spanish fort, Castillo de San Marcos, and a walkable old town.

- **Tampa and Busch Gardens:** about 1.5 hours west. Busch Gardens has excellent roller coasters, and Tampa's Ybor City is good for Cuban food.

- **Mount Dora:** about 45 minutes northwest. A small lakeside town with antiques and cafés, a calm half day.

## Common mistakes and scams

- **Timeshare "free ticket" offers:** booths on International Drive and some hotel lobbies offer discounted park tickets if you attend a "short presentation". It usually takes hours of hard-sell timeshare pitching.

- **Unofficial ticket resellers:** used or partly used multi-day park tickets are non-transferable and often won't work. Buy from Disney, Universal or authorised sellers only.

- **Resort fees:** many hotels add €20-45 ($25-50) per night on top of the advertised rate. Check the final price before booking.

- **Rental car toll packages:** some companies charge a high daily fee for their toll transponder even on days you don't use it. Read the terms.

- **Forgetting tax and tips:** budget around 25% on top of restaurant prices.

- **Underestimating the heat:** bring water, sunscreen and a cheap rain poncho; afternoon storms are near-daily in summer.

## Orlando FAQ

### How many days do you need in Orlando?

Five days is ideal: two or three theme park days, one day in the city and Winter Park, and one day trip. With three days, pick your two favourite parks and one day off.

### Is Disney or Universal better?

Disney is better for younger children and classic characters. Universal is better for teenagers, thrill rides and Harry Potter fans. Couples and adults often enjoy Universal and EPCOT most.

### Do you need a car in Orlando?

Not if you stay in a Disney or Universal hotel and only visit the parks. For anything else, including Winter Park, the coast or the springs, a car makes life much easier.

### What is the cheapest time to go to Orlando?

Late August to September and early December are usually cheapest, when school is in session. Avoid Christmas, Easter and July if you are on a budget.

### What can you do in Orlando besides theme parks?

Swim and kayak in springs like Wekiwa, visit Winter Park and the Morse Museum, eat in Mills 50 and see Kennedy Space Center. Rocket launches and manatees at Blue Spring are big highlights.

### Is Orlando safe for tourists?

The tourist areas are generally safe. Use normal city caution at night downtown, don't leave valuables visible in your car, and keep away from alligators near any lake.

### Is Orlando close to the beach?

Cocoa Beach and New Smyrna Beach on the Atlantic are both about an hour's drive. The Gulf Coast beaches near Tampa are about two hours away.

### Is Orlando expensive?

It depends on how you travel. This guide puts the "shoestring" level at about €110-160 / $125-185 on non-park days per person per day and the "mid-range" level at about €220-350 / $250-400 on park days. Flights are extra: this page shows real fares checked by Happy Low Cost.
