Why go to Singapore

Singapore is the easiest big Asian city to enjoy on a first trip. Everything runs on time, the tap water is safe, English works everywhere, and you can eat some of the best food in the region for the price of a coffee back home. It is also a real mix: Chinese temples, Indian spice shops and Malay mosques sit a few MRT stops apart, and plenty of the city is green. It is hot, yes, and it is not cheap for hotels. But a long layover here beats most destinations' full holidays.

What to see: 8 places worth your time

  • Gardens by the Bay. The Supertree Grove is free to walk around, and the light show at dusk is genuinely good. Pay for the Cloud Forest dome if you want the indoor waterfall and a break from the heat.
  • Marina Bay Sands and the waterfront. The SkyPark deck is pricey for what it is, but the skyline view at sunset justifies it once. Otherwise, walk the Helix Bridge and the promenade for nothing.
  • Chinatown. Visit the Buddha Tooth Relic Temple and the colourful Sri Mariamman Temple, then eat at Maxwell Food Centre. Go early; the souvenir streets are best before 11am.
  • Little India. Walk Serangoon Road, step into the Sri Veeramakaliamman Temple, and wander through Tekka Centre. It is loud, fragrant and the most lived-in part of the centre.
  • Kampong Glam. The golden-domed Sultan Mosque anchors this Malay quarter. Arab Street has fabric shops and Turkish cafés, and Haji Lane has the street art and small bars.
  • Singapore Botanic Gardens. A UNESCO site and a proper escape. The National Orchid Garden charges a small fee, but the rest is free. Early morning, when locals jog and do tai chi, is the quietest hour.
  • Sentosa. It is a resort island, with Universal Studios, beach clubs and aquarium-style attractions. Worth it with kids, skippable otherwise.
  • Pulau Ubin. A short bumboat ride from Changi Point takes you to a granite island with rutted tracks and a few villages. Rent a bike and you are in another decade.

Things to do beyond the sights

Do a hawker crawl. Pick three centres in one day and eat small portions at each. Take the Southern Ridges walk, which links parks via the Henderson Waves bridge and gives you canopy views without the crowds. At night, the Night Safari beside the zoo is a good one if you like animals, since it lets you see nocturnal species with little light disturbance. Spend an evening on the river between Boat Quay and Clarke Quay; it is touristy, but the lit-up bridges are pretty. For a free show, catch Spectra, the light and water display at Marina Bay, in the evening.

Singapore itinerary: 4 days, day by day

Three full days covers the main sights without rushing, and four lets you slow down. If you have just a long layover, Changi's Jewel with its indoor waterfall is a decent consolation, but do leave the airport if you have six hours or more.

4-day itinerary for Singapore
DayPlan
Day 1Chinatown in the morning, lunch at Maxwell, Marina Bay at sunset, Gardens by the Bay after dark.
Day 2Botanic Gardens early, then Little India for lunch, then Kampong Glam in the late afternoon.
Day 3Sentosa or Pulau Ubin, depending on whether you want beaches or bikes. Finish with satay.
Day 4(optional). Southern Ridges, shopping on Orchard Road if that is your thing, Night Safari.

When to go

There is no cold season. Temperatures sit around 25-32°C all year, with high humidity. The wettest months are roughly November to January, when afternoon downpours are common but short. February to April is usually the driest and most comfortable. From around June to October, smoke from regional fires can occasionally cause haze. Chinese New Year in January or February brings lights and festivities but also higher hotel prices. The Formula 1 night race in September or October pushes prices up around Marina Bay. Carry a small umbrella whenever you go.

Where to stay

Budget

Chinatown, Little India and the Bugis area have the best hostels and small guesthouses, all near MRT stations. Expect roughly 25-50 USD for a dorm bed or a tiny private room. Little India is generally the cheapest.

Mid-range

Tanjong Pagar and the Clarke Quay and Riverside area are well placed, with plenty of restaurants nearby. Expect around 120-220 USD a night. Kampong Glam has charming boutique options.

Splurge

Marina Bay and the Raffles Hotel area offer the classic big names, including Marina Bay Sands with its famous rooftop pool, the Fullerton, and Raffles itself. Prices start at roughly 350 USD. Orchard Road has good luxury hotels too but is more of a shopping district.

What to eat and where

Eat at hawker centres. They are cheap, clean and licensed, and the food is the main reason to visit. Chicken rice is the national comfort dish; try it at Maxwell Food Centre. Laksa, a spicy coconut noodle soup, is best in the Katong area. Char kway teow is wok-fried flat noodles with a smoky edge, and roti prata is a crisp flatbread served with curry, perfect for breakfast in Little India. For breakfast, order kaya toast with soft-boiled eggs and sweet coffee at a Ya Kun outlet or a kopitiam. Chilli crab is messy and expensive, and you should do it once at a seafood restaurant. For satay, go to Lau Pa Sat in the evening, when the street outside fills with grills. A typical hawker meal costs roughly 3-6 USD, while a restaurant meal is closer to 15-30 USD.

Getting from the airport and getting around

Changi Airport is one of the world's best, and it connects directly to the MRT. Trains run from Changi Airport station on the East-West line, and you usually change at Tanah Merah for the city. Expect about 30-45 minutes and a couple of dollars. A taxi or ride-hail into the centre takes roughly 20-30 minutes and costs around 20-30 USD, more at night.

Within the city, the MRT is clean, air-conditioned and easy to follow. Buses cover everywhere else. You can tap in with a contactless bank card or use an EZ-Link card, so there is no need to buy tickets one by one. Taxis and ride-hailing apps are plentiful and affordable. Walking is pleasant in the mornings and evenings, but at midday use the underground walkways and malls to stay cool.

Rough daily budget

  • Budget: around 60-90 USD, with a hostel, hawker meals, MRT and one paid attraction.
  • Mid-range: around 150-250 USD, with a decent hotel, a mix of hawker and restaurant meals, and a couple of attractions.
  • Comfortable or high-end: 350 USD and up, mostly driven by hotel and drinks. Alcohol is heavily taxed, so cocktails can cost as much as a hawker dinner.

FAQs

Do I need a visa for Singapore?

Many nationalities can enter visa-free for short stays, but rules vary, so check your passport's requirements. Most visitors must also complete the online arrival card before landing.

Is Singapore safe?

Very. Crime is low and walking at night is generally fine. The bigger risk is breaking a rule by accident, so be aware that fines apply for littering, and that vaping is illegal.

Is the tap water drinkable?

Yes. Bring a refillable bottle, as you will need it in the heat.

Do I need to tip?

Not really. Restaurants usually add a service charge, and hawker stalls and taxis do not expect a tip.

Is Singapore worth it for a short stopover?

Absolutely. With even 8 to 10 hours, you can reach Marina Bay, eat at a hawker centre and be back at Changi on the MRT with time to spare.

How many days do you need in Singapore?

4 days are enough to see the essentials of Singapore without rushing. The route in this guide is organised day by day.
