# San Sebastian in 3 Days: Pintxos, Beaches and Hikes

> San Sebastian travel guide by Happy Low Cost, 3-day itinerary. Original page: https://www.happylowcost.com/en/destinations/san-sebastian/
> Updated: 2026-11-21

San Sebastian (Donostia in Basque) is a small, elegant seaside city that punches far above its size for food. It has a horseshoe bay, two green hills at either end, a compact Old Town packed with pintxo bars, and a surf beach a ten-minute walk from a belle époque promenade. It is not cheap, it rains a lot, and in August it is rammed. It is also one of the few places in Europe where you can eat brilliantly for €25 standing at a bar.

It suits food lovers, couples, walkers and surfers more than anyone looking for big-ticket museums or nightlife until dawn. You can see the main sights in two days, but **3 days** is the right number: two for the city and one for the coast (Pasaia, Hondarribia or Getaria). Any longer and you are basically on an eating holiday, which is not the worst idea.

## What to see in San Sebastian: the places worth your time

### La Concha beach and promenade

La Concha is the city's postcard: a curved golden beach backed by white railings and old lamp posts. Walk the full promenade from the Old Town to Ondarreta (about 30-40 minutes, free) rather than just sitting at one end. **Our tip:** go early in the morning or at low tide in the evening, when the sand is wide and the locals are out with their dogs.

### Parte Vieja (Old Town)

The Old Town is a grid of narrow streets between the port and the river Urumea, and it is where most of the pintxo bars are. Calle 31 de Agosto, Calle Fermín Calbetón and Plaza de la Constitución (the old bullring square with numbered balconies) are the core. Allow a couple of hours by day and a whole evening for eating. It is free to wander but it will cost you about €30-40 a head if you do the bar crawl properly.

### Monte Urgull

The hill at the eastern end of the bay, reachable on foot from the Old Town in about 20 minutes. Paths lead past old cannons and fortifications up to the Mota Castle and a big statue of Christ (Sagrado Corazón). It is free, shady and much quieter than Igueldo. Take the path that starts behind the port near the aquarium for the best views back over La Concha.

### Monte Igueldo

The classic panoramic viewpoint over the whole bay, at the western end. A wooden funicular dating from 1912 runs up from beside Ondarreta (about €5-6 return). The view is the best in the city; the old-school amusement park at the top is charmingly tired. Go just before sunset, then walk down to the Peine del Viento.

### Peine del Viento

Eduardo Chillida's three rusted steel sculptures bolted into the rocks at the far end of Ondarreta beach. On a rough day waves blast up through holes in the paving, which is the real show. Free, 20-30 minutes. Come at high tide with a westerly swell for maximum drama, and mind your phone.

### San Telmo Museoa

The best museum in town, set in a 16th-century convent at the foot of Urgull. It tells the story of Basque society, with the cloister and the gold-and-sepia murals by Josep Maria Sert in the old church as highlights. Allow 1.5-2 hours; tickets about €6, and it is usually free on Tuesdays (check before you go). Ideal rainy-day plan.

### Basilica of Santa María del Coro

A baroque church whose ornate facade sits at the end of Calle Mayor, perfectly framing the Buen Pastor cathedral at the other end of the street. Entry is cheap or free outside of guided visits. The photo from the church steps down Calle Mayor is the one worth taking.

### Zurriola beach and Gros

Across the river from the Old Town, Zurriola is the surf beach, with a younger, scruffier crowd than La Concha. Gros, the neighbourhood behind it, has some of the best pintxo bars in the city and fewer tour groups. Walk over the Kursaal bridge past Rafael Moneo's glass cube concert hall. Free, as long as you like.

### Miramar Palace gardens

The former summer palace of the Spanish royal family sits on a small hill splitting La Concha from Ondarreta. The building is mostly closed, but the lawns are open and free, with a great angle over the bay and Santa Clara island. A good spot for a picnic from the market.

### Santa Clara island

The small island in the middle of the bay, reachable by boat from the port in summer only (about €5-6 return). There is a tiny beach, a bar and a lighthouse. Worth it on a hot, clear day for a swim with a different view of the city; skip it otherwise.

### Tabakalera

A former tobacco factory near the train station turned into a free contemporary culture centre. The real reason to go is the rooftop terrace, with a free view across the city that most visitors never see. Allow 30-45 minutes, more if there is an exhibition on.

### Mercado de la Bretxa

The traditional market on the edge of the Old Town, with fish, cheese and vegetables downstairs. It is smaller and less glamorous than you might expect, but it is where to buy Idiazabal cheese and Cantabrian anchovies to take home. Go in the morning, Monday to Saturday.

## Things to do in San Sebastian

- **Do a proper pintxo crawl.** One or two pintxos and a drink per bar, then move on. Order the hot dishes from the blackboard, not just what is sitting on the counter.

- **Take a surf lesson at Zurriola.** Several schools along the promenade offer group lessons for about €35-50 including board and wetsuit. Waves are friendly for beginners most of the year.

- **Swim in La Concha.** Calm, clean and lifeguarded in summer. Locals swim well into autumn; the water is bracing but fine.

- **Hike to Pasaia along the coast.** The path from Gros over Mount Ulia to Pasai San Pedro takes about 2.5-3 hours, with cliffs and sea views the whole way. Cross to Pasai Donibane by the small ferry (about €1) and bus back.

- **Visit a cider house (sagardotegi).** From roughly January to April, the cider houses in Astigarraga serve a fixed menu with unlimited cider poured straight from the barrel when someone shouts "txotx". Expect about €40-50 per person.

- **Take a cooking or pintxo class.** Plenty of small schools run half-day classes from about €80-120. Good if you want to understand what you are eating.

- **Catch a festival.** The Jazzaldia jazz festival in July, the film festival in September and the Tamborrada drum parade on 20 January are the big three.

## San Sebastian for free (or almost)

The best things here cost nothing. Walking La Concha, climbing Monte Urgull, watching the waves at the Peine del Viento and lounging in the Miramar gardens are all free, and between them they fill a full day.

- The Tabakalera rooftop for a free city view.

- San Telmo Museoa on Tuesdays (usually free, check first).

- Walking up Monte Igueldo on foot instead of taking the funicular (about 30 minutes uphill).

- Cristina Enea park, a quiet wooded park near the station with peacocks wandering around.

- The Pasaia coastal walk, with only the €1 ferry and bus fare back.

- Picnics with bread, cheese and anchovies from the Bretxa market instead of a sit-down lunch.

## San Sebastian itinerary: 3 days

### Day 1: Old Town, Urgull and your first pintxos

**Morning:** Start in the Parte Vieja, see Santa María del Coro and Plaza de la Constitución, then visit San Telmo Museoa. **Afternoon:** Climb Monte Urgull from behind the museum and come down via the port, where the fishermen's bars serve grilled sardines. **Evening:** First pintxo crawl in the Old Town, finishing with a slice of cheesecake at La Viña.

### Day 2: The bay end to end

**Morning:** Walk La Concha promenade to the Miramar Palace gardens, then on to Ondarreta for a swim. **Afternoon:** Peine del Viento, then the funicular up Monte Igueldo for the late-afternoon view. **Evening:** Head to Gros for dinner: pintxos along Calle Zabaleta and around, with fewer crowds than the Old Town.

### Day 3: The coast

**Morning:** Hike from Zurriola over Ulia to Pasaia, take the little ferry to Pasai Donibane and have a seafood lunch there. **Afternoon:** Bus back, or carry on to Hondarribia for its walled old town and painted fishermen's quarter. **Evening:** Final round of pintxos, or a grilled steak (txuleta) dinner if you have budget left.

## What is overrated (and what to do instead)

- **The cold pintxos piled on Old Town counters.** Many have been sitting there for hours and the bars charge €3-4 each. Order the hot dishes from the board instead, or eat in Gros.

- **The amusement park on Igueldo.** Fine with small kids, pointless otherwise. Go for the view, skip the rides.

- **La Concha at midday in August.** Towels touch towels. Swim early, or go to Zurriola or Ondarreta.

- **Booking a Michelin tasting menu just to tick it off.** At €250-350+ a head they are excellent, but if you are not a serious food person, three nights of good pintxos and one txuleta dinner will make you just as happy for a fraction of the price.

## When to visit San Sebastian

The best months are **May, June and September**: warm enough to swim (around 20-25°C), long evenings, and prices below the peak. July and August are hot, sunny and packed, with hotel prices at their highest, especially during Semana Grande in mid-August and the film festival in late September.

This is the green Basque coast, so expect rain at any time of year. Winter (November to March) is mild, wet and cheapest, with hotels sometimes half their summer price; it is also cider house season and the best time to eat without queues. Note that many bars close on Sunday evenings or Mondays.

## Where to stay in San Sebastian: best areas

- **Parte Vieja (Old Town):** for first-timers who want to roll out of bed into pintxo bars. It is noisy at night, especially at weekends. About €120-250 per night for a mid-range double in season.

- **Centro / Área Romántica:** the grid of 19th-century streets behind La Concha, near the Buen Pastor cathedral. Central, quieter and more elegant. About €150-300.

- **Gros:** our pick for most people. Near Zurriola beach, good local bars, a 10-minute walk to the Old Town. About €110-220.

- **Antiguo / Ondarreta:** calm and residential, by the quieter end of the bay and Igueldo. Good for families. About €100-200.

- **Amara and Egia:** less charming but noticeably cheaper, 15-20 minutes on foot from the centre. About €80-150, with hostels from €35-60 a bed in summer.

Book early for July to September. Last-minute prices in August are painful.

## What to eat in San Sebastian

Pintxos are the point. Expect €3-5 for a standard one and €5-10 for a hot dish from the kitchen, with a glass of txakoli (the local sparkling-ish white) or a small beer (zurito) at about €2.50-4.

- **Gilda:** the original pintxo: an olive, an anchovy and a pickled green chilli on a stick.

- **Tortilla at Bar Nestor:** only two tortillas a day, one at lunch and one at dinner. Put your name down in person well before they come out.

- **Anchovies:** at Txepetxa in the Old Town, which has built its reputation on them.

- **Hot pintxos:** veal cheek, foie and risotto at La Cuchara de San Telmo; mushrooms at Ganbara; and the classic counter at Gandarias.

- **Basque cheesecake:** the burnt, gooey original at La Viña. About €6-7 a slice.

- **Txuleta:** a huge grilled rib steak from older cattle, for sharing. About €50-80 per kilo.

- **Grilled fish:** turbot (rodaballo) or sea bream, best in Getaria or Pasaia.

- **Idiazabal:** smoked sheep's cheese, often served with quince and walnuts.

A **menú del día lunch** (three courses with wine) costs about €15-22 on weekdays outside the Old Town. A **pintxo dinner** runs about €30-45 per person with drinks. A sit-down dinner at a good mid-range restaurant is about €50-70.

## Airport to city centre and getting around

There are three airports to know. **San Sebastian Airport (EAS)** is tiny and sits in Hondarribia, about 20 km away; the Lurraldebus E21 bus takes about 30 minutes to the centre for around €2-3, and a taxi is about €35-45. **Bilbao Airport** has far more flights, often cheaper ones, with direct buses to San Sebastian taking around 1h15-1h30 for about €20. **Biarritz** in France is another option, about 45-60 minutes away by bus.

Once there, you walk. The centre, the Old Town, Gros and La Concha are all within 30 minutes of each other on foot. Dbus city buses cover the rest (about €2 single, less with a rechargeable Mugi card, which also works on regional buses and Euskotren). The **Euskotren** local train runs to Pasaia, Irun and Hendaye on the French border. There are bike lanes along the bay too, and the public bike scheme dBizi is handy for longer rides.

## How much a trip to San Sebastian costs

- **Shoestring: about €80-110 per day.** Hostel bed (€35-60), market picnic lunch, a modest pintxo dinner (€20-25), walking everywhere and free sights.

- **Mid-range: about €200-300 per day per person sharing a double.** Hotel (€70-150 per person), menú del día lunch, a full pintxo crawl or restaurant dinner, the funicular, a museum and the odd taxi.

- **Add-ons:** surf lesson €35-50, cider house €40-50, Michelin tasting menu €250+.

## Day trips from San Sebastian

- **Pasaia:** a fishing village in a narrow inlet, with Victor Hugo's former house. Walk there (about 3 hours) or take a bus (about 20 minutes). Half a day.

- **Hondarribia:** a walled medieval town with a colourful fishermen's quarter and excellent pintxo bars on Calle San Pedro. About 40 minutes by bus. Half to a full day.

- **Getaria and Zarautz:** Getaria is a small fishing town famous for grilled turbot and txakoli vineyards; Zarautz next door has a long surf beach. About 40-50 minutes by bus. A full day.

- **Zumaia:** dramatic layered flysch cliffs around Itzurun beach, used as a Game of Thrones location. About an hour by bus or Euskotren. Check the tide; the rock platforms show best at low tide.

- **Bilbao:** for the Guggenheim and a different, grittier city. About 1h15 by bus, roughly €8-12 each way. A full day.

Biarritz and Saint-Jean-de-Luz in France are also easy: Euskotren to Hendaye, then a French train or bus.

## Common mistakes and scams

San Sebastian is a very safe city, so real scams are rare. The mistakes are mostly about money and timing.

- **Grabbing plates of counter pintxos without asking the price.** They add up fast. Ask, or order from the board.

- **Arriving at popular bars at 21:30 on a Saturday.** Go for an early round from around 19:30, or do lunch pintxos instead.

- **Forgetting Sundays and Mondays.** Many bars and restaurants close on one of the two. Check before planning your best dinner.

- **Booking unlicensed apartments.** The city has cracked down on illegal tourist flats; stick to licensed places on reputable platforms.

- **Pickpockets in crowded bars and during festivals.** Keep your phone out of back pockets in packed Old Town bars.

- **Not packing a rain jacket.** Even in summer. Umbrellas are useless in the wind on the promenade.

## San Sebastian FAQ

### How many days do you need in San Sebastian?

Three days is ideal: two for the city and one for a coastal day trip. Two days works if you are short on time.

### Is San Sebastian expensive?

Yes, it is one of the most expensive cities in Spain, especially for hotels in summer. Food is good value, though: you can eat very well for €30-40 a head.

### How does a pintxo bar work?

Order at the bar, eat standing up, and pay at the end by telling the staff what you had. Have one or two pintxos per bar and move on.

### Can you swim in San Sebastian?

Yes. La Concha and Ondarreta are calm and great for swimming, while Zurriola has waves and is better for surfing.

### What is the best viewpoint in San Sebastian?

Monte Igueldo has the classic view over the whole bay. Monte Urgull is free and quieter, with great views back over the Old Town and La Concha.

### Is San Sebastian worth visiting in winter?

Yes, if you come to eat. It is rainy and too cold to swim, but hotels are much cheaper, bars are less crowded and cider house season starts in January.

### Which airport is best for San Sebastian?

Bilbao usually has more and cheaper flights, about 1h15-1h30 away by direct bus. San Sebastian's own airport is closer but has few routes, mostly to Madrid and Barcelona.

### Do I need to book restaurants in San Sebastian?

Pintxo bars do not take bookings. Sit-down restaurants, asadores and Michelin places should be booked ahead, weeks or months ahead for the famous ones.
