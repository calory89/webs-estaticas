# Things to Do in Kuala Lumpur: A 3-Day Guide With Food Tips

> Kuala Lumpur travel guide by Happy Low Cost, 3-day itinerary. Original page: https://www.happylowcost.com/en/destinations/kuala-lumpur/
> Updated: 2026-11-21

Kuala Lumpur is not pretty in the postcard sense. It's a hot, humid, traffic-jammed city of glass towers, flyovers and shopping malls, stitched together with Chinese shophouses, Indian temples, mosques and some of the best cheap food in Asia. Most people fly in expecting a stopover and leave wishing they'd stayed longer, mostly because of what they ate.

It suits food lovers, budget travellers, people breaking a long-haul flight to Bali, Australia or Thailand, and anyone who likes cities with several cultures living side by side. **Three days is the right amount**: two for the city and one for Batu Caves plus a bit more eating, or a day trip to Malacca. With less than that you'll spend most of your time in a Grab car and a mall.

## What to see in Kuala Lumpur: the places worth your time

### Petronas Twin Towers and KLCC Park

The towers are the one thing everyone comes for, and they deserve it: up close, the steel facade is more impressive than any photo. Going up to the Skybridge and the observation deck on level 86 costs about €20 (RM 100) and takes around 45 minutes. Tickets often sell out, so book online a day or two ahead. Our tip: the best view of the towers is from outside, either from KLCC Park at dusk or from a rooftop bar nearby. The park and the Lake Symphony fountain show in the evening are free.

### Batu Caves

A huge limestone cave temple north of the city, reached by 272 rainbow-painted steps beside a 43-metre golden statue of Lord Murugan. Entry to the main Temple Cave is free. Take the KTM Komuter train from KL Sentral (about 30 minutes, under €1) and go first thing, before the tour buses and the midday heat arrive. Allow 2 to 3 hours. Knees must be covered, the monkeys will steal any food or plastic bag they see, and you can skip the overpriced sarong rental by wearing long trousers.

### Merdeka Square and the Sultan Abdul Samad Building

Malaysia declared independence on this big lawn in 1957. The Moorish-style Sultan Abdul Samad Building with its copper domes faces it, and the whole area is free to walk around. Allow 30 to 45 minutes. It looks best at night, when the buildings are lit up, and it's a short walk from Masjid Jamek LRT station.

### Masjid Jamek and the River of Life

KL's oldest mosque sits where the Klang and Gombak rivers meet, the spot where the city was founded. After dark the riverbank turns into the "Blue Pool", with misty blue lights along the water. Visiting is free. The mosque has visiting hours for non-Muslims and robes at the door, so check times before going and avoid Friday midday prayers.

### Chinatown, Petaling Street and Kwai Chai Hong

Petaling Street's covered market is mostly fake watches and handbags, but the streets around it are the best part of old KL. Look for Kwai Chai Hong, a restored back lane with murals, the Guan Di Temple and the Sri Mahamariamman Temple, KL's oldest Hindu temple, almost opposite each other on Jalan Tun H.S. Lee. Allow half a day if you're eating as you go. Come hungry and skip the shopping.

### Central Market (Pasar Seni)

An Art Deco building from the 1930s that's now a handicraft market. It's touristy, but it's the easiest place to buy batik, songket fabric and pewter without trekking out to the suburbs. Free entry, 45 minutes is plenty, and it's air-conditioned, which by 2pm matters a lot.

### Islamic Arts Museum Malaysia

This is the best museum in the city, and one of the best of its kind anywhere: Qur'an manuscripts, Ottoman tiles, scale models of famous mosques and a wonderful jewellery and textile collection, all under painted domes. Entry is about €4-5 (RM 20). Give it 2 hours, then walk over to the National Mosque next door.

### National Mosque (Masjid Negara)

A modernist mosque from 1965 with a folded-umbrella roof and a 73-metre minaret. Free, with robes and headscarves provided at the entrance. It's only open to visitors outside prayer times, so check before going. Half an hour is enough.

### Perdana Botanical Gardens

KL's main green space, also known as the Lake Gardens, is a good shady break between the museums. Entry is free, and there's a deer park and a hibiscus garden. Go in the morning, because by midday it's sticky even under the trees.

### Thean Hou Temple

A three-tier Chinese temple on a hill south of the centre, covered in red lanterns, with a good view of the skyline. Free. It's awkward to reach by public transport, so take a Grab (about €2-3 from Brickfields). Allow 45 minutes. It's prettiest around Chinese New Year, though also at its most crowded then.

### Kampung Baru

A Malay village of wooden stilt houses that has somehow survived a 10-minute walk from the Petronas Towers. The contrast is the reason to come. Walk around in the late afternoon and stay for dinner at one of the Malay food stalls. The pedestrian bridge to KLCC gives you the classic "village and towers" photo.

### KL Forest Eco Park

A patch of original rainforest right below KL Tower, with a canopy walkway between the trees. Entry for foreigners is a few euros. It's short (about an hour), but seeing rainforest in the middle of a capital is unusual. Combine it with the tower if you're going up anyway.

### Brickfields (Little India)

Next to KL Sentral, Brickfields is all garland stalls, sari shops and Tamil music on Jalan Tun Sambanthan. Free to wander, and the main reason to come is lunch on a banana leaf. An hour or two is enough.

## Things to do in Kuala Lumpur

Beyond the sights, the KL experience is mostly about eating, drinking and escaping the heat in creative ways.

- **Eat your way down Jalan Alor at night.** It's touristy and pricier than elsewhere, but the grilled chicken wings at Wong Ah Wah and the general chaos are worth one evening.

- **Drink on a rooftop.** Heli Lounge Bar on Jalan Sultan Ismail is a working helipad that turns into a bar at night, with no railings worth mentioning. Expect €10-15 a cocktail, because alcohol is heavily taxed in Malaysia.

- **Do a mamak night.** Mamak restaurants are Indian-Muslim canteens, many open 24 hours. Order roti canai and teh tarik at midnight like the locals do.

- **Take a cooking class.** Several schools offer half-day classes with a market visit for about €40-60, and you'll learn to make rendang or nasi lemak properly.

- **Watch the Lake Symphony fountain show** in KLCC Park. The shows run several times each evening.

- **Get a foot massage on Bukit Bintang.** About €10-15 for an hour. Agree the price first and say no to the upsell.

- **Visit a Ramadan bazaar** if you're there during the fasting month. Kampung Baru's bazaar is a huge street of food stalls before sunset.

- **Escape into a mall.** Pavilion and Suria KLCC are huge, cold and full of excellent food courts. In KL, malls are part of the culture, not just shops.

## Kuala Lumpur for free (or almost)

A surprising amount of KL costs nothing, and transport between the free sights is cheap.

- Batu Caves main temple: free, and the train costs under €1.

- KLCC Park and the fountain show: free.

- Merdeka Square, Masjid Jamek, the National Mosque and the Blue Pool at night: all free.

- Thean Hou Temple, Sri Mahamariamman and Guan Di Temple: free.

- Perdana Botanical Gardens: free.

- The free GoKL city buses run loops around Bukit Bintang, KLCC and Chinatown.

- The air-conditioned elevated walkway between Bukit Bintang and KLCC: free, shaded, and quicker than the traffic.

## Kuala Lumpur itinerary: 3 days

### Day 1: Old KL and Chinatown

**Morning:** start at the Islamic Arts Museum, then walk to the National Mosque and through the Perdana Botanical Gardens. **Afternoon:** head down to Merdeka Square and Masjid Jamek, then into Chinatown: Central Market, Kwai Chai Hong, and the two temples on Jalan Tun H.S. Lee. Have a late lunch of Hokkien mee or chicken rice. **Evening:** come back to the river for the Blue Pool lights, then have a drink on a Chinatown rooftop.

### Day 2: Batu Caves, Brickfields and the towers

**Morning:** take the early KTM train from KL Sentral to Batu Caves and climb before 9am. **Afternoon:** ride back to KL Sentral and walk into Brickfields for a banana leaf lunch, then rest during the hottest hours. Go to Thean Hou Temple late in the afternoon. **Evening:** do your booked Petronas Skybridge slot or just KLCC Park at sunset, watch the fountain show, then take the walkway to Bukit Bintang for dinner on Jalan Alor.

### Day 3: Malay KL or a day trip

**Morning:** have nasi lemak for breakfast in Kampung Baru and walk the village lanes. **Afternoon:** visit KL Forest Eco Park, and KL Tower if you skipped Petronas, or go shopping and eating in Pavilion. **Evening:** Heli Lounge Bar for sunset, then a mamak dinner. If you'd rather leave the city, use this day for Malacca instead.

## What is overrated (and what to do instead)

**Going up both towers.** Petronas and KL Tower give you very similar views of the same skyline, and together they cost around €40. Pick one, or skip both and pay for a cocktail at Heli Lounge Bar, which has a view that includes the Petronas Towers.

**Shopping on Petaling Street.** The market itself is fake handbags and pushy sellers. The value of Chinatown is the food, the temples and Kwai Chai Hong, so spend your money there.

**Eating every dinner on Jalan Alor.** Prices are inflated, touts wave menus at you, and the quality is mixed. Go once for the atmosphere, then eat in Kampung Baru, Brickfields or a mall food court such as Lot 10 Hutong.

**Genting Highlands.** It's a casino and theme park complex on a cool, foggy mountain. Unless you gamble or have kids, Malacca is a much better day out.

## When to visit Kuala Lumpur

KL is hot and humid all year, at around 31-33°C during the day, so the question is how much rain you get rather than whether it's warm. Expect short, heavy afternoon thunderstorms in any month. The wettest periods are roughly October to November and March to April, while June to August is a bit drier. From August to September, smoke from forest fires in Indonesia sometimes brings haze.

Flights and hotels are cheapest outside the European summer and the big local holidays. Chinese New Year (January or February) brings decorations and crowds. During Hari Raya at the end of Ramadan, the city empties as people go back to their home towns, and many local restaurants close for a few days. During Ramadan itself, food is still easy to find and the evening bazaars are a bonus.

## Where to stay in Kuala Lumpur: best areas

- **Bukit Bintang:** the most convenient base, with malls, Jalan Alor, nightlife and the Monorail and MRT. It's best for first-timers. Mid-range hotels cost €40-80 a night.

- **KLCC:** polished and quiet at night, with the towers and the park on your doorstep. This is where the rooftop-pool hotels and serviced apartments are, from about €60 to €150+.

- **Chinatown:** the cheapest and most atmospheric area, with hostels and boutique hotels in old shophouses. It's best for budget travellers and those who want to walk everywhere. Dorms cost €8-15, and doubles €25-60.

- **KL Sentral and Brickfields:** right next to the airport train, so ideal for a short stopover or an early flight. Prices run €35-90 a night.

- **Bangsar:** a leafy, residential area with cafés and good restaurants, popular with expats. It suits repeat visitors more than first-timers. Expect €45-100.

## What to eat in Kuala Lumpur

Food is the best reason to come. Malay, Chinese and Indian cooking all overlap here, and most of the best of it is served in plastic-chair restaurants for a few euros.

- **Nasi lemak:** coconut rice with sambal, crispy anchovies, peanuts and egg, often with fried chicken. It's the national breakfast. Try it in Kampung Baru.

- **Roti canai and teh tarik:** flaky flatbread with dhal or curry, plus sweet "pulled" milk tea, at any mamak.

- **KL Hokkien mee:** thick noodles fried in dark soy sauce with pork lard. Kim Lian Kee in Chinatown claims to have invented it.

- **Banana leaf rice:** rice, vegetables and curries served on a leaf, refilled until you stop them. Try it in Brickfields.

- **Char kway teow, curry laksa, satay and bak kut teh** (pork rib herbal soup) cover the rest of the classics.

- **Cendol:** shaved ice with coconut milk, palm sugar and green rice-flour jelly. It's the best cure for the 3pm heat.

Lunch at a hawker stall or mamak costs €2-5 (RM 10-25). A sit-down dinner in a decent restaurant costs €10-20 per person. Alcohol is the budget killer: a beer is €5-8 in a bar, much more than the food. Durian is worth trying once, but don't bring it into your hotel, because most of them ban it.

## Airport to city centre and getting around

Both KLIA terminals (KLIA and KLIA2) are about 50 km south of the city.

- **KLIA Ekspres train:** the fastest option, at about 30-35 minutes to KL Sentral. It costs about €11 (RM 55) one way. Book online or tap a contactless card for a small discount.

- **Airport coach:** about an hour to KL Sentral, depending on traffic. It costs about €3 (RM 15).

- **Grab:** 45-75 minutes to your door, for about €15-20 (RM 75-100) plus tolls. This is the best value for two or more people with luggage.

Inside the city, the LRT, MRT, Monorail and KTM lines meet at KL Sentral. Rides cost €0.30-1. Get a Touch 'n Go card from a station counter, or use a contactless bank card where gates accept it. Grab is cheap (€2-5 for most trips in town) and often faster than walking in the heat. That said, avoid Grab between 5 and 8pm on weekdays, when traffic is awful and the trains win. The free GoKL buses are useful for short hops around the centre.

## How much a trip to Kuala Lumpur costs

- **Shoestring: €30-40 a day (RM 150-200).** This covers a hostel dorm or a basic room, hawker food, trains, and mostly free sights.

- **Mid-range: €80-130 a day (RM 400-650).** This gets you a good hotel with a pool, a mix of hawker stalls and restaurants, Grab everywhere, one paid attraction a day, and a rooftop drink.

- **Typical extras:** the Petronas Skybridge costs about €20, a Malacca day trip by bus €8-10 return, and a foot massage €10-15.

## Day trips from Kuala Lumpur

- **Malacca:** a UNESCO-listed colonial port with Dutch red buildings, Peranakan houses and Jonker Street. Buses from TBS terminal take about 2-2.5 hours and cost around €4-5 each way. It's worth a full day or, better, one night.

- **Putrajaya:** the government capital, with the pink Putra Mosque and a huge lake. Take the KLIA Transit train to Putrajaya station (about 20 minutes), then a short Grab. Half a day is enough.

- **Kuala Selangor fireflies:** boat trips to see fireflies lighting up the mangroves, usually combined with the monkeys on Bukit Melawati. It's about 1.5 hours by car, so book a tour or hire a Grab driver.

- **Ipoh:** a laid-back old town with cave temples, street art and famous white coffee. The ETS train takes about 2.5 hours. It's a long day, so better as an overnight.

## Common mistakes and scams

- **Street taxis that "forget" the meter**, especially around Bukit Bintang and KL Sentral. Use Grab and avoid the argument.

- **Touts on Jalan Alor** who seat you and hand over a menu without prices. Check what things cost before ordering, particularly seafood sold by weight.

- **Massage upsells** on Bukit Bintang. Agree what's included and the total price before you lie down.

- **Bag snatching from motorbikes** still happens. Walk with your bag on the side away from the road.

- **Feeding or carrying food near the Batu Caves monkeys**, which leads to bites and stolen sunglasses.

- **Underestimating the heat.** Plan indoor things between 12 and 4pm and drink more water than you think you need.

- **Dressing wrongly for mosques and temples.** Cover your shoulders and knees, and take your shoes off.

## Kuala Lumpur FAQ

### How many days do you need in Kuala Lumpur?

Three days is ideal: enough for the main sights, Batu Caves and plenty of eating. Two days works for a stopover if you skip the day trip.

### Is Kuala Lumpur safe for tourists?

Yes, violent crime against tourists is rare. The main risks are bag snatching, taxi overcharging and bill padding in touristy restaurants.

### Is it worth going up the Petronas Towers?

It's worth it once if you book ahead, but the towers look better from outside. If you're on a budget, KLCC Park at sunset and a rooftop bar give you the better view.

### What should I wear in Kuala Lumpur?

Light, loose clothes for the heat, plus something that covers your shoulders and knees for mosques, temples and Batu Caves. Carry a light layer, because malls and trains are freezing.

### Can you drink alcohol in Kuala Lumpur?

Yes. Bars and restaurants in Bukit Bintang, Chinatown, KLCC and Bangsar serve alcohol, but it's expensive because of high taxes. Buy beer at a convenience store if you're counting euros.

### How do I get from KL to Batu Caves?

Take the KTM Komuter train from KL Sentral to Batu Caves station, which takes about 30 minutes and costs under €1. A Grab costs around €5-7.

### Is Kuala Lumpur expensive?

No, it's one of the cheapest capitals in Asia for food and transport. Hotels are good value too. Only alcohol and the main paid attractions feel pricey.
