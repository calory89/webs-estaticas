Phuket travel guide: beaches, Old Town and island hopping

Why go to Phuket

Phuket is Thailand's biggest island, and it is easy to dismiss as a package-holiday factory. Parts of it are. Patong after midnight is not subtle. But the island is large enough to hold very different places: a Sino-Portuguese old town with excellent food, quiet southern coves, rainforest in the middle, and a boat dock from which you can reach limestone islands in under an hour. Pick your base carefully and Phuket works very well. Pick it carelessly and you will spend your week in traffic.

It is also one of the easiest places in Southeast Asia to reach, with plenty of flights and a long list of seasonal deals from Europe via Gulf and Asian hubs, and from Bangkok at almost any hour.

What to see: the places worth your time

Phuket Old Town

Walk Thalang Road, Dibuk Road and little Soi Romanee, lined with pastel shophouses built by Chinese tin-mining families in the 19th and early 20th centuries. Go in the morning, before the heat, for coffee and a look at the Chinese shrines. On Sunday evening, Thalang Road closes to traffic for the Lard Yai walking street market, which is crowded but good for snacks.

Big Buddha

A 45-metre white marble seated Buddha on the Nakkerd Hills, with views over Chalong Bay and the southern half of the island. It is free, and the quietest hour is early morning. Dress modestly.

Wat Chalong

The island's most important Buddhist temple, and an active one. Locals come to make offerings, so keep your voice down and cover your shoulders and knees. It takes 45 minutes, not half a day.

Promthep Cape

The southern tip of the island, and the classic sunset spot. It gets busy, so arrive an hour early, find a spot on the rocks and let the tour buses leave before you do.

Kata, Karon and Kata Noi beaches

On the southwest coast, long beaches with soft sand and a manageable amount of development. Kata Noi, the smaller one to the south, is the calmest of the three. Nai Harn, further south, is a lovely curve of sand backed by a lagoon.

Surin and Kamala

Smaller, slightly more upmarket beaches on the west coast. They are good for a swim and a long lunch, but the current can be strong outside the dry season.

Phang Nga Bay

Limestone towers rising out of green water, usually visited by boat or kayak, with a stop at Khao Phing Kan, the "James Bond Island" of the movie. The rock itself is crowded. The scenery around it, especially from a kayak in a quiet lagoon, is the real reward.

Phi Phi and Coral Islands

Phi Phi is a full-day speedboat trip and very popular. Coral Island (Koh Hae) is closer and easier for a half-day of snorkelling. Choose a small-group departure over a giant boat if your budget stretches.

Khao Phra Thaeo National Park

A patch of remaining rainforest in the north of the island, with short trails and the Bang Pae waterfall. It is the best place on Phuket to get out of the beach mindset for a couple of hours.

Things to do

  • Island hop from Chalong Pier. The Racha Islands are better for clear water and snorkelling than the busiest Phi Phi stops.
  • Dive or snorkel. The Similan Islands are the headline destination, but they only open to visitors in the dry season, roughly November to May.
  • Take a Thai cooking class, many of which start with a market visit.
  • Try Muay Thai. Gyms around Chalong run drop-in classes for beginners, and watching a night of fights is a more honest cultural experience than most shows.
  • Visit an ethical elephant sanctuary where there is no riding and no performances. Check recent reviews before booking.
  • Rent a kayak or paddleboard on a calm morning, before the wind picks up.

How many days and a suggested route

Four to five days is enough for a first visit. With a week you can add a second island trip and slow down.

  • Day 1: Arrive, settle into your beach, swim and eat nearby.
  • Day 2: Old Town in the morning, a long lunch, then Wat Chalong and Big Buddha in the afternoon.
  • Day 3: Full-day boat trip, either Phang Nga Bay or Phi Phi.
  • Day 4: Southern beaches: Nai Harn, Kata Noi, then sunset at Promthep Cape.
  • Day 5: A cooking class, Khao Phra Thaeo, or a second island.

When to go: weather and seasons

The dry season runs from about November to April. December to February is the best stretch: sunny, less humid, calm seas. It is also the most expensive and crowded. March and April get very hot, though the sea is warm and flat.

From May to October the southwest monsoon arrives. Expect heavy showers, rougher seas and red flags on west-coast beaches, where rip currents are a real danger and people do drown every year. Take the flags seriously. Prices fall sharply, boat trips are sometimes cancelled, and the island is much quieter. If you are flexible and happy to swim in a hotel pool on bad days, the shoulder months of May and October can be good value.

Where to stay by area and budget

  • Patong: The most developed beach, with the widest choice of hotels and the loudest nightlife on Bangla Road. Good if you want everything on your doorstep. Not for light sleepers.
  • Kata and Karon: The sweet spot for most visitors. Good beaches, plenty of restaurants, and mid-range hotels and guesthouses at fair prices.
  • Old Town: No beach, but the best food and character, and cheaper boutique hotels. A good choice for a few nights combined with a beach stay.
  • Nai Harn and Rawai: Quieter southern options, with excellent seafood in Rawai and easy access to boats.
  • Kamala, Surin and Bang Tao: More upscale, with resorts and beach clubs. Better with a car or scooter.
  • Mai Khao and Nai Yang: Near the airport, long empty sands, a good first or last night.

Budget travellers will find hostels and basic guesthouses in Patong, Kata and Old Town. Mid-range is wide and competitive. High-end resorts cluster on the west coast.

What to eat and where

Phuket has its own food, shaped by Hokkien Chinese and southern Thai traditions. Look for mee hokkien (stir-fried egg noodles), moo hong (slow-braised pork belly with pepper and garlic), and nam prik kung siap (a dried-shrimp chilli dip with vegetables). Southern curries like gaeng som are properly spicy, so ask for less chilli if you are cautious. For breakfast, try Old Town dim sum with strong local coffee, and for dessert, oh-aew, a sweet iced jelly drink.

In Old Town, restaurants such as One Chun Cafe and Raya serve local dishes in old shophouses and are well known for good reason. For seafood, head to Rawai, where you pick fish at the market and have it grilled at a nearby restaurant. In Patong, the Banzaan fresh market is a cheaper, more local option than the beachfront places. Street stalls and night markets are good everywhere, and a plate of noodles or rice rarely costs more than a few dollars.

Getting from the airport and getting around

Phuket International Airport (HKT) is in the north of the island, about 45 minutes to an hour from Old Town and Kata, and longer to the far south in traffic. Options:

  • Airport bus to Phuket Town, cheap and slow but reliable.
  • Phuket Smart Bus, a cheap shuttle along the west coast toward Rawai, with stops near the main beaches.
  • Official taxis from the airport counter at a fixed price, pricey but simple.
  • Ride-hailing apps like Grab and Bolt work on the island, though pick-up points at the airport can be restricted.

Around the island, the shared songthaews (open-sided trucks) run between Phuket Town and the main beaches, but they are slow and infrequent. Taxis in tourist areas tend to quote high prices, so agree a fare or use an app. Many visitors rent a scooter. It is cheap and convenient, but accidents are common and hospitals see a lot of tourists. Only ride if you have experience, wear a helmet, and check your insurance covers it. Otherwise a car with a driver for a day costs less than you might expect.

Rough daily budget

  • Budget: about 40 to 60 USD per day, with a hostel or simple guesthouse, street food and local transport.
  • Mid-range: about 90 to 160 USD, with a comfortable hotel, restaurant meals and a boat trip every few days.
  • Comfortable: 200 USD and up, with a beach resort and private transport.

Boat tours, scooters and drinks in beach clubs are where budgets leak. Phuket is more expensive than Bangkok or Chiang Mai, especially in high season.

FAQ

How many days do I need in Phuket?

Four to five days covers the main beaches, Old Town and a boat trip. A week is better if you want to relax.

What is the best beach in Phuket?

For a balance of sand, calm water and facilities, Kata Noi and Nai Harn are hard to beat in the dry season. Patong is busier but more convenient.

Is Phuket safe to swim in?

Yes in the dry season, but from May to October west-coast beaches can have dangerous currents. Swim only where lifeguards are present and respect the red flags.

Do I need to rent a scooter?

No. It is useful but risky. Apps, taxis and day hire with a driver are safer, especially if you have little riding experience.

Is Phuket worth visiting in the rainy season?

It can be, if you accept some bad weather. Prices are lower and crowds thinner, but sea conditions limit swimming and some boat trips may be cancelled.
