Mallorca in 6 Days: Palma, Mountains and Quiet Coves

Mallorca is three islands in one. There's Palma, a proper Mediterranean city with a Gothic cathedral and a serious food scene. There's the Serra de Tramuntana, a wall of limestone mountains along the northwest coast with stone villages and hairpin roads. And there's the coast, which is everything from turquoise coves on the east side to the package-holiday strips you've heard about. The trick is knowing which Mallorca you're booking. Most of the island has nothing to do with Magaluf.

It suits almost anyone who likes sun, walking and eating well: couples, hikers, cyclists, families, and budget travellers who catch a cheap flight into Palma. You need 6 days to do it properly: two for Palma and around, two for the mountains and the north, and two for beaches and the east. With 3 or 4 days, base yourself in Palma and pick one region. Rent a car for at least part of the trip. The buses are decent but slow for the coves.

What to see in Mallorca: the places worth your time

Palma Cathedral (La Seu)

This is the one building on the island everyone should see. The Gothic cathedral sits right above the seafront, and inside it has one of the biggest rose windows in Europe plus a strange, brilliant canopy by Gaudí over the altar. Give it about an hour. Entry is roughly €10. Our tip: go first thing, or come for Sunday mass if you just want to see the interior free. Get the classic photo from the Parc de la Mar lake below.

Palma old town and Santa Catalina

The old town behind the cathedral is a grid of honey-coloured lanes and old courtyards (the patios of Carrer de Sant Feliu are worth a look). Walk from Plaça Major down to Passeig del Born, then west into Santa Catalina, the old fishermen's quarter that's now full of brunch spots and tapas bars. Half a day, free. Have breakfast or lunch at the stalls of Mercat de Santa Catalina.

Bellver Castle

Bellver is a round 14th-century castle on a wooded hill west of the centre, with the best view over Palma Bay. Entry is about €4, and it's free on Sundays. Allow 1.5 hours including the walk up through the pine woods. You can get a bus most of the way, but the climb on foot from El Terreno is shaded and pleasant. Avoid the midday heat in July.

Fundació Miró Mallorca

Joan Miró lived and worked in Palma for the last decades of his life, and his studio has been kept much as he left it. It's in Cala Major, a short bus ride from the centre. Entry is about €10 and you need 1.5 hours. It's a good choice for a rainy or too-hot afternoon. Art lovers can pair it with Es Baluard, the modern art museum in the old city walls, which is also worth it for the rooftop view.

Sóller and Port de Sóller

Sóller is an orange-growing town in a valley ringed by mountains, and it's where most people fall for the island. The vintage wooden train from Palma takes about an hour through the Tramuntana. From Sóller, an old tram rattles down to the port and its horseshoe bay. Plan a full day. The train is about €25 one way and the tram about €9. Take the train one way only and the TIB bus back, which is far cheaper and quicker. Order a fresh orange juice in Plaça de la Constitució.

Valldemossa

Valldemossa is a pretty mountain village 30 minutes from Palma, famous because Chopin and George Sand spent a damp winter in the Carthusian monastery here. Entry to the Real Cartuja is around €15. The village itself is free and lovely: cobbles, potted geraniums and stone houses. Two to three hours is enough. Get there before 10am, because the tour buses arrive mid-morning. Eat a coca de patata, the local sweet potato bun.

Deià

Deià is a cluster of stone houses on a hill above the sea, long loved by artists. The poet Robert Graves lived here, and his house is now a small museum. Walk down to Cala de Deià (about 30 minutes), a rocky cove with clear water and a couple of fish restaurants. Half a day, mostly free. Parking is tiny and fills fast, so come by bus or early.

Sa Calobra and Torrent de Pareis

The road to Sa Calobra is the most dramatic drive on the island. It drops in loops to a cove, and from there a path through tunnels leads to the mouth of the Torrent de Pareis gorge, a pebble beach between huge cliffs. It's free apart from parking, which is expensive. In summer the place is packed by noon. The nicer way in is by boat from Port de Sóller (around €30-40 return), with cliff views the whole way.

Cap de Formentor

Formentor is the northern tip of the island, a thin rocky peninsula with a lighthouse and sheer drops into the sea. Stop at the Mirador Es Colomer on the way. In high summer, private cars are restricted during the day, so take the bus from Port de Pollença or go early or late. Allow half a day. Formentor beach, with its pine trees, is reached by boat from Port de Pollença.

Pollença and Alcúdia

These two old towns in the north are close together and very different. Pollença has the Calvari, 365 stone steps up to a chapel with a view, and a lovely main square. Alcúdia has intact medieval walls, the Roman ruins of Pollentia and a big market on Tuesdays and Sundays. Give each 2-3 hours. They're mostly free, and the Roman site costs a few euros.

Caves of Drach

The Coves del Drach near Porto Cristo are huge caverns with an underground lake, and the visit ends with a short classical concert on boats. It's touristy and the tours are tightly run, but it's also impressive. Tickets are about €25-30 and you need 1.5 hours. Book a time slot online, because walk-ups often wait.

Es Trenc

Es Trenc is a long, undeveloped stretch of white sand and shallow turquoise water in the south. It's the closest Mallorca gets to the Caribbean. The beach is free, and parking costs about €8-10. Bring shade and water, as there's little of either. Walk 15 minutes away from the car park and the crowds thin out a lot.

Cala Mondragó and the east coast coves

The southeast is where you find the classic small coves with pine trees and bright water. Cala Mondragó is inside a natural park, has two beaches and is easy to reach, which makes it our pick for families. Cala Llombards and Cala s'Almunia are smaller and prettier. Entry is free. Arrive before 10am in July and August or forget about parking.

Things to do in Mallorca

Sightseeing is half the trip. The other half is outdoors.

  • Hike in the Tramuntana. The GR221 "dry stone route" runs the length of the range. An easy start is the Archduke's path near Valldemossa, or Sóller to Deià (about 3 hours). Go from October to May. Summer midday hikes are miserable.
  • Cycle like the pros. Mallorca is a winter training base for cyclists. Rent a road bike in Port de Pollença or Palma for about €25-40 a day and tackle the Sa Calobra climb.
  • Kayak or snorkel the coast. Guided kayak trips to sea caves run from Cala Figuera, Port de Sóller and the north coast, typically €40-60.
  • Go to a weekly market. Sineu (Wednesday) is the most traditional, with livestock still traded. Alcúdia and Pollença are good too.
  • Taste wine in Binissalem. Small bodegas in the centre of the island do tastings for €15-25. Try wines made from the local Manto Negro grape.
  • Watch the sunset in Sant Elm. The view over Dragonera island at dusk is lovely, and the village is quiet.
  • Take a boat on the bay. Small sailing or catamaran trips from Palma run about €50-80. Choose a small group over the party boats.

Mallorca for free (or almost)

The best of the island costs nothing: beaches, mountain walks and old towns. In Palma, these are free or close to it:

  • Walk the seafront path from the cathedral to Portixol, then have a cheap drink at the old fishing harbour.
  • Visit Bellver Castle free on Sundays.
  • Walk the Palma city walls behind the cathedral, especially at sunset.
  • Look into the medieval Arab Baths courtyard (a couple of euros) and the patios of the old town.
  • Climb the Calvari steps in Pollença.
  • Hike any stretch of the GR221, with free mountain views all day.
  • Swim at Es Trenc, Cala Mondragó or Cala de Deià.

Mallorca itinerary: 6 days

Day 1: Palma

Morning: See the cathedral at opening, then walk the old town and Plaça Major. Afternoon: Have tapas at the Mercat de l'Olivar, siesta, then head up to Bellver Castle for late light. Evening: Dinner and drinks in Santa Catalina.

Day 2: Palma to Sóller

Morning: Take the vintage train to Sóller. Afternoon: Ride the tram to Port de Sóller, then swim or take the boat to Sa Calobra. Evening: Sleep in Sóller or Port de Sóller and eat seafood by the harbour.

Day 3: Deià and Valldemossa

Morning: Hike from Sóller to Deià, or drive there, then swim at Cala de Deià. Afternoon: Visit Valldemossa once the coaches have gone. Evening: Drive to the north and sleep in Pollença or Alcúdia.

Day 4: The north

Morning: Go to Cap de Formentor early (or by bus in summer) and stop at Es Colomer. Afternoon: Walk the Alcúdia walls and visit Pollentia, then climb the Calvari in Pollença. Evening: Eat in Pollença's main square.

Day 5: The east

Morning: Drive to Porto Cristo for a booked slot at the Caves of Drach. Afternoon: Head south and swim at Cala Mondragó or Cala Llombards. Evening: Have fish in Cala Figuera or Santanyí.

Day 6: The south and back

Morning: Spend the morning at Es Trenc before it fills. Afternoon: Stop for lunch in Colònia de Sant Jordi, then drive back towards Palma (about 1 hour). Evening: Watch the sunset from the Palma walls and have a last ensaïmada.

What is overrated (and what to do instead)

  • Magaluf and Playa de Palma. Unless a pub crawl is the whole point, skip them. Stay in Palma city or Port de Sóller instead.
  • Caló des Moro in summer. It's the most photographed cove on the island and is now so crowded that the photo is a lie. Go to Cala s'Almunia nearby, or visit outside July and August.
  • The Sóller train both ways. It's charming once, but at about €25 a leg it gets expensive. Go one way and come back on the TIB bus.
  • Organised coach "island tours". They rush through Valldemossa and Sa Calobra at peak time. A rental car for one day gives you the same route without the crowds.

When to visit Mallorca

May, June and late September to October are the best months. The sea is swimmable, it's warm but not brutal, and prices are lower than in summer. July and August bring 30-35°C, full beaches, the highest hotel rates and real traffic jams near the coves.

Winter (November to March) is quiet and mild, often 15°C, and perfect for hiking and cycling. Many beach hotels close, but Palma and Sóller stay open. Late January and February bring almond blossom across the island. It's the cheapest time to go, and flights can be very low.

Where to stay in Mallorca: best areas

  • Palma old town and Santa Catalina: best for first trips, foodies and anyone without a car. Hostels from about €35, mid-range hotels €100-200, more in summer.
  • Sóller and Port de Sóller: best for hikers and couples, with mountains and sea. Around €90-180 per night.
  • Pollença and Port de Pollença: a calm, family-friendly base for the north and Formentor. Around €80-170.
  • Alcúdia: long shallow beaches, good for families with small kids. Apartments from about €70-150.
  • Santanyí and Cala d'Or: best for the east coast coves. Around €80-160.

What to eat in Mallorca

Mallorcan food is rustic, porky and good. Order these:

  • Ensaïmada: a spiral pastry dusted with icing sugar, plain or filled. Have it at breakfast with coffee.
  • Pa amb oli: bread rubbed with tomato and olive oil, topped with ham or cheese. The island's comfort lunch.
  • Sobrassada: soft, spreadable paprika sausage, often with honey.
  • Tumbet: layered aubergine, potato and peppers in tomato sauce.
  • Frit mallorquí: fried offal and vegetables, for the brave.
  • Arròs brut: a soupy, spiced rice with meat, good in winter.
  • Herbes: the local herbal liqueur, served after dinner.

A weekday menú del día (three courses with wine or water) costs about €14-20 and is the best value on the island. A dinner with wine runs €30-45 per person in a decent restaurant. Avoid the seafront places on Passeig Marítim with photos on the menu, and the paella in Port de Sóller sold for €25 that comes out suspiciously fast.

Airport to city centre and getting around

Palma airport is about 8 km from the centre. Bus A1 runs to Plaça d'Espanya and the port in about 25 minutes for €5, and you can pay by card. A taxi costs about €25-30 to the old town. TIB buses also run straight from the airport to many resorts.

Around the island, TIB buses leave from the Estació Intermodal under Plaça d'Espanya for Sóller, Valldemossa, Alcúdia, Pollença and the east. Single fares are usually €2-6, and you pay less with a contactless bank card. The SFM train goes inland to Inca, Sa Pobla and Manacor. For the coves and the Tramuntana, a rental car (from about €25-50 a day out of season, much more in August) saves hours. Rent a small one, because village streets and mountain roads are narrow.

How much a trip to Mallorca costs

  • Shoestring: €60-80 per day. Hostel bed €35, menú del día at lunch, supermarket dinner, buses, free beaches.
  • Mid-range: €140-200 per day per person (sharing). A double hotel room, a sit-down dinner, a rental car split two ways, one paid sight a day.
  • Extras: the tourist tax of €1-4 per person per night depending on hotel category, beach sunbeds at about €15-25 for two, and the Sóller train at about €25.

Day trips from Mallorca

  • Menorca: the ferry from Port d'Alcúdia to Ciutadella takes about 1-2 hours. It's calmer and greener, and worth at least a long day.
  • Cabrera National Park: boats leave from Colònia de Sant Jordi. It's a protected archipelago with a castle and clear water, about 45 minutes each way.
  • Dragonera: a short boat ride from Sant Elm (about 20 minutes) to an uninhabited island with lighthouse walks and lots of lizards.
  • Ibiza: ferries from Palma take roughly 2-4 hours. Better as an overnight than a rushed day.

Common mistakes and scams

  • Car rental extras. Watch for "full to empty" fuel policies and hard upselling on insurance at the desk. Pick "full to full" and read the deposit terms.
  • Rosemary sellers near the cathedral. Women hand you a sprig "for luck", then ask for money. Just say no and keep walking.
  • Pickpockets on the A1 bus, around La Seu and in Sóller on busy train days.
  • Driving to Formentor in summer without checking the access restrictions. You'll be turned back.
  • Arriving at a cove at noon in August. No parking, no sand. Go early or late.
  • Drinking in the street in Playa de Palma and Magaluf. Fines exist and are enforced.

Mallorca FAQ

How many days do you need in Mallorca?

Six days lets you see Palma, the Tramuntana, the north and the east coast without rushing. With three days, stay in Palma and add one day trip to Sóller.

Do you need a car in Mallorca?

Not for Palma, Sóller or Valldemossa, which buses and the train cover well. For the coves and Formentor, a car makes life much easier.

What is the most beautiful part of Mallorca?

The Serra de Tramuntana, around Sóller and Deià, is the most striking. For beaches, the southeast coves and Es Trenc win.

Is Mallorca expensive?

Summer is pricey, especially hotels and car hire. Spring, autumn and winter are reasonable, and the menú del día keeps food costs down.

What is the best month to visit Mallorca?

June and September give you warm sea and fewer crowds than July and August. For hiking, go from March to May or in October.

Is Mallorca just party resorts?

No. The party scene is mostly limited to Magaluf and part of Playa de Palma. The rest of the island is villages, mountains and quiet beaches.

Can you swim in Mallorca in winter?

The sea drops to around 14-15°C from January to March, which is bracing. Most people swim comfortably from late May to October.
