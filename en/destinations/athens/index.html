Travel guide

Athens in 3 days: an honest travel guide to Greece's capital

🏨 Hotels in Athens

Why go to Athens

Athens is loud, a bit scruffy, and wonderful. Concrete apartment blocks sprawl in every direction, and then you turn a corner and the Parthenon is sitting above the rooftops like it's no big deal. The city rewards people who stay a few days, wander, and sit down for long dinners. It is also one of the cheaper capitals in Western Europe, which is why it appears so often in flight deals.

Three days is the sweet spot for the city itself. Add a day or two if you want a coastal trip or an island.

What to see: the places worth your time

  • The Acropolis and the Parthenon. Obvious, and still the best thing in the city. Arrive when the gates open at 8:00 or in the last hour before closing. Midday in summer is hot, slow and crowded. Book your ticket online (around €30 in high season) and wear shoes with grip, because the marble is polished slick.
  • Acropolis Museum. At the foot of the hill, and far better than its glass-box exterior suggests. The top floor shows the Parthenon frieze at the same orientation as the temple itself. Go after your Acropolis visit so it all clicks.
  • Ancient Agora. Less famous, much calmer. The Temple of Hephaestus is among the best-preserved Greek temples anywhere, and the grounds have shade, which you will appreciate in July.
  • Plaka and Anafiotika. Plaka is touristy, but walk up into Anafiotika, a tiny cluster of whitewashed houses that feels like a Cycladic island dropped under the Acropolis. Go early in the morning.
  • Monastiraki and the flea market. Part souvenir tat, part genuine junk-shop treasure. The square is the city's crossroads, and the rooftop bars look straight at the Acropolis.
  • Mount Lycabettus. The highest point in central Athens. Take the funicular or walk up through the pines, then watch the sun go down over the whole basin and out to the sea.
  • Panathenaic Stadium. An all-marble stadium that hosted the first modern Olympics in 1896. Quick visit, good photos, and you can run a lap of the track.
  • Temple of Olympian Zeus. Fifteen surviving columns beside a busy road. Worth it for the scale, though you only need half an hour.
  • National Archaeological Museum. Heavy but brilliant: the Mask of Agamemnon, the bronze Poseidon, the Cycladic figures. Pick two or three halls rather than trying everything.
  • Philopappou Hill. Free, quiet and often overlooked. The path to the top gives you the classic view of the Acropolis from the west.

Things to do beyond the ruins

Do a food walk around the Central Market on Athinas Street, where the meat hall is loud and not for the squeamish. Spend an evening in Psyrri, where small bars and mezedes places fill the lanes. Catch the changing of the guard at Syntagma Square on Sunday morning, when the full ceremonial version takes place. Take the tram down to the coast for a swim at the Athens Riviera, which is not Mykonos but is perfectly good on a hot afternoon.

For a half-day trip, Cape Sounion and the Temple of Poseidon at sunset is the classic. Buses run from the centre, and the light over the Aegean is worth the ride.

How many days and a suggested route

Day 1: Acropolis at opening, Acropolis Museum, lunch in Koukaki, then Anafiotika and Plaka. Finish with dinner in Psyrri.

Day 2: Ancient Agora in the morning, Monastiraki market, the Central Market, then the National Archaeological Museum. Sunset on Lycabettus.

Day 3: Panathenaic Stadium, Temple of Olympian Zeus, then a slow afternoon in Pangrati or Kolonaki. Or swap in Sounion for a coastal day.

Two days works if you're rushing. A fourth day is best spent on a day trip to Delphi or a ferry from Piraeus to Aegina or Hydra.

When to go

April to early June and September to October are the best windows: warm, bright, and comfortable for climbing. July and August regularly hit 35 to 40°C, and the Acropolis can close in the hottest hours during heatwaves. If you go in summer, plan sightseeing for early morning and evening.

Winter is mild, around 10 to 15°C, with rain and grey spells, but also much shorter queues and cheaper flights and rooms. It is a fine choice if you care more about the ruins than the beach.

Where to stay

  • Koukaki: Residential, walkable to the Acropolis, with good cafés and a local feel. Best all-round base for first-timers.
  • Plaka and Monastiraki: Most central and atmospheric, but noisy on weekends and pricier for what you get.
  • Psyrri: For people who want nightlife outside the door. Not ideal for light sleepers.
  • Kolonaki: Polished, upmarket, good for boutique hotels and a calmer evening.
  • Exarchia: Student, alternative and cheap, with a counter-culture edge. Fine if you know what you're signing up for.
  • Pangrati: Quieter and local, a short walk from the stadium. Good value.

As a rough guide, hostel beds start around €20 to €35, decent mid-range hotels sit around €80 to €140, and rooms with Acropolis views cost more, especially in summer.

What to eat and where

Start with souvlaki or gyros, and know the difference: souvlaki is skewered meat, gyros is shaved from a rotating spit. Both come in pita with tomato, onion and tzatziki, and both cost a few euros. Try spanakopita from a bakery for breakfast, or a koulouri sesame ring from a street vendor. Sit down properly for moussaka, horiatiki (the real Greek salad, with no lettuce), grilled octopus and fried zucchini balls.

For dessert, loukoumades are hot honey doughnuts, usually with cinnamon and walnuts. Coffee means a freddo espresso or freddo cappuccino in summer, and Greek coffee in the evening.

Eat around Psyrri, Koukaki and Pangrati for better value than the main Plaka strip, where waiters on the street try to pull you in. If a restaurant has someone waving a menu at you, keep walking. Dinner is late: many locals sit down at 9 pm.

Getting from the airport and getting around

Athens International (Eleftherios Venizelos) is about 35 to 40 km from the centre. Your options:

  • Metro Line 3: Direct to Syntagma and Monastiraki in about 40 minutes. A single ticket costs around €9.
  • X95 bus: Runs to Syntagma, 24 hours a day, around €6. Slower in traffic.
  • Taxi: A fixed fare applies to the centre, roughly €40 by day and a bit more at night. Check the current rates when you land.

In the city, the compact core is very walkable, though hills and heat can wear you down. The metro is clean and cheap, with a basic ticket around €1.20 valid for 90 minutes. The tram connects to the coast. Taxis are affordable, but agree on the meter or use a ride-hailing app.

Rough daily budget

  • Budget: about €60 to €80, with a hostel, street food and walking.
  • Mid-range: about €120 to €180, with a nice hotel, sit-down meals and entry tickets.
  • Comfortable: €250 and up, with a boutique stay, wine with dinner and a few taxis.

Ticket combos covering several archaeological sites usually save money if you plan to see four or five. Check current prices before you go.

FAQs

Is three days enough for Athens?

Yes, for the main sights at a relaxed pace. Add a day if you want Sounion, Delphi or an island.

Is Athens safe for tourists?

Generally yes. Watch for pickpockets on the metro and around Monastiraki, and keep your phone out of sight in crowds.

Do I need to book the Acropolis in advance?

It's wise, especially from April to October. Booking online saves you time in the ticket queue.

Can I drink the tap water?

Yes, it's safe, though many visitors prefer bottled water for the taste.

Do I need cash?

Cards are widely accepted, but carry some euros for bakeries, small tavernas, kiosks and taxis.
