Abu Dhabi itinerary: what to see in 3 days

Abu Dhabi is the calmer, richer and more grown-up sibling of Dubai. There is less shouting from the skyline and more space: wide boulevards, a long seafront Corniche, mangroves on the edge of town and a handful of world-class cultural sites. The city is spread across islands, so you will spend more time in taxis than on foot. Distances that look short on a map are not walkable in the heat.

It suits travellers who want architecture, museums and beaches without Dubai's frantic energy, plus families heading for the Yas Island theme parks. Three days is the right amount: one for the mosque and the old centre, one for Saadiyat Island's museums and beach, and one for Yas Island or the mangroves. A fourth day only makes sense if you want a day trip to Al Ain or Dubai.

What to see in Abu Dhabi: the places worth your time

Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque

This is the one place you cannot skip. It is a vast white marble mosque with 82 domes, one of the world's largest hand-knotted carpets and chandeliers heavy with Swarovski crystal. Entry is free, but you need to book a time slot online beforehand. Allow 1.5-2 hours. Our tip: go about an hour before sunset, so you see the marble turn gold and then the blue lighting come on. Bring your own modest clothing (long sleeves, long trousers or skirt, and a headscarf for women) to avoid queueing for the loan abayas.

Louvre Abu Dhabi

Jean Nouvel's museum on Saadiyat Island sits under a huge perforated dome that creates a "rain of light" over the galleries. The collection runs chronologically across civilisations, which makes more sense than it sounds, and includes loans from French museums. Tickets cost about AED 63 (around €16). Allow 2-3 hours. Arrive at opening, walk the galleries first, then sit by the water under the dome when the light is strongest.

Qasr Al Watan

The working presidential palace opens to visitors, and it is grand in a very deliberate way: a white granite dome, a library of Arabic manuscripts and the Great Hall. Tickets are about AED 65 (€16). Allow around 2 hours. It sits close to the Emirates Palace, so you can pair the two. Skip the evening light show unless you are staying nearby, as getting back is a long taxi ride.

Qasr Al Hosn

This is the oldest stone building in the city, a fort that began as a watchtower guarding a freshwater well. It is now a well-presented museum on how a pearling village became a capital. Entry is about AED 30 (€8) and you need about an hour. The site also includes the Cultural Foundation and House of Artisans, where you can watch traditional weaving. It is the closest thing downtown has to an "old town".

The Corniche

The Corniche is an 8 km seafront promenade with cycle paths, lawns and lifeguarded public beaches (some sections are family-only). It is free, apart from a small fee for certain beach areas. The best time is early morning or after sunset. Rent a bike from the docking stations and ride the whole length rather than walking it in the heat.

Emirates Palace

This gold-trimmed hotel at the western end of the Corniche is as much a sight as a place to sleep. You can wander the lobby for free if you are dressed smartly. The famous gold-flake cappuccino costs about AED 70 (€18) and is pure novelty. Allow 30-45 minutes. Have a look, then spend your money elsewhere.

Abrahamic Family House

On Saadiyat, this complex holds a mosque, a church and a synagogue designed by David Adjaye, three cubes of the same footprint. Entry is free with online booking, and a visit takes about an hour. The architecture is the main draw, so go in the late afternoon when the shadows are sharp. Dress as you would for the Grand Mosque.

Saadiyat Beach

This is the best beach in Abu Dhabi: long, pale and with water calm enough for swimming most of the year. Sea turtles nest here, and you may spot dolphins offshore. The public section costs about AED 25 (€6) and includes sunbeds. Spend a half day here after the Louvre.

Mangrove National Park

The Eastern Mangroves, just off the city centre, are best seen by kayak or paddleboard. You glide through quiet channels with herons, crabs and occasional turtles, with the skyline behind you. A guided 90-minute kayak tour costs about AED 150-200 (€38-50). Book an early-morning or sunset slot and check the tides, because low water makes some channels impassable.

Yas Island

Yas Island is the city's entertainment district: Ferrari World (with one of the world's fastest roller coasters), Warner Bros. World, Yas Waterworld, SeaWorld Yas Island and the Yas Marina Formula 1 circuit. A single park ticket runs about AED 300-350 (€75-90). Each park is a full day. Buy multi-park passes online in advance, because gate prices are noticeably higher.

Al Mina Fish Market and Iranian Souk

At Mina Zayed port, fishermen sell hammour, prawns and crab off ice. Next door there is a hall where they will clean and grill your fish for a small fee. The Iranian Souk nearby sells pots, spices and plastic everything. Entry is free and you need about an hour. Go in the morning, when the catch is fresh and before the heat sets in.

Heritage Village

This is a small recreated village on the Breakwater with a palm-frond house, a falconry display some days and craft workshops. It is free and takes about 45 minutes. It is a bit stagey, but the view back across the water to the Corniche skyline is the real reason to come at dusk.

Things to do in Abu Dhabi

Abu Dhabi is better at experiences than at checklists. These are the ones worth your money:

  • Desert safari: dune bashing, sandboarding and a camp dinner. Expect about AED 200-350 (€50-90). Choose a smaller operator that skips the belly-dance package.
  • Abu Dhabi Falcon Hospital: a genuinely interesting guided tour of a working bird hospital. Booking is required and it costs about AED 170 (€43).
  • Cycle Hudayriyat Island: flat, well-built tracks with sea views and bike rental on site. Go at dawn.
  • Watch a race at Yas Marina Circuit: the F1 weekend in late November or early December is expensive. Ordinary days offer track tours and drive experiences.
  • Karak chai crawl: sweet spiced tea from roadside cafeterias costs about AED 1-2 a cup. It is the city's real social glue.
  • Dhow cruise along the Corniche: a cheesy but pleasant evening dinner cruise for about AED 150-250 (€38-63).
  • Sunset from Observation Deck at 300 in Etihad Towers: high tea with a view over the Corniche. Check the current price, which usually includes some food and drink credit.

Abu Dhabi for free (or almost)

The two best sights in town cost nothing, which is not something you can say about Dubai.

  • Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque (free, book online)
  • Abrahamic Family House (free, book online)
  • The Corniche and its public beach areas
  • Heritage Village and the Breakwater skyline view
  • The Emirates Palace lobby (dress smartly)
  • Al Mina Fish Market and the Iranian Souk
  • Mall air-conditioning, especially Marina Mall and The Galleria on Al Maryah Island, with a waterfront promenade
  • Public buses at about AED 2 (€0.50) base fare

Abu Dhabi itinerary: 3 days

Day 1: The old city and the Corniche

Morning: Start at Al Mina Fish Market and the Iranian Souk, then go to Qasr Al Hosn while it is still cool. Afternoon: Eat lunch at a downtown Lebanese or Indian cafeteria, then visit Qasr Al Watan, followed by a quick look at Emirates Palace next door. Evening: Ride a bike along the Corniche at sunset, then head to Heritage Village for the skyline view.

Day 2: Saadiyat Island

Morning: Be at the Louvre Abu Dhabi at opening. Afternoon: Spend the hottest hours at Saadiyat public beach. Evening: Visit the Abrahamic Family House in late light, then take a 25-minute taxi to the Grand Mosque for your booked pre-sunset slot. Eat dinner at Souk Al Qanater (Souk Qaryat Al Beri) across the creek, with mosque views.

Day 3: Mangroves and Yas Island

Morning: Do a sunrise kayak in Mangrove National Park. Afternoon: Choose one Yas Island theme park, or Yas Waterworld in summer. Evening: Have dinner at Yas Bay waterfront, or catch a sunset cycle session on Yas Marina Circuit if it runs that night (check in advance).

What is overrated (and what to do instead)

  • The gold cappuccino at Emirates Palace. You pay for a photo. Instead, walk through the lobby, then have karak chai on the Corniche.
  • Visiting the Grand Mosque at midday. The marble glares, the crowds peak and the heat is brutal. Book a slot for the last hour of daylight instead.
  • Doing every theme park. Ferrari World's main ride lasts under a minute and the queues can be long. One park is enough for most adults. Pick Waterworld if it is hot.
  • Doing Dubai as a "quick" day trip from here. It takes 1.5-2 hours each way in traffic. If Dubai matters to you, give it its own nights.

When to visit Abu Dhabi

November to March is the best time to visit, with days around 24-30°C and pleasant evenings. This is also high season: hotel prices jump, especially around the F1 Grand Prix and New Year. April and October are a good compromise, hot but manageable, with better rates.

From June to September, temperatures pass 40°C with heavy humidity, and outdoor sightseeing becomes miserable by 10am. It is, however, the cheapest time of year, and luxury hotels drop dramatically, so it works if you plan around pools and museums. During Ramadan, the city is quieter by day and lively after iftar, and you should not eat or drink in public during daylight.

Where to stay in Abu Dhabi: best areas

Pick your area based on what you will do most, because taxis between islands add up.

  • Corniche and downtown (Al Markaziyah, Tourist Club Area/Al Zahiyah): These are the most central areas, with buses and cheap food. They suit first-timers on a budget. Expect about €60-130 a night.
  • Saadiyat Island: This is the area for beach resorts next to the Louvre, and it suits couples and splurgers. Expect €200-500 or more.
  • Yas Island: These hotels sit next to the parks and circuit. It is ideal for families, but feels isolated from the city. Expect €90-250.
  • Al Maryah and Al Reem Islands: These are modern towers with the Galleria mall and a waterfront walk, a good mid-range business-style base. Expect €100-200.
  • Between the Bridges / Khor Al Maqta: This area has resorts near the Grand Mosque and Souk Al Qanater, though it is far from everything else. Expect €100-220.

What to eat in Abu Dhabi

Emirati food is harder to find than Lebanese or Indian, but it is worth seeking out. Order machboos (spiced rice with lamb, chicken or fish), harees (slow-cooked wheat and meat), balaleet (sweet vermicelli with omelette, for breakfast) and luqaimat (fried dough balls drizzled with date syrup). Dedicated Emirati restaurants and hotel Ramadan buffets are the easiest places to try them.

The everyday food of the city comes from its South Asian and Levantine communities. Shawarma stands, Kerala parotta and curry, Pakistani karahi and Lebanese grills are everywhere and excellent. At Al Mina, buy fresh fish and have it grilled next door for one of the best-value meals in town.

  • Cafeteria lunch (shawarma, biryani, chai): about AED 15-35 (€4-9)
  • Mid-range dinner at a Lebanese or Emirati restaurant: about AED 80-150 (€20-38) per person
  • Hotel restaurant dinner with wine: AED 300 (€75) and up. Alcohol is only served in licensed venues, mostly hotels, and it is expensive.

Airport to city centre and getting around

Zayed International Airport is about 30-40 minutes from the Corniche. A taxi costs about AED 90-120 (€23-30). Official airport taxis are metered, and Uber and Careem work well too. Public buses (lines A1 and A2) connect the airport with the city for a few dirhams and take about an hour.

The city has no metro. Taxis are the default and are reasonably priced, with most cross-town rides costing AED 25-50 (€6-13). Public buses are cheap and air-conditioned. You pay with a rechargeable Hafilat card (bought at the bus station or at machines) and the Darb app helps with routes. A hop-on hop-off bus exists, but taxis are usually better value for a group. Car hire is easy if you plan day trips, but parking downtown is tight.

How much a trip to Abu Dhabi costs

  • Shoestring: about €70-100/day (AED 280-400). This covers a budget downtown hotel or shared room, cafeteria meals, buses and free sights (mosque, Corniche, Abrahamic Family House), with one paid museum.
  • Mid-range: about €180-280/day (AED 720-1,100). This covers a 4-star hotel, restaurant dinners, taxis everywhere, the Louvre plus Qasr Al Watan, a kayak tour and one theme park day over the trip.
  • Extras: a desert safari costs €50-90, and a beer in a hotel bar costs €10-14.

Day trips from Abu Dhabi

  • Al Ain: A green oasis city with a UNESCO-listed palm grove, Al Jahili Fort and Jebel Hafeet mountain. It takes about 1.5-2 hours by car, or 2 hours by intercity bus from the main bus station for about AED 25 (€6). Take a full day.
  • Dubai: It is about 1.5 hours by bus (around AED 25) or by car. Go for the Burj Khalifa, Al Fahidi and the creek abra ride, but ideally spend a night there.
  • Liwa Oasis: This is home to some of the highest dunes in the world, including Moreeb Dune, on the edge of the Empty Quarter. It is about 2.5-3 hours by car. Go with a tour or a 4x4, and stay overnight if you can.
  • Sir Bani Yas Island: This wildlife island has a reserve with oryx, giraffes and cheetahs. It is a long drive west plus a boat transfer, so it is best as an overnight stay at one of its resorts.

Common mistakes and scams

Abu Dhabi is very safe, and outright scams are rare. Most problems come from underestimating the heat and the distances.

  • Planning to walk between sights. The Louvre to the Grand Mosque is about 25 minutes by car. Budget for taxis.
  • Turning up at the Grand Mosque without a booking or the right clothes. You may wait or be refused entry.
  • Unlicensed "taxis" and touts at the airport. Only use metered cabs or ride-hailing apps.
  • Overpriced desert safaris sold by hotel desks. Compare prices online, and check what the price includes (transfers, dinner, sandboarding).
  • Breaking local rules without realising. Public drunkenness, public displays of affection and photographing people (especially women) without asking can get you into real trouble.
  • Booking dates during the F1 weekend by accident. Hotel prices can triple.

Abu Dhabi FAQ

How many days do you need in Abu Dhabi?

Three days covers the main sights: the Grand Mosque, Saadiyat Island's museums and beach, and Yas Island or the mangroves. Add a fourth day for Al Ain or Dubai.

Is Abu Dhabi worth visiting if I've been to Dubai?

Yes. It is more cultural and relaxed, and the Grand Mosque and Louvre Abu Dhabi have no real equivalent in Dubai.

Is the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque free?

Yes, entry is free, but you must book a time slot online and follow the dress code.

What should I wear in Abu Dhabi?

Beachwear is fine at beaches and pools. Elsewhere, cover your shoulders and knees, especially in malls, government buildings and religious sites.

Can you drink alcohol in Abu Dhabi?

Yes, in licensed hotel bars and restaurants. Drinking in public is illegal.

Is there a metro in Abu Dhabi?

No. You get around by taxi, Uber or Careem, and by public bus using a Hafilat card.

What is the cheapest time to visit Abu Dhabi?

June to August has the lowest hotel prices, but temperatures regularly exceed 40°C.

Is Abu Dhabi safe for tourists?

Very safe, including for solo women. The main risks are the heat and the traffic.
