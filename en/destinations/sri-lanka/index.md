# Sri Lanka travel guide: where to go, costs and best time

> Sri Lanka travel guide by Happy Low Cost, 9-day itinerary. Original page: https://www.happylowcost.com/en/destinations/sri-lanka/
> Updated: 2026-10-21

## Why go to Sri Lanka

Sri Lanka is small, roughly the size of Ireland, yet in a single week you can climb a rock fortress at sunrise, watch elephants at a waterhole, ride a train through tea country and end up on a surf beach. Distances look short on the map and feel long on the road, which is the one thing to plan around. The people are famously helpful, the food is deeply spiced and cheap, and flights are often a bargain outside peak season.

## What to see: the places worth your time

- **Sigiriya Rock:** a 5th-century palace-fortress on top of a sheer rock. Go at opening time, before the heat and the tour groups. Foreign visitors pay a steep ticket, around US$35, and it is still worth it. Pidurangala, the neighbouring rock, is cheaper and gives you the best view of Sigiriya itself.

- **Dambulla Cave Temples:** five caves of painted ceilings and rows of Buddhas. Shoulders and knees covered, shoes off.

- **Polonnaruwa:** the medieval capital, best seen by bicycle. The Gal Vihara rock carvings are the highlight.

- **Anuradhapura:** older and more spread out, with huge dagobas and a sacred bo tree. It is a pilgrimage site, so expect worshippers in white.

- **Kandy:** the Temple of the Tooth, the lake and the Peradeniya Botanical Gardens. The evening puja at the temple is crowded but atmospheric.

- **Ella and the hill country:** Little Adam's Peak is an easy morning walk, Ella Rock a tougher one, and the Nine Arches Bridge is a short stroll from town. Nearby Nuwara Eliya has tea estates and a faintly colonial air.

- **Horton Plains and World's End:** a cool plateau walk of around 9 km. Arrive early, because the cliff view is usually lost in cloud by mid-morning.

- **Galle Fort:** a Dutch-era walled town on the south coast. Walk the ramparts at sunset, then wander the lanes of cafés and small galleries.

- **Yala and Udawalawe national parks:** Yala is the place for leopards but gets busy with jeeps. Udawalawe is better for elephants and is calmer.

- **Colombo:** not a showpiece, but good for a day. Try Pettah market, Galle Face Green at dusk and the cafés of Colombo 7.

## Things to do

Take the train from Kandy to Ella. It takes about six to seven hours through tea slopes, tunnels and little stations, and it is one of the great cheap train rides anywhere. Reserve seats ahead in high season, or take an unreserved third-class carriage and stand at the open door like everyone else.

On the south coast, go whale watching from Mirissa between roughly November and April. Blue whales are possible, though sightings are never guaranteed, and it is worth choosing an operator that keeps a respectful distance. Surfers should look at Weligama for beginners and Arugam Bay on the east coast for the April to October season.

Book a cooking class in the hills, and do a tea factory tour in Nuwara Eliya or around Ella. If your timing is right, the August to October elephant gathering at Minneriya is a spectacle.

## How many days and a suggested route

Ten days is the comfortable minimum for a first visit, and two weeks is better. With less than a week, pick one region instead of trying to cross the island.

- **Days 1-2:** arrive, a short stay in Colombo or straight to Negombo near the airport if you land late.

- **Days 3-4:** Sigiriya, Dambulla and Polonnaruwa, the Cultural Triangle.

- **Day 5:** Kandy.

- **Days 6-8:** train to Ella, with a stop in Nuwara Eliya if you like cooler air.

- **Day 9:** a safari at Udawalawe or Yala on the way south.

- **Days 10-12:** Galle, Mirissa or Unawatuna for the beach.

- **Days 13-14:** back up the Expressway to Colombo and the airport.

Do not underestimate road times. A 100 km drive in the hills can take three hours or more.

## When to go: weather and seasons

Sri Lanka has two monsoons, so there is always a dry coast somewhere. The southwest monsoon brings rain to the west and south coasts and the hills from roughly May to September. The northeast monsoon hits the east and north from about October to January.

The classic window for the west and south coast, Colombo and the hills is December to March. April to September is the time for the east coast, including Arugam Bay and Trincomalee. The Cultural Triangle is hot and dry in the middle of the year and wetter in October and November. The hills can be damp and cool at any time, so pack a light rain jacket. Check the current rules for the ETA, the electronic travel authorisation most visitors need, before you book.

## Where to stay by area and budget

### Colombo

Fort is handy for trains and the old centre, while Colombo 7 (Cinnamon Gardens) is greener and has the better restaurants. Budget guesthouses cost about US$25-40, and good mid-range hotels are not much more.

### Kandy

Stay on the hillsides above the lake for quiet and views. Family-run guesthouses are good value, and there are some lovely old villas at the upper end.

### Sigiriya and Dambulla

Choose a small lodge or homestay among the paddies and tanks, with a view of the rock if possible. Mid-range eco-lodges are plentiful here.

### Ella

The town is basic, noisy at night and very popular. The guesthouses on the ridges are quieter and often have better views and breakfasts. Book ahead from December to March.

### South coast

Galle Fort has boutique hotels at a price. Unawatuna, Weligama and Mirissa suit surfers, backpackers and anyone who wants a beach lunch and a cheap room by the sea.

## What to eat and where

Rice and curry is the staple: a mound of rice with dhal, a few vegetable curries, pol sambol (coconut with chilli and lime) and often fish or chicken. It is usually cheap and generous, and the best versions come from small family places at lunch. Breakfast is hoppers (appa), a crisp bowl-shaped pancake, often with an egg in the middle, or string hoppers with dhal and sambol.

For a late-night snack look for kottu roti, chopped flatbread stir-fried loudly on a griddle with vegetables, egg or meat. You will hear it before you see it. In Colombo, try lamprais, rice and curry baked in a banana leaf. On the coast, ask for grilled fish and the sour, peppery fish curry known as ambul thiyal. End with watalappan, a coconut and jaggery custard, and a pot of real Ceylon tea. Street food is generally fine if it is hot and busy.

## Getting from the airport and getting around

Bandaranaike International Airport (CMB) is at Katunayake, about 35 km north of Colombo and close to Negombo. A taxi, either pre-booked at the arrivals desk or through the PickMe app, is the easy way in. The Expressway makes the trip to Colombo about 45 minutes outside rush hour. Buses to Colombo are very cheap but slow with luggage.

Within the country, trains are cheap and scenic but slow, and buses are cheaper still, fast, and an adventure. Tuk-tuks are everywhere for short trips, so agree the price before you climb in, or use an app. For long hops many visitors hire a car with a driver, usually around US$50-80 a day with fuel, and it is a sensible option given the road conditions. Scooter hire is common on the south coast, but traffic is chaotic, so only ride if you have experience.

## Rough daily budget

- **Backpacker:** about US$30-45 a day for a dorm or basic guesthouse, local meals, buses and trains.

- **Mid-range:** about US$70-120 for comfortable rooms, a mix of restaurants and the odd driver.

- **Comfortable:** US$150 and up for boutique hotels, private drivers and safaris.

The big extras are park entry, safari jeeps and the Sigiriya ticket. These are the costs that surprise people, so budget for them on top.

## FAQ

### Do I need a visa for Sri Lanka?

Most visitors need an ETA, applied for online before travel. Rules and fees change, so check the official government site.

### Is Sri Lanka safe for tourists?

Generally yes. Use normal precautions, watch your belongings on crowded trains, and check your government's travel advice before you go.

### What is the best month to visit?

February is a safe choice for the west, south and hills. For the east coast, aim for May to August.

### Can I drink the tap water?

No. Stick to bottled or filtered water, and many guesthouses have refill stations that cut down on plastic.

### Is two weeks enough?

Yes, it is enough for the main sights and a few beach days at a relaxed pace. Even ten days works if you keep the route simple.
