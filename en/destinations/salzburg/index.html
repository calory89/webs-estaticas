Why go to Salzburg

Salzburg is small enough to walk end to end in twenty minutes, and it still feels like a capital. Baroque domes, a fortress on a rock, the Salzach river and the Alps just behind the last tram stop. Yes, there is a lot of Mozart and a lot of Julie Andrews, but the old town has real texture once you step off the main lanes. It is also an easy add-on to Munich, Vienna or the Lakes of the Salzkammergut, which is why flight deals into the region are worth watching.

What to see: the places worth your time

  • Hohensalzburg Fortress. One of the largest medieval castles in Central Europe, and the reason the skyline looks the way it does. Take the funicular up, then walk down through the Nonnberg side. Go first thing or late afternoon to avoid the tour groups.
  • Mirabell Palace and Gardens. Free to wander. The view of the fortress behind the formal flowerbeds is the classic photo, and the Pegasus fountain and the steps from The Sound of Music are here too.
  • Getreidegasse. The famous shopping lane with wrought-iron guild signs, even on the global-brand shops. Crowded by 11:00, lovely at 8:30.
  • Mozart's Birthplace. The yellow house at number 9. Worth it if you care about the music; the violin and clavichord are the highlights. Otherwise, the facade does the job.
  • Salzburg Cathedral and Residenzplatz. The Dom is calm and bright inside, and the square outside is where the Christmas market sets up in Advent.
  • St. Peter's Abbey and cemetery. Old, shaded and quiet. The catacombs cut into the Mönchsberg rock are a short, atmospheric detour.
  • Mönchsberg. Take the lift up from the old town and walk along the ridge for the best views over the rooftops. The Museum der Moderne sits at the top, with a terrace café.
  • Hellbrunn Palace. A short ride south of the centre. The trick fountains are silly, soaking and genuinely fun, especially if you have a bit of a sense of humour about guided tours.
  • Kapuzinerberg. The wooded hill across the river. Fewer people, a steep path, and a different angle on the city.

Things to do

Book a concert in a historic room, whether it is a chamber evening in the fortress or something in the Mozarteum. It is touristy, but done well. Walk the Salzach at dusk from the Makartsteg bridge, where the padlocks hang. Rent a bike and follow the river path south toward Hellbrunn, flat and easy.

If you want the film, a Sound of Music tour covers locations around the city and out toward the lakes, and it is oddly good fun with a decent guide. Otherwise skip it and visit the places on your own.

For a day trip, the Werfen ice caves (Eisriesenwelt) are dramatic and cold even in summer. Hallstatt is beautiful and swamped; go early or go in the off-season. Berchtesgaden and the Königssee lie just across the German border and are easy by bus.

How many days and a suggested route

Two full days cover the old town properly. Three lets you add Hellbrunn and a slower pace, or a day trip. One day is possible if you are passing through, but you will rush the fortress.

Day one: Mirabell Gardens at opening time, cross the river, walk Getreidegasse before the crowds, then Residenzplatz and the Cathedral. Lunch in the old town. Afternoon at the fortress, back down by dusk for dinner.

Day two: St. Peter's and the cemetery, the Mönchsberg lift, a walk along the ridge, then a late lunch near Mülln. Finish with Kapuzinerberg or the riverside at sunset.

Day three, if you have it: Hellbrunn in the morning, then a lake or cave trip.

When to go: weather and seasons

Summer is warm, usually mid-20s °C, but rain is part of Salzburg's personality and arrives often, even in July. The Salzburg Festival runs from late July through August, and prices and crowds both rise. Spring and early autumn are the sweet spot: softer light, fewer people, easier hotel rates.

Winter is cold, often below freezing, and the Advent markets are charming, with mulled wine and roasted chestnuts. It is also the busiest and priciest time for rooms. January and February are quiet and cheap, with short days.

Where to stay by area and budget

  • Altstadt (old town). Everything on your doorstep, rooms tend to be small and pricey. Good if you want to walk out at 7:00 into empty streets.
  • Right bank, around Linzer Gasse and Mirabell. Slightly cheaper, still a five-to-ten-minute walk to the old town. A good mid-range option.
  • Near the main station (Hauptbahnhof). The best value for budget travellers, with easy train and bus connections. Not pretty, but practical.
  • Mülln and Nonntal. Quieter neighbourhoods with local cafés and a more lived-in feel, a short walk from the centre.

Hostels and basic guesthouses sit at the low end, family-run pensions and mid-range hotels in the middle, and grand riverside hotels at the top. Book early for festival weeks and Advent.

What to eat and where

Try Salzburger Nockerl, a huge, airy, sweet soufflé meant for sharing. Käsespätzle (cheesy egg noodles with crispy onions) is the best comfort dish after a long walk. Wiener Schnitzel is everywhere, and Kaiserschmarrn, a torn pancake with fruit compote, makes a good late-afternoon stop.

For a fast, cheap bite, find a Bosna, a bratwurst-style sausage in a toasted roll with onions, curry powder and mustard. It is a local snack and costs very little. Buy a Mozartkugel from Fürst, the original producer, rather than the foil-wrapped souvenirs.

For atmosphere, go to the Augustiner Bräu Müllner Bräustübl, a big monastery brewery with beer in stone mugs, chestnut-shaded gardens, and stalls where you can pick up food. Coffee-wise, Café Tomaselli on Alter Markt is a classic, if a bit formal. For a more historic dinner, St. Peter Stiftskulinarium is one of Europe's oldest restaurants.

Getting from the airport and getting around

Salzburg Airport (W. A. Mozart) is about 5 km from the centre. Trolleybus line 2 runs to the main station and into the city centre, and a taxi takes roughly fifteen minutes. Many travellers also fly into Munich and take the train, around ninety minutes to two hours. Vienna to Salzburg by Railjet takes around two and a half hours.

Within the city, you walk. The old town is compact, the sights are close, and the fortress, Mönchsberg and Mirabell connect on foot. Buses and trolleybuses cover the rest, including Hellbrunn. If you plan to see many paid sights, the Salzburg Card bundles entries and transport and is worth calculating before you buy.

Rough daily budget

  • Budget: about €70-100 a day, with a hostel or simple room, Bosna and bakery lunches, a pub dinner and one paid sight.
  • Mid-range: about €150-220, with a comfortable hotel, café stops, a proper dinner and a few entry tickets.
  • Comfortable: €250 and up, with a central hotel, restaurant meals and concerts.

Prices jump during the festival and at Christmas. Rough figures only, so check current rates before you book.

FAQ about Salzburg

Is Salzburg worth visiting if I am not into Mozart or classical music?

Yes. The old town, the fortress, the river, the mountains and the food stand on their own. Music is a bonus, not a requirement.

Is two days enough?

For the main sights, yes. Add a third day if you want Hellbrunn, a day trip, or a slower pace.

Is Salzburg expensive?

It is pricier than most of Central Europe, especially for old-town hotels. Stay near the station, eat at casual spots and walk a lot, and it becomes manageable.

Can I visit Salzburg as a day trip from Munich or Vienna?

You can, but it makes for a long day. Munich is closer, at around ninety minutes to two hours each way. An overnight stay is much better.

What is the best time of year to visit?

Late spring and early autumn offer mild weather and fewer crowds. December is the most atmospheric, though also the busiest and costliest.
