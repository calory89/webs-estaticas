# Things to Do in Seattle: 3 Days of Markets and Mountains

> Seattle travel guide by Happy Low Cost, 3-day itinerary. Original page: https://www.happylowcost.com/en/destinations/seattle/
> Updated: 2026-11-21

Seattle is a hilly, water-wrapped city where the best views cost nothing and the most famous attractions cost a lot. It's a working port and a tech town at the same time: Amazon's campus sits a short walk from fish stalls that have been there for a century, and on clear days Mount Rainier appears over the skyline. It also rains a lot, though mostly as a steady drizzle, and nobody carries an umbrella.

The city suits people who like food, ferries, coffee and walking up steep streets more than those who want blockbuster sights. **Three days is the right amount**: two for the city itself and one for a ferry or a trip to the mountains. If you only have a stopover, one full day covers Pike Place, the waterfront and a viewpoint. Budget-wise, be ready: Seattle is one of the most expensive cities in the US, especially for hotels.

## What to see in Seattle: the places worth your time

### Pike Place Market

The city's public market since 1907 is a maze of fish stalls, flower sellers, bakeries and odd little shops on several levels above the waterfront. It's free to wander and you need about two hours, more if you eat your way through it. **Our tip:** arrive by 9am. By late morning, especially on cruise days from May to September, the main arcade is shoulder to shoulder. Go down to the lower levels, which most visitors never reach.

### The Seattle Waterfront and Elliott Bay

Once the elevated Alaskan Way Viaduct came down, the waterfront was rebuilt with a promenade, and the Overlook Walk now links Pike Place directly to the water. It's free and pleasant for an hour's stroll. Skip the souvenir shops on the piers and look at the views across to the Olympic Mountains instead. The Seattle Aquarium (about €35-40 / $40-45) is fine for kids, but not essential otherwise.

### Space Needle

The 1962 World's Fair tower is the city's icon, with a glass floor that rotates and glass panels on the deck. Tickets run about €35-45 ($40-50) depending on time and day, and you'll spend around an hour up there. It's good, but the irony is that the Space Needle is the one thing you can't see from it. Book a timed slot online and aim for sunset only if the forecast is clear.

### Chihuly Garden and Glass

Right next to the Needle, this museum shows Dale Chihuly's huge, colourful glass sculptures indoors and in a garden. Allow about an hour, and expect to pay roughly €30-35 ($35-40), or less with a combo ticket for the Needle. It's the most photogenic museum in the city. The glasshouse looks best in late afternoon light.

### Museum of Pop Culture (MoPOP)

Frank Gehry's crumpled metal building at Seattle Center covers music, sci-fi and gaming, and has a large section on Nirvana and Jimi Hendrix, both local. Plan for 2 hours at about €30-35 ($35-40). Worth it for music fans. If you're not one, the outside of the building is the best part.

### Kerry Park

This small viewpoint on the south slope of Queen Anne Hill gives the postcard view: the Space Needle, downtown and, on a clear day, Mount Rainier behind it. It's free and takes 20 minutes. Go at sunset or after dark. Bus 2 or 13 gets you close, or walk up from Seattle Center if your legs can handle the hill.

### Ballard Locks

The Hiram M. Chittenden Locks lift boats between saltwater Puget Sound and the freshwater lakes, and they're oddly hypnotic to watch. Entry is free and you need an hour. From roughly July to September, the underwater fish ladder windows show salmon swimming upstream. Combine it with lunch in Ballard.

### Gas Works Park

An old gasification plant turned into a park on the north shore of Lake Union, with rusting industrial towers and one of the best skyline views in the city. Free, 45 minutes. Climb the grassy kite hill and watch the seaplanes land on the lake. It's an easy walk from Fremont.

### Fremont

The neighbourhood calls itself the centre of the universe and keeps the joke going with public art: the Fremont Troll crushing a real Volkswagen under the Aurora Bridge, a Lenin statue and a rocket on a shop roof. Free, about 2 hours with a coffee. Come on Sunday for the Fremont Sunday Market.

### Pioneer Square and the Underground Tour

Seattle's oldest district has red-brick buildings and, below them, the remains of streets buried when the city was raised after the 1889 fire. The Underground Tour lasts about 75 minutes and costs around €25-30 ($30-35). The guides are funny, the tunnels are dusty, and you'll learn a lot. Pioneer Square has a visible homelessness problem, so stay aware of your surroundings at night, as you would anywhere.

### Columbia Center Sky View Observatory

On the 73rd floor of the tallest building in the city, this is higher than the Space Needle and, crucially, has the Needle in the view. Tickets are around €25-30 ($30-35), and you'll want about an hour. Our honest take: if you're only doing one paid viewpoint, choose this one. Lines are much shorter, too.

### Discovery Park

Over 200 hectares of forest, meadows and bluffs in Magnolia, with a lighthouse on the beach at West Point. It's free and needs half a day. The loop trail plus the walk down to the lighthouse takes about 2-3 hours. Bus 33 runs from downtown.

### Seattle Art Museum and Olympic Sculpture Park

SAM downtown has good Indigenous Northwest Coast art and costs about €30 ($30-35), with suggested-admission days worth checking. The outdoor Olympic Sculpture Park on the waterfront is free and open, with Richard Serra and Alexander Calder pieces against the sound. If you're short on time, do the free park and skip the museum.

## Things to do in Seattle

Some of the best things to do in Seattle involve water, coffee and food more than buildings.

- **Take the ferry to Bainbridge Island.** A 35-minute ride from Pier 52 with skyline and mountain views, for around €9-10 ($10-11) walk-on, paid only westbound. It's the best value boat trip in the city.

- **Kayak or paddleboard on Lake Union.** Rentals near the lake run about €25-35 ($30-40) an hour. Paddle past the houseboats, including the one from Sleepless in Seattle.

- **Drink coffee properly.** Skip the queue at the Pike Place Starbucks and try Victrola, Caffe Vita or Elm Coffee Roasters. Espresso Vivace on Capitol Hill is an old-school institution.

- **Catch a gig on Capitol Hill.** Neumos and the smaller bars along Pike and Pine streets carry the city's music reputation. Tickets for local bands are often €10-20 ($12-25).

- **Watch a game.** The Mariners (baseball) play at T-Mobile Park and cheap seats are often €15-25 ($15-30). It's a fun, relaxed evening with a view of the city.

- **Visit the Museum of Flight.** South of the city near Boeing Field, it has a Concorde and the first presidential jet. Around €25-30 ($30-35) and a half day. Aviation fans will love it.

- **Go to the Japanese Garden at the Washington Park Arboretum.** Small, peaceful and cheap (about €8-10 / $10). It's especially good in autumn.

## Seattle for free (or almost)

Seattle's best views and most of its character cost nothing, so you can balance out the expensive hotels.

- Pike Place Market, the waterfront and the Overlook Walk

- Kerry Park, Gas Works Park and Volunteer Park (the conservatory charges a small fee of a few dollars)

- The Olympic Sculpture Park and Discovery Park

- Ballard Locks and the salmon ladder

- The Fremont Troll and street art

- The 5th-floor-and-up reading room spaces of the Central Library, a striking Rem Koolhaas building downtown with a good view from the upper floor

- The Amazon Spheres at the base of Amazon HQ: free to see from outside, with occasional public visits that need booking

- A ferry ride: only around €10 ($11) for a round trip to Bainbridge

## What is overrated (and what to do instead)

- **The "first" Starbucks.** The Pike Place store isn't the original location (it moved there in 1976), and the line can be 45 minutes for the same coffee. Take a photo of the sign and drink coffee elsewhere.

- **The Space Needle at full price on a cloudy day.** You pay over $40 to look at fog. Check the forecast, or go to the Columbia Center, or just go to Kerry Park for free.

- **The Pike Place fish throwing.** Fun for two minutes, a crowd crush the rest of the time. Watch it on your way past, don't wait for it.

- **The Gum Wall.** Exactly what it sounds like. A quick look is enough.

## When to visit Seattle

**July to September is the best time**: dry, sunny, around 24-28°C, with long evenings and the mountains in view. It's also the most expensive time, with cruise ships filling Pike Place and hotel prices peaking. Late summer can bring wildfire smoke from fires in the region, so check air quality before you go.

From October to April it's grey, mild (5-12°C) and drizzly, but rarely stormy. Hotels are much cheaper, the city is quieter and the coffee shops make more sense. May, June and early October are a good compromise, though June can be cloudy. If you like the mountains, skiing at Snoqualmie Pass is an hour away in winter.

## Where to stay in Seattle: best areas

Downtown is the most practical place to stay, but the neighbourhoods are better value and more fun. Expect high prices: decent hotels rarely go below $180 a night in summer.

- **Downtown / Pike Place:** walkable to everything, direct light rail to the airport. Best for a first visit. About €180-350 ($200-400) a night for mid-range.

- **Belltown:** between Pike Place and Seattle Center, with bars and restaurants. Can be rowdy at night. €150-280 ($170-320).

- **Capitol Hill:** nightlife, LGBTQ+ scene, good food, on the light rail. Fewer hotels, more apartment rentals. €130-250 ($150-290).

- **South Lake Union / Queen Anne:** quieter, newer hotels, close to Seattle Center. €150-270 ($170-310).

- **U District:** cheapest option on the light rail, around 15 minutes north of downtown. €110-190 ($130-220).

Hostels downtown (like the HI or Green Tortoise near Pike Place) start around €45-60 ($50-70) for a dorm bed. Remember that hotel taxes add about 15-17% on top of the listed price.

## What to eat in Seattle

Seafood is the main reason to eat in Seattle, plus a strong Asian food scene and excellent coffee.

- **Oysters:** Taylor Shellfish (several locations) or The Walrus and the Carpenter in Ballard. Happy hour oysters are the way to do it.

- **Dungeness crab:** sweet local crab, expensive (often $50+ whole). Try it at a market stall or in a seafood restaurant.

- **Salmon:** wild sockeye or king salmon, ideally in summer when it's fresh.

- **Clam chowder:** Pike Place Chowder has a line for a reason. Try the seafood bisque.

- **Piroshky:** Piroshky Piroshky at Pike Place, a Russian pastry. The smoked salmon pâté one is the local favourite.

- **Beecher's mac and cheese:** from the cheesemaker in Pike Place, where you can watch cheese being made through the window.

- **Seattle-style teriyaki:** cheap chicken teriyaki plates, the city's everyday comfort food.

- **Seattle dog:** a hot dog with cream cheese and grilled onions, sold from late-night carts. Odd, but good after a few beers.

- **Dim sum and pho:** in the Chinatown-International District. Look for crowded places on a weekend morning.

Costs: a casual lunch (teriyaki, pho, sandwich) is €15-22 ($17-25). A mid-range dinner with a drink is €45-70 ($50-80) per person. Tax is around 10% and tipping 18-22% is expected at sit-down restaurants, so budget about 30% on top of the menu price.

## Airport to city centre and getting around

The **Link light rail (1 Line)** is the cheapest and easiest way from Seattle-Tacoma International Airport (SeaTac) to downtown: about 40 minutes for a flat $3 (about €2.60). It also continues to Capitol Hill and the University of Washington. The station is a short walk from the terminal, past the parking garage.

A taxi or Uber/Lyft costs around €45-60 ($50-70) and takes 25-45 minutes, depending on traffic, which is often bad. Only take one if you have a lot of luggage or are arriving late.

In the city, buses cover most places, and the light rail is useful for the north-south route. Get an **ORCA card** (physical or the app) for buses, light rail, the Monorail, streetcar and water taxi. Standard fares are about $3 a ride. Downtown, the old town and the waterfront are walkable, but the hills are steep. You don't need a car to see the city, but you'll want one for Mount Rainier and Olympic National Park.

## Day trips from Seattle

- **Bainbridge Island:** 35 minutes by ferry from the waterfront. Walk to the town of Winslow for a bakery, wine tasting and a slower pace. Half a day.

- **Mount Rainier National Park:** about 2-2.5 hours by car to the Paradise area, with glaciers and wildflower meadows in summer. Park entry is about $30 per car. Full day, and timed entry reservations are sometimes required in summer, so check before going.

- **Snoqualmie Falls:** around 40 minutes by car east. A large waterfall with a short trail and free viewing (parking is paid). Combine with a hike near North Bend. Half a day.

- **Leavenworth:** about 2.5 hours across the Cascades, a Bavarian-themed mountain town. Kitsch, but the setting and hikes are great. Long day, better with an overnight stay.

- **Olympic National Park:** 2.5-3 hours via ferry and car to Hurricane Ridge or the Hoh Rain Forest. It's a big area, so better as a 2-day trip than a rushed day.

## Common mistakes and scams

- **Forgetting tax and tips.** Prices rarely include tax, and service is expected on top. A "€20" meal often costs €26.

- **Booking hotels late in summer.** Cruise season and conventions can push prices past $400 a night. Book early or stay near the light rail outside downtown.

- **Driving in the city.** Parking downtown can cost $30-50 a day. Rent a car only for days out of the city.

- **Leaving things in a parked car.** Car break-ins are common, especially at trailheads and viewpoints. Leave nothing visible.

- **Underestimating the hills.** Google Maps says a 15-minute walk, but your legs will feel 30. Take the bus up to Queen Anne.

- **Waiting in the long lines at Pike Place.** The Starbucks and the chowder line are long, but the market has plenty of good food with no queue at all.

- **Fake ticket sellers:** buy tickets from official websites, especially for the Space Needle and sports games.

## Seattle FAQ

### How many days do you need in Seattle?

Three days is ideal: two for the city and one for a ferry trip or Mount Rainier. One day is enough for the main sights in a rush.

### What are the top things to do in Seattle for first-timers?

Pike Place Market, the waterfront, one viewpoint (Columbia Center or the Space Needle), Kerry Park at sunset and the Bainbridge ferry. Add Ballard Locks and Fremont if you have a second day.

### Is Seattle expensive to visit?

Yes, especially hotels, which often cost $200+ a night in summer. Food and transport are pricey too, but many of the best views and parks are free.

### Is the Space Needle worth it?

On a clear day, if you've never been, yes. On a cloudy day, no, and the Columbia Center Sky View is better value either way.

### Does it rain all the time in Seattle?

It drizzles often from October to May, but heavy rain is unusual. Summers are dry and sunny.

### How do I get from Seattle airport to downtown?

Take the Link light rail: about 40 minutes and $3. A taxi or Uber costs $50-70.

### Do you need a car in Seattle?

Not in the city, where buses, light rail and walking work fine. You'll need one for Mount Rainier or the Olympic Peninsula.

### Where is the best place to stay in Seattle?

Downtown near Pike Place for a first visit. Capitol Hill for nightlife, and the U District to save money while staying on the light rail.
