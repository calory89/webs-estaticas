San Diego is the most relaxed big city in California: 70 miles of coast, weather that barely changes all year, and a Mexican food scene that Los Angeles quietly envies. It isn't a city of grand monuments. You come for the ocean, the parks, the neighbourhoods and the eating. That suits beach people, families, surfers, craft beer fans and anyone who wants a US city that doesn't wear them out.

Three days is the right amount for the city itself: one for Balboa Park and downtown, one for La Jolla and the beaches, one for Coronado and Point Loma. Add a fourth or fifth if you want a day trip to Tijuana or the desert. The catch is that San Diego is spread out. Without a car you'll lean on the trolley, buses and the odd Uber, so pick your base carefully.

What to see in San Diego: the places worth your time

Balboa Park

Balboa Park is the cultural centre of San Diego: 1,200 acres of Spanish Revival buildings, gardens and 17 museums, and walking around it is free. The El Prado promenade, the Botanical Building with its lily pond and the Spanish Village Art Center are the highlights. Give it half a day. Museums cost about €15-25 ($15-28) each. If you'll visit three or more, look at the Balboa Park Explorer pass. Our tip: the free Sunday organ concert at the Spreckels Organ Pavilion is lovely, and plenty of people have no idea it exists.

San Diego Zoo

Inside Balboa Park, and one of the best zoos in the world, with very good habitats. Allow 4-5 hours, and wear proper shoes because it's hilly. Tickets run about €70 ($75) for adults. Go first thing, when the animals are active and the paths are quieter, and use the Skyfari aerial tram to save your legs. Skip the separate Safari Park unless you love zoos. It's 45 minutes away by car.

La Jolla Cove

The most photographed stretch of coast in the city: sandstone cliffs, sea lions on the rocks at Point La Jolla and harbour seals at the Children's Pool. It's free, and two hours is enough, longer if you snorkel or kayak through the sea caves (kayak tours about €45-60, $50-65). Keep your distance from the sea lions, because they bite and rangers will fine you. Parking is a nightmare after 10am, so come early or take an Uber.

Torrey Pines State Natural Reserve

These are clifftop trails above the Pacific, lined with the rarest pine tree in the US. The Guy Fleming and Razor Point trails are short and easy, and the Beach Trail drops down to the sand. Entry on foot is free. Parking at the bottom costs about €15-25 ($15-25) depending on the season. Plan on 2-3 hours, and go at sunset if you can.

Coronado

Coronado is a polished island-ish town across the bay, with one of the best beaches in the country and the red-roofed Hotel del Coronado from 1888. Take the ferry from Broadway Pier (about €8, $8 each way), rent a bike on Orange Avenue and ride to the beach. Give it half a day. Walk through the Hotel del for free. A drink on its terrace costs as much as lunch elsewhere.

USS Midway Museum

This is a decommissioned aircraft carrier on the downtown waterfront. Even people who don't care about the military tend to love it, thanks to the volunteer docents (many of them veterans) and the planes on the flight deck. Tickets are about €35 ($38). Allow 3 hours. Start on the flight deck before the crowds and leave the lower decks for later.

Old Town San Diego State Historic Park

The site of the first Spanish settlement in California, now restored adobe buildings, small free museums and shops. Entry is free. An hour or two is plenty. It's a bit theme-park around the edges, but the Whaley House and the old schoolhouse are worth a look. The Old Town Transit Center is right there, so it's easy to reach by trolley.

Cabrillo National Monument

Cabrillo sits at the tip of Point Loma, with the best view in San Diego: the bay, downtown, Coronado and, on clear days, Mexico. There's also an 1855 lighthouse and tide pools below. Entry is about €18 ($20) per car, or about €10 ($10) on foot or by bike. Plan 2 hours. Check the tide tables and go at low tide for the pools, which are best in winter.

Sunset Cliffs

A free stretch of eroded cliffs in Point Loma, made for the sunset. Locals bring a beer they technically shouldn't. Stay well back from the edge, because the sandstone collapses and people fall every year. Combine it with dinner in Ocean Beach next door.

Little Italy

This is the most pleasant neighbourhood downtown, packed with restaurants, wine bars and gelato. The Saturday Mercato farmers market along Date and Cedar Streets is one of the best in Southern California. Go hungry and spend a morning there.

Gaslamp Quarter

A 16-block grid of Victorian buildings, now bars, clubs and restaurants around Fifth Avenue. It's lively at night and the architecture is worth an hour, but most of the food is mediocre and overpriced. Drink here, and eat in Little Italy or Barrio Logan.

Chicano Park and Barrio Logan

Chicano Park sits under the Coronado Bridge, where the concrete pillars are covered in murals about Mexican-American history. It's free and one of the most striking sights in the city. Spend an hour on the murals, then eat tacos on Logan Avenue. Go in daylight.

Pacific Beach and Mission Beach

The classic boardwalk scene: skaters, cyclists, surf schools and the old wooden Giant Dipper rollercoaster at Belmont Park. Walking the boardwalk is free, and surf lessons cost about €70-90 ($75-100). It's loud and young. Weekday mornings are calmest.

Things to do in San Diego

The best things to do in San Diego happen outdoors, and plenty of them have nothing to do with ticking off sights.

  • Take a surf lesson in La Jolla Shores or Pacific Beach. The waves at La Jolla Shores are gentle and the beach is wide.
  • Kayak the La Jolla sea caves, with sea lions and leopard sharks (harmless) below you in summer.
  • Do a craft beer crawl in North Park, which has dozens of breweries within walking distance along 30th Street.
  • Whale watching from mid-December to April, when grey whales migrate. Trips cost about €45-60 ($50-65) from the harbour.
  • Cycle the Bayshore Bikeway around Coronado and the Silver Strand, flat and easy.
  • Watch a Padres game at Petco Park. Cheap seats run about €20 ($20), and the Park at the Park lawn has cheap tickets too.
  • Spend an afternoon at Liberty Station, a former naval training centre turned food hall (Liberty Public Market), galleries and lawns.

San Diego for free (or almost)

A lot of San Diego costs nothing, which helps in a pricey US city.

  • Walking Balboa Park, the Botanical Building and the Sunday organ concert.
  • Residents' free Tuesdays at Balboa Park museums apply only to locals, so don't plan around them.
  • La Jolla Cove, the seals and sea lions, and the coastal walk to La Jolla Shores.
  • Old Town State Historic Park and its small museums.
  • Sunset Cliffs, Ocean Beach pier and every public beach.
  • Chicano Park murals.
  • Torrey Pines trails if you arrive on foot or by bus.
  • The ferry to Coronado is the cheapest "harbour cruise" you'll find, at about €8.

What is overrated (and what to do instead)

  • Seaport Village: These are fake-quaint souvenir shops on the waterfront. Walk past to the Embarcadero and spend the time at Liberty Station or Little Italy instead.
  • Eating in the Gaslamp: You get average food at premium prices with aggressive door staff. Better food at fairer prices waits in Little Italy, North Park or Barrio Logan.
  • SeaWorld: It's expensive (often €90+, $100+) and divisive. If you want marine life, the Birch Aquarium in La Jolla costs a third of that, and the wild sea lions are free.
  • The Old Town restaurant strip: Margaritas the size of buckets and tired Tex-Mex. Do the park, then eat Mexican food elsewhere.

When to visit San Diego

San Diego is good all year, but September and October are the best months: the ocean is at its warmest, the sky is clear and the summer crowds have gone. Summer (July-August) is sunny and around 25°C, but hotels are at their most expensive and the beaches are packed.

Know about "May Gray" and "June Gloom": a marine layer can sit over the coast until afternoon for weeks. It isn't cold, just grey. Winter (December-February) runs 18-20°C by day, with the odd rain, whale season and the cheapest hotels outside holiday weeks. Avoid the week of Comic-Con in July, when prices triple.

Where to stay in San Diego: best areas

  • Little Italy: This is the best all-rounder. It's walkable and near the trolley, the airport and the waterfront, with great restaurants. Expect about €180-300 ($200-330) a night.
  • Gaslamp Quarter / downtown: Good for nightlife and first-timers without a car. It's noisy at weekends. Rooms run about €180-320 ($200-350).
  • Pacific Beach: For beach life and a younger crowd. It's far from the centre and loud at night. Hotels cost about €150-280 ($160-300), and hostels run about €40-60 ($45-65).
  • La Jolla: Beautiful, quiet and expensive, best with a car. Expect about €250-500 ($270-550).
  • Ocean Beach: A laid-back, slightly hippie area with cheap eats and hostels. Hotels cost about €130-220 ($140-240).

What we would do: Little Italy if you have no car, Pacific Beach or Ocean Beach if you do and want the sea at your door.

What to eat in San Diego

Mexican food is the reason to eat here, and the city's own invention is the California burrito: carne asada, cheese and fries rolled inside a burrito. Order it at a 24-hour "-berto's" taco shop (Lolita's, Roberto's and dozens more). Expect about €10-12 ($11-13), enough for two meals.

  • Fish tacos: Baja-style battered fish with cabbage and crema. Oscar's Mexican Seafood in Pacific Beach is a local favourite.
  • Carne asada fries: Fries, steak, guacamole and cheese. It's late-night food.
  • Burgers at Hodad's in Ocean Beach, a legendary institution with long queues. Go mid-afternoon.
  • Sea urchin and local seafood at Saturday's Tuna Harbor Dockside Market downtown.
  • Craft beer: San Diego is one of the best beer cities in the US. Try a West Coast IPA.

Rough costs: a taco-shop lunch runs €10-15 ($11-16), a sit-down lunch €20-30 ($22-33), and dinner for one with a drink at a mid-range restaurant €45-70 ($50-75). Add 18-20% tip and sales tax on top of menu prices.

Airport to city centre and getting around

San Diego International Airport is only about 5 km (3 miles) from downtown, one of the closest in the US. An Uber or Lyft to downtown or Little Italy takes 10-15 minutes and costs about €15-25 ($15-25). The MTS bus 992 goes to downtown for about €2.50 ($2.50). There's also a free airport shuttle to the Old Town Transit Center, where you can connect to the trolley. Check the current schedule before you fly.

The trolley (Blue, Green and Orange lines) covers downtown, Old Town, the Mexican border at San Ysidro and UC San Diego. A single fare costs about €2.50 ($2.50) and a day pass about €6 ($6), both via the PRONTO app or card. Buses fill the gaps, but beaches like La Jolla are slow to reach by public transport. For three days without a car, mix the trolley with a few Ubers. Renting a car (about €50-80, $55-90 a day, plus €20-50 parking at hotels) makes sense only if you're staying in the beach areas or doing day trips.

Day trips from San Diego

  • Tijuana, Mexico: Take the Blue Line trolley to San Ysidro (about 50 minutes), then walk across the border. Bring your passport, and allow for long queues coming back, often 1-2 hours. Go for Avenida Revolución, tacos and the Baja food scene. Stick to central areas and use licensed taxis or Uber.
  • Julian: An old gold-mining town in the mountains, famous for apple pie. It's about 1h15 by car and makes a good autumn trip.
  • Anza-Borrego Desert State Park: About 2 hours by car. Spring wildflowers, giant metal sculptures in Borrego Springs and dark skies. It's too hot in summer.
  • Carlsbad and Oceanside: The Coaster train from Santa Fe Depot gets to Oceanside in about an hour. The Carlsbad Flower Fields bloom from March to early May.
  • Temecula wine country: About 1 hour by car, with dozens of wineries. Don't drive after tastings. Book a tour.

Common mistakes and scams

  • Underestimating distances: La Jolla, Coronado and Balboa Park are not a walkable cluster. Plan one area per day.
  • Forgetting tax and tip: Menu and hotel prices rise 20-30% by the time you pay. Hotels also add "destination" or resort fees.
  • Getting too close to the sea lions and seals in La Jolla. It's illegal, and they're aggressive in pupping season.
  • Parking tickets: Read every sign, because street cleaning and time limits are strictly enforced.
  • Gaslamp club promoters: "Free entry" often comes with expensive bottle minimums.
  • Timeshare pitches disguised as free tours or discounted attraction tickets. Say no.
  • Driving a US rental car into Mexico: Most contracts forbid it or require extra Mexican insurance. Walk across instead.
  • Car break-ins at beach and trailhead car parks: Leave nothing visible.

San Diego FAQ

How many days do you need in San Diego?

Three days covers the main things to do in San Diego: Balboa Park, La Jolla, Coronado and Point Loma. Add one or two more for Tijuana or a desert or wine day trip.

Do you need a car in San Diego?

Not if you stay downtown or in Little Italy and combine the trolley, ferry and Uber. A car helps for La Jolla, Torrey Pines and day trips, but parking is expensive.

Is the San Diego Zoo worth it?

Yes. It's one of the best zoos in the world, and a full morning there is money well spent. Go at opening time.

Is San Diego expensive?

It's cheaper than San Francisco or Los Angeles for food, but hotels are pricey, especially in summer. Expect at least €110 ($120) a day on a tight budget.

Can you swim in the ocean in San Diego?

Yes, but the water is cool, around 15-17°C in winter and 20-22°C in late summer. La Jolla Shores and Coronado are the calmest beaches.

Is it safe to visit Tijuana from San Diego?

Day trips to central Tijuana are common and generally fine if you stay in busy areas and use Uber or official taxis. Check your government's travel advice before going.

What is the best month to visit San Diego?

September and October have the warmest sea, the clearest skies and fewer crowds. May and June are often grey on the coast.

What is San Diego famous for food-wise?

The California burrito, Baja fish tacos and craft beer. Eat at local taco shops rather than tourist-strip restaurants.

What to see in San Diego in one day?

If you only have one day, follow day 1 of this itinerary: Balboa Park and downtown. Morning: Get to the San Diego Zoo when it opens and stay until lunch.

What to see in San Diego in two days?

With two days, follow days 1 and 2 of this itinerary. Day 1: Balboa Park and downtown. Morning: Get to the San Diego Zoo when it opens and stay until lunch. Day 2: La Jolla and the beaches. Morning: Go to La Jolla Cove early for the sea lions, then kayak the caves or walk to La Jolla Shores.
