Ho Chi Minh City (still Saigon to almost everyone who lives here) is loud, hot, fast and much more fun than its first impression suggests. You land, a wall of motorbikes rolls past, and for an hour you wonder why you came. Then you sit on a plastic stool with an iced coffee, and the city starts to make sense. It isn't a pretty city in the postcard sense. It's a working city of nine-plus million people, with French colonial buildings, Chinese temples, glass towers and some of the best cheap food in Asia, all within a short Grab ride of each other.

It suits food lovers, history readers, night owls and anyone who likes cities with a pulse more than cities with a skyline. Three days is the right amount: two for the city itself and one for the Cu Chi Tunnels or the Mekong Delta. Add a fourth if you want both day trips. One note before you start: in 2025 Vietnam scrapped district-level government, so "District 1" no longer officially exists. Locals, taxi drivers and every hotel listing still use the old names, and so do we.

What to see in Ho Chi Minh City: the places worth your time

War Remnants Museum

This is the one place in the city you shouldn't skip. It documents the Vietnam War (the "American War" here) through photographs, with rooms on Agent Orange and on the foreign photographers who died covering the conflict. It's one-sided and openly so, but the images are powerful and it explains a lot about modern Vietnam. Allow 2 hours, entry about 40,000 VND (€1.50). Go first thing: by late morning tour groups pack the upper floors.

Independence Palace

The former presidential palace of South Vietnam is a 1960s modernist time capsule, famous as the place where a North Vietnamese tank crashed through the gates in April 1975. The upstairs reception rooms are pure retro design, but the real reason to come is the basement: war rooms, old radio equipment and maps still on the walls. About 1.5 hours, tickets about 65,000 VND (€2.50). It's a 10-minute walk from the War Remnants Museum, so do them together.

Saigon Notre-Dame Cathedral and Central Post Office

Two French-era landmarks facing each other across a small square. The red-brick cathedral, built with bricks shipped from Marseille, has been under long-running restoration, so expect scaffolding and check whether the interior is open. The Post Office next door, with its iron vaulting and huge painted maps, is free, still works as a post office and takes 20 minutes. Come early morning for photos without crowds, then grab a coffee on the steps of the nearby Book Street (Nguyen Van Binh).

Ben Thanh Market

The city's most famous market is worth a look for the building and the food stalls in the middle, less so for shopping. Prices for clothes and souvenirs start at tourist level and need serious haggling. Give it 45 minutes. Our tip: skip the stalls on the edges and head straight for the food section for a bowl of bun rieu or a sugarcane juice, about 40,000-60,000 VND (€1.50-2).

Cholon and Binh Tay Market

Cholon, the old Chinatown in the former District 5, is where Saigon feels least touristy. Binh Tay Market is the wholesale version of Ben Thanh: sacks of dried shrimp, spices and sweets, with almost no one trying to sell to you. Wander the surrounding streets of herbal medicine shops and Chinese-Vietnamese temples. Half a day, free, and a Grab from the centre costs about 60,000-80,000 VND (€2-3).

Thien Hau Temple

The best of Cholon's temples, dedicated to the sea goddess and built by Cantonese settlers in the 18th century. Look up at the ceramic figures on the roof and the giant incense coils hanging from the ceiling, each one carrying a wish. Free entry, 30 minutes. Combine it with Binh Tay Market, which is a short ride away.

Jade Emperor Pagoda

A smoky, atmospheric Taoist pagoda in Da Kao, full of carved wooden deities and turtles in a pond outside. It's small, so 30-40 minutes is enough, and it's free. Barack Obama visited in 2016, which is the one fact every guide will tell you. Go in the morning when locals come to pray.

Nguyen Hue Walking Street and The Café Apartments

Nguyen Hue is a wide pedestrian boulevard running from the City Hall (People's Committee building) to the river. It wakes up in the evening, when families, skaters and teenagers come out. Halfway down, the old apartment block at 42 Nguyen Hue is now stacked with tiny cafés and shops on every floor. Pay the small lift fee (a few thousand dong), pick a café with a balcony and watch the street below. Free to walk, coffee about 50,000-70,000 VND (€2-2.50).

Saigon Skydeck or Landmark 81

For a view, you have two options. The Saigon Skydeck in Bitexco Financial Tower is central and costs about 200,000-250,000 VND (€7-9). Landmark 81 is Vietnam's tallest building, out in Binh Thanh, and its SkyView observation deck costs more. Our honest take: skip both and have a drink at a rooftop bar instead (see below), which costs about the same and comes with a seat.

Ho Chi Minh City Museum of Fine Arts

A faded yellow colonial mansion near Ben Thanh with tiled floors, an old cage lift and a solid collection of Vietnamese lacquer painting and propaganda art. It's rarely busy, which on a hot afternoon is part of the appeal. About an hour, entry roughly 30,000 VND (€1).

Saigon Opera House

The 1900 Municipal Theatre is lovely from the outside and free to admire. If you want to go in, book the AO Show or a similar Vietnamese bamboo-circus performance, roughly 700,000-1,500,000 VND (€25-55). It's touristy, but well done and an hour long. Book a few days ahead in high season.

Thao Dien

The expat neighbourhood across the river in the former District 2 (now part of Thu Duc). It's leafy, with good cafés, brunch spots, concept stores and bars by the water. It's not a sight as such, but it shows you the new Saigon, and it's a relief after a day in the centre. Go late afternoon and stay for dinner.

Things to do in Ho Chi Minh City

The best things to do in Ho Chi Minh City happen on the street, not in museums. These are the ones we'd make time for.

  • Take a motorbike food tour at night. A guide drives, you sit on the back and eat your way through four or five districts. It's the single best way to understand the city. Expect about €40-60 for 3-4 hours with all food included.
  • Eat snails on Vinh Khanh Street. In the former District 4, this street of oc (shellfish) restaurants fills up with locals from early evening. Order scallops grilled with spring onion, snails in coconut sauce and a cold beer. Around €8-12 per person.
  • Ride the Saigon Waterbus. From Bach Dang pier near the river, the public waterbus runs up the Saigon River towards Thanh Da and Thu Duc. It's a cheap, slow sightseeing cruise for about 15,000 VND (€0.50) a ride. Check the schedule, as it changes.
  • Have a drink on a rooftop. Chill Skybar, Social Club at Hotel des Arts and the Saigon Saigon bar on top of the Caravelle hotel are the classics. Cocktails run €8-15. Go for sunset and dress smart-casual.
  • Do a coffee crawl. Vietnamese coffee is strong and served over ice with condensed milk (ca phe sua da). Try coconut coffee, salted coffee and egg coffee, then sit in a tiny alley café like the ones down the lanes off Ton That Dam.
  • Take a cooking class. Most include a market visit and three or four dishes, around €30-45. A good rainy-afternoon option.
  • Get a haircut or massage. A traditional barber shave and ear clean, or a foot massage, is cheap and very Vietnamese. Expect €8-20, and choose a busy place with posted prices.
  • Ride the new metro. Line 1 opened in late 2024, running from Ben Thanh out to Suoi Tien. It's clean, cheap and good for reaching Thao Dien without traffic.

Ho Chi Minh City for free (or almost)

Saigon is already cheap, but plenty of what's worth seeing costs nothing at all.

  • The Central Post Office, Notre-Dame Cathedral exterior, Opera House exterior and City Hall are all free and within five minutes of each other.
  • Thien Hau Temple, Jade Emperor Pagoda and the other Cholon temples charge nothing.
  • Nguyen Hue Walking Street and Book Street are free to wander, and best in the evening.
  • Tao Dan Park in the early morning, where locals do tai chi, play badminton and bring songbirds in cages to "sing" together.
  • The riverfront at Bach Dang is free, and the waterbus is the cheapest boat ride you'll ever take.
  • Street food: a banh mi costs about 25,000-40,000 VND (€1-1.50) and is a full lunch.

Ho Chi Minh City itinerary: 3 days

Day 1: Colonial centre and war history

Morning: Be at the War Remnants Museum when it opens, then walk 10 minutes to Independence Palace. Have lunch at a com tam place nearby.

Afternoon: Walk to the cathedral and Central Post Office, then down Dong Khoi past the Opera House. Retreat from the heat at The Café Apartments on Nguyen Hue.

Evening: Sunset drink at a rooftop bar, then a motorbike food tour, or walk over to Vinh Khanh Street for snails.

Day 2: Cu Chi Tunnels and Thao Dien

Morning: Leave early for the Cu Chi Tunnels (see Day trips). Half-day tours are back by early afternoon.

Afternoon: Rest, then take metro Line 1 or a Grab to Thao Dien for coffee and a wander.

Evening: Dinner in Thao Dien or back in the centre, then a drink on Nguyen Hue. Look in at Bui Vien only if you're curious (see below).

Day 3: Cholon, markets and temples

Morning: Start at Jade Emperor Pagoda, then Grab to Cholon for Binh Tay Market and Thien Hau Temple.

Afternoon: Lunch in Cholon (hu tieu or dim sum), then back to the centre for the Fine Arts Museum and Ben Thanh Market.

Evening: Banh xeo for dinner, then the Saigon Opera House show or one last coffee on Book Street.

What is overrated (and what to do instead)

  • Bui Vien Walking Street. The backpacker street is loud, sticky and full of overpriced beer, laughing-gas balloons and touts. Look once at night if you must. For an evening out with locals, sit on the plastic stools along Vinh Khanh or around Ho Thi Ky night market instead.
  • Shopping at Ben Thanh Market. Prices are inflated and the "branded" goods are fake. For food, it's fine. For souvenirs, the shops around Le Loi and the Saigon Square mall are less pushy.
  • Observation decks. Paying for the Skydeck to stand behind glass makes little sense when a rooftop bar gives you the same view, a seat and a drink.
  • One-day Mekong Delta tours from the city. You spend six hours on a bus to see a coconut candy workshop that exists mostly for tour groups. If the Delta interests you, stay overnight in Can Tho or Ben Tre (see below).

When to visit Ho Chi Minh City

The best time to visit is December to March, the dry season, with sunny days around 30°C and the lowest humidity of the year. April and early May are the hottest months, often over 35°C, and best saved for people who like heat. From May to November it's the wet season. Rain usually comes in heavy afternoon bursts rather than all day, so sightseeing still works if you plan museums for after lunch.

Avoid or plan carefully around Tet (Lunar New Year, late January or February). Many people leave the city, family-run restaurants close for a week, and flights and buses around Vietnam fill up and get more expensive. The wet season (especially September and October) is quieter and hotel prices drop.

Where to stay in Ho Chi Minh City: best areas

  • District 1 (Ben Nghe and Dong Khoi area). The best base for a first visit: walking distance to the cathedral, Nguyen Hue, the river and most museums. Mid-range hotels from €40-80 a night, luxury from €120.
  • Pham Ngu Lao / Bui Vien. Still technically District 1 and the cheapest place to sleep. Hostels from €7-12, budget hotels from €20-30. Noisy until very late, so pick a room on a side alley.
  • District 3. Quieter, greener and more local, with tree-lined streets, good food and cafés, and 10-15 minutes by Grab to the centre. Very good value, €25-60.
  • Thao Dien. Calm, leafy and full of cafés, ideal for longer stays or families. Serviced apartments and boutique hotels from €40-90. It's further out, but the metro helps.

What to eat in Ho Chi Minh City

Saigon's food is sweeter and more generous with herbs than in the north, and most of the best of it is cooked on the pavement. Here's what to order.

  • Com tam (broken rice): the city's signature lunch. Grilled pork chop, broken rice, a slice of egg meatloaf and a fried egg, with fish sauce on top. About 40,000-70,000 VND (€1.50-2.50).
  • Banh mi: Banh Mi Huynh Hoa is the famous one, overstuffed and pricier (about 70,000 VND), with a queue. A street cart will do you a good one for half that.
  • Pho: southern style comes with a plate of fresh herbs and bean sprouts. Pho Hoa Pasteur is the classic, though locals have dozens of favourites.
  • Banh xeo: a crispy turmeric pancake with shrimp and pork, wrapped in lettuce and herbs. Banh Xeo 46A on Dinh Cong Trang is the well-known spot.
  • Bun thit nuong: cold rice noodles with grilled pork, herbs, peanuts and fish sauce. Perfect in the heat.
  • Hu tieu: a Chinese-influenced noodle soup, best eaten in Cholon for breakfast.
  • Oc: snails and shellfish, the classic evening meal with beer.

A street-food lunch costs €2-4. A dinner at a casual local restaurant with a beer runs €6-12, and a smart Vietnamese restaurant like those in District 1 or Thao Dien is €20-35 per person. Our tip: if a stall has no menu, ask the price before ordering.

Airport to city centre and getting around

Tan Son Nhat Airport (SGN) is right inside the city, about 7 km from District 1. Your options:

  • Grab: the easiest. Book in the app and meet the driver at the marked pick-up area. About 120,000-200,000 VND (€4-7), 25-45 minutes depending on traffic.
  • Bus 109: the airport bus runs to Ben Thanh and the backpacker area for about 20,000-25,000 VND (€1), around 45 minutes. Bus 152 is a slower, cheaper local alternative.
  • Metered taxis: use Vinasun (white) or Mai Linh (green) only, from the official rank, and make sure the meter is on.

In town, most people use Grab (motorbike rides of 15-30,000 VND are common across the centre) or the similar Be and Xanh SM apps. Metro Line 1 runs from Ben Thanh to Thao Dien and on to Suoi Tien, with fares around 7,000-20,000 VND. City buses are cheap but slow. There's no tourist travel pass worth buying. Walking works in District 1, though pavements are often used as motorbike parking.

How much a trip to Ho Chi Minh City costs

Ho Chi Minh City is cheap by any European standard, and a comfortable trip costs far less than you'd guess. Rough daily budgets per person:

  • Shoestring: €25-35 a day. Hostel dorm (€8-12), street food for all meals (€6-8), buses and Grab bikes (€2-4), one museum (€1-3), a couple of coffees and beers (€4-5).
  • Mid-range: €60-100 a day. A good hotel room shared (€25-40 each), a mix of street food and restaurants (€15-25), Grab cars (€5-8), a tour or rooftop drink (€15-25).
  • Extras: Cu Chi half-day tour €20-35, motorbike food tour €40-60, AO Show €25-55.

Day trips from Ho Chi Minh City

  • Cu Chi Tunnels: the underground network used by Viet Cong fighters, about 1.5-2 hours north-west by road. Ben Duoc is more authentic and less crowded than Ben Dinh. Join a half-day tour (€20-35) or take the Saigon Waterbus-style speedboat tours for more money. Entry is about 125,000 VND (€4.50). Plan on 5-6 hours total.
  • Mekong Delta: My Tho and Ben Tre are 2-2.5 hours away and are what day tours offer. For floating markets, go to Can Tho (about 3.5-4 hours by bus with Phuong Trang/FUTA) and stay overnight, so you're at Cai Rang floating market at dawn.
  • Cao Dai Holy See, Tay Ninh: the colourful headquarters of the Cao Dai religion, about 2.5-3 hours away. Often combined with Cu Chi on a full-day tour. Aim to arrive for the midday ceremony.
  • Can Gio Mangrove Forest: a UNESCO biosphere reserve about 2 hours south-east, with mangroves, macaques and seafood. Best by tour or private car.
  • Vung Tau: the nearest beach town, about 2-2.5 hours by bus or car. It's a local weekend escape rather than a dream beach, but good seafood and sea air make it worth the trip if you have an extra day.

Common mistakes and scams

  • Phone snatching: thieves on motorbikes grab phones and bags from people at the kerb or on the back of a bike. Keep your phone away from the road side and your bag across your body.
  • Fake taxis: look-alike cabs with rigged meters wait at the airport and in tourist areas. Use Grab or the real Vinasun and Mai Linh.
  • Cyclo rides: a fixed price that becomes "per person" or "per hour" at the end. Agree the total in writing, or just skip it.
  • Coconut and shoe-shine hustles: street vendors put a fruit basket on your shoulder for a photo, then demand payment; shoe-shiners start before you agree. Say no firmly and keep walking.
  • Counting the zeros: 20,000 and 200,000 VND notes look alike. Separate your big notes and check change.
  • Crossing the road: walk slowly and steadily so bikes can predict you. Don't stop suddenly or run.
  • Overplanning: the heat drains you. Do sights in the morning, rest at lunch, go back out after 4pm.

Ho Chi Minh City FAQ

How many days do you need in Ho Chi Minh City?

Three days is ideal: two for the city and one for the Cu Chi Tunnels or the Mekong Delta. Four lets you do both day trips without rushing.

Is Ho Chi Minh City safe for tourists?

Yes, violent crime against tourists is rare. The main risks are traffic and phone or bag snatching, so keep valuables away from the street.

Is it Ho Chi Minh City or Saigon?

Officially Ho Chi Minh City, but Saigon is used every day by locals, and the airport code is still SGN. Both are fine to say.

Do I need a visa for Vietnam?

Many nationalities get visa-free entry for a set number of days, and others can apply for an e-visa online. Check the official Vietnamese government e-visa site before you book.

Can I use cards in Ho Chi Minh City?

Hotels, malls and most restaurants take cards, but street food, markets and small cafés are cash only. Carry dong, and withdraw from bank ATMs rather than standalone machines.

What is the best area to stay in Ho Chi Minh City for first-timers?

District 1, around Dong Khoi and Nguyen Hue, puts you within walking distance of most sights. District 3 is a quieter, cheaper alternative 10 minutes away.

Is the Cu Chi Tunnels trip worth it?

Yes, especially paired with the War Remnants Museum. Choose Ben Duoc over Ben Dinh if you want fewer crowds.

What is Ho Chi Minh City famous for food-wise?

Com tam (broken rice) is the city's signature dish, followed by banh mi, banh xeo and seafood on Vinh Khanh Street. The iced coffee with condensed milk is a close second to all of them.
