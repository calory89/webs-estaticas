# Things to Do in Quito: A 3-Day Guide Above the Clouds

> Quito travel guide by Happy Low Cost, 3-day itinerary. Original page: https://www.happylowcost.com/en/destinations/quito/
> Updated: 2026-11-21

Quito sits at about 2,850 metres in a long, narrow valley wrapped by volcanoes. It has one of the best-preserved colonial centres in Latin America (it was among the first places UNESCO listed, back in 1978), alongside a modern north of glass towers, craft beer bars and decent coffee. It is not a polished city. Traffic is heavy, the air is thin, and some streets empty out fast after dark. But the churches are extraordinary, the food is cheap and filling, and you can be standing on a volcano by mid-morning.

Quito suits travellers who like history, markets and a bit of hiking. It also works as the base for the Andes, Galápagos flights and the Amazon. **Three days** is the right amount: one for the Old Town, one for the heights and museums, and one for the equator or a day trip. Add a fourth or fifth day if you want Cotopaxi or Quilotoa. Ecuador uses the US dollar, so prices below are in dollars with a rough euro equivalent.

## What to see in Quito: the places worth your time

### Plaza Grande (Plaza de la Independencia)

This is the main square of the Old Town and the place to start. The Cathedral, the Archbishop's Palace and the Carondelet Presidential Palace all face it, and on most days the benches are full of shoe-shiners, pensioners and men selling lottery tickets. Spend 30 minutes here, more if you take the free palace tour (bring your passport and expect a queue). **Our tip:** on Monday mornings there is a changing of the guard with a band, which is worth timing your visit around.

### La Compañía de Jesús

This Jesuit church is the reason many people come to Quito. Almost every surface inside is covered in gold leaf, and it is one of the most lavish baroque interiors in the Americas. Entry is about $6 (around €5.50), and 30-45 minutes is enough. Photos inside are restricted, so look properly instead of shooting it. Go early, before the tour groups arrive.

### Iglesia y Convento de San Francisco

The huge cobbled square of San Francisco and its whitewashed church are among the oldest in South America. The church itself is free to enter during services. The convent museum costs a few dollars and has good colonial art from the Quito School. Allow an hour. Sit on the church steps at sunset when the square glows and the pigeons take over.

### Basílica del Voto Nacional

This neo-Gothic basilica has gargoyles shaped like Ecuadorian animals: tortoises, iguanas, armadillos. The real reason to come is the climb. For about $2-4 (€2-4) you go up into the towers, then cross a wooden plank above the nave and climb narrow ladders into the clock tower. It is not for people with vertigo. Allow an hour and go on a clear morning for views of Panecillo and the Old Town roofs.

### La Ronda

This is a restored colonial street with painted balconies, small artisan workshops and cafés selling canelazo, a hot cinnamon drink with or without sugar-cane spirit. By day it is quiet and a bit staged. On Friday and Saturday nights it fills with locals and live music. Give it an hour in the evening. Eat elsewhere, because the menus here are priced for tourists.

### Casa del Alabado

This is the best small museum in the Old Town: pre-Columbian ceramics and stone pieces, displayed beautifully in a colonial house near San Francisco. Entry is around $6 (€5.50), and you need about an hour. It gives you context for everything else you will see in Ecuador, so go early in your trip.

### El Panecillo

The hill crowned by the aluminium Virgin of Quito gives you the classic view over the Old Town and the length of the valley. The viewpoint is free, and climbing inside the statue costs a dollar or two. **Do not walk up the stairs from the Old Town.** Robberies on that route are common. Take a taxi up for around $5 and ask the driver to wait, or book a ride both ways on an app.

### TelefériQo

This cable car runs from the western edge of the city up the slopes of Pichincha volcano to roughly 3,950 metres. The view of the city with Cotopaxi on the horizon is the best in Quito. Foreigners pay around $9-10 (€8-9) return. Go first thing in the morning, because clouds roll in by midday and you might see nothing but white. Fit hikers can continue to Rucu Pichincha's summit (about 4,700 m, a 3-4 hour hike up), but only after a couple of days acclimatising, and not alone.

### Capilla del Hombre and Casa Museo Guayasamín

Oswaldo Guayasamín is Ecuador's best-known painter, and this chapel-like museum in Bellavista holds his huge, angry, moving canvases about Latin American suffering. His house next door has his art collection. Combined entry is roughly $8 (€7.50), and you need 1.5-2 hours. Take a taxi, because it is up a hill away from the metro.

### Mitad del Mundo

The equator monument sits about 25 km north of the centre. The big monument park (Ciudad Mitad del Mundo, around $5) is actually a few hundred metres off the true equator. The small **Museo Intiñan** next door claims to sit on it and runs fun, slightly theatrical demonstrations with water draining and egg balancing. If you only have time for one, choose Intiñan. Allow half a day including travel.

### Parque Itchimbía

This park sits on a hill east of the Old Town, with a restored iron-and-glass palace at the top and a viewpoint looking straight across the colonial centre. It is free and much quieter than Panecillo. Go late afternoon, and take a taxi up rather than walking the steep streets from below.

### Guápulo

Guápulo is an old village tucked below the cliff behind the Hotel Quito, built around a whitewashed 17th-century church. The walk down from the viewpoint on Avenida González Suárez is steep but lovely. There are a couple of laid-back bars for a beer at the bottom. Get a taxi back up unless your lungs are fully acclimatised.

## Things to do in Quito

Beyond churches and viewpoints, these are the experiences that tell you more about how quiteños actually live.

- **Eat lunch at Mercado Central.** The upstairs food stalls do locro de papa, fried corvina (sea bass) and fruit juices for $3-5. It is loud and busy, and it is real.

- **Browse Mercado Santa Clara.** This north-side market has fruit you have never seen (naranjilla, tomate de árbol, babaco) plus cheap juices.

- **Take a chocolate tasting.** Ecuadorian cacao is among the best in the world. Several shops in the Old Town and La Floresta run short tastings for about $10-20.

- **Spend Sunday in Parque La Carolina.** Families, football, paddle boats and street food fill the park. The Vivarium and the botanical garden are next to it.

- **Drink coffee and craft beer in La Floresta.** This is the city's creative neighbourhood, with independent cafés, Ocho y Medio cinema and some of the best restaurants.

- **Join a free walking tour of the Old Town.** These tours are tip-based (budget $10), and they are useful on day one for orientation and safety tips.

- **Catch a football match.** LDU Quito play at the Rodrigo Paz Delgado stadium in the north. Tickets are cheap, and the atmosphere is serious.

## Quito for free (or almost)

Quito is already cheap, but a lot of the best bits cost nothing at all:

- The Plaza Grande, San Francisco square and Plaza Santo Domingo.

- The Carondelet Palace tour (free, passport needed).

- Several municipal museums have free entry on certain days, often the last Sunday of the month. Check before you go.

- The view from Parque Itchimbía and the Guápulo viewpoint.

- Parque El Ejido at weekends, when painters sell their work along the fence on Avenida Patria.

- Pedestrian Sundays (the ciclopaseo), when main avenues close to cars. You can rent a bike or just walk.

- The metro, at well under a dollar, which counts as almost free.

## What is overrated (and what to do instead)

- **La Mariscal and Plaza Foch.** This is the backpacker nightlife zone: generic bars, touts, and petty crime late at night. Go to La Floresta instead.

- **The big Mitad del Mundo monument.** It is not actually on the equator, and the surrounding complex is mostly souvenir shops. Spend your time at Intiñan, or skip the area and see the Pululahua crater nearby.

- **Restaurants on La Ronda.** They are pretty but overpriced and average. Have a drink there, then eat at a market or a local spot.

- **Climbing Rucu Pichincha on day one.** It sounds efficient but makes people sick. Do the TelefériQo views first and save summits for later in the trip.

## When to visit Quito

Quito has spring-like weather all year: about 18-22°C by day and 8-10°C at night. The **dry season, June to September**, brings the clearest volcano views and is the best time for Cotopaxi and hiking. July and August are also the busiest and priciest months.

October to May is wetter, usually as afternoon downpours after sunny mornings, so plan sightseeing early. Flights and hotels are cheaper in May, October and November. Early December brings the Fiestas de Quito, which is fun but noisy. Easter week is when fanesca soup appears, and it is worth timing your visit for if you like food.

## Where to stay in Quito: best areas

- **Centro Histórico (Old Town):** Best for first-timers who want atmosphere and walking access to the sights. You get boutique hotels in colonial houses for $60-150 (€55-135) and hostels from $12. It gets very quiet after 9pm, so take taxis at night.

- **La Floresta:** Best for couples and foodies. It is leafy and residential, with good cafés and restaurants. Guesthouses and small hotels cost $50-110.

- **La Mariscal:** Best for budget backpackers who want nightlife. It has the most hostels ($10-25) and mid-range hotels ($40-80). Watch your bag at night.

- **González Suárez / La Carolina:** Best for business travellers and families. Expect modern hotels, safer streets and metro access around La Carolina, at $70-180.

## What to eat in Quito

Ecuadorian highland food is hearty, potato-heavy and cheap. Here is what to order:

- **Locro de papa:** potato and cheese soup with avocado. It is perfect on a cold evening.

- **Hornado:** slow-roasted pork with llapingachos (fried potato-and-cheese patties) and mote (hominy).

- **Fritada:** fried pork chunks, usually with corn and ripe plantain.

- **Empanadas de viento:** huge, puffy fried empanadas dusted with sugar. Have them with a coffee.

- **Seco de chivo:** a rich goat or lamb stew with rice.

- **Helados de paila:** fruit sorbet stirred in copper pans.

- **Canelazo:** a hot cinnamon and fruit drink, spiked or not.

- **Seasonal specialities:** fanesca at Easter, and colada morada with guaguas de pan (bread babies) around Day of the Dead.

The best value is the **almuerzo**, a set lunch of soup, main and juice for $3-5 (€3-4.50) at local cafés. A sit-down dinner in a mid-range restaurant runs $12-25 (€11-23) per person. A tasting menu at one of Quito's top restaurants costs $60-100.

## Airport to city centre and getting around

Mariscal Sucre Airport is in Tababela, about 40 km from the centre, and traffic makes it a 45-90 minute trip. Your options:

- **Official taxi:** fixed fares of about $25-35 (€23-32), depending on area. App rides like Uber, Cabify or InDrive are often a bit cheaper.

- **Aeroservicios shuttle bus:** about $8 (€7.50) to the old airport at Parque Bicentenario, then a short taxi to your hotel.

- **Public bus:** around $2 to the Río Coca terminal. It is slow and awkward with luggage.

The **Quito Metro**, opened at the end of 2023, runs one line from Quitumbe in the south to El Labrador in the north. Stations include San Francisco in the Old Town, El Ejido and La Carolina. A single ride costs under half a dollar, and it is clean, safe and fast. The Trole and Ecovía bus lines also run north-south but are crowded and known for pickpockets. There is no tourist pass, and you simply pay per ride. Use apps or metered yellow taxis at night, and the meter (taxímetro) should be on.

## Day trips from Quito

- **Cotopaxi National Park:** 1.5-2 hours south. A tour ($50-80) gets you to the refuge car park and a hike up to around 4,800 m, plus the Limpiopungo lagoon. Do it at the end of your trip, once acclimatised.

- **Otavalo:** about 2 hours north by bus from the Carcelén terminal (around $3). The indigenous textile market is biggest on Saturday. Combine it with Peguche waterfall.

- **Mindo:** about 2 hours west by bus from La Ofelia terminal. It is a cloud forest with hummingbirds, butterflies, waterfalls and chocolate farms. An overnight stay is better than a day trip.

- **Quilotoa Lagoon:** 3.5-4 hours each way, so it is a long day. The turquoise crater lake is spectacular, but the hike back up from the water is brutal. Better as part of the multi-day Quilotoa Loop.

- **Papallacta hot springs:** about 1.5 hours east. Thermal pools at 3,300 m surrounded by mountains cost $10-20 entry.

## Common mistakes and scams

- **Ignoring the altitude.** Drink water, go easy on alcohol, and do not plan big hikes for your first 48 hours. Coca tea helps some people.

- **Walking up to El Panecillo.** Always take a taxi.

- **The mustard or "spill" trick.** Someone squirts something on you, a helpful stranger cleans you up, and your bag vanishes. Walk away.

- **Bag slashing on buses.** On the Trole and Ecovía, keep your bag on your front.

- **Taxis without meters.** Insist on the taxímetro or use an app. Unofficial cars at bus terminals are riskier.

- **Phones out on the street.** Snatch thefts happen, especially in La Mariscal and the Old Town at night.

- **Underestimating the sun.** You are near the equator and very high, so you will burn fast even on cool, cloudy days.

## Quito FAQ

### How many days do you need in Quito?

Three days covers the Old Town, the TelefériQo, museums and the equator. Add one or two days for Cotopaxi, Otavalo or Mindo.

### Is Quito safe for tourists?

Quito is reasonably safe if you are careful. The main risks are petty theft and snatch robberies, so use taxis at night, avoid walking up Panecillo and keep your phone away on busy streets.

### Does the altitude in Quito affect you?

Most people feel breathless or get headaches on the first day or two at 2,850 m. Rest, hydrate and avoid alcohol early on.

### What currency is used in Quito?

Ecuador uses the US dollar. Bring small notes, because $50 and $100 bills are often refused.

### What is the best area to stay in Quito?

The Old Town is best for sightseeing, and La Floresta is best for food and a calmer evening scene. La Carolina suits those who want modern hotels near the metro.

### Is the TelefériQo worth it?

Yes, if you go early on a clear morning. Go in the afternoon and you may only see clouds.

### How do I get from Quito airport to the Old Town?

A taxi or app ride takes 45-90 minutes and costs about $25-35. The cheaper option is the Aeroservicios shuttle for around $8 plus a short taxi.

### Can you visit the equator from Quito?

Yes. Mitad del Mundo and Museo Intiñan are about an hour north by taxi, and you need half a day for the round trip.
