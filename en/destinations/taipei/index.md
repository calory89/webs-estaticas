# Taipei in 4 Days: Night Markets, Temples and Hot Springs

> Taipei travel guide by Happy Low Cost, 4-day itinerary. Original page: https://www.happylowcost.com/en/destinations/taipei/
> Updated: 2026-11-21

Taipei is not a pretty city at first glance. It is a grid of tiled apartment blocks, scooters and covered arcades, and then you notice the temple smoke drifting across a side street, the queue for soy milk at 7am and a forested mountain at the end of almost every road. It is one of the safest, cleanest and easiest capitals in Asia, with a metro that works perfectly and food that is cheap and very good.

It suits food lovers, first-timers to Asia, solo travellers and anyone who wants a big city with hiking trails and hot springs a metro ride away. We would give it **4 days**: three for the city itself and one for a day trip to Jiufen, Tamsui or the Pingxi line. Two days is too rushed, and five works well if you add a second day trip.

## What to see in Taipei: the places worth your time

### Elephant Mountain (Xiangshan)

This short, steep hike is the best free view in Taipei, looking straight at Taipei 101 across the Xinyi skyline. Take the MRT Red Line to Xiangshan station and climb about 20-30 minutes of stairs to the famous boulders. It costs nothing. Our tip: arrive 45 minutes before sunset, stay for the city lights, and bring water because the humidity makes those stairs feel twice as long.

### Taipei 101

Once the world's tallest building, it is still the symbol of the city, and the mall at its base is useful for air conditioning and food. The observatory on the 89th floor costs about €17 (NT$600) and takes about an hour including queues. The view is good, but you cannot see Taipei 101 from inside Taipei 101, which is why we prefer Elephant Mountain. Go up if it rains, and do not miss the giant tuned mass damper ball that stops the tower swaying in typhoons.

### National Palace Museum

One of the great museums of Chinese art, with jade, bronzes, ceramics and calligraphy that came over from Beijing's Forbidden City. Entry is about €10 (NT$350) and you need at least 2-3 hours. It sits in Shilin, reached by bus from Shilin MRT station. Go first thing on a weekday to beat the tour groups clustered around the famous Jadeite Cabbage, and note that star pieces are sometimes rotated or on loan.

### Longshan Temple

The busiest and most atmospheric temple in the city, in old Wanhua next to Longshan Temple MRT station. It is free and 30-45 minutes is enough. Locals come here to pray for exams, love and health, so you will see people throwing moon blocks to ask questions of the gods. Visit in the early evening when it is lit up and full of worshippers, then walk to Bopiliao Historic Block nearby.

### Chiang Kai-shek Memorial Hall

A huge white monument with a blue roof, set in Liberty Square between the National Theater and Concert Hall. It is free and takes about an hour. The changing of the guard happens on the hour inside the main hall and is worth catching once. It is also a politically sensitive place, and recent years have seen debate about its future, so check whether the guard ceremony is still running.

### Dihua Street and Dadaocheng

The old trading district by the river, lined with restored shophouses selling dried mushrooms, herbal medicine, fabric and tea. Allow 2 hours to walk it and drop into the Xiahai City God Temple, a tiny temple famous for its matchmaking deity. It is free to wander. Finish at Dadaocheng Wharf for sunset by the Tamsui River, where there are food stalls and beer containers.

### Beitou Hot Springs

A hot spring valley inside the city, at the end of the short branch line from Beitou to Xinbeitou MRT. Walk up through the park to the free Beitou Hot Spring Museum (an old Japanese bathhouse) and Thermal Valley, a steaming green lake. Budget half a day. The public outdoor bath costs only a couple of euros (about NT$60-100), while private rooms in hotels run €20-40 per hour.

### Maokong Gondola

A cable car from Taipei Zoo up into tea plantation hills, about 30 minutes each way. A one-way ride costs around €3-4 (NT$120). At the top, sit in a teahouse with a pot of local tieguanyin oolong and watch the city below. Try for a crystal cabin with a glass floor, and check before going because the gondola closes for maintenance on some days and in bad weather.

### Huashan 1914 Creative Park

A former sake and wine factory turned into galleries, design shops, cafes and exhibition spaces, near Zhongxiao Xinsheng MRT. Free to walk around, with paid exhibitions inside. One to two hours is plenty. It is liveliest on weekend afternoons, and Songshan Cultural and Creative Park, a former tobacco factory, is a similar idea in Xinyi.

### Raohe Street Night Market

Our favourite of the big night markets: one long, walkable street next to Ciyou Temple and Songshan MRT. Entry is free and you can eat well for €8-12. The pepper buns baked in clay ovens at the temple end have a permanent queue for good reason. Go hungry around 6-7pm, before it gets packed.

### Ningxia Night Market

Smaller and more local than Shilin, near Zhongshan and Shuanglian MRT stations, and focused almost entirely on food. Look for oyster omelettes, taro balls and lu rou fan. An hour is enough to graze. Stalls with long local queues are usually the right choice here.

### Ximending

Taipei's youth and shopping district, full of neon, street performers, sneaker shops and bubble tea. It is free and best for an hour in the evening. The Red House, a 1908 octagonal theatre, anchors the area, and the streets around it have one of the city's liveliest LGBTQ+ bar scenes. Ay-Chung's flour rice noodles here are a classic standing snack.

## Things to do in Taipei

The best things to do in Taipei involve eating, soaking and hiking more than ticking off monuments. These are the ones we would plan around.

- **Eat a proper Taiwanese breakfast** at Fuhang Soy Milk, upstairs in Huashan Market by Shandao Temple MRT. Expect a 30-60 minute queue for thick flatbread with egg and warm soy milk, about €3.

- **Do a tea tasting** in Maokong or at a traditional shop on Dihua Street. High mountain oolong is the thing to try.

- **Hike Yangmingshan National Park**, which has volcanic fumaroles, grassland and Qixing Mountain, the highest peak near the city. Buses run from Jiantan and Shilin.

- **Cycle the riverside paths** on a YouBike. Dadaocheng to Guandu along the Tamsui River is flat and easy.

- **Take a cooking class** to learn xiaolongbao or beef noodle soup. Expect about €40-70.

- **Get a foot massage**. Plenty of places around Zhongshan and Ximending charge €15-25 for 40 minutes, and they press hard.

- **Catch a Taiwanese baseball game** at Taipei Dome or Tianmu. Tickets are cheap and the cheering squads are half the show.

## Taipei for free (or almost)

Taipei is easy to do cheaply because many of its best sights cost nothing. Temples are free, the hikes are free, and you can eat a full meal for under €5.

- Elephant Mountain and the other Four Beasts trails (Tiger, Leopard, Lion) are all free.

- Longshan Temple, Bao'an Temple and Xiahai City God Temple: free, always open in the daytime.

- Chiang Kai-shek Memorial Hall and the changing of the guard: free.

- Beitou Hot Spring Museum and Thermal Valley: free, and the public bath costs pennies.

- Huashan 1914 and Songshan Cultural Park grounds: free.

- Treasure Hill Artist Village, a hillside squatter settlement turned art colony near Gongguan: free.

- Night markets: free to walk, and most snacks cost €1-3 (NT$40-100).

## Taipei itinerary: 4 days

### Day 1: Old Taipei and Xinyi

**Morning:** Breakfast at Fuhang Soy Milk, then walk to Chiang Kai-shek Memorial Hall for the changing of the guard.

**Afternoon:** MRT to Longshan Temple and Bopiliao, then lunch nearby. Head to Huashan 1914 for coffee.

**Evening:** Climb Elephant Mountain for sunset, then eat at the Taipei 101 food court or walk to Raohe Night Market (about 20 minutes by MRT).

### Day 2: Museum, Dadaocheng and Ningxia

**Morning:** National Palace Museum when it opens. Allow until lunch.

**Afternoon:** MRT to Dadaocheng and walk Dihua Street, stopping for tea and the City God Temple. Walk to Dadaocheng Wharf for sunset.

**Evening:** Ningxia Night Market for dinner, which is a short walk away.

### Day 3: Hot springs and mountain tea

**Morning:** MRT to Xinbeitou. Walk through Beitou Park, visit the museum and Thermal Valley, and soak in a hot spring.

**Afternoon:** Ride back across the city to Taipei Zoo station for the Maokong Gondola. Drink tea at the top until late afternoon.

**Evening:** Ride the gondola down after dark for city lights, then end in Ximending for snacks and a drink.

### Day 4: Jiufen and the northeast coast

**Morning:** Bus 1062 from near Zhongxiao Fuxing MRT to Jiufen. Arrive early, before the tour groups.

**Afternoon:** Continue to Shifen by bus or train via Ruifang for the waterfall, or go to Houtong cat village.

**Evening:** Back in Taipei for a final beef noodle soup and a foot massage.

## What is overrated (and what to do instead)

**Shilin Night Market.** It is the most famous, but it is huge, sprawling, crowded and more about cheap clothes and games than great food. Go to Raohe or Ningxia instead, where the food is better and you can actually move.

**The Taipei 101 observatory.** It is not bad, just expensive for a view that lacks the tower itself. Elephant Mountain is free and gives you the postcard shot. If you want a drink with a view, a rooftop bar in Xinyi costs about the same as the ticket and comes with a cocktail.

**Jiufen on a weekend afternoon.** The narrow lanes become a slow-moving human queue. Go on a weekday morning, or stay overnight and walk it at dusk when the lanterns come on and the day trippers have left.

**Releasing a sky lantern in Shifen.** It is photogenic, but you are standing on a railway track with hundreds of others, and the paper lanterns end up as litter in the hills. Watch them, then walk to Shifen Waterfall instead.

## When to visit Taipei

The best time to visit Taipei is October to early December, with dry, warm days around 20-27°C. March and April are also good, with mild weather and fewer crowds, though the air can be hazy.

Summer (June to September) is hot and sticky, often over 35°C with afternoon thunderstorms, and July to September is typhoon season. Typhoons can cancel flights and close the city for a day, so book flexible accommodation. May and June bring the plum rains, a period of grey, drizzly weather.

Winter (January and February) is cool and damp, around 12-18°C, which feels colder because buildings have no heating. It is cheaper and quieter, except around Chinese New Year, when prices rise, trains fill up and some small restaurants close for a week.

## Where to stay in Taipei: best areas

Stay near an MRT station and the rest is easy. These are the areas we would choose.

- **Zhongshan:** our pick for most people. Central, close to Ningxia and Dadaocheng, full of cafes and boutique hotels. Mid-range hotels €70-130 per night.

- **Taipei Main Station:** best for transport, with the airport train and high speed rail right here. Hostels from €20-30, hotels €60-110.

- **Ximending:** lively, young and cheap, good for backpackers and night owls. Hostels €18-30, hotels €50-100.

- **Xinyi:** modern towers, shopping malls and Taipei 101. Pricier and less character, best for business travellers or luxury. Hotels €120-300.

- **Da'an:** leafy and residential with great restaurants around Yongkang Street and Dongmen. Good for couples and repeat visitors. Hotels €80-150.

## What to eat in Taipei

Taipei is a food city first. A street lunch costs €3-6 (NT$100-200), and a sit-down dinner at a good local restaurant is €10-25 per person.

- **Beef noodle soup:** rich braised broth, thick noodles and tender beef shank. Try the red-braised version. Lin Dong Fang near Zhongshan is a local favourite.

- **Xiaolongbao:** soup dumplings. Din Tai Fung is famous and consistent, but queues are long, so take a number and go for a walk. Hangzhou Xiaolongbao near the CKS Memorial Hall is cheaper.

- **Lu rou fan:** braised pork over rice, the ultimate cheap meal at about €1.50 (NT$50).

- **Gua bao:** steamed buns stuffed with pork belly, pickled greens and peanut powder.

- **Hujiao bing:** pepper buns baked in a clay oven, best at Raohe.

- **Oyster omelette and stinky tofu:** night market staples. Stinky tofu tastes far better than it smells.

- **Mango shaved ice:** a summer essential, with fresh mango over snowy ice. Yongkang Street has several famous shops.

- **Bubble tea:** invented in Taiwan. Order it half sugar, because full sugar is very sweet.

One rule: the best places often have menus only in Chinese. Use a translation app on the menu or point at what the next table is eating.

## Airport to city centre and getting around

From Taoyuan International Airport, the Airport MRT express train reaches Taipei Main Station in about 35-40 minutes and costs about €4.50 (NT$160). The commuter train takes about 50 minutes for the same price. Buses run to Taipei Main Station for about €4 (NT$140) and take around an hour depending on traffic. A taxi costs €35-45 (NT$1,200-1,500) and takes 40-60 minutes.

Some flights use **Songshan Airport**, which sits inside the city on the Brown Line, so you can be at your hotel in 20 minutes.

Getting around is simple. The MRT is clean, fast and signposted in English, with fares around €0.60-2 (NT$20-65). Buy an **EasyCard** at any station for about €3 (NT$100), top it up, and use it on the metro, buses, YouBike bicycles and even at convenience stores. Day passes and the Taipei Fun Pass exist, but they only pay off if you ride a lot, because single fares are already cheap. Taxis are yellow, metered and honest.

## How much a trip to Taipei costs

Taipei is cheaper than Tokyo or Seoul and far cheaper than Hong Kong, mostly because food and transport cost so little.

- **Shoestring:** €45-60 (NT$1,600-2,100) per day. Hostel dorm, night market meals, MRT, free sights and hikes.

- **Mid-range:** €110-170 (NT$3,900-6,000) per day. Private hotel room, one sit-down meal a day, museum tickets, Maokong, hot springs and occasional taxis.

- **Comfortable:** €250+ per day. Xinyi hotel, cocktail bars, private hot spring rooms and day tours.

## Day trips from Taipei

**Jiufen:** a hillside gold-mining town with lantern-lit teahouses and sea views. Bus 1062 from Zhongxiao Fuxing takes about 1 hour 15 minutes, or take the train to Ruifang and a short bus. Give it half a day.

**Pingxi line (Shifen and Pingxi):** a small branch railway through green valleys, with Shifen Waterfall and old mining villages. Take the train to Ruifang and change. A full day if you combine it with Jiufen or Houtong.

**Tamsui:** a riverside town at the end of the MRT Red Line, about 40 minutes from the centre. Fort San Domingo, the old street, and the sunset at Fisherman's Wharf. Half a day.

**Yehliu Geopark:** odd wind-sculpted rock formations on the north coast, including the Queen's Head. Buses from Taipei Main Station take about 1.5 hours. Entry is about €3 (NT$120). Go early because the main rock draws a crowd.

**Wulai:** an Atayal Indigenous village in the mountains south of the city, with hot springs, a waterfall and a small train. About an hour by bus from Xindian MRT.

## Common mistakes and scams

Taipei has very little crime and almost no tourist scams. The mistakes people make are mostly practical.

- **Eating or drinking on the MRT.** It is banned, even water or gum, and fines start around €45 (NT$1,500).

- **Standing on the wrong side of the escalator.** Stand on the right, walk on the left.

- **Relying only on cards.** Hotels and malls take them, but night markets and small restaurants want cash. Use ATMs in 7-Eleven or FamilyMart.

- **Visiting museums on a Monday.** Many places close that day, so check before you go.

- **Ignoring the weather.** Carry an umbrella year-round, and check typhoon warnings in summer.

- **Booking Jiufen tours that only stop for an hour.** The bus is cheap and you can set your own pace.

- **Tipping.** It is not expected. Many restaurants add a 10% service charge, so check the bill.

## Taipei FAQ

### How many days do you need in Taipei?

Four days is ideal: three for the city and one for a day trip to Jiufen, Tamsui or the Pingxi line. With five days you can add a second trip to Yangmingshan or Wulai.

### Is Taipei expensive to visit?

No, not by East Asian standards. Food and transport are cheap, and you can eat well for under €15 a day if you stick to night markets and local restaurants.

### Is Taipei safe for tourists?

Yes. Taipei is one of the safest big cities in Asia, including at night and for solo women travellers, though normal care with belongings is always sensible.

### Which is the best night market in Taipei?

Raohe is our favourite for food and atmosphere, with Ningxia close behind. Shilin is the largest but the most crowded and touristy.

### Do I need cash in Taipei?

Yes. Carry NT$1,000-2,000 for night markets, small restaurants and taxis, since many smaller places do not take cards.

### Do people speak English in Taipei?

Younger people and hotel staff often speak some English, and the MRT signs are in English. In small restaurants, a translation app helps.

### What is the best month to visit Taipei?

October and November offer the best weather, with dry, warm days and no typhoons. March and April are a good second choice.
