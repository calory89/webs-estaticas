# Las Vegas in 4 days: a no-nonsense guide for first-timers

> Las Vegas travel guide by Happy Low Cost, 4-day itinerary. Original page: https://www.happylowcost.com/en/destinations/las-vegas/
> Updated: 2026-10-21

## Why go to Las Vegas

Las Vegas is absurd, and that is the appeal. You can watch a replica Venetian canal glow at midnight, eat a serious bowl of Thai noodles at 2 a.m. and be standing in red desert canyons by sunrise. Nowhere else in the United States packs so much spectacle into so little space, and flights are often cheap precisely because the city makes its money once you land.

Go with the right expectations. The casinos are designed so you lose track of time, the drinks are cheap only if you gamble, and the Strip is bigger than it looks. Plan a little and it is one of the best-value big trips in the country.

## What to see in Las Vegas

### The Strip on foot, at night

Walk the stretch between Bellagio and the Venetian after dark. Distances are deceptive: two hotels that look side by side can be 20 minutes apart. Wear comfortable shoes.

### Bellagio Fountains and Conservatory

The fountains run every 15 to 30 minutes in the evening and are free. The conservatory inside changes its flower displays with the seasons and costs nothing either.

### Sphere

The giant LED dome near the Venetian opened in 2023. Even if you skip a show inside, the exterior animations are a strange sight from the street.

### Fremont Street Experience

Downtown is older, grittier and cheaper. The canopy light show is touristy, but the surrounding streets, with their vintage signs and lower-minimum tables, give the city some of its soul.

### The Neon Museum

A yard of retired casino signs, best visited at dusk when some are lit. Book ahead, as slots sell out.

### The Mob Museum

Housed in a former courthouse downtown, it covers organised crime and law enforcement with more depth than you would expect.

### High Roller

The observation wheel at the LINQ is pricey for what it is, but a late-afternoon ride gives you a good sense of the city's layout and the surrounding mountains.

### Red Rock Canyon

About 30 minutes west of the Strip, a scenic loop road winds past orange sandstone cliffs. Go early, especially in warm months, and check whether a timed-entry reservation is required.

### Hoover Dam

Roughly 45 minutes southeast. It is an impressive piece of engineering, and the new bypass bridge offers the best view back at the dam.

### Valley of Fire

About an hour northeast, with fiery rock formations and far fewer people than the Grand Canyon. It is my pick if you only have time for one desert trip.

## Things to do

- **See a show.** Cirque du Soleil has several productions on the Strip, and residencies by major artists come and go. Check what is on before booking flights.

- **Play a little, with a limit.** Set a number, take it in cash, and stop when it is gone. Blackjack tables at lower minimums are easier to find downtown.

- **Hit a pool.** Many resort pools are free for guests, and some day clubs charge heavily for entry.

- **Visit the Venetian canals and Grand Canal Shoppes.** Kitsch, yes, but the painted ceiling and gondolas are oddly charming.

- **Drive to Seven Magic Mountains.** Stacked, brightly painted boulders in the desert south of town, good for a quick photo stop.

- **Peppermill Lounge.** A retro, neon-lit bar with a fire pit that still feels like old Vegas.

## Las Vegas itinerary: 4 days, day by day

Three nights is enough for the city itself. Four lets you add a desert day without rushing. More than five and you will want to leave, or you will be spending money you did not plan to.

**4-day itinerary for Las Vegas**

| Day | Plan |
|---|---|
| Day 1 | Settle in, walk the south Strip, see the Bellagio fountains at sunset, then dinner. |
| Day 2 | Morning at the pool or sleeping in. Afternoon at the Venetian and Sphere area, then a show. |
| Day 3 | Early start for Red Rock Canyon or Valley of Fire. Evening downtown on Fremont Street and the Neon Museum. |

## When to go

Spring (March to May) and autumn (October to November) are the sweet spots, with warm days and comfortable nights. Summer is brutal: daytime highs regularly pass 40°C (105°F), and walking the Strip at noon is a bad idea. Hotels are cheaper then, but you will live indoors. Winter days are mild, though nights can drop near freezing and desert mornings are cold. Major conventions and big weekends push room prices up sharply, so check the calendar before you book.

## Where to stay

### Center Strip (Bellagio, Caesars, Cosmopolitan)

The most convenient base for first-timers, with the best views and the highest prices. The Cosmopolitan is stylish and packed with good restaurants.

### South Strip (Mandalay Bay, Luxor, MGM Grand)

Often better value, closer to the airport, and a little less hectic. You will want transport to reach the centre.

### North Strip and Venetian area

Good for suites and Sphere, with a more upscale feel and fewer budget options.

### Downtown (Fremont Street)

The budget pick, with lower table minimums and a more local atmosphere. It is rougher at the edges, so stick to the busy streets at night.

**Budget note:** nearly every Strip hotel adds a daily resort fee, and parking is often charged too. Compare the final price, not the headline rate.

## What to eat and where

Las Vegas has a seriously good food scene, from celebrity-chef restaurants to cheap late-night plates.

- **Buffets:** Bacchanal at Caesars Palace is the famous one. It is expensive, so go hungry and skip breakfast.

- **Thai:** Lotus of Siam, off the Strip, is known for northern Thai dishes. Order the crispy rice salad.

- **Chinatown:** Spring Mountain Road has dozens of restaurants serving hot pot, dim sum, Korean barbecue and ramen, and the prices are far kinder than on the Strip.

- **Pizza and late snacks:** Secret Pizza at the Cosmopolitan is a hallway counter with a long late-night queue. Eggslut in the same hotel does a good breakfast sandwich.

- **Cheap and fast:** In-N-Out is a Western burger chain with a cult following, and the double-double does the job.

- **Downtown:** Carson Kitchen serves modern small plates in a relaxed room.

## Getting from the airport and getting around

Harry Reid International Airport (LAS) is close to the Strip, usually 10 to 20 minutes by road. Taxis and rideshares are the easiest option, with fares of roughly $20 to $35 depending on destination and traffic. Pickup areas are clearly marked. Public buses run from the airport area, but they are slower and not ideal with luggage.

On the Strip itself, walking is the main way to get around, supported by the Las Vegas Monorail on the east side. It is handy for the MGM Grand to Sahara stretch but does not reach every major hotel. RTC buses run along the Strip and downtown, with day passes available. Rideshare is simple for longer hops. A rental car makes sense only if you plan desert day trips, and you can rent for a single day instead of the whole stay.

## Rough daily budget

- **Budget:** about $120 to $180 per day, with a downtown or off-Strip room, cheap eats and free sights.

- **Mid-range:** about $250 to $400 per day, with a Strip hotel, a show ticket every couple of days and decent restaurants.

- **Splurge:** $500 and up, with suites, big-name dining and pool cabanas.

Prices swing wildly depending on events and weekdays. Midweek is almost always cheaper than Friday and Saturday.

## FAQs

### How many days do I need in Las Vegas?

Three nights covers the main sights. Add a day if you want a desert trip.

### Is Las Vegas expensive?

It can be, mostly because of resort fees, show tickets and drinks. Eating off the Strip and visiting midweek keeps costs down.

### Can I walk the whole Strip?

You can, but it is around 7 km (4 miles) from end to end and the heat adds up. Break it into sections and use the monorail or rideshare.

### Do I need a car?

Not for the city. Rent one only for Red Rock Canyon, Valley of Fire or Hoover Dam, or join a guided tour.

### Is it safe for tourists?

The Strip is heavily policed and busy. Use common sense: watch your belongings in crowds, avoid empty streets away from the main areas at night, and keep an eye on your drinks.

### Is Las Vegas worth visiting?

For the traveller who wrote this guide, yes: Las Vegas is absurd, and that is the appeal. 4 days are enough for the essentials.

### What to see in Las Vegas in one day?

If you only have one day, follow day 1 of this itinerary: Settle in, walk the south Strip, see the Bellagio fountains at sunset, then dinner.

### What to see in Las Vegas in two days?

With two days, follow days 1 and 2 of this itinerary. Day 1: Settle in, walk the south Strip, see the Bellagio fountains at sunset, then dinner. Day 2: Morning at the pool or sleeping in. Afternoon at the Venetian and Sphere area, then a show.
