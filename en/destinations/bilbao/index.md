# Bilbao in 3 Days: Pintxos, Guggenheim and Beyond

> Bilbao travel guide by Happy Low Cost, 3-day itinerary. Original page: https://www.happylowcost.com/en/destinations/bilbao/
> Updated: 2026-11-21

Bilbao is an old iron-and-shipbuilding town that reinvented itself around a titanium museum and never lost its working-class edges. It's compact, green, often rainy, and has some of the best bar food in Europe. People come for the Guggenheim and stay for the pintxos, the riverside walks and the fact that the city still feels lived-in rather than staged for visitors.

It suits food lovers, architecture fans, couples and anyone who prefers a city where the best plan is wandering from bar to bar. Two days covers the city itself comfortably. **Three days is the sweet spot** because it lets you add a day on the coast or in San Sebastián, which is what we would do.

## What to see in Bilbao: the places worth your time

### Guggenheim Museum Bilbao

Frank Gehry's titanium museum is the reason Bilbao is on the map, and the building alone justifies the trip. Inside, the permanent highlight is Richard Serra's "The Matter of Time", huge steel spirals you walk through. Temporary exhibitions vary a lot in quality, so check what's on before paying.

Allow 2-3 hours. Tickets are around €18-20 for adults, cheaper if booked online. **Our tip:** book a morning slot, and see the exterior twice: once in daylight and once after dark from the La Salve bridge.

### Puppy and Maman

Jeff Koons' giant flower-covered dog guards the museum entrance, and Louise Bourgeois' spider "Maman" stands on the river side. Both are free and take 15 minutes. Go early if you want a photo without forty strangers in it.

### Casco Viejo (the Old Town)

The medieval core is known as the Siete Calles (Seven Streets), a grid of narrow lanes packed with bars, small shops and old balconies. This is where you eat, drink and see how the locals use their city. Allow half a day, partly because you'll keep stopping for pintxos.

Key spots: Santiago Cathedral, Calle Somera for cheap bars, and the shops along Calle Tendería. Free to wander.

### Plaza Nueva

This arcaded neoclassical square is the pintxo headquarters of the old town. Bars line all four sides, and on Sunday mornings there's a small market of stamps, coins and old books. It gets very busy from about 13:00 and again after 20:00. Go just before those peaks to actually reach the counter.

### Mercado de la Ribera

One of the largest covered markets in Europe, sitting on the riverbank in an Art Deco building. Downstairs is the fish and produce market; upstairs there's a gastro area with pintxo stalls. Go in the morning when the stalls are full. The food court is fine but not where Bilbao's best pintxos are. Free to enter, allow an hour.

### Museo de Bellas Artes

Bilbao's fine arts museum is excellent and much less crowded than the Guggenheim: El Greco, Goya, Zurbarán, plus a strong Basque collection. It's in Doña Casilda park, a 5-minute walk from the Guggenheim. Tickets are around €10-12, with free hours on certain days, so check the website. Allow 1.5-2 hours.

### Artxanda Funicular

A short funicular ride from Calle Castaños up to Monte Artxanda gives you the best panorama of the city, river and surrounding hills. A one-way ticket is a couple of euros, and Barik card users pay less. **Our tip:** go at sunset, then eat at one of the asador grill restaurants up top or walk back down.

### The Ría walk and Zubizuri bridge

The riverside promenade from the old town to the Guggenheim and on to Deusto is the backbone of the city. On the way you cross Calatrava's white Zubizuri footbridge, which famously turns slippery in the rain (the city added non-slip matting). Allow an hour to walk it slowly. Free.

### Azkuna Zentroa

A former wine warehouse in Indautxu turned into a culture centre by Philippe Starck. The atrium has 43 columns, each in a different style, and the rooftop pool has a glass bottom you can look up through from the lobby. Free to enter the main areas; 30-45 minutes is enough unless there's an exhibition you care about.

### Basilica of Begoña

The city's patron saint lives up on the hill above the old town, in a Gothic basilica that matters a lot to locals. Take the lift from Calle Esperanza (Ascensor de Mallona) or the stairs from Plaza Unamuno, which are steep but give good views. Free, allow an hour including the climb.

### Vizcaya Bridge (Puente Bizkaia)

The world's oldest transporter bridge, a UNESCO site, links Portugalete and Getxo at the mouth of the river. A hanging gondola carries people and cars across for well under €1; walking the top walkway costs around €10 and is worth it if you're fine with heights. Take metro line 1 or 2 to Areeta or Portugalete (about 25 minutes), and allow 2 hours including a walk along the waterfront.

### Basque Museum (Euskal Museoa)

Small museum in the Casco Viejo explaining Basque culture, fishing, shepherding and traditions. Good on a rainy morning. Tickets are a few euros; allow an hour.

### San Mamés stadium

Athletic Club only fields players from the Basque Country, which makes them a curious case in modern football. Even if you don't follow football, the stadium tour and museum explain a lot about local identity. Around €15-20; better still, see a match if the dates work.

## Things to do in Bilbao

Bilbao is a city for doing more than ticking off monuments. These are the experiences we'd prioritise.

- **A proper pintxo crawl:** one pintxo and one drink per bar, then move on. Start in Plaza Nueva, then Calle Ledesma in the Ensanche for after-work crowds.

- **Pintxo-pote night:** on Thursday evenings (Fridays in some areas), many bars offer a drink plus a pintxo for about €2.50-3. Try Calle Pozas or the old town.

- **A Basque pelota match:** the fast handball and cesta games are local obsessions. Check the Bizkaia frontón schedule.

- **Txakoli tasting:** the young, slightly sparkling white wine of the region is poured from a height. Order it at any bar, or visit a winery around Bakio.

- **Sunday morning in Plaza Nueva:** the flea market, then vermouth with the locals.

- **Surf at Sopelana:** metro line 1 takes you to some of the best beaches on the coast in 30 minutes. Board and wetsuit rental runs about €20-30.

- **Cooking class:** several schools run pintxo classes for about €60-90, a good plan when it rains.

## Bilbao for free (or almost)

A surprising amount of Bilbao costs nothing, because the best things here are architecture and streets.

- The Guggenheim exterior, Puppy, Maman and the fog sculpture outside the museum.

- The riverside promenade, Zubizuri, and Doña Casilda park.

- Casco Viejo, Plaza Nueva and Mercado de la Ribera.

- Azkuna Zentroa's atrium.

- Bellas Artes during its free hours.

- Walking up to Begoña, or up to Artxanda on foot instead of taking the funicular.

- The Vizcaya Bridge gondola, under €1.

- Looking at Norman Foster's metro entrances, nicknamed "fosteritos".

## Bilbao itinerary: 3 days

### Day 1: Old town and the river

**Morning:** start at Mercado de la Ribera, then wander the Casco Viejo, Santiago Cathedral and the Basque Museum. **Afternoon:** climb to Begoña for views, come back down and have lunch with a menú del día. **Evening:** pintxos in Plaza Nueva and Calle Santa María, then a drink by the river.

### Day 2: Guggenheim and modern Bilbao

**Morning:** the Guggenheim first thing, plus Puppy and Maman. **Afternoon:** lunch in the Ensanche, then Bellas Artes and Doña Casilda park, and Azkuna Zentroa on the walk back. **Evening:** the Artxanda funicular at sunset, then pintxos on Calle Ledesma or in Indautxu.

### Day 3: The coast

**Morning:** metro line 1 to Areeta, then cross the Vizcaya Bridge to Portugalete. **Afternoon:** back to Getxo for the old fishing port (Puerto Viejo) and a beach walk, or continue to Sopelana for surf and cliffs. **Evening:** back in town for a last dinner of grilled fish or a txuleta. If San Sebastián is a priority, swap this day for that trip.

## What is overrated (and what to do instead)

**The Guggenheim's interior, depending on the exhibition.** The building is world-class; some of the shows are not. If nothing appeals, admire the outside for free and spend the money at Bellas Artes.

**Eating only in Plaza Nueva.** It's good but busy and slightly pricier. The Ensanche (Calle Ledesma, Calle Diputación) and Indautxu have quieter, often better bars.

**The Ribera food court.** Fine for a quick snack, but it's more food hall than pintxo culture. Visit the market downstairs and eat elsewhere.

**Gaztelugatxe in peak summer.** It's spectacular, but in August you'll need a booking, deal with crowds and climb 241 steps in the heat. Go early in the morning in spring or autumn instead.

## When to visit Bilbao

The best months are **May-June and September-October**: mild temperatures (18-24°C), fewer crowds and reasonable hotel prices. Summer is warm but rarely scorching, and it's the busiest and priciest time, especially during Aste Nagusia, the big city festival in mid-to-late August.

Bilbao rains a lot all year, so pack an umbrella whatever the month. Winter (November-March) is grey and wet but cheap and quiet, and the bars stay full regardless. Expect hotel prices to drop noticeably outside summer and big events.

## Where to stay in Bilbao: best areas

- **Casco Viejo:** for atmosphere and pintxos at your door. It's noisy on weekends. About €80-140 a night for a double.

- **Abando / Ensanche:** central, elegant and well connected, between the old town and the Guggenheim. Our pick for most first-timers. About €100-180.

- **Indautxu:** residential, quieter, with excellent local bars and close to Azkuna Zentroa. About €90-150.

- **Deusto:** across the river from the Guggenheim, calmer and a little cheaper, with an easy metro ride in. About €70-120.

- **Bilbao La Vieja / San Francisco:** creative bars and cheap stays, but rougher around the edges, especially late at night. About €50-90.

## What to eat in Bilbao

Eat pintxos at the counter and save one meal for a proper sit-down Basque restaurant. Pintxos usually cost **€2.50-4.50** each, and a crawl of six or seven with drinks runs about €20-30.

- **Gilda:** olive, anchovy and pickled guindilla pepper on a stick. The original pintxo.

- **Bacalao al pil-pil:** salt cod in an emulsified olive oil and garlic sauce. Very Bilbao.

- **Txipirones en su tinta:** baby squid in their own ink.

- **Kokotxas:** cod or hake throats, a delicacy.

- **Txuleta:** a huge grilled bone-in steak, usually for sharing (about €50-80 per kilo).

- **Talo:** corn flatbread, often with chorizo, especially at festivals.

- **Txakoli and kalimotxo:** the local white wine, and red wine mixed with cola.

Classic addresses: Café Iruña for its tiled neo-Mudéjar interior, La Viña del Ensanche for ham and wine, and Gure Toki in Plaza Nueva for creative pintxos. A weekday menú del día (three courses plus wine) costs about **€15-22**. A sit-down dinner at a good mid-range restaurant is about **€35-50** per person; asadores serving txuleta will push past that.

## Airport to city centre and getting around

The easiest way from the airport to the centre is the **Bizkaibus A3247**, which runs to Plaza Moyua and the Intermodal bus station in about 20-30 minutes for about €3. A taxi costs about €25-35 and takes 15-20 minutes. There is no metro or train to the airport.

The centre is walkable. For longer rides, the metro (two lines, linking to the coast at Getxo, Sopelana and Portugalete), the tram along the river and the Bilbobus buses all work well. Buy a **Barik card** (about €3 deposit and rechargeable): it cuts fares significantly and works on metro, tram, buses, Euskotren and the funicular. The Bilbao Bizkaia Card combines transport and museum entry, but it's only worth it if you plan to visit a lot of museums.

## How much a trip to Bilbao costs

- **Shoestring, about €60-80 a day:** hostel bed (€25-40), menú del día at lunch, pintxo-pote evenings, free sights, Barik transport.

- **Mid-range, about €140-200 a day:** central 3-star hotel (€100-150 per double, split), one museum, a pintxo crawl, one good sit-down meal.

- **Extras:** Guggenheim about €18-20, San Sebastián day trip about €20-30 in transport, a txuleta dinner €50+ per person.

## Day trips from Bilbao

### San Sebastián (Donostia)

La Concha beach, Monte Igueldo and pintxos in the Parte Vieja. Buses from the Bilbao Intermodal station take about 1 hour 15 minutes and cost about €8-15. Take a full day.

### San Juan de Gaztelugatxe

A tiny hermitage on a rocky islet reached by a stone path and steps (Game of Thrones fans will recognise it). Go by car or Bizkaibus towards Bakio, about an hour. A free booking is needed in high season. Half a day.

### Gernika

The town bombed in 1937, now home to a peace museum and the Gernika Tree, the historic symbol of Basque self-government. Euskotren takes about 1 hour. Half a day, or combine with Mundaka.

### Mundaka and Urdaibai

A surf village with a legendary left-hand wave at the edge of the Urdaibai Biosphere Reserve. Euskotren via Gernika, about 1.5 hours. Pair it with Gernika for a full day.

### Getxo and the coast

Mansions along the waterfront, the Puerto Viejo and cliff walks towards Sopelana. Metro line 1, about 25-35 minutes. Half a day.

## Common mistakes and scams

Bilbao is a safe city with very few scams. Most mistakes travellers make are about timing.

- **Eating at the wrong hours:** kitchens open for lunch around 13:30 and dinner around 20:30. Arrive at 18:00 and you'll find only pintxos.

- **Taking pintxos without keeping track:** in some bars you pay at the end and they trust you to say what you ate. Be honest, and remember what you had.

- **Ignoring Monday and Sunday closures:** many shops close on Sundays, and some museums close on Mondays. Check the hours.

- **Not booking Gaztelugatxe:** in high season you can't get onto the path without a reservation.

- **Late nights in San Francisco:** it's fun in the evening, but watch your phone and bag late at night. Pickpockets also work crowded festivals.

- **Forgetting rain gear:** umbrellas sold in tourist areas on rainy days are flimsy and overpriced.

## Bilbao FAQ

### How many days do you need in Bilbao?

Two days covers the city's main sights. Three days is ideal if you want to add the coast, Gaztelugatxe or San Sebastián.

### Is Bilbao worth visiting?

Yes. It combines world-class architecture, superb food and easy access to the coast, and it's usually cheaper and less crowded than San Sebastián.

### Is the Guggenheim Bilbao worth it?

The building is worth seeing no matter what. Whether the inside justifies about €18-20 depends on the current exhibitions, so check before you go.

### Is Bilbao expensive?

It's moderately priced for Spain. Pintxos at €2.50-4.50 and menús del día at €15-22 keep food reasonable, though hotels rise in summer.

### Can you do a day trip from Bilbao to San Sebastián?

Yes, easily. Buses take about 1 hour 15 minutes and run frequently throughout the day.

### Do people speak English in Bilbao?

In hotels, museums and many bars, yes. Basic Spanish helps in traditional places, and you'll see Basque (Euskara) on all signs.

### What is the best area to stay in Bilbao?

Abando/Ensanche for most visitors, Casco Viejo if you want to be among the bars, and Indautxu or Deusto for quieter stays.

### How do I get from Bilbao airport to the city?

Take the Bizkaibus A3247 to the centre (about €3, 20-30 minutes), or a taxi (about €25-35).
