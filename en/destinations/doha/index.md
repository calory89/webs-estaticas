# Things to Do in Doha: A 3-Day Guide Without the Hype

> Doha travel guide by Happy Low Cost, 3-day itinerary. Original page: https://www.happylowcost.com/en/destinations/doha/
> Updated: 2026-11-21

Doha is a city of museums, malls and highways, built fast and mostly in the last 25 years. Underneath the glass towers it is quieter and more interesting than its reputation. The Museum of Islamic Art is one of the best museums in the Gulf, Souq Waqif is a pleasant place to spend an evening even though it was rebuilt, and the desert starts less than an hour from your hotel. Doha does not do nightlife, cheap beer or old towns with centuries of patina. If that is what you want, you will be disappointed.

It suits stopover travellers, museum lovers, families and anyone who wants winter sun six hours from Europe without a long-haul flight. **Three days is the right amount.** That gives you the main sights, one desert trip and a slow evening or two in the souq. With a 24-hour layover you can still see the Museum of Islamic Art, the Corniche and Souq Waqif. A week is too long unless you are lying by a resort pool.

## What to see in Doha: the places worth your time

### Museum of Islamic Art

The Museum of Islamic Art is the best thing to see in Doha. I.M. Pei designed the building, a stack of pale stone cubes that sits on its own island off the Corniche. Inside are ceramics, manuscripts, carpets and metalwork covering about 1,400 years and three continents. Allow 2-3 hours. Entry for non-residents is about QAR 50 (€13). Look up when you walk into the atrium, then go to the big window facing the West Bay skyline. It is the best indoor view in the city.

### National Museum of Qatar

Jean Nouvel based the design on the desert rose crystal, and the building is more impressive than most of the exhibits. The galleries cover geology, Bedouin life, pearl diving and oil, with lots of film projections. Some visitors find it immersive. Others find it thin on objects. Plan on 2 hours and about QAR 50 (€13). If you buy a Qatar Museums pass covering several museums, it usually works out cheaper. Check the current options before you go.

### Souq Waqif

This is the old market, rebuilt in the 2000s in traditional mud-and-timber style. It is touristy, but it is also where Qataris, migrant workers and visitors all spend their evenings. You will find spice and incense stalls, shisha cafés, cheap scarves and some decent restaurants. Entry is free. Go after sunset, when it is cooler and the lanes fill up. Afternoons are dead and many shops close.

### Falcon Souq and the Falcon Hospital

The Falcon Souq is right next to Souq Waqif. Hooded falcons perch on stands while men discuss birds that can cost more than a car. Browsing is free and takes about 30 minutes. Ask politely before taking close-up photos. Most sellers are happy to show off their birds.

### The Corniche

The Corniche is a 7 km waterfront promenade curving from the Museum of Islamic Art towards the West Bay towers. It is free and best walked from late afternoon into the evening, when the skyline lights up. Do not try to cover all of it on foot. Walk the stretch from MIA Park to the dhow harbour, then take the metro (Corniche station, Red Line) onwards.

### MIA Park

MIA Park wraps around the museum and has lawns, cafés and Richard Serra's sculpture "7" at the tip of the promenade. It is free. Come at sunset for the clearest view across the water to West Bay. On some winter weekends there is a bazaar with food stalls.

### Katara Cultural Village

Katara is a purpose-built cultural district between West Bay and The Pearl. It has an amphitheatre, galleries, a tiled mosque, pigeon towers and a public beach. Wandering around is free, while the beach has a small fee and is more relaxed. Spend 2 hours. The real draw for many people is **Chapati & Karak**, a tiny stall serving karak tea and stuffed chapati for a couple of riyals.

### Msheireb Downtown and Msheireb Museums

Msheireb is a rebuilt district right behind Souq Waqif. It is walkable, shaded and full of modern takes on Qatari architecture. The Msheireb Museums occupy four restored heritage houses covering family life, the history of slavery in the Gulf and the oil industry. They are among the most honest exhibits in the country. Give it 1.5 hours. Entry is free or close to it, so check before you go. A free tram loops around the district.

### Qatar National Library and Education City

Rem Koolhaas designed the library as one huge open hall with bookshelves stepping up like terraces. In the basement there is a sunken heritage library holding old manuscripts. Entry is free, and you need about an hour. Take the Green Line to Education City. The Education City Mosque is nearby, with its two minarets rising out of the ground, and visitors can usually look around outside prayer times.

### 3-2-1 Qatar Olympic and Sports Museum

This sports museum sits beside Khalifa International Stadium and is better than it sounds. It is interactive, well designed and especially good with kids. Allow 2 hours and about QAR 50 (€13). Combine it with nearby Aspire Park, which is free and has lots of grass.

### Mathaf: Arab Museum of Modern Art

Mathaf, in Education City, holds one of the strongest collections of modern Arab art anywhere. It is small, rarely crowded and makes a good pairing with the library. Plan on an hour. Entry has historically been free, but confirm before you visit.

### The Pearl-Qatar and Qanat Quartier

The Pearl is an artificial island of marinas and luxury flats. Its Qanat Quartier is a Venice-inspired pastel area of canals and bridges, built mostly for Instagram. It is free to walk around and you need about 1.5 hours. It makes a nice spot for a coffee by the marina, but you do not need to make a special trip unless you like this kind of thing.

### Lusail Boulevard and Place Vendôme

Lusail is the new city north of Doha. Its boulevard with fountains and towers is best seen at night, and the Lusail Stadium from the World Cup final is nearby. Place Vendôme is a giant mall with a dancing fountain show. Both are free to visit, and you need 2 hours. Take the Red Line to Lusail and use the tram to get around inside the district.

## Things to do in Doha

Beyond the museums, the best experiences in Doha are outdoors and mostly happen after the sun drops.

- **Take a dhow cruise:** wooden boats leave from the Corniche harbour near MIA Park. A shared 30-45 minute loop costs about QAR 25-50 (€6-13) per person. You need to haggle. Go at sunset.

- **Go dune bashing at Sealine:** a 4x4 tour into the dunes south of the city runs €60-90 for half a day, including a camel photo stop. A good tour goes on to Khor Al Adaid, the "inland sea" (see day trips).

- **Kayak the Al Thakhira mangroves:** this is calm water north of Doha with herons and crabs. Tours or rentals cost around €30-50.

- **Smoke shisha in Souq Waqif:** pull up a chair on the main lane and watch the evening go by. A shisha costs about QAR 40-60 (€10-15).

- **See a falcon or camel race:** camel racing at Al Shahaniya runs in the cooler months, with robot jockeys controlled from SUVs. Schedules vary, so ask your hotel.

- **Catch a match:** Qatar Stars League tickets are cheap, and the World Cup stadiums are very comfortable venues to watch from.

- **Visit Fire Station:** a converted fire station near the Corniche with artist studios and changing exhibitions. It is often free.

## Doha for free (or almost)

Doha is expensive, but many of its best sights cost nothing.

- Walk the Corniche and MIA Park at sunset.

- Wander Souq Waqif and the Falcon Souq.

- Visit Qatar National Library and see the Education City Mosque from outside.

- Explore Katara's amphitheatre, mosque and pigeon towers.

- Ride the free Msheireb tram and visit the Msheireb Museums.

- Drink karak tea at roadside cafeterias, about QAR 1-2 (€0.30-0.50). It is the cheapest pleasure in the country.

- Take a metro day pass for about QAR 6 (€1.50) and see half the city's skyline above ground.

## Doha itinerary: 3 days

### Day 1: Old Doha and the waterfront

**Morning:** Get to the Museum of Islamic Art first thing, while you still have energy for three floors. Have coffee in the museum café.

**Afternoon:** Walk through MIA Park to the Msheireb Museums (10 minutes on foot), then have lunch in Msheireb. Rest in the hotel through the hottest hours.

**Evening:** Take a sunset dhow cruise from the Corniche, then walk to Souq Waqif and the Falcon Souq. Have dinner and shisha in the souq.

### Day 2: Museums and modern Doha

**Morning:** Visit the National Museum of Qatar, which is walkable from Souq Waqif or one metro stop away (National Museum station, Red Line).

**Afternoon:** Take the Green Line to Education City for the National Library, Mathaf and the mosque.

**Evening:** Go to Katara for sunset, karak and chapati, then take a short taxi to The Pearl to stroll along the marina.

### Day 3: Desert

**Morning to afternoon:** Take a half-day tour to the dunes at Sealine and Khor Al Adaid. Most tours pick you up in the morning or early afternoon and run 5-6 hours.

**Evening:** Head to Lusail Boulevard at night, or go back to Souq Waqif for a final meal. If your flight leaves late, the Red Line goes direct to the airport.

## What is overrated (and what to do instead)

- **Mall-hopping:** Villaggio's indoor canal and gondolas are kitsch rather than fun, and the shops are the same as at home. Spend the time in the Msheireb Museums instead.

- **The Pearl as a destination:** it is pretty but sterile and very pricey. Go to Katara, which is next door and has more life.

- **Hotel-bar nights:** alcohol is only served in licensed hotels, and a beer costs QAR 45-60 (€12-16). Treat it as a one-off and spend your other evenings in the souq, where the atmosphere is better.

- **Cheap desert "safaris" sold at the souq:** some cut the trip short or skip Khor Al Adaid. Book with a company with recent reviews and confirm the itinerary in writing.

## When to visit Doha

The best time to visit Doha is **November to March**, when days are 20-28°C and evenings are made for being outside. December to February is peak season, with festivals, events and the highest hotel prices. October and April are good shoulder months: hot middays, pleasant nights and cheaper rooms. From May to September it regularly passes 40°C with heavy humidity. Life moves indoors and hotel rates drop sharply. That is fine for a stopover, but grim for walking. During Ramadan, eating in public by day is restricted and hours change, but the evenings are lively.

## Where to stay in Doha: best areas

- **Souq Waqif and Msheireb:** the best area for first-timers. You can walk to the souq, the museums and the Corniche, and there are metro stations close by. Boutique souq hotels cost €100-180, and Msheireb's newer hotels run €150-250.

- **Al Sadd and Bin Mahmoud:** the budget base, with apartment hotels and mid-range chains near the Red and Gold Lines. Expect €50-90.

- **West Bay:** towers, big international chains and beach access. It suits business travellers and anyone who wants a pool view. Rooms cost €120-300, and you will take taxis everywhere.

- **The Pearl and Lusail:** quieter and more resort-like, with lots of serviced apartments. Prices are €100-250, but you are far from the old centre.

Our tip: stay near Souq Waqif. Being able to walk back from dinner is worth more than a skyline view.

## What to eat in Doha

Qatari cuisine is close to Gulf and Levantine cooking, and most of what you eat will be cooked by Indian, Yemeni, Syrian and Iranian chefs. That is a good thing. Look out for these dishes:

- **Machboos:** spiced rice with chicken, lamb or fish, the national dish.

- **Harees:** wheat and meat slow-cooked to a porridge, common in Ramadan.

- **Thareed:** bread soaked in meat and vegetable stew.

- **Luqaimat:** fried dough balls with date syrup.

- **Karak:** sweet, cardamom-heavy milk tea.

- **Mandi:** Yemeni slow-cooked meat on rice. Bandar Aden in Souq Waqif is a reliable place to try it.

In Souq Waqif, Damasca One is the Syrian classic, with mezze and grills. Parisa is the place for Iranian food in an over-the-top mirrored dining room. A lunch of shawarma or biryani at a cafeteria costs €3-8 (QAR 12-30). A sit-down dinner in the souq runs €15-30 per person. A hotel restaurant with wine easily reaches €60-100. The roadside cafeterias are where you get the best value, so do not skip them.

## Airport to city centre and getting around

Hamad International Airport is about 15-20 minutes from the centre. The **Doha Metro Red Line** goes from the airport to Msheireb (the interchange for Souq Waqif) in about 20 minutes. Standard class costs about QAR 2 (€0.50), and Gold Club costs about QAR 10. Buy a travel card at the machines. A standard day pass is about QAR 6 (€1.50).

**Taxis:** Karwa taxis from the airport rank cost about QAR 50-80 (€13-20) to the centre or West Bay. Uber and Careem are usually cheaper and widely used.

**In the city:** the metro has three lines (Red, Green, Gold) and is clean and air-conditioned. Free Metrolink feeder buses connect stations to neighbourhoods. Lusail and Msheireb have their own trams, and the Msheireb one is free. Doha is not a walking city outside the old centre and the Corniche, so expect to take short Uber rides.

## How much a trip to Doha costs

- **Shoestring, €60-90 per day:** budget hotel in Al Sadd (€50-70), metro, cafeteria meals, free sights, one paid museum.

- **Mid-range, €150-250 per day:** 4-star hotel near the souq, sit-down dinners, Uber, museums, one dhow cruise.

- **Extras:** desert tour €60-90, a hotel bar drink €12-16, kayaking €30-50.

Accommodation is the real cost. Look for stopover hotel deals sold alongside Qatar Airways flights, which can be very cheap.

## Day trips from Doha

- **Khor Al Adaid (Inland Sea):** a UNESCO-listed spot where dunes meet the sea, on the Saudi border. It takes about 1.5 hours by 4x4 and you can only go with a tour or an experienced driver. A half day is enough.

- **Al Zubarah Fort and archaeological site:** a UNESCO-listed former pearling town and a restored fort in the northwest. It is about 1.5 hours by rental car or taxi, so plan a half day.

- **Richard Serra's "East-West/West-East":** four steel plates stretching about a kilometre across the Brouq Nature Reserve near Zekreet. It takes about 1.5 hours to drive, and the final stretch is unpaved, so ideally go by 4x4. Combine it with Zubarah.

- **Al Khor and the Al Thakhira mangroves:** a fishing town with a corniche, plus kayaking in the mangroves. It is about 45 minutes by car.

- **Al Shahaniya:** camel racing in season and the Sheikh Faisal Bin Qassim Al Thani Museum, a big, eccentric private collection. It is about 40 minutes by car.

Car rental is easy and petrol is cheap, but the main roads are fast and aggressive. Avoid driving in the dunes yourself.

## Common mistakes and scams

- **Underestimating the heat:** even in April, midday walks are punishing. Plan museums for the middle of the day and outdoor sights for the evening.

- **Dress code:** cover shoulders and knees in museums, malls, government buildings and the souq. Women need a headscarf in mosques (abayas are often provided).

- **Taking unofficial taxis:** stick to Karwa, Uber or Careem. Unlicensed drivers at the airport and the souq overcharge.

- **Not haggling on dhows:** the first price is often double the real one. Agree on a price per person and the length of the trip before boarding.

- **Bringing in alcohol or vapes, or carelessly packed medicines:** check the import rules. Drinking in public or being drunk in public can get you arrested.

- **Photographing people without asking:** this especially applies to women and families. Always ask first.

- **Arriving on a Friday morning:** many places open only after midday prayers, and the metro starts later. Plan around it.

## Doha FAQ

### How many days do you need in Doha?

Three days covers the main sights, a desert trip and some relaxed evenings. With one day you can still do the Museum of Islamic Art, the Corniche and Souq Waqif.

### Is Doha worth visiting?

Yes, especially as a stopover or a winter sun break. It has world-class museums, safe streets and easy desert access, but do not go expecting nightlife or a historic old town.

### Can you do Doha on a layover?

Yes. With 8 hours or more and a visa-free nationality, take the Red Line to Msheireb, visit the Museum of Islamic Art and walk to Souq Waqif. Allow plenty of time to go back through security.

### Is Doha expensive?

Hotels and alcohol are expensive. Transport, karak tea and cafeteria food are cheap. A careful traveller can manage on €60-90 a day.

### What should I wear in Doha?

Wear loose clothing that covers shoulders and knees in public. Swimwear is fine at hotel pools and private beaches.

### Can tourists drink alcohol in Doha?

Yes, but only in licensed hotel bars and restaurants, and it is pricey. Drinking in public is illegal.

### What is the best time of year to visit Doha?

November to March, when temperatures are comfortable. Summer is extremely hot but much cheaper for hotels.

### Is Doha safe for tourists?

Doha is one of the safest cities in the world for street crime. The main risks are heat, fast traffic and breaking local laws without knowing it.
