Miami travel guide: what to see, eat and when to go (3-4 days)

🏨 Hotels in Miami

Why go to Miami

Miami works because it is two trips in one. There is the beach city, with warm water, pastel lifeguard towers and Art Deco hotels. And there is the Latin American city, where Spanish is the default, coffee comes in thimble-sized cups and half the conversation is about Havana, Bogotá or Caracas. Add a decent art scene and the Everglades 45 minutes away, and you get a destination that rewards more than a tan.

It is also expensive and spread out, so a little planning goes a long way. That's what the rest of this guide is for.

What to see in Miami

  • South Beach and Ocean Drive: Yes, it's touristy. Go anyway, but go early, around 7 a.m., when joggers outnumber posers and the Art Deco facades catch the light. Walk Lummus Park and skip the overpriced terrace drinks.
  • Art Deco Historic District: Start at the Art Deco Welcome Center on Ocean Drive and just look up. The hotels along Ocean Drive and Collins Avenue are the best free architecture lesson in Florida.
  • Wynwood Walls: A cluster of large murals by international street artists, with more painted on the surrounding blocks. Entry to the walled courtyard is ticketed. Come on a weekday morning to avoid the crowds.
  • Little Havana: Calle Ocho (SW 8th Street) is the spine. Watch the domino players at Máximo Gómez Park, buy a cigar, and drink a cafecito at a walk-up window. Evenings bring live music at places like Ball & Chain.
  • Vizcaya Museum and Gardens: An early-1900s Italian-style villa on Biscayne Bay, with formal gardens that deserve at least an hour. Probably the most beautiful thing in the city that isn't a beach.
  • Coral Gables: Leafy, Mediterranean-style and calmer than the beach. See the Biltmore Hotel and swim in the Venetian Pool, a spring-fed pool carved from an old quarry.
  • Pérez Art Museum Miami (PAMM): Strong contemporary collection, a waterfront setting and hanging gardens. The terrace view of the bay is half the point.
  • Key Biscayne: Cross the Rickenbacker Causeway to Bill Baggs Cape Florida State Park. There's a historic lighthouse, a quiet beach and none of the South Beach noise.
  • Design District: Luxury boutiques, yes, but also public art and architecture you can enjoy without buying anything.
  • Everglades National Park: Alligators, birds and mangroves. Airboat rides are noisy and very popular. For something quieter, try the Shark Valley tram or bike loop.

Things to do

Rent a bike or scooter and ride the Miami Beach boardwalk, which runs for miles along the dunes. Take a boat tour on Biscayne Bay to see the waterfront mansions of Star Island. Browse Lincoln Road and the quieter Española Way in the late afternoon. Visit the Frost Science museum with kids, or spend a sunset on a rooftop in Brickell. If you like lively evenings, Miami delivers: clubs on the beach, salsa in Little Havana, and relaxed bars in Wynwood. Haulover Beach has a clothing-optional section, if that is your thing.

How many days and a suggested route

Three full days covers the essentials. Four lets you slow down or add the Everglades. A week is only worth it if you want to add a Keys road trip.

  • Day 1, South Beach: Early walk on Ocean Drive, Art Deco tour, beach time, then dinner and a stroll on Lincoln Road.
  • Day 2, the city: Wynwood in the morning, Design District for lunch, Little Havana for the late afternoon and dinner.
  • Day 3, bay and gardens: Vizcaya, then Coconut Grove for a waterfront lunch, then Coral Gables or Key Biscayne.
  • Day 4, optional: Everglades half-day, or PAMM and a boat tour if the weather is poor.

When to go

The best window is November to April: dry, around 24 to 28 °C, and comfortable at night. It is also the priciest. March brings spring break crowds and big events like the Miami Open, and early December is Art Basel, when hotel rates jump.

From June to October it is hot, humid and wet, with highs of about 32 °C and sudden afternoon storms. Hurricane season runs June to November, peaking from August to October. The upside is cheaper flights and rooms, and the sea is like a warm bath. If you travel then, buy flexible fares and travel insurance, and plan indoor backups.

Where to stay

  • South Beach: Best for first-timers who want the beach and nightlife on the doorstep. Noisy, and not cheap. Budget travellers can look at hostels like Freehand Miami, which also has private rooms.
  • Mid-Beach (Collins Ave, 30s to 50s): Calmer, with larger resort hotels and wider beaches. A good choice for couples and families.
  • Brickell and Downtown: Towers, restaurants and the free Metromover. Handy for transit, and often better value than the beach midweek.
  • Wynwood and the Design District: Good for art lovers and food-focused trips, but you'll need rideshares to reach the sand.
  • Coconut Grove and Coral Gables: Quiet, green and residential. Good if you have a car and want peace.

Watch for resort fees and daily parking charges, which can add a lot to the advertised rate.

What to eat and where

Start with Cuban food. A Cuban sandwich (ham, roast pork, Swiss cheese, pickles, pressed hard) is the classic. Add croquetas, pastelitos and a cafecito from any ventanita, the walk-up coffee windows found across the city. Versailles on Calle Ocho is the famous sit-down option, and it's lively and unapologetically Cuban. For a cheaper, quicker bite, look for fritas (Cuban-style burgers) at El Palacio de los Jugos.

Seafood is the other pillar. Joe's Stone Crab in South Beach is an institution, open only from mid-October to mid-May, and it is a splurge. Zak the Baker in Wynwood is excellent for breakfast and sandwiches. Around the city, you'll also find strong Peruvian, Colombian, Venezuelan and Haitian food, so order beyond the obvious. Finish with key lime pie, though quality varies wildly.

Getting from the airport and getting around

Miami International (MIA) sits west of downtown, about 15 to 25 minutes from the centre by road. The Metrorail Orange Line links the airport area with Brickell and downtown for a couple of dollars. For South Beach, the Route 150 Airport Flyer bus is the cheap option. Taxis and rideshares to the beach usually cost somewhere around $35 to $55. Fort Lauderdale airport (FLL) is about 45 minutes north and often has good deals, so compare both. The Brightline train connects downtown Miami with Fort Lauderdale and Orlando.

Within the city, the Metromover loop in downtown and Brickell is free, as are the Miami Beach and City of Miami trolleys. Rideshares fill the gaps. A car makes sense only for the Everglades, Key Biscayne or the Keys, and beach parking is pricey. If you stay on the beach and don't plan day trips, skip the rental.

Rough daily budget

  • Budget: $120 to $170. Hostel bed, Cuban lunches, free beach and public transport.
  • Mid-range: $250 to $400. Decent hotel, restaurant dinners, a tour or museum, some rideshares.
  • Comfortable: $500 and up. Beachfront hotel, good restaurants, cocktails and private transfers.

These are rough figures. Hotel prices swing hard by season, and tipping (around 18 to 20 percent) adds to every bill.

Miami FAQs

Is Miami worth visiting without a car?

Yes, if you stay on the beach or in Brickell. Trolleys, Metromover, buses and rideshares cover most places. You only need a car for the Everglades or the Keys.

How many days do I need?

Three days gives you South Beach, Wynwood, Little Havana and Vizcaya. Add a fourth for the Everglades or a slower pace.

Is Miami safe for tourists?

Tourist areas are generally fine with normal city sense. Don't leave valuables in cars, be careful late at night in unfamiliar areas, and use rideshares after dark.

When is the cheapest time to visit?

From late May to October. Expect heat, humidity and storms, plus hurricane risk, but flights and hotels are noticeably cheaper.

Do I need Spanish?

Not really, as English works everywhere. But a few words of Spanish will be appreciated, especially in Little Havana and other neighbourhoods where Spanish is spoken first.
