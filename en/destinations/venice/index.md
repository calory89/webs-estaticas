# Venice travel guide: how to spend 3 days without the crowds

> Venice travel guide by Happy Low Cost, 3-day itinerary. Original page: https://www.happylowcost.com/en/destinations/venice/
> Updated: 2026-10-21

## Why go to Venice

Venice is absurd, and that is the point. A city of palaces built on wooden piles, with no cars, where the ambulance is a boat and the bin collection is too. You can see the photographs a thousand times and still stop dead when you come out of the station and the Grand Canal is just there.

The honest caveat: the busiest streets between the station, Rialto and St Mark's can feel like a conveyor belt from about 10:00 to 17:00. The fix is simple. Get up early, stay at least one night, and spend half your time in the quieter districts. Venice rewards people who wander badly.

## What to see in Venice

### St Mark's Square and Basilica

Go at 8:00 or after dinner, when the square is nearly empty and the orchestras at the cafés are still playing. Book the Basilica in advance for the gold mosaics, and skip the café coffee unless you enjoy paying a small fortune for a view.

### Doge's Palace

The seat of Venetian power, with huge council chambers and the Bridge of Sighs connecting it to the old prison. Buy a timed ticket online and allow two hours.

### The Grand Canal by vaporetto

Take line 1 from Piazzale Roma or the station down to San Marco. It is slow, it stops everywhere and it costs a fraction of a gondola. Try for a seat outside near the front, ideally late in the day.

### Rialto Bridge and market

The bridge is more crowded than beautiful at midday. Come for the market instead: the fish market (closed Sunday and Monday) and the fruit stalls are best before 10:00.

### Dorsoduro: the Accademia and Peggy Guggenheim

The Gallerie dell'Accademia holds the great Venetian painters. The Peggy Guggenheim Collection, in her old canal-side home, is the place for Picasso, Pollock and Ernst. Walk afterwards to Santa Maria della Salute and along the Zattere, a wide, sunny promenade facing Giudecca.

### Frari and Scuola Grande di San Rocco

In San Polo, the Basilica dei Frari has Titian's Assumption over the altar. Five minutes away, the Scuola di San Rocco is covered in Tintoretto from floor to ceiling. Bring a jacket; the ceiling mirrors they hand out are worth using.

### Cannaregio and the Jewish Ghetto

The world's original Ghetto, established in 1516, is still a living quarter with synagogues and a small museum. The nearby Fondamenta della Misericordia and Fondamenta degli Ormesini are good for an aperitivo.

### Castello and the Arsenale

Walk east past the old shipyards toward the Giardini and Sant'Elena. This is where you will meet Venetians hanging washing and playing football, not tourists with selfie sticks.

### San Giorgio Maggiore

Take the vaporetto across for the bell tower. The view of St Mark's and the lagoon is better than from the more famous Campanile, and the queue is usually shorter.

### Burano and Murano

Burano is the one with the painted houses and the lace; go early for photos. Murano is for glass, and for a look at a furnace demonstration if you can find an honest one. Torcello, one stop past Burano, is nearly empty and has a remarkable old cathedral.

## Things to do

- **Cicchetti crawl:** hop between bacari (wine bars) for small bites and a glass of wine or an ombra.

- **Traghetto crossing:** the standing-room gondola ferries across the Grand Canal cost a couple of euros and give you the gondola feel for almost nothing.

- **Gondola ride:** official rates are around 80 euros for roughly 30 minutes, per boat. Fine for a splurge, but not essential.

- **Get lost on purpose:** turn the map off in Cannaregio or Castello for an hour.

- **Lido day:** bike or walk the island, with a beach and a very different, calm atmosphere.

## How many days and a suggested route

Two full days is the minimum; three is comfortable, and gives you one for the islands. Day trippers should know that Venice charges an access fee (roughly 5 to 10 euros) on selected peak days, bookable online. Guests staying overnight in the city are exempt, but check the current rules before you travel.

- **Day 1:** early St Mark's Square, Doge's Palace, lunch in Castello, afternoon in the Arsenale area, sunset on the Zattere.

- **Day 2:** Rialto market, Frari and San Rocco, Dorsoduro museums, cicchetti in the evening, Cannaregio for dinner.

- **Day 3:** boat to Burano and Torcello in the morning, Murano on the way back, San Giorgio Maggiore at the end of the day.

## When to go

April, May, late September and October are the sweet spot: mild, long evenings, still busy but manageable. July and August are hot, humid and packed, with mosquitoes thrown in. November to January can bring acqua alta, the seasonal high tides that flood low streets for a few hours. The MOSE barriers now protect the city from the worst, but pack waterproof shoes anyway. Winter is cold and often foggy, and it is also the moodiest, quietest and cheapest time. Carnival in February brings crowds and high prices again.

## Where to stay

### Cannaregio

Residential, close to the station, with the best food-to-price ratio. Good for mid-range travellers who want local atmosphere.

### Dorsoduro

Students, galleries and a calmer pace. A bit pricier, and the nicest area for an evening walk.

### Castello

Quieter toward the east, with some of the best-value rooms near the lagoon edge.

### San Marco and San Polo

Central, convenient and the most expensive. Worth it if you want to be in the middle of everything, otherwise skip them.

### Mestre (budget)

On the mainland, ten minutes by train from Santa Lucia. Hotels can cost half as much, but you lose the empty-streets mornings and evenings, which is the best part of Venice.

## What to eat and where

Start with cicchetti: little plates of baccalà mantecato (whipped salt cod) on bread, sarde in saor (sweet and sour sardines with onions), meatballs and fried olives. Mains to look for include risotto al nero di seppia (cuttlefish ink), bigoli in salsa (thick pasta with onions and anchovy) and fritto misto from the lagoon. The aperitivo is a spritz, ideally the local Aperol or Select version.

- **Cantina Do Mori** near Rialto, a very old bacaro, small and loud.

- **All'Arco**, also near Rialto, popular for market-fresh cicchetti.

- **Cantine del Vino già Schiavi** in Dorsoduro, by a boatyard canal, with a good wine list and quick snacks.

- **Pasticceria Tonolo**, also Dorsoduro, for a morning pastry and coffee standing at the bar.

A rule of thumb: if the menu has photos and a waiter is standing in the street, walk on. Prices drop sharply within three streets of St Mark's and Rialto.

## Getting from the airport and getting around

Marco Polo Airport (VCE) is on the mainland. The cheapest and fastest option is the bus to Piazzale Roma (ATVO or ACTV line 5, about 20 to 25 minutes, a few euros). The Alilaguna boats are slower but scenic, and take 60 to 90 minutes depending on the stop. A water taxi is quick and glamorous, but costs well over 100 euros per boat. Many low-cost flights land at Treviso (TSF), about 40 minutes by bus to Piazzale Roma or Mestre station.

Once in the city, you walk. A single vaporetto ticket costs around 9 to 10 euros, so for more than two or three rides, a 24, 48 or 72-hour pass is better. Wear comfortable shoes, because you will cross dozens of small bridges with steps, and leave the large suitcase at home if you can.

## Rough daily budget

- **Budget:** 80 to 110 euros, with a hostel or Mestre hotel, cicchetti lunches and one museum.

- **Mid-range:** 150 to 250 euros, with a central double room, sit-down dinner and a couple of tickets.

- **Comfortable:** 300 euros and up, with a canal-view hotel, nice restaurants and a gondola.

Prices swing a lot between seasons and with events, so treat these as a guide only.

## FAQs

### Is Venice worth it if it is so crowded?

Yes, if you plan for it. Go early and late, stay overnight and spend time in Cannaregio, Castello and Dorsoduro.

### How many days do you need in Venice?

Two full days for the main sights, three if you want to see the islands or take it slower.

### Is it better to stay in Venice or Mestre?

Venice, for atmosphere and quiet evenings. Mestre is fine if your budget is tight, as the train is fast and frequent.

### Do I need to book anything in advance?

St Mark's Basilica and the Doge's Palace are worth booking, as are any popular dinners. Check the access fee calendar if you visit for the day.

### Is a gondola ride worth the price?

It is a pleasant splurge but not essential. A traghetto crossing or a vaporetto along the Grand Canal gives you much of the feeling for a few euros.
