Los Angeles travel guide: what to see, eat and skip (4 days)

🔥 4 deals to Los Angeles🏨 Hotels in Los Angeles

Why go to Los Angeles

Los Angeles is not one city you can take in from a single viewpoint. It is a few dozen neighbourhoods stitched together by freeways, and it takes about a day to stop expecting a centre. After that it gets good. You can eat superb tacos at lunch, hike to a sign-framed view in the afternoon and watch the sun drop into the Pacific before dinner. Few big cities let you do all that in one day.

It rewards people who pick a few areas and go deep. It punishes anyone who tries to cross town at 5 pm to tick off one more thing.

What to see: the places worth your time

  • Griffith Observatory. Admission is free, and the view over the basin to the Hollywood Sign is the best orientation you will get. Go on a weekday morning or at dusk. Parking is tight, so walk up from Los Feliz or take the DASH shuttle.
  • The Getty Center. A hilltop museum with free entry (you pay for parking), good art and gardens, and a view that makes the whole city make sense. Allow three hours. The tram from the car park is part of the fun.
  • Santa Monica Pier and the beach. The pier is touristy but cheerful, and the sand is wide and easy. Walk or cycle south along the path towards Venice.
  • Venice Boardwalk and the canals. The boardwalk is a loud circus of skaters, street performers and vendors. Ten minutes inland, the residential canals are quiet and lovely. Do both.
  • Downtown: Walt Disney Concert Hall, The Broad and Grand Central Market. Gehry's steel curves are worth a look even if you skip the concert. The Broad has free general admission with a queue. The market is a historic food hall that is best at lunch.
  • LACMA and Urban Light. The museum's campus on Wilshire is undergoing a big rebuild, but the lamp-post installation out front is free to see at any hour. It is a popular photo stop for good reason.
  • The Getty Villa. A Roman-style villa above the Pacific, holding the Getty's antiquities. Entry is free but needs a timed reservation. A good pairing with a Malibu beach stop.
  • Olvera Street and Union Station. The oldest corner of the city. Olvera is a bit souvenir-heavy, but Union Station next door is a beautiful 1930s building and a useful transport hub.
  • The Arts District. Warehouses turned into murals, coffee roasters and galleries. Good for an unhurried afternoon walk.

Hollywood Boulevard, with its Walk of Fame and the TCL Chinese Theatre forecourt, is a ten-minute stop at most. It is crowded and a bit scruffy. See it once so you can say you did.

Things to do

  • Hike at sunrise or late afternoon. Runyon Canyon is easy and social, while the Griffith Park trails give you a proper view of the sign.
  • Watch a sunset from the Santa Monica shoreline or from a Malibu beach. El Matador is the one with sea stacks and a staircase down, so wear shoes you do not mind getting sandy.
  • Spend a day at Universal Studios Hollywood if you are travelling with children or are a film obsessive. Book ahead and arrive at opening time.
  • Browse the shops and vintage stores on Melrose Avenue or in Silver Lake. Catch a game at Dodger Stadium in season, because the setting is lovely.
  • Take a studio tour. Paramount and Warner Bros. both run them, and they are more interesting than most of Hollywood Boulevard.

How many days and a suggested route

Four days is the sweet spot for a first visit. Three is rushed, and five lets you add Pasadena or a day on the coast. Group things by area, not by a checklist.

  • Day 1, Downtown. Union Station, Olvera Street, Grand Central Market for lunch, the Broad and the Disney Hall, then the Arts District for dinner.
  • Day 2, Hollywood and the hills. Griffith Observatory in the morning, a quick look at Hollywood Boulevard, lunch in Los Feliz or Silver Lake, then Melrose.
  • Day 3, the Westside. The Getty Center in the morning, then Santa Monica for a late lunch and an afternoon on the beach. Walk or cycle to Venice for sunset.
  • Day 4, your choice. Malibu and the Getty Villa for coast, a studio tour for cinema, or Koreatown and Thai Town for a food crawl.

When to go: weather and seasons

LA is sunny and dry for most of the year, but the details matter. Spring and autumn are the best seasons, with warm days and little rain. The coast can be grey in May and June, locals call it "May Gray" and "June Gloom", and the sun often burns through by early afternoon. July to September is hot, especially inland, while the beach stays several degrees cooler. Most rain falls between December and March, usually in short bursts.

Autumn brings Santa Ana winds and elevated wildfire risk. Parts of Pacific Palisades and Altadena were badly hit by fires in early 2025, so check current conditions and access before planning Malibu or hillside hikes.

Where to stay: areas and budgets

  • Santa Monica. Best for beach lovers and for travelling without a car. It has the Metro E Line to Downtown and plenty of walkable streets. Prices are on the high side.
  • Hollywood. Central, well connected by the Metro B Line and full of mid-range hotels and hostels. It is not the prettiest area, but it is practical.
  • Downtown. Good for transit, since all the major lines meet here. Hotels are decent value on weekends. It can feel empty and rough around the edges in the evenings, so choose your block with care.
  • Silver Lake and Los Feliz. Cafes, independent shops and a local feel. Smaller hotels and rentals are the main choices.
  • West Hollywood. Nightlife, restaurants and a lively LGBTQ+ scene. It is central for the Westside and pricey.
  • Venice. Atmospheric and a bit scruffy. Good if you want boardwalk mornings and a creative neighbourhood.

Budget travellers can find hostel beds and basic motels at a modest price. Expect mid-range hotels to cost roughly $180-300 a night and upscale places well above that. Always check the "resort fee" and parking charges before you book.

What to eat and where

LA is one of the best food cities in the country, partly because of its immigrant communities and partly because the produce is excellent.

  • Tacos. Street stands and trucks are a rite of passage. Look for al pastor sliced from the trompo, with pineapple, onion and salsa. Boyle Heights and East LA are strong hunting grounds.
  • Korean barbecue in Koreatown. Grilled galbi at the table and plenty of banchan. Go in a group and share.
  • Pastrami at Langer's. The hot pastrami on rye near MacArthur Park is a classic. Order it and nothing fancy.
  • French dip at Philippe the Original. A sandwich counter near Union Station with sawdust on the floor and a long history. Cheap and filling.
  • Grand Central Market. A one-stop sampler of ramen, pupusas, tacos and egg sandwiches, best at an off-peak hour.
  • In-N-Out. A California burger chain that is simple, cheap and worth one visit. Ask for it "animal style" if you like it messier.
  • Japanese food in Sawtelle and Little Tokyo. Ramen, sushi counters and good bakeries.

Getting from the airport and getting around

LAX is the main gateway, with Burbank a smaller, easier alternative for the north. From LAX, rideshares and taxis use designated pick-up areas, and the trip to Santa Monica or Hollywood can take 30 minutes or an hour and a half depending on traffic. The FlyAway bus runs to Union Station for a fraction of the taxi fare. A free shuttle also connects the terminals to the LAX/Metro Transit Center, where you can pick up the Metro.

The city is built around cars, and renting one gives you freedom, especially for Malibu and the hills. Traffic is heavy, parking is expensive and rush hour can be punishing. You can manage without one if you stay in Santa Monica, Hollywood or Downtown and use the Metro (A, B, D, E and K lines) with a reloadable TAP card. Fares are under two dollars a ride. Buses cover the rest, and rideshares fill the gaps. Plan around one or two areas per day and the city becomes manageable.

Rough daily budget

  • Tight: about $120-170 a day with a hostel bed, Metro, tacos and free sights like Griffith and the beach.
  • Comfortable: about $250-400 with a mid-range hotel, a rental car or regular rideshares, and a restaurant dinner.
  • Treat yourself: $500 and up, with upscale hotels, tasting menus and a studio tour or theme park ticket.

Prices are approximate, and accommodation is the line that moves most. Tax and tips are added on top of menu prices, so allow roughly 18-20% for a tip in restaurants.

FAQs

Do I need a car in Los Angeles?

Not strictly, but it helps. If you base yourself near a Metro line and stick to a few areas, you can manage with trains, buses and the occasional rideshare. For Malibu and spread-out sights, a car is much easier.

How many days do I need?

Four days covers the main areas without a rush. Three works if you keep it tight, and five gives you room for a day trip.

Is Los Angeles safe for visitors?

Mostly, with ordinary big-city care. Do not leave anything visible in a parked car, take care around Downtown and parts of Hollywood late at night, and keep valuables out of sight on the Metro.

Where is the best place to stay for a first visit?

Santa Monica if you want a beach and an easy base, or Hollywood if you want a central, cheaper option. Downtown suits you if you plan to rely on the Metro.

Is the Hollywood Sign worth a special trip?

Not a special trip. You can see it from Griffith Observatory and from several viewpoints, so you do not need to hike right up to it.
