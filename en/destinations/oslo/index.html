A traveller's guide to Oslo: sights, food and budget tips

🔥 2 deals to Oslo🏨 Hotels in Oslo

Why go to Oslo

Oslo is a capital that feels more like a big town with a fjord attached. You can eat shrimp on the harbour at lunch, ride the metro up into pine forest in 30 minutes, and be back for dinner in a neighbourhood full of natural-wine bars. It is expensive, and I won't pretend otherwise. But the best things here (the waterfront, the parks, the forest, the swimming spots) cost nothing, and the museums are among the best in Northern Europe.

It also works well as a short break. Everything central is walkable or one tram ride away, and the city rarely feels crowded outside peak summer.

What to see in Oslo

  • Vigeland Sculpture Park (Frogner Park): More than 200 bronze and granite figures by Gustav Vigeland, free and open all hours. Go early on a weekday, when the monolith is nearly yours alone.
  • The Opera House: You can walk up the sloping white marble roof for free. Come at sunset and watch the fjord turn pink.
  • MUNCH: The tall museum in Bjørvika dedicated to Edvard Munch. The top floors have good views, and it's the easiest way to see more than just The Scream.
  • The National Museum: Norway's largest art collection, in a big, calm building near the City Hall. Allow two hours and skip the rest of the day's plans.
  • Akershus Fortress: A medieval castle and later military base over the harbour. The grounds are free and the views of the fjord are good. It's lovely in the late afternoon.
  • Bygdøy peninsula: Home to the Fram Museum (polar ships), the Kon-Tiki Museum and the Norwegian Folk Museum. The Viking ship collection has been closed for rebuilding as a new museum, so check opening dates before you count on it.
  • Holmenkollen: The ski jump above the city, with a museum and a long view over the fjord. Take the T-bane line 1 up; the ride is half the fun.
  • Tjuvholmen and Aker Brygge: A modern waterfront district with the Astrup Fearnley Museum and a sculpture park by the water. Touristy at the edges, but pleasant for a stroll.
  • Grünerløkka and the Akerselva: A former mill district, now full of cafés and vintage shops. Follow the river path upstream for waterfalls and old factories turned into studios.

Things to do

Take a ferry to the fjord islands. Hovedøya, a short hop from the city centre, has ruins of a monastery, rocky coves and small swimming spots. The ferries run on the regular city ticket, so it's cheap.

Try a sauna. Oslo has taken to floating and waterfront saunas in a big way. Several operate in the harbour, and the cold plunge into the fjord afterwards is the whole point. Book ahead in summer and winter weekends.

Head into Nordmarka, the forest north of the city. Take the T-bane to Sognsvann and walk the lake loop, roughly 3 km and flat. In winter, locals ski there after work. Bring a snack and a thermos.

For a view with your cake, ride to Frognerseteren at the end of line 1. The old restaurant is known for apple cake and the panorama over the city and fjord.

How many days and a suggested route

Three days is a comfortable length. Two will do if you skip the museums on Bygdøy.

Day 1, city centre: Start at the Opera House, walk to MUNCH, then cross to Akershus Fortress. Lunch at the harbour, then the National Museum. End with a drink in Tjuvholmen.

Day 2, west and parks: Vigeland Park in the morning, then lunch around Frogner. In the afternoon take the ferry or bus to Bygdøy for one or two museums. Dinner in Grünerløkka.

Day 3, outdoors: Holmenkollen in the morning, then a forest walk or a ferry to Hovedøya. Finish with a sauna if you can get a slot.

When to go

June to August is the easiest time. Days are long, and in June it barely gets dark; temperatures are usually 15 to 25°C. Locals swim off the islands and everyone eats outside. It's also the most expensive period for hotels.

May and September are my pick. Quieter, cheaper, and still mild enough for walking. Autumn colours in Nordmarka are lovely in late September.

Winter is dark and cold, often around -5 to 0°C, with only about six hours of daylight in December. But there's snow, cross-country skiing close to the metro, and a cosy café culture. Pack proper boots. Spring thaw (March and April) is the least appealing: grey, wet and slushy.

Where to stay

  • Sentrum and around Oslo S: The most practical base for first-timers, close to trains, the airport express and most sights. It's a bit rough around the central station at night, but fine overall.
  • Grünerløkka: Best for cafés, bars and a local feel. Fewer big hotels, more apartments.
  • Frogner and Majorstuen: Quiet, residential, elegant, close to Vigeland Park. Good if you want calm.
  • Bjørvika: Modern and close to the Opera House and MUNCH. Convenient, though less character.

As a rough guide: hostel dorms cost around 450 to 700 NOK a night, simple private rooms and budget hotels about 900 to 1,500 NOK, and mid-range hotels 1,500 to 2,500 NOK. Weekends in summer can be cheaper than weekdays, because business travellers drive the weekday prices.

What to eat and where

Norwegian food is better than its reputation, especially seafood. At Rådhusbrygge, by the City Hall, you can buy a bag of boiled shrimp straight from the fishing boats, with bread, mayonnaise and lemon. It's the most Oslo lunch there is.

Look out for kjøttkaker (meatballs in brown gravy with lingonberries), fiskesuppe (creamy fish soup), brunost (sweet brown cheese on waffles or bread) and skillingsboller, the cinnamon buns local bakeries do well. Reindeer and cured fish show up at traditional restaurants, but they're pricey.

Mathallen, the food hall by the Akerselva in Vulkan, is a handy spot to try a few things at once. For coffee, Oslo takes it seriously: Fuglen and Tim Wendelboe have international reputations. Wine and beer are heavily taxed, so a pint typically costs 90 to 110 NOK. Budget for that, or do as locals do and drink less, but better.

The cheapest filling meals are hot dogs at kiosks, bakery lunches, and the lunch specials at Asian and Middle Eastern places in Grünerløkka and Tøyen.

Getting from the airport and getting around

Oslo Gardermoen (OSL) is about 45 km north of the city. The Flytoget express train reaches Oslo Central Station in about 20 minutes and costs roughly 230 to 250 NOK. The regional trains run on the same line, take around 25 to 30 minutes, and cost about half. For most people, the regional train is the sensible choice. Airport buses are slower and not much cheaper.

Some budget airlines use Sandefjord Torp, about two hours away by bus. Factor in that time and the transfer cost before you celebrate a cheap fare.

Within the city, Ruter runs trams, buses, the T-bane (metro) and ferries on one ticketing system. A single ticket is around 40 to 50 NOK and is valid for an hour; day passes make sense if you ride a lot. Buy through the Ruter app. The centre is compact, so you'll walk plenty. The Oslo Pass, covering transport and many museums, only pays off if you plan to visit several paid sights a day.

Rough daily budget

  • Tight budget: around 900 to 1,200 NOK. Dorm bed, bakery and supermarket meals, free sights, public transport.
  • Mid-range: around 1,800 to 2,800 NOK. Private hotel room, a couple of museums, a restaurant dinner with a drink.
  • Comfortable: 3,500 NOK and up. Better hotel, restaurant meals, saunas and paid tours.

Cards are accepted almost everywhere, including small kiosks; you hardly need cash. Tap water is excellent, so bring a bottle.

FAQ

Is Oslo worth visiting for a weekend?

Yes. Two or three days covers the main museums, the waterfront and a taste of the forest or islands.

Is Oslo really that expensive?

Yes, particularly food, alcohol and hotels. You can keep costs down with free sights, bakery lunches and self-catering.

Do I need Norwegian kroner?

The currency is the Norwegian krone, but you'll rarely need cash. Cards and phone payments work almost everywhere.

Can I see the Northern Lights in Oslo?

Almost never. Oslo is too far south and too bright. For the aurora you need to travel to northern Norway.

What's the best way to get from Oslo airport to the centre?

Take the regional train from Gardermoen to Oslo Central. It's about 25 to 30 minutes and cheaper than the Flytoget express.
