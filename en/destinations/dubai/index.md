# Dubai travel guide: what to see, eat and skip in four days

> Dubai travel guide by Happy Low Cost, 4-day itinerary. Original page: https://www.happylowcost.com/en/destinations/dubai/
> Updated: 2026-10-21

## Why go to Dubai

Dubai is a city built at absurd speed, and that is both the appeal and the catch. You get the world's tallest tower, reliable sunshine, spotless beaches and a food scene drawn from half the planet. You also get heat, distances and malls the size of small towns. Go with the right expectations and it's a surprisingly easy, rewarding trip. Go expecting old-world charm everywhere and you'll be disappointed.

The trick is to mix the spectacle with the older parts of town along the Creek, where the city still feels like a trading port.

## What to see in Dubai

### Burj Khalifa and Downtown

Book the observation deck for sunset and buy the ticket online in advance. The view as the lights come on across the desert is the one time the hype holds up. Afterwards, watch the Dubai Fountain from the waterfront promenade; the show runs every thirty minutes or so in the evening.

### The Dubai Mall

Even if you hate shopping, walk through it once. The aquarium window is free to look at, and the sheer scale tells you something about the city. Go on a weekday morning, because weekends are a crush.

### Al Fahidi Historic District

Also called Bastakiya. Low wind-tower houses, sandy lanes, small galleries and courtyard cafés. Come early, before the heat, and stop for tea at the Arabian Tea House.

### Dubai Creek and the abra boats

Cross the Creek on a wooden abra for about one dirham. It's the cheapest and best boat ride in the city, linking Bur Dubai and Deira in a few minutes.

### Gold Souk and Spice Souk

Deira's souks are touristy but still worth an hour. The Spice Souk smells of saffron, dried lime and frankincense; the Gold Souk is mostly window shopping unless you know your carats. Haggling is expected.

### Jumeirah Mosque

One of the few mosques open to non-Muslims, via guided visits (check the current schedule). Dress modestly and go with an open mind and a good camera.

### Museum of the Future

The torus-shaped building is striking from outside, and the interior exhibits are well made. Book ahead, as slots sell out.

### Dubai Frame

A giant picture frame in Zabeel Park with views of both old and new Dubai. Fun, quick, and a good way to grasp the city's geography.

### Palm Jumeirah

The monorail ride and the Atlantis resort are the headline acts, but the best bit is the view of the skyline from the western crescent. Do it once, don't spend a day.

### Alserkal Avenue

A warehouse district in Al Quoz with galleries, specialty coffee and concept stores. It's the closest Dubai gets to alternative culture, and it's genuinely good.

## Things to do

- **Desert safari:** dune driving, a camel stop and a barbecue dinner. It's commercial, yes, but the dunes at sunset are lovely. Choose a smaller-group operator if you can.

- **Beach time:** Kite Beach has food trucks and a sporty crowd, JBR is lively with a view of Ain Dubai, and La Mer is more relaxed and photogenic. For quiet, go early morning.

- **Marina walk:** stroll the waterfront at dusk when it's cooler, then eat along the promenade.

- **Global Village:** a seasonal fair of pavilions and street food, running roughly from late autumn to spring.

- **Skiing in a mall:** Ski Dubai is silly and fun if the kids are along, or if you simply want to see snow in the Gulf.

## Dubai itinerary: 4 days, day by day

Three full days covers the essentials. Four lets you slow down, which in summer heat matters. Five suits those wanting the desert, a beach day and a day trip to Abu Dhabi.

**4-day itinerary for Dubai**

| Day | Plan |
|---|---|
| Day 1 | Old Dubai. Al Fahidi in the morning, the abra across the Creek, the souks, then dinner in Deira or Bur Dubai. |
| Day 2 | Downtown. Museum of the Future or the Frame in the morning, the Dubai Mall at midday, Burj Khalifa at sunset, the fountain after dark. |
| Day 3 | Coast. Jumeirah Mosque, a beach morning, an afternoon around Palm or Marina, evening at JBR or La Mer. |
| Day 4 | Desert. A late-afternoon safari, or a lie-in and Alserkal Avenue if you've had enough of sand. |

## When to go

November to March is the sweet spot, with pleasant days around 24 to 30°C and cooler evenings. It's also the most expensive season. April and October are warm but workable. From June to September, daytime temperatures often pass 40°C with heavy humidity, so you'll live between air-conditioned spaces. Prices drop sharply then, and if you're mostly after a hotel pool and a few indoor sights, it can be a bargain.

Ramadan shifts the rhythm. Daytime eating and drinking in public is discouraged, many cafés open late, and evenings are festive. It's interesting, but check the dates before booking.

## Where to stay in Dubai

### Budget: Deira and Bur Dubai

The old districts have the cheapest rooms, the best cheap food and easy metro and abra access. Not glamorous, but practical and full of life.

### Mid-range: Downtown, Business Bay and JLT

Downtown puts you near the main sights; Business Bay is slightly cheaper with similar metro links. Jumeirah Lakes Towers is residential, quieter and good value.

### Beach and families: Dubai Marina and JBR

Walkable, with beach access, a tram and plenty of restaurants. It gets lively at night, so ask for a room away from the main strip if you sleep lightly.

### Splurge: Palm Jumeirah and Jumeirah

Resort hotels with private beaches. You're paying for the pool and service, not for convenience, as you'll rely on taxis to get anywhere. A tourism dirham fee is added per room per night, so check the final price.

## What to eat and where

Dubai is a city of migrants, and the best meals reflect that. Start with **shawarma** from any busy counter: a few dirhams, wrapped and ready in a minute. Try **machboos**, a spiced rice dish with lamb or chicken, and **luqaimat**, sweet fried dumplings drizzled with date syrup. Finish the evening with **karak chai**, strong, sweet and milky.

For Emirati food in a calm setting, head to the Al Fahidi area. For grilled meats, Al Ustad Special Kebab in Bur Dubai has been a favourite for decades. Ravi's in Satwa is a classic for cheap, hearty Pakistani curries, and Bu Qtair near the Jumeirah fishing harbour does simple fried fish with sauces, with sea air thrown in. For a splurge, the Marina and Downtown are packed with high-end tables.

Alcohol is served only in licensed venues, mostly hotels and some clubs, and it's pricey. Plan for that.

## Getting from the airport and getting around

Dubai International (DXB) sits close to town. The **Red Line metro** stops at Terminals 1 and 3 and runs to the Burj Khalifa/Dubai Mall station in roughly 25 to 30 minutes for a few dollars. Terminal 2, used mainly by budget carriers, has no direct metro, so take a taxi or a short transfer. A taxi to Deira takes 10 to 15 minutes, and to Downtown or the Marina 20 to 40 minutes depending on traffic, typically around 10 to 30 USD.

Get a rechargeable **Nol card** for the metro, trams, buses and abras. The metro is clean, cheap and air-conditioned, and the front carriages are reserved for women and children, with a Gold class option. Taxis and ride-hailing apps are plentiful and affordable for short hops. Don't plan to walk between sights. Distances are bigger than they look, and the heat is real.

## Rough daily budget

- **Budget:** around 80 to 120 USD per day, with a simple room in Deira or Bur Dubai, cheap local meals and public transport.

- **Mid-range:** around 200 to 300 USD, with a good hotel, a restaurant dinner and an attraction ticket.

- **Comfortable or luxury:** 500 USD and up, mostly driven by the hotel.

The big costs are accommodation and attractions. Eating well can be cheap if you skip hotel restaurants.

## FAQs

### Is Dubai safe for tourists?

Yes, it's one of the safest cities in the world for visitors. Just respect local laws on public behaviour, drugs and alcohol.

### What should I wear?

Casual, covered clothing is fine in malls and the street. Cover shoulders and knees for mosques and traditional districts. Swimwear belongs on beaches and by pools.

### Do I need a visa?

It depends on your passport. Many nationalities get visa-free entry or visa on arrival, but check the official requirements before booking.

### Is Dubai very expensive?

It can be, but it doesn't have to be. Hotels and big attractions drive the cost, while metro rides, street food and the Creek are cheap.

### Can I visit Abu Dhabi from Dubai?

Yes, easily. It's roughly 90 minutes by road or intercity bus. The Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque is the main reason to go, and it makes a good day trip.

### How many days do you need in Dubai?

4 days are enough to see the essentials of Dubai without rushing. The route in this guide is organised day by day.
