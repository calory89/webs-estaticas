Agadir is not old Morocco, and it doesn't pretend to be. An earthquake flattened the city in 1960, so what you get is a modern, low-rise resort with wide boulevards, a long sandy beach, reliable sunshine and almost none of the hassle you find in Marrakech. The medina vibe, the snake charmers and the maze of alleys are elsewhere. Here the draw is the 6 km beach, the fish, the mild winter weather and a coastline that gets wilder and better the further north or south you drive.

It suits sun-seekers escaping a European winter, families, surfers using it as a base for Taghazout, and anyone who wants a gentle first taste of Morocco on a cheap direct flight. Three days is the right amount for the city itself plus one day trip. Stay longer only if you plan to lie on the beach, surf or explore the Souss region. Many people book a week, get bored of the promenade by day four, and wish they'd split the trip with Taroudant or Essaouira.

What to see in Agadir: the places worth your time

Agadir Oufella (the kasbah hill)

The ruined 16th-century kasbah on the hill above the port is the best viewpoint in Agadir and the one sight you shouldn't skip. Little remains apart from walls and the gate, but the view over the bay, the marina and the whole beach is excellent. The giant Arabic letters on the hillside read "God, Country, King". Allow about an hour up top. Our tip: go for sunset, then come down before dark, when the car park gets chaotic.

The Agadir Oufella cable car

The cable car runs from the Founty side near the old Talborjt area up to the kasbah and is the easiest way up. A return ticket costs roughly €8-10 (80-110 MAD). The ride takes a few minutes and the lower station has a small exhibit on the old city. Taking a petit taxi up and walking down works too and costs less, but the gondola is good fun on a clear day. Queues build in late afternoon, so go before 4pm or accept a wait.

Souk El Had

Souk El Had is a huge walled market with thousands of stalls, and it's the most Moroccan thing inside the city. Locals do their weekly shopping here for fruit, spices, olives, argan oil, tagine pots, slippers and phone chargers. Give it 2 hours and wander the produce sections, not just the tourist-facing stalls by the main gate. It's closed on Mondays and busiest on Sundays. Expect to haggle on anything that isn't food.

The beach and the promenade

The main beach is wide, clean, sandy and long enough that you can always find space. The paved promenade (the corniche) runs along it from the marina south towards the big hotels, lined with cafés, ice cream and joggers. It's free, and you'll end up here every evening whether you plan to or not. The Atlantic is cool even in summer (around 19-21°C) and the waves are mild, but swim near the lifeguard flags.

Agadir Marina

The marina at the northern end of the beach is a polished development of apartments, yachts and restaurants. It's pleasant for a coffee or an evening stroll, but prices run 30-50% above the rest of town. Half an hour is enough. Boat trips and fishing excursions leave from here, usually €25-40 for a half day.

The fishing port and fish market

Agadir has one of the biggest sardine ports in Africa, and the working harbour is a real contrast to the marina next door. Just outside the port entrance, a row of simple grill stalls serves the day's catch. You pick your fish, they weigh it and grill it, and you eat it with bread and salad for €6-12. It's the best meal in town for the price. Go at lunchtime, agree the price per kilo before they cook, and ignore the touts waving menus.

Medina Polizzi (Medina d'Agadir)

This is a reconstructed "medina" built from the 1990s by Italian artist Coco Polizzi in Ben Sergao, south of the centre. It's a stage set, not a historic town, but the craftsmanship is good: carved doors, traditional building techniques, workshops and quiet courtyards. Entry is around €4 (40 MAD), and an hour covers it. It's quiet and photogenic. Pair it with Souk El Had or a taxi run south, because it's not walkable from the beach.

Amazigh Heritage Museum

The small Musée du Patrimoine Amazigh off Avenue Hassan II explains the Berber culture of the Souss region through jewellery, carpets, tools and pottery. It takes 45 minutes and costs a couple of euros. It's worth doing on an overcast morning, and it gives context to the silver you'll see in every souk.

Jardin de Olhão and the Memory Museum

This quiet walled garden near the Amazigh museum houses the small Mémoire d'Agadir exhibition with photos of the city before and after the 1960 earthquake. The pictures make the city make sense. Garden entry is free and the museum costs a few euros. Allow 30-45 minutes.

Vallée des Oiseaux

The Valley of the Birds is a strip of green running down from Avenue Hassan II towards the beach, with aviaries, a few animals and playgrounds. Entry is free or close to it. It's a nice shortcut for families with small children, though it's no zoo, so 20 minutes is plenty.

Crocoparc

Crocoparc is a crocodile park in Drarga, about 15 km east, with several hundred Nile crocodiles and well-kept botanical gardens. Tickets are roughly €10-12 (110-130 MAD) for adults. Plan on 2 hours plus the taxi. It's the best rainy or windy day option with kids, and the gardens are better than you'd expect.

Inezgane market

Inezgane, the town next door, is where Agadir's working population actually shops. Its market is cheaper, louder and far less touristy than Souk El Had, and it's the main transport hub for grand taxis and buses. Go if you want an unfiltered half-morning and you don't mind crowds.

Things to do in Agadir

Most of the best things to do in Agadir involve getting out of the hotel zone. Here's what we would book:

  • Surf lessons in Taghazout or Tamraght. The breaks north of the city are some of the best in Morocco. A group lesson with board and wetsuit costs about €25-35, and multi-day packages are cheaper per day.
  • A hammam and gommage. A local public hammam costs a few euros, and you bring your own kit or buy it outside. Hotel spas charge €25-50 for scrub and massage. The local version is more authentic, the spa version more comfortable.
  • Camel or horse ride on the beach. Short rides start around €10-15, typically south of the city towards the Souss river mouth. Agree price and duration up front.
  • Quad biking in the dunes. Tours run south towards the Souss and cost €30-50 for 2 hours. It's touristy, but fun if you go with a reputable operator and wear the helmet.
  • Watching the sunset with mint tea from a café on the corniche, then walking the beach after dark when locals come out.
  • Birdwatching at the Souss river mouth just south of town, where flamingos and waders gather in winter. It's free and best at low tide.
  • Golf. Agadir has several decent courses (Golf du Soleil, Golf de l'Océan, Golf des Dunes), and winter green fees are a fraction of Spain's.

Agadir for free (or almost)

Plenty of what to see in Agadir costs nothing. The beach and promenade are free, as is the walk along the marina. Wandering Souk El Had costs nothing until you start buying. The Vallée des Oiseaux and the Jardin de Olhão are free or symbolic.

Walking up to Agadir Oufella instead of taking the cable car takes about 40 minutes from the port side. It's steep and exposed, so do it early. Watching the fishing boats unload at the port, the Souss estuary birds, and the Friday afternoon atmosphere around the big mosques are all free. For a cheap meal, grilled sardines at the port and a bowl of bissara (fava bean soup) from a local café cost under €3.

What is overrated (and what to do instead)

  • The marina restaurants. They're pretty but overpriced, and the food is generic. Eat fish at the port grills or tagine in Talborjt for half the price.
  • The "Paradise Valley" day tours. The palm gorge near Imouzzer is lovely, but on organised tours in high season it's crowded and littered. Go early with a private taxi or a rental car, or skip it for Taroudant.
  • Staying all week in the hotel strip. The all-inclusive zone is comfortable and forgettable. Split your stay with a few nights in Taghazout or Taroudant.
  • Buying argan oil in the city souks. Much of it is diluted. Buy at a women's cooperative on the road to Essaouira or Taroudant, and check that it's labelled with the cooperative's name.

When to visit Agadir

Agadir is a year-round destination, and that's its whole selling point. Winter (November to March) brings daytime highs of 20-23°C and cool nights, which is why Northern Europeans flock here. It's also when flight and hotel prices are highest around Christmas and February half-term.

Spring (April-May) and autumn (late September-October) are our favourite times: warm, quieter and cheaper. Summer is rarely brutal on the coast, at 26-28°C, but morning sea fog is common in June and July, and Moroccan domestic tourists fill the beach in August. Occasional chergui winds from the desert can push temperatures well above 35°C for a few days. During Ramadan, many cafés close by day and the mood changes, though tourist restaurants keep serving. Surfers want October to April for the swells.

Where to stay in Agadir: best areas

  • Seafront (Boulevard du 20 Août / Secteur Touristique): the big hotels and beach access. It suits first-timers, families and people who want to walk to the sand. Expect €60-150 a night mid-range, more for big resorts.
  • Talborjt and the city centre: cheaper, more local and full of cafés, a 15-minute walk from the beach. Guesthouses and simple hotels cost €25-50.
  • Marina: modern apartments, quiet and polished, with easy access to the port and the kasbah. Plan on €70-140.
  • Founty / Sonaba (south): newer resorts and the golf courses, with a calmer beach. It's good for couples who don't need nightlife. Prices run €70-200.
  • Taghazout / Tamraght (25 km north): surf camps, hostels and boutique places. It's best for surfers and younger travellers. A dorm costs €12-20 and a nice double €50-120.

What to eat in Agadir

Fish is the reason to eat in Agadir. Order grilled sardines at the port, fish tagine with tomato and peppers, and pastilla au poisson (seafood pastilla, a local twist on the sweet chicken version). Tagine with lamb and prunes and couscous on Fridays are the classics. For breakfast, try amlou (argan oil, almond and honey spread) with msemen or harcha flatbreads, which is a Souss speciality. Ask for bissara on a cool morning, and drink mint tea everywhere.

A local lunch in Talborjt or at the port costs €4-8 (40-90 MAD). A sit-down dinner with a starter, tagine and drinks in a mid-range place runs €12-20 a person, and at the marina or in a hotel restaurant €25-40. Alcohol is only sold in licensed hotels, bars and a few restaurants, and it isn't cheap: a beer is around €3-5.

Airport to city centre and getting around

Agadir Al Massira airport is about 25 km southeast of the centre. The simplest option is a grand taxi at fixed rates posted at the exit, around €20-30 (200-300 MAD) to the hotel zone. The ride takes 25-35 minutes. To Taghazout, expect €35-45. A cheaper route is a taxi to Inezgane and a city bus from there, but the airport bus service is patchy, so check the current options on arrival if you're travelling light.

In town, orange petit taxis are cheap (€1-3 for most rides). Insist on the meter (the "compteur"), as drivers often skip it for tourists. City buses run by Alsa cost under €1 a ride and cover Inezgane and the coast up to Taghazout. Agadir has also added a modern BRT bus line along its main axis. There's no tourist travel pass worth buying: pay per ride. Renting a car (from €25-35 a day) makes sense only for day trips.

Day trips from Agadir

  • Taghazout and Tamraght: 30-40 minutes north by bus or taxi. Surf, cafés and a more relaxed vibe make for a half or full day.
  • Taroudant: about 1h15-1h30 east by grand taxi or bus. It's a walled market town with intact ramparts and two good souks, often called "little Marrakech" but much calmer. This is the best full day out.
  • Imouzzer Ida Outanane and the palm gorge: about 1h-1h30 inland on a winding road. Rock pools on the way and waterfalls in Imouzzer that run only after rain. Go early.
  • Souss-Massa National Park: about 1 hour south. Coastal wilderness, birds and, with luck, the rare northern bald ibis. It's best with a guide or car.
  • Essaouira: 3-3.5 hours north by bus (Supratours or CTM). It's too far for a comfortable day trip, so stay a night if you can.

Common mistakes and scams

The classic is the unmetered taxi. Ask for the compteur before you move, and if the driver refuses, get out. At the port grills, agree the price per kilo and confirm the weight before your fish hits the coals, because inflated bills are common.

Avoid the "cooperative" tours sold by hotel touts that end in a hard-sell argan shop. Fake guides at Souk El Had will offer to "show you the best shop": decline politely and keep walking. Don't drink tap water, and don't exchange money with street changers, since banks and airport bureaux give fair rates. One more mistake is visiting Souk El Had on a Monday, when it's shut.

Agadir FAQ

How many days do you need in Agadir?

Three days covers the main sights in Agadir plus a day trip to Taghazout or Taroudant. Add more only for beach time or surfing.

Is Agadir worth visiting?

Yes, if you want sun, a good beach and easy, low-hassle Morocco. If you want historic medinas and old architecture, go to Fes, Marrakech or Essaouira instead.

Is Agadir safe for tourists?

Agadir is one of the safest and most relaxed cities in Morocco. Use normal precautions with valuables on the beach and in crowded markets.

Can you swim in the sea in Agadir?

Yes. The main bay is fairly sheltered and lifeguarded in season, though the water stays cool at around 17-21°C depending on the month.

What is the best month to visit Agadir?

April, May and October offer warm weather, fewer crowds and lower prices. Winter is great for escaping the cold but costs more.

Can women dress in shorts in Agadir?

Swimwear and shorts are fine on the beach and in the hotel zone. Cover shoulders and knees in Souk El Had, Inezgane and smaller towns out of respect.

Do I need to speak French in Agadir?

No. English is widely spoken in hotels and tourist areas, but a few words of French or Darija help in taxis and markets.

What to see in Agadir in one day?

What to see in Agadir in two days?
