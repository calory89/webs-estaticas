# Things to Do in Siem Reap and Angkor: 3 Days, No Fluff

> Siem Reap and Angkor travel guide by Happy Low Cost, 3-day itinerary. Original page: https://www.happylowcost.com/en/destinations/siem-reap-and-angkor/
> Updated: 2026-11-21

Siem Reap is a scruffy, friendly provincial town that exists mostly to serve one of the most extraordinary archaeological sites on earth. The town gives you cheap guesthouses, cold beer on Pub Street and decent Khmer food. Angkor, a few kilometres north, gives you hundreds of temples spread through forest, from the postcard towers of Angkor Wat to sandstone faces at the Bayon and fig roots crushing the walls of Ta Prohm. It suits almost anyone who can handle heat and early alarms: history fans, photographers, families with kids who like climbing, and backpackers on tight budgets.

You need **3 full days**. That matches the 3-day Angkor pass. It gives you one sunrise, the big three temples, the outer ring and a morning out at Banteay Srei or the Kulen hills, with afternoons free for a pool, a nap or the town. One day is a frantic tick-box run. A week only makes sense if you are adding Beng Mealea, Koh Ker and the Tonle Sap.

## What to see in Siem Reap and Angkor: the places worth your time

### Angkor Wat

Angkor Wat is the largest religious monument in the world and the one place here you cannot skip. Go for the bas-reliefs as much as the towers: the galleries run for hundreds of metres, and the Churning of the Ocean of Milk panel on the east side is the highlight. Allow 2-3 hours. Entry is covered by the Angkor pass: 1 day about €34 ($37), 3 days about €57 ($62), 7 days about €66 ($72). Our tip: after sunrise, when the crowds head back to their hotels for breakfast, walk straight in. The upper level (Bakan) has a queue and a dress code (shoulders and knees covered), and it closes on Buddhist holy days.

### Bayon

The Bayon sits at the centre of Angkor Thom and is covered in more than 200 huge smiling stone faces. Up close it is a confusing pile of stone. Once you climb to the upper terrace, the faces stare at you from every angle, and it is far stranger and more personal than Angkor Wat. Allow 1-1.5 hours, included in the pass. Go early morning or after 3pm, when tour buses thin out and the light hits the faces sideways.

### Ta Prohm

Ta Prohm is the jungle temple, left partly unrestored, with silk-cotton and strangler fig roots pouring over the galleries. It is also the "Tomb Raider temple", which explains the queue to photograph one particular tree. Allow 1 hour, included in the pass. Arrive right at opening and you will get the famous roots almost to yourself for 20 minutes.

### Angkor Thom gates and terraces

Angkor Thom is the walled royal city around the Bayon. The South Gate has a causeway lined with gods and demons pulling a giant serpent, and the Terrace of the Elephants and the Terrace of the Leper King are a short walk north. Budget about an hour for the terraces and the Baphuon (the temple-mountain with a reclining Buddha built into its back wall). Ask your driver to drop you at the South Gate and pick you up at the terraces.

### Banteay Srei

Banteay Srei is a small 10th-century temple in pink sandstone with the finest carving at Angkor: lintels so deep and crisp they look cast rather than chiselled. It is about 35 km from town, so it takes a dedicated half-day by tuk-tuk. Inside, 45 minutes is enough. It is included in the pass. Go first thing, before the light flattens and the groups arrive from 9am.

### Preah Khan

Preah Khan is the best reason to do the "grand circuit". It is a huge, half-ruined temple of long corridors, collapsed galleries and trees growing out of the roofs. It feels like Ta Prohm did 20 years ago, with a fraction of the visitors. Allow 1-1.5 hours, included in the pass. Enter from the west and walk through to the east, where your driver can meet you.

### Neak Pean and Ta Som

Neak Pean is a small shrine on an island in a square pool, reached by a long boardwalk across a reservoir. Ta Som nearby has a gate swallowed by a fig tree that rivals anything at Ta Prohm. Together they need about an hour. Both are on the grand circuit and included in the pass.

### Pre Rup

Pre Rup is a steep temple-mountain of brick and laterite that glows orange in late afternoon. It is our pick for sunset over Phnom Bakheng, which now has a visitor cap and a long queue. Allow 45 minutes, included in the pass. Get there 45 minutes before sunset to find a spot on the upper tier.

### Banteay Kdei and Srah Srang

Banteay Kdei is a quiet, crumbling monastery temple. Srah Srang, the royal bathing pool across the road, is a calm sunrise alternative to Angkor Wat. Allow an hour for both, included in the pass. If you hate crowds, do sunrise here and go to Angkor Wat at 7:30am.

### Angkor National Museum

The Angkor National Museum on Charles de Gaulle Road gives you the context the temples do not: who built what, when, and why the faces are smiling. The Gallery of 1,000 Buddhas is the standout. Allow 1.5-2 hours. Entry is about €11 ($12), and an audio guide costs extra. Visit on your first afternoon, before the temples, rather than after.

### Old Market (Psar Chas) and Pub Street

Psar Chas is the town's original market. The tourist stalls of scarves and elephant trousers are on the outside, and the real market sits at the back: fish, dried goods and prahok, the pungent fermented fish paste. Pub Street, a block away, is loud, neon and cheap, with draught beer around €0.50-1. Spend an hour at the market in the morning. Pub Street is best at night, and once is usually enough.

### Wat Preah Prom Rath and Wat Bo

These two working monasteries are free and peaceful. Wat Preah Prom Rath is on the river near Pub Street. Wat Bo, on the east bank, has 19th-century murals inside its main hall. Allow 30-45 minutes each. Dress modestly and leave a small donation if you go inside.

## Things to do in Siem Reap and Angkor

Siem Reap is more than temples, and some of the best evenings here have nothing to do with sandstone.

- **Phare, the Cambodian Circus:** acrobatics and theatre by graduates of a social enterprise school in Battambang. It is the best night out in town. Tickets run about €18-35 depending on seat.

- **Cycle the temples:** the small circuit is flat, about 30 km round trip, and shaded in parts. Bike rental costs €2-4 a day, and e-bikes around €8-10.

- **Take a Khmer cooking class:** many restaurants run half-day classes with a market visit, about €20-30.

- **Visit the APOPO Visitor Center:** meet the rats trained to sniff out landmines. It is short, moving and good for kids. About €9 ($10).

- **Visit the Cambodia Landmine Museum:** founded by a former child soldier who cleared mines himself. It is near Banteay Srei, so combine the two. About €5.

- **Go to a quad bike or horse ride at sunset:** rides through rice fields and villages on the edge of town. It is touristy but fun, from about €25.

- **Get a Khmer massage:** one hour costs €6-10 around Sivatha Boulevard. Skip the €2 fish foot spas on Pub Street.

## Siem Reap and Angkor for free (or almost)

The Angkor pass is the big unavoidable cost, but plenty of things are free. The riverside path along the Siem Reap River is pleasant for an evening walk, as are the Royal Independence Gardens with their fruit bats roosting in the trees at dusk. The Wat Preah Prom Rath and Wat Bo monasteries cost nothing.

**Artisans Angkor**, near the Old Market, runs free workshop tours where you can watch young craftspeople carving stone and wood. The Angkor Night Market and the Old Market cost nothing to wander. You can also drive past the outer moat of Angkor Wat at golden hour without entering the park, because the road along the moat is public.

## What is overrated (and what to do instead)

- **Sunset at Phnom Bakheng:** there is a capped number of people, a long climb and a hazy view of distant Angkor Wat. Pre Rup or Srah Srang are better.

- **Chong Kneas floating village:** it is the closest to town and the most notorious for overpriced boats and "donate rice to the orphanage" pressure. Kampong Khleang or Kampong Phluk are more genuine.

- **Pub Street dinners:** you get mediocre food at inflated prices. Walk five minutes to Wat Bo or Taphul Road for better and cheaper meals.

- **Trying to see everything in one day:** you will be overheated and templed out by noon. Get the 3-day pass and pace it.

## When to visit Siem Reap and Angkor

The best weather runs from **November to February**: dry, with temperatures around 25-32°C. This is also peak season, with the highest hotel prices and busy sunrises, especially around Christmas and Chinese New Year. March to May is brutally hot, often near 40°C, so temple visits shrink to early mornings only.

The rainy season, from **June to October**, is underrated. Rain usually comes in afternoon bursts, the countryside turns green, the moats fill and reflect the towers, and hotel prices drop by 30-50%. September and October bring the heaviest rain and some flooding. Late June to August is the sweet spot for quiet temples and low prices.

## Where to stay in Siem Reap and Angkor: best areas

- **Old Market and Pub Street:** everything is walkable, but it is noisy at night. It suits first-timers and party backpackers. Hostels cost €5-10 a night, and mid-range hotels €25-50.

- **Wat Bo area:** this area is across the river, quieter, with good restaurants and boutique guesthouses, and still a 10-minute walk to the centre. It is our pick for couples. Expect €25-70.

- **Taphul and Sok San Road:** these are backstreets west of the centre with good-value guesthouses and pool hostels. Expect €10-35.

- **Charles de Gaulle Road and Sivatha Boulevard:** this is where the big resorts and luxury hotels are, closest to the temple road. Expect €80-300 and up.

- **Sala Kamreuk:** a residential area of villa-style boutique hotels with pools. It is good for families who want space. Expect €40-90.

## What to eat in Siem Reap and Angkor

Khmer food is milder than Thai, built on lemongrass, kaffir lime, galangal and the fish-paste depth of prahok. Order **fish amok** first: freshwater fish steamed in coconut curry inside a banana leaf. It is the national dish, and versions vary hugely. **Beef lok lak** comes stir-fried with a lime and black pepper dipping sauce from Kampot. **Nom banh chok** is rice noodles with a green fish curry, sold by street vendors in the morning.

For breakfast, find **kuy teav** (pork noodle soup) or **bai sach chrouk** (grilled pork on rice), about €1-2 at markets. **Nom pang**, the Cambodian baguette sandwich, is another cheap option. Adventurous eaters can try red tree ant stir-fry, which is genuinely tasty, or fried crickets from the stalls (mostly a photo op).

A street or market lunch costs €2-4. A sit-down Khmer dinner with a beer runs €8-15. Training restaurants like Marum (part of the Friends network) and Haven serve good food that supports young Cambodians, at about €10-15 a head. For a splurge, Cuisine Wat Damnak does a modern Khmer tasting menu, from about €35-45.

## Airport to city centre and getting around

Flights land at **Siem Reap-Angkor International Airport (SAI)**, which opened in 2023 about 50 km from town, a lot farther than the old airport. The official shuttle bus runs to the centre for about €7-8 ($8) and takes roughly an hour. A taxi costs about €30-35 and takes 50-60 minutes. A tuk-tuk is around €25 and takes longer in the dust. Many hotels include a free or discounted pickup, so ask when you book.

In town, use **Grab** or **PassApp** for fixed-price tuk-tuks (€1-3 for short hops). For the temples, hire a tuk-tuk driver for the day: about €15-20 for the small circuit, €20-25 for the grand circuit, and €25-35 for Banteay Srei. A private car with air conditioning costs €30-50. There are no public buses or travel passes in town, and the Angkor pass is the only "pass" you need.

## Day trips from Siem Reap and Angkor

- **Beng Mealea:** a vast collapsed jungle temple you scramble through on wooden walkways. It is about 1.5 hours by car, with a separate ticket of about €9 ($10). Combine it with Koh Ker for a long day.

- **Koh Ker:** a remote temple group with a striking seven-tier pyramid, listed by UNESCO in 2023. It is about 2.5 hours each way, with a separate ticket of about €14 ($15).

- **Phnom Kulen:** a sacred mountain with a waterfall, a reclining Buddha and riverbed carvings. It is about 1.5 hours away, and entry for foreigners costs about €18 ($20), which is steep for what you get in the dry season.

- **Tonle Sap floating villages:** Kampong Phluk (about 45 minutes) has stilt houses and flooded forest. Kampong Khleang (about 1.5 hours) is bigger and less visited. Boat tickets cost €18-25. The best time is August to November, when water is high.

- **Battambang:** technically a destination rather than a day trip. It is about 3-3.5 hours by bus (€5-8) and has French colonial streets, the bamboo train and Phare's home circus school.

## Common mistakes and scams

Buy your Angkor pass only at the official ticket office on the road to the temples, or on the official Angkor Enterprise website. Passes sold by drivers or hotels at a "discount" are a risk, and the pass has your photo printed on it, so it cannot be shared or resold. Inspectors check it at every major temple.

- **Wrong clothes:** tank tops and short shorts will get you turned away from the upper level of Angkor Wat and other sacred areas.

- **Not agreeing a price:** always settle a tuk-tuk price and route in advance, including whether sunrise costs extra (it usually does, €3-5).

- **The milk-powder scam:** a mother or child asks you to buy milk formula for a baby. The shop overcharges, then the formula is sold back afterwards.

- **Orphanage tourism:** do not visit orphanages. Many keep children in poverty to attract donations. Donate to established NGOs instead.

- **Fake monks:** real monks do not ask tourists for money.

- **Dehydration:** carry at least two litres of water per person into the temples. Heat exhaustion is the most common way a trip here goes wrong.

## Siem Reap and Angkor FAQ

### How many days do you need in Siem Reap?

Three days is right for most people. It covers the main temples without rushing, and matches the 3-day Angkor pass, which is valid on any three days within ten.

### How much is the Angkor Wat ticket in 2026?

The Angkor pass costs $37 for one day, $62 for three days and $72 for seven days (about €34, €57 and €66). Children under 12 enter free with a passport.

### Is the Angkor Wat sunrise worth it?

Yes, once. It is crowded, but the silhouette over the lotus ponds is spectacular. Get there by 5am to claim a spot by the left-hand pond.

### Can you visit Angkor without a guide?

Yes. Most visitors use a tuk-tuk driver who knows the circuits plus a guidebook or app. A licensed guide (about €35-45 a day) adds a lot of context for a first visit.

### Should I use dollars or riel in Cambodia?

US dollars are used for almost everything. Bring clean, untorn notes, because damaged ones get refused. Small change comes back in riel, which is useful for street food and tips.

### Is Siem Reap safe for tourists?

Siem Reap is generally safe. Petty theft (bag snatching from tuk-tuks) is the main risk, so keep bags on your lap at night. Land mines have been cleared from all tourist areas.

### What should I wear to Angkor Wat?

Wear clothing that covers your shoulders and knees, and bring comfortable shoes with grip for steep stairs. A light long-sleeved shirt also helps against sun and mosquitoes.

### What to see in Siem Reap and Angkor in one day?

If you only have one day, follow day 1 of this itinerary: Sunrise and the small circuit. Morning: buy your pass at the official ticket office (or online), then sunrise at Angkor Wat.

### What to see in Siem Reap and Angkor in two days?

With two days, follow days 1 and 2 of this itinerary. Day 1: Sunrise and the small circuit. Morning: buy your pass at the official ticket office (or online), then sunrise at Angkor Wat. Day 2: The grand circuit. Morning: leave at 7am for Preah Khan, Neak Pean, Ta Som and East Mebon.

### Is Siem Reap and Angkor expensive?

It depends on how you travel. This guide puts the "shoestring" level at about about €30-40 per person per day and the "comfort" level at about €150 and up. Flights are extra: this page shows real fares checked by Happy Low Cost.
