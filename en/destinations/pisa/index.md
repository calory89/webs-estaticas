# Pisa in 2 Days: What to See Beyond the Leaning Tower

> Pisa travel guide by Happy Low Cost, 2-day itinerary. Original page: https://www.happylowcost.com/en/destinations/pisa/
> Updated: 2026-11-21

Most people do Pisa as a three-hour stop: train in, photo holding up the tower, overpriced panino, train out. That's a shame, because once you walk ten minutes south of Piazza dei Miracoli, Pisa turns into a normal Tuscan university town. It has arcaded streets, a busy produce market, students drinking spritz on the steps and some of the best riverside light in Italy. It is compact, flat and cheaper than Florence, and the airport is five minutes from the centre.

Two days is the right amount. One day covers the Piazza dei Miracoli monuments properly and the second covers the river, the old town and a museum or two. Add a third day only if you want to use Pisa as a base for Lucca, the coast or the Cinque Terre, which, honestly, is one of the smartest things you can do with it. It suits budget travellers, couples, people on a long layover and anyone who wants Tuscany without Florence's crowds and hotel prices.

## What to see in Pisa: the places worth your time

### Leaning Tower (Torre di Pisa)

Yes, climb it. The tower is the bell tower of the cathedral. It started leaning during construction in the 12th century and was stabilised in the early 2000s. The climb is about 250 worn marble steps, and you feel the tilt in your legs as you spiral up, which is the fun part. Tickets cost about €20, entry is by timed slot and the visit lasts around 30 minutes. Children under 8 aren't allowed up.

**Our tip:** book online a few days ahead in summer, because walk-up slots for the same day often sell out by mid-morning. The first slot of the day or one near sunset gives the best light and the thinnest crowds.

### Cathedral (Duomo di Santa Maria Assunta)

The cathedral is the real masterpiece of the square, and plenty of people skip it for the tower. Inside you get striped marble, a gilded coffered ceiling and Giovanni Pisano's carved pulpit, which is worth ten minutes on its own. Entry is free, but you need a timed coupon from the ticket office or it comes with a monument ticket. Allow 30-40 minutes.

### Baptistery (Battistero)

This is the largest baptistery in Italy, and its party trick is the acoustics. Every half hour or so a custodian sings a few notes and the echo layers into a chord. Climb to the upper gallery for a view straight across to the Duomo. Expect to pay about €7-10 alone, less as part of a combo ticket. Allow 30 minutes.

### Camposanto Monumentale

The monumental cemetery is the quietest place on the square and the one we'd least want you to miss. It is a long marble cloister with Roman sarcophagi and huge medieval frescoes, including the grim Triumph of Death, restored after Allied bombing damaged them in 1944. The price is similar to the Baptistery and a visit takes 45 minutes. It works best on a combo ticket.

### Museo dell'Opera del Duomo and Sinopie

The Opera museum holds original sculptures from the cathedral and the tower, plus a terrace with a close-up angle on the tower. The Sinopie museum shows the preparatory drawings found under the Camposanto frescoes. Both are interesting rather than essential. Combo tickets covering several monuments run roughly €10-27, so check at the office which bundle fits your time.

### Piazza dei Cavalieri

This was the political centre of medieval Pisa, a big open square ten minutes from the tower and almost tourist-free by comparison. The sgraffito facade of the Palazzo della Carovana now houses the Scuola Normale, one of Italy's most elite universities. Next to it is the church of Santo Stefano dei Cavalieri. The square is free and takes 15 minutes, and after dark it fills with students.

### Borgo Stretto and the Mercato delle Vettovaglie

Borgo Stretto is Pisa's prettiest street: narrow, arcaded, lined with cafes and old shopfronts, running north from the river. Just off it, Piazza delle Vettovaglie hosts a small fruit and vegetable market each morning under its porticoes. The same square becomes the centre of aperitivo life in the evening. Both are free. Go in the morning for the market and at 7pm for the drinks.

### The Lungarni and Santa Maria della Spina

The lungarni are the riverside avenues along the Arno, and walking them at sunset is the single best free thing in Pisa. The pastel palazzi curve with the river, and the light turns everything gold. On Lungarno Gambacorti, the south bank, sits Santa Maria della Spina. It is a tiny Gothic church covered in pinnacles, built to hold a thorn said to be from Christ's crown. It is often closed, but the outside is the point.

### Palazzo Blu

You can't miss this palazzo on the Lungarno: it is painted bright blue. It hosts the city's big temporary exhibitions, often on modern masters, and the permanent collection rooms are free. Allow an hour and roughly €10-15 for the major exhibitions. Check what is on before you go.

### Museo Nazionale di San Matteo

This former convent on Lungarno Mediceo holds one of the best collections of medieval Tuscan painting and sculpture anywhere, including works by Simone Martini and Masaccio. It is rarely busy. Tickets cost about €5-8 and a visit takes 1-1.5 hours. It is ideal on a rainy afternoon.

### Tuttomondo, the Keith Haring mural

Keith Haring painted this huge mural on the back wall of the church of Sant'Antonio Abate in 1989, one of his last public works. It is a few minutes' walk from Pisa Centrale station, it's free, and you need ten minutes. Pass it on your way in or out by train.

### Orto Botanico

Pisa's botanical garden is one of the oldest university gardens in Europe, founded in the 16th century, and it sits a few minutes from the tower. It is a shady escape when the Piazza dei Miracoli feels like an oven. Entry is about €4-5 and a visit takes 45 minutes.

### Museo delle Navi Antiche

The ancient ships museum is in the old Medici Arsenals near the Cittadella. It displays Roman boats found preserved in mud during works near San Rossore station, along with their cargo. It is surprisingly well done and kids love it. Expect roughly €8-12 and an hour or more.

### Mura di Pisa (the city walls)

You can walk along the top of sections of the medieval walls. One access point is right by the Piazza dei Miracoli, and from up there you look down on the tower and the Duomo over the rooftops. A ticket costs a few euros and the walk takes 30-60 minutes. Go late afternoon, when the marble glows.

## Things to do in Pisa

Pisa's best moments are often the unplanned ones. A few ideas beyond the monuments:

- **Do aperitivo like a student.** Piazza delle Vettovaglie and Piazza Garibaldi fill up from about 7pm. A spritz costs €4-7, often with snacks.

- **Eat cecina standing up.** This chickpea flatbread comes from a pizzeria's wood oven. Order it straight or stuffed into a focaccia.

- **Cross the Ponte di Mezzo at dusk.** From the central bridge you get the classic view of the river in both directions.

- **Cycle to the Parco di San Rossore.** This huge regional park of pine woods and wetlands stretches toward the sea. Rent a bike in town and make a half-day of it.

- **Take the bus to the beach.** Marina di Pisa and Tirrenia are about 30 minutes away by local bus. The beaches are relaxed and the seafood is fresh.

- **Time your trip for June.** On the evening of 16 June, the Luminara lights the riverside palazzi with thousands of candles. On the last Saturday of June, the Gioco del Ponte is a costumed tug-of-war across the Ponte di Mezzo.

- **Browse Corso Italia and Borgo Stretto.** These are the two main shopping streets, one more high-street and one more boutique.

## Pisa for free (or almost)

A surprising amount of Pisa costs nothing:

- The Piazza dei Miracoli itself, its lawns and the exterior of all four monuments.

- The cathedral interior, with a free timed coupon.

- The Tuttomondo mural, Piazza dei Cavalieri, Borgo Stretto and the morning market.

- The lungarni walk and the outside of Santa Maria della Spina.

- The permanent rooms at Palazzo Blu.

- Many churches, including Santo Stefano dei Cavalieri and San Michele in Borgo.

- The Parco di San Rossore on foot or by bike.

- Free water from the public fountains. Bring a bottle.

## What is overrated (and what to do instead)

- **The tower photo scrum.** Fighting fifty people for the "holding it up" shot is joyless. Take a quick one, then go to the walls or the Opera museum terrace for a better angle.

- **Eating on Via Santa Maria near the tower.** Prices are inflated, the food is reheated and the menus come in six languages. Walk ten minutes toward Borgo Stretto and you'll eat better for less.

- **Doing Pisa as a two-hour stop.** You see the busiest, least interesting part of the city. If you only have a day, at least walk to the river.

- **Buying every monument ticket.** The Sinopie and the full combo aren't essential for most people. The tower, Duomo, Baptistery and Camposanto cover the best of it.

## When to visit Pisa

The best months are April to early June and September to October. Expect daytime temperatures of roughly 18-27°C, long evenings and manageable queues. July and August are hot (often 30°C+) and the square is packed with cruise and day-trip groups by late morning. Accommodation is pricier then, and some locals head to the beach.

Winter is mild, rainy and very quiet, with hotel rates often the year's lowest. The trade-off is shorter days. June is the exception to the rule about summer: the Luminara and the Gioco del Ponte make it worth the heat. Book early if you go then.

## Where to stay in Pisa: best areas

- **Near Piazza dei Miracoli:** best for first-timers who want early tower slots. It is quiet at night once the day-trippers leave. Expect about €90-160 a night for a double in a mid-range hotel.

- **Centro storico (Borgo Stretto, Piazza dei Cavalieri):** our pick. You get restaurants, aperitivo, the market and everything within a 10-minute walk. Doubles run €80-150, and apartments are common.

- **Lungarni and south bank (San Martino):** river views and a more local, residential feel. It is good for couples, at about €80-140.

- **Near Pisa Centrale station:** the cheapest area and the most practical for day trips and the airport. It is less charming and a bit scruffy at night. Expect €50-100, and hostel beds from about €25-40.

## What to eat in Pisa

Pisan cooking is simple, coastal-meets-peasant Tuscan, and the signature is **cecina**, a thin chickpea-flour flatbread baked in a wood oven. Eat it hot with black pepper or in a focaccia sandwich. Il Montino, a pizzeria in Vicolo del Monte, is the classic place to try it.

Other dishes to look for:

- **Zuppa pisana:** a thick bread and vegetable soup, close to ribollita.

- **Baccalà alla pisana:** salt cod stewed with tomato.

- **Trippa alla pisana:** tripe in tomato sauce with Parmesan, for the brave.

- **Torta co' bischeri:** a local tart filled with rice, chocolate and candied fruit.

- **Castagnaccio:** a dense chestnut-flour cake, best in autumn and winter.

- **Cacciucco:** the big fish stew of nearby Livorno, found on menus near the coast.

Wash it down with a house red from the Pisan hills or a Chianti. Lunch at a simple trattoria or a cecina and focaccia stop costs €10-20, and a sit-down dinner with wine runs €25-40 per person. Most places add a small coperto, a cover charge of €2-3 per person.

## Airport to city centre and getting around

Pisa's Galileo Galilei airport is about 2 km from the centre, one of the closest in Europe. The **Pisa Mover** shuttle train runs to Pisa Centrale station in about 5 minutes for roughly €6-7. From the station it is a 20-25 minute walk to the tower, or a short city bus ride.

A taxi to the centre costs about €15-20. If you're travelling light, you can even walk to the station in around 30 minutes, though it isn't a scenic route.

Once in town, walk. The historic centre is flat, and you can cross it on foot in 30 minutes. Local buses (CPT/Autolinee Toscane) cost about €1.70-2 a ride and are mostly useful for the beaches. Bikes are a great way to reach San Rossore. Don't drive into the centre: it is a ZTL, a limited traffic zone, and the cameras send fines straight to your rental company.

## Day trips from Pisa

- **Lucca:** about 30 minutes by regional train, €3-5. Cycle the tree-lined Renaissance walls and climb the Torre Guinigi with the oaks on top. This is our favourite day trip from Pisa.

- **Florence:** 50-80 minutes by regional train, around €9 each way. The trains are frequent, so it is easy to do Florence as a day trip and sleep cheaper in Pisa.

- **Cinque Terre:** about 1-1.5 hours by train to La Spezia, then the local Cinque Terre line. Leave early, and avoid summer weekends if you can.

- **Livorno and the coast:** 15 minutes by train to Livorno for cacciucco and the canals of the Venezia quarter. Viareggio is about 20 minutes away for the seafront promenade.

- **Certosa di Calci:** about 30 minutes by bus or car. This grand charterhouse houses the University of Pisa's natural history museum. Check the bus times first, because services are limited.

## Common mistakes and scams

- **Not booking the tower in advance.** In high season you can easily lose half a day waiting for a slot.

- **Bracelet and "free gift" sellers.** Around the Piazza dei Miracoli, someone may tie a string bracelet on your wrist and then demand money. Keep your hands to yourself and walk on.

- **Pickpockets during the photo poses.** When everyone is busy propping up the tower, bags go unwatched. Wear yours in front.

- **Fake designer bags.** These are sold on blankets near the square, and buying counterfeit goods in Italy can get you fined.

- **Not validating train tickets.** Paper regional tickets must be stamped in the machines before boarding, or you risk a fine. Digital tickets often need check-in in the app.

- **Driving into the ZTL.** Rental car fines arrive months later. Park outside and walk.

- **Arriving at midday in August.** You get heat, coaches and no shade. Come early or late.

## Pisa FAQ

### How many days do you need in Pisa?

Two days is ideal: one for the Piazza dei Miracoli and one for the river, the old town and the museums. Add more days only if you're using Pisa as a base for Lucca, Florence or the coast.

### Is it worth climbing the Leaning Tower of Pisa?

Yes. At about €20 it isn't cheap, but feeling the lean on the stairs and the view over the square make it worth doing once. Book a timed slot online.

### Is Pisa worth visiting or just the tower?

It's worth more than the tower. The lungarni, Borgo Stretto, Piazza dei Cavalieri and the evening student atmosphere are what make Pisa enjoyable.

### How do I get from Pisa airport to the Leaning Tower?

Take the Pisa Mover to Pisa Centrale (about 5 minutes, €6-7), then walk 20-25 minutes or take a local bus. A taxi direct costs about €15-20.

### Can you do Pisa and Lucca in one day?

Yes, the train between them takes about 30 minutes. Do the tower in the morning and Lucca's walls in the afternoon.

### Is the Pisa cathedral free?

Entry to the Duomo is free, but you need a timed coupon from the ticket office, or it comes included with a tower or monument ticket.

### Is Pisa cheaper than Florence?

Generally yes, especially for accommodation and food. That's why many travellers sleep in Pisa and day-trip to Florence by train.
