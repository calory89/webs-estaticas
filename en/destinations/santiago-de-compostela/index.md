# Santiago de Compostela in 2 Days: Beyond the Cathedral

> Santiago de Compostela travel guide by Happy Low Cost, 2-day itinerary. Original page: https://www.happylowcost.com/en/destinations/santiago-de-compostela/
> Updated: 2026-11-21

Santiago de Compostela is a small granite city that punches far above its size. The old town is a compact maze of arcaded streets, wet stone and bell towers. It ends, quite literally, at the cathedral where the Camino de Santiago finishes. Exhausted pilgrims cry on the square, students from one of Spain's oldest universities fill the bars, and it rains. A lot. The locals say the rain is art, and after a day you start to see why: the old town looks better wet.

It suits people who like walking, eating and drinking well for not much money, and anyone curious about the Camino even if they have never walked a step of it. **Two days is the right amount** for the city itself. Add a third if you want a day trip to the coast at Fisterra or the cities of A Coruña or Pontevedra. More than that and you will be repeating the same five streets, pleasant as they are.

## What to see in Santiago de Compostela: the places worth your time

### Santiago de Compostela Cathedral

The cathedral is the reason the city exists, and it is worth at least two hours. Entry to the main church is free. Inside you can queue to hug the silver bust of Saint James behind the altar and visit the crypt with his tomb. The Romanesque **Pórtico da Gloria**, a carved stone entrance from the 12th century, is visited on a timed ticket (around €12-15, often combined with the museum). Book it online, because slots sell out in summer.

Our tip: the famous **botafumeiro**, the giant incense burner swung across the nave, does not fly at every mass. It swings on major religious holidays and when a group pays for it, so check the cathedral's website before planning your day around it.

### Cathedral rooftop tour

Walking on the stepped granite roofs of the cathedral is the single best thing to do in Santiago de Compostela, and too many visitors skip it. The guided tour takes about an hour and costs around €15. You get views across the whole old town and the green hills beyond. Book ahead, and go on a dry day if you can: the stone gets slippery and the tour may be cancelled in heavy rain.

### Praza do Obradoiro

The big square in front of the cathedral's west facade is where the Camino ends, and it is free to stand in for as long as you like. Four very different buildings frame it: the cathedral, the Baroque-era town hall in the Pazo de Raxoi, the old pilgrim hospital that is now the luxury Parador (Hostal dos Reis Católicos), and the Colexio de San Xerome. Sit on the stones mid-afternoon and watch pilgrims arrive. Come back at night, when the facade is lit and the square is half empty.

### Praza da Quintana and the Holy Door

Behind the cathedral, this stepped square is where students and locals sit when the sun appears. The Holy Door (Porta Santa) on its eastern side only opens in Holy Years, when the feast of Saint James on 25 July falls on a Sunday. The next one is 2027, so expect bigger crowds and higher prices then. At night, look for the shadow of the "pilgrim" cast by a pillar on the cathedral wall, a local legend that costs nothing to see.

### Mercado de Abastos

The city's food market is the most fun you can have in Santiago before noon. The granite halls sell Galician octopus, razor clams, percebes (goose barnacles), cheese like tetilla and San Simón, and vegetables from local farms, often sold by older women from the surrounding villages. Allow an hour, more if you eat. Go first thing in the morning from Tuesday to Saturday. It is closed on Sundays and quiet on Mondays, and Saturday is the busiest and liveliest day.

### Rúa do Franco, Rúa do Vilar and Rúa Nova

These three parallel streets run south from the cathedral and hold most of the old town's life: arcades, shops, cafés and dozens of bars. Rúa do Franco is the food street, with octopus restaurants side by side. Rúa do Vilar and Rúa Nova have the best arcades for walking in the rain. Free, and you will cross them ten times a day without trying.

### Parque da Alameda and Paseo da Ferradura

The Alameda gives you the classic postcard view of the cathedral across the rooftops. Walk the horseshoe-shaped Paseo da Ferradura at the park's edge for the best angle. It is free and needs 30-45 minutes. Go at sunset, or in the late afternoon in winter, when the light hits the towers. Next to the park is the statue of the "Two Marías", two eccentric sisters who walked the city daily for decades and are now a local symbol.

### San Martiño Pinario Monastery

This enormous Benedictine monastery, right behind the cathedral, is the second biggest building in the old town and gets a fraction of the visitors. The church has an over-the-top Baroque altarpiece and a carved choir that is worth seeing up close. Entry is around €5, and an hour is enough. It is a good rainy-day stop when the cathedral queues are long.

### Museo das Peregrinacións e de Santiago

This museum on Praza das Praterías explains the Camino, the cult of Saint James and the city's history. It is cheap (a few euros) and takes about an hour. The top floor has a window with a close-up view of the cathedral towers that alone justifies the ticket.

### Museo do Pobo Galego and Parque de Bonaval

Up in the San Domingos de Bonaval convent, a 10-minute walk from the old town, this museum covers rural Galician life: fishing, farming, costumes, bagpipes. The highlight is the **triple spiral staircase**, three intertwined stone staircases that are hard to photograph and harder to believe. Entry is a few euros, sometimes free on certain days. Next door is the Bonaval park, a former convent cemetery turned peaceful hillside garden with views of the old town. Allow two hours for both.

### Centro Galego de Arte Contemporánea (CGAC)

Right beside Bonaval, this contemporary art centre was designed by Portuguese architect Álvaro Siza and the building is as much the point as the exhibitions. Entry is free, and the shows rotate. An hour is plenty, and the rooftop terrace has good views when it is open.

### Colexiata de Santa María a Real do Sar

A 15-minute walk downhill from the old town, this Romanesque church is famous for its leaning columns, which tilt so much that buttresses had to be added centuries ago. Entry costs a couple of euros. It is a short visit (30 minutes) in a quiet residential area, so pair it with a walk along the little Sar river.

### Cidade da Cultura de Galicia

On Monte Gaiás, outside the centre, sits a huge, wave-shaped cultural complex by Peter Eisenman that was never fully finished and went wildly over budget. It is controversial with locals, but the architecture is striking and walking the grounds is free. The library and museum host exhibitions. Take a city bus or a taxi (around €8-10), and give it two hours. Skip it if you only have one day.

## Things to do in Santiago de Compostela

Beyond the monuments, Santiago is best experienced through its bars, music and Camino culture. A few ideas:

- **Do a tapas crawl on Rúa da Raíña and Rúa do Franco.** Many bars still give a small free tapa with each drink, so a few rounds can be dinner.

- **Have the market cook your seafood.** Buy at the Abastos stalls, then take it to one of the market's cooking counters, which prepare it for a small fee per person. Ask the stallholders where to go.

- **Attend the Pilgrim's Mass at noon.** It is free, the cathedral fills with pilgrims, and if you are lucky the botafumeiro swings. Arrive at least 30 minutes early.

- **Listen for the gaiteiro.** A bagpiper often plays under the Arco do Palacio, the passage between Obradoiro and the Praza da Inmaculada. The acoustics are excellent.

- **Catch the tuna.** University student groups in black capes sing traditional songs at night around the old town, especially near Quintana and Obradoiro. Tip them if you stay.

- **Walk the last kilometres of the Camino.** Get a bus or taxi to Monte do Gozo, the hill where pilgrims first see the cathedral spires, and walk the 5 km into town along the Camino Francés.

- **Visit the Pilgrim Office.** Even if you are not earning a Compostela, it is moving to see people collecting their certificates. You can buy a pilgrim passport (credencial) as a souvenir for a couple of euros.

## Santiago de Compostela for free (or almost)

Santiago is one of the cheapest well-known cities in Spain to visit, and most of it costs nothing. Free options:

- The cathedral nave, the crypt and the hug of Saint James.

- Praza do Obradoiro, Quintana, Praterías and Inmaculada, the four squares surrounding the cathedral.

- The view from the Alameda and the Bonaval park.

- The CGAC contemporary art centre.

- The grounds of the Cidade da Cultura.

- The Pilgrim's Mass at noon.

- Browsing the Mercado de Abastos.

- Free tapas with drinks at many bars (a glass of wine costs €2-3).

Some museums have free entry at certain times or on certain days, so check before you pay.

## Santiago de Compostela itinerary: 2 days

### Day 1: cathedral and old town

**Morning:** Start at Praza do Obradoiro before the groups arrive, then go into the cathedral and visit the Pórtico da Gloria on your pre-booked slot. Stay for the noon Pilgrim's Mass if you want to see the cathedral full.

**Afternoon:** Lunch on a menú del día around Rúa da Raíña. Then do the rooftop tour, followed by the Museo das Peregrinacións on Praterías and a wander through Quintana and San Martiño Pinario.

**Evening:** Walk to the Alameda for the sunset view of the towers. Then go back into the old town for a tapas crawl on Rúa do Franco and Rúa da Raíña, finishing with a late look at Obradoiro lit up at night.

### Day 2: market, museums and beyond the walls

**Morning:** Go to the Mercado de Abastos first thing. Have a coffee, buy seafood and have it cooked for an early lunch.

**Afternoon:** Walk up to San Domingos de Bonaval for the Museo do Pobo Galego and its spiral staircase, then the Bonaval park and the CGAC next door. If the weather holds, walk down to Santa María do Sar to see the leaning columns.

**Evening:** Head to the Ensanche, the modern part of town around Rúa da República Arxentina, for dinner where locals eat. Then go for a drink in the old town, where you might catch the tuna playing.

## What is overrated (and what to do instead)

**Eating on Rúa do Franco without looking around.** Some of the restaurants with plastic seafood displays and photo menus charge a premium for average octopus. Instead, check prices per ración before sitting down, or eat at the bars a street or two away on Rúa da Raíña and in the Ensanche.

**Waiting all day for the botafumeiro.** Many visitors plan their trip around it and leave disappointed. Instead, check the cathedral's schedule. If it is not swinging, the rooftop tour gives you a far more memorable hour.

**Visiting in Holy Year in July.** Around 25 July, and especially in Holy Years like 2027, the city is packed and hotel prices double. Instead, visit in May, June or September for good weather and normal prices.

**Souvenir shops on the cathedral streets.** The scallop shells, swords and plastic pilgrim gourds are the same everywhere. Instead, buy Galician cheese, tarta de Santiago or a bottle of Albariño, which taste better and cost less.

## When to visit Santiago de Compostela

The best time to visit Santiago de Compostela is May, June or September, when days are long, temperatures are mild (around 18-25°C) and the rain eases off. July and August are warmest but bring the most pilgrims and the highest prices, peaking around the Feast of Saint James on 25 July.

From November to March it rains often and temperatures sit around 8-14°C, but this is when the city is quietest and cheapest, with hotels sometimes half the summer price. Bring a proper raincoat and waterproof shoes at any time of year. An umbrella alone will not save you.

## Where to stay in Santiago de Compostela: best areas

For a short trip, stay in or right next to the old town. Everything is walkable.

- **Zona Vella (old town):** The atmospheric choice, within five minutes of the cathedral. Best for first-time visitors and couples. Expect around €60-120 a night for a double in a guesthouse, and €200+ at the Parador on Obradoiro.

- **Ensanche:** The modern district just south of the old town, around Rúa do Hórreo and Rúa da República Arxentina. More local restaurants and nightlife, and cheaper hotels (€50-90). Good for groups and night owls.

- **San Pedro:** The neighbourhood where the Camino Francés enters the city, near Porta do Camiño. Quieter, with good pensions and pilgrim hostels (beds €15-25, doubles €50-80).

- **Near the train station:** Convenient if you plan day trips by train, about 15 minutes' walk from the cathedral. Practical but plain, from around €50.

## What to eat in Santiago de Compostela

Santiago is one of the best and cheapest places to eat in Spain, and Galician seafood is the star. What to order:

- **Pulpo á feira:** boiled octopus with olive oil, paprika and salt, served on a wooden plate. Around €14-18 a ración.

- **Pimientos de Padrón:** small fried green peppers. Most are mild, and the occasional one is hot.

- **Empanada gallega:** a savoury pie filled with tuna, cod or scallops.

- **Caldo gallego:** a hearty broth of greens, potatoes and beans, perfect on a rainy day.

- **Zamburiñas and navajas:** small scallops and razor clams, usually grilled.

- **Tetilla and San Simón cheese:** one mild and creamy, the other smoked.

- **Tarta de Santiago:** an almond cake with the cross of Saint James stencilled in sugar.

To drink, order Albariño or Ribeiro wine, the latter traditionally served in a white ceramic bowl called a cunca. Estrella Galicia is the local beer.

A menú del día at lunch (starter, main, dessert, drink) costs around €12-16. A dinner of shared raciones with wine runs €20-30 per person, and a seafood blowout with percebes and lobster can easily reach €50+.

## Airport to city centre and getting around

Santiago Rosalía de Castro Airport (SCQ) is about 12 km from the centre, roughly 20-30 minutes away. A bus runs to the city centre and the bus and train stations for a few euros. Check the current line and timetable before you fly. A taxi costs around €22-25.

Plenty of travellers fly into A Coruña or Vigo instead, both connected to Santiago by frequent trains. Porto in Portugal is about 2.5-3 hours away by bus. High-speed trains from Madrid take around 3.5 hours.

Once in Santiago, walk. The old town is small and largely pedestrian. City buses cost a little over €1 a ride and are only useful for the Cidade da Cultura, Monte do Gozo or the airport. There is no tourist travel pass worth buying for a two-day visit.

## How much a trip to Santiago de Compostela costs

Santiago is affordable by Western European standards. Rough daily budgets per person:

- **Shoestring: €50-70 a day.** A hostel or pilgrim albergue bed (€15-25), market breakfast, a menú del día lunch, free tapas with drinks for dinner and free sights.

- **Mid-range: €110-160 a day.** A share of a double in the old town (€40-60), a sit-down seafood dinner, the rooftop tour, a couple of museums and a few drinks.

- **Day trip extra:** €10-40 for transport, depending on whether you take a train, bus or tour.

## Day trips from Santiago de Compostela

If you add a third day, these are the best options:

- **Fisterra and the Costa da Morte:** The rocky cape once believed to be the end of the world, where many pilgrims continue to watch the sunset. Buses take around 2-3 hours each way, and an organised tour covers more of the coast in one day.

- **A Coruña:** A seaside city with the Roman Tower of Hercules lighthouse, a long beach promenade and glassed-in balconies along the harbour. Trains take about 30-40 minutes.

- **Pontevedra and Combarro:** Pontevedra has one of the prettiest pedestrian old towns in Galicia, about 30-40 minutes by train. Combarro is a nearby fishing village known for its stone granaries (hórreos) lining the water, best reached by bus or car.

- **Cíes Islands:** White-sand beaches and clear water that look nothing like Spain, reached by train to Vigo (about an hour) and then a ferry. Visitor numbers are limited in summer, so you need a free online permit plus a ferry ticket, booked in advance.

## Common mistakes and scams

Santiago is a safe city, but a few things catch visitors out:

- **Not booking the rooftops or Pórtico da Gloria.** Timed slots sell out in summer, so book online a few days ahead.

- **Expecting a Compostela without walking.** The certificate requires walking at least the last 100 km (or cycling 200 km) with stamps to prove it. Anyone selling you a "certificate" on the street is selling a souvenir.

- **Petition and charity scams.** Occasionally people around the cathedral squares ask you to sign a petition and then pressure you for money. Walk on.

- **Not checking seafood prices.** Percebes and some shellfish are priced by weight. Ask how much a portion costs before ordering, so the bill holds no surprises.

- **Dressing for sunshine.** Even in summer, rain can roll in fast. Pack a waterproof jacket.

- **Visiting the market on a Sunday.** The Abastos is closed, so plan it for a weekday or Saturday.

## Santiago de Compostela FAQ

### How many days do you need in Santiago de Compostela?

Two days is enough to see the cathedral, old town, market and museums at a relaxed pace. Add a third day for a trip to Fisterra, A Coruña or the Cíes Islands.

### Is the Santiago de Compostela cathedral free to enter?

Yes, the main church, crypt and the hug of Saint James are free. The Pórtico da Gloria, cathedral museum and rooftop tour require paid tickets, roughly €12-15 each.

### When does the botafumeiro swing?

It swings on major religious holidays and when groups pay for it, not at every mass. Check the cathedral's website for the latest schedule before your visit.

### Is Santiago de Compostela worth visiting if you are not walking the Camino?

Yes. The old town is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, the food is excellent and cheap, and watching pilgrims arrive in Obradoiro is moving even if you came by plane.

### What is the best time to visit Santiago de Compostela?

May, June and September offer the best mix of mild weather and manageable crowds. Winter is cheapest but very rainy.

### How do I get from Santiago airport to the city centre?

A bus connects the airport to the centre in 20-30 minutes for a few euros. A taxi costs around €22-25.

### What food is Santiago de Compostela famous for?

Pulpo á feira (Galician octopus), pimientos de Padrón, empanada and tarta de Santiago. Pair them with Albariño or Ribeiro wine.

### Is Santiago de Compostela expensive?

No, it is one of Spain's more affordable cities. A menú del día costs €12-16, and many bars still serve free tapas with drinks.
