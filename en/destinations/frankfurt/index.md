# Frankfurt in 2 Days: Apple Wine, Museums and Skyline

> Frankfurt travel guide by Happy Low Cost, 2-day itinerary. Original page: https://www.happylowcost.com/en/destinations/frankfurt/
> Updated: 2026-11-21

Frankfurt has a reputation as a city of bankers, airport transfers and glass towers, and most people only see it from a layover. That reputation is half right. The skyline is the most American thing in Europe, the station district is rough, and nobody would call it pretty from end to end. But it also has one of the best museum strips in Germany, a rebuilt old town that looks better than you would expect, and a pub culture built around sour apple wine that you will not find anywhere else.

It suits culture lovers, food people who like heavy, honest cooking, and anyone flying into Germany's biggest airport who wants more than a hotel room. **Two days is the right amount**: enough for the old town, a couple of museums, a night in Sachsenhausen and a slow walk along the Main. Add a third day only if you want a day trip to the Rhine or Heidelberg.

## What to see in Frankfurt: the places worth your time

### Römerberg and the Neue Altstadt

The Römerberg is the old town square, with the stepped-gable Römer (city hall since the 1400s) on one side and a row of half-timbered houses on the other. Almost all of it was destroyed in 1944 and rebuilt, and the streets behind it (the DomRömer quarter, finished in 2018) are a careful reconstruction of the old coronation route. Give it 45 minutes, free. Our tip: walk the narrow Hinter dem Lämmchen lane early, before the tour groups arrive around 10am.

### Kaiserdom (St. Bartholomew's Cathedral)

The red sandstone cathedral is where Holy Roman Emperors were elected and later crowned. The inside is free and takes 20 minutes. The real reason to go is the tower: about 330 steps for roughly €3-5, and one of the best views of the old town with the skyscrapers behind it. Skip it if you have bad knees; the staircase is narrow.

### Städel Museum

The best art museum in the city and one of the best in Germany, running from medieval altarpieces to Rembrandt, Vermeer, Monet and German expressionists. Plan 2-3 hours and around €16-18 (more during big temporary shows). Go on a weekday morning, and book online for blockbuster exhibitions, which do sell out at weekends.

### Museumsufer (Museum Embankment)

The Sachsenhausen bank of the Main has more than a dozen museums in a row: the Städel, the Liebieghaus sculpture collection, the Museum Angewandte Kunst, the film museum and the architecture museum. Walking the stretch is free and takes 30 minutes. If you plan three or more, the two-day Museumsufer ticket (about €21) pays for itself fast.

### Main Tower

The only skyscraper with a public viewing platform, at about 200 metres, open-air. Tickets run about €10, and you need 45 minutes including the security check, which is airport-style. Go on a clear late afternoon and stay for sunset. On a grey, low-cloud day it is a waste of money.

### Eiserner Steg and the riverbanks

This iron footbridge from 1869 links the old town to Sachsenhausen and gives the postcard view of the skyline over the river. It is covered in love locks and is free. Walk west along the Mainkai on the north bank or the Schaumainkai on the south; on summer evenings both are full of locals drinking beer on the steps.

### Kleinmarkthalle

The covered market off Hasengasse is where Frankfurt actually shops: cheese, Italian deli counters, Turkish olives and the famous sausage stall at Schreiber, where the queue at lunchtime tells you everything. Allow an hour and €10-15 for grazing. Go late morning on a weekday; on Saturdays the upstairs wine terrace turns into a standing party.

### Goethe House

The family home where Goethe was born in 1749, rebuilt after the war with original furniture saved from the bombing. It is worth it if you care about Goethe, less so if you do not. About €10 including the adjoining Romantik museum, and 1-1.5 hours.

### Paulskirche

The round church where Germany's first elected parliament met in 1848. It matters more historically than visually (the interior is plain and post-war), but entry is free and takes 20 minutes. Check before going: it closes for events and has had renovation work.

### Senckenberg Natural History Museum

The dinosaur skeletons here are among the best in Europe, and it is the obvious pick if you are travelling with kids. Plan 2 hours and about €12-15. It is in Bockenheim, a short ride on the U4 or U6 to Bockenheimer Warte.

### Palmengarten

A large botanical garden with glasshouses full of tropical plants, cacti and orchids, next to the leafy Westend. Budget about €8-10 and 1.5-2 hours. It is at its best in spring and on rainy days when the glasshouses earn their keep.

### Sachsenhausen apple wine quarter

The old streets around Schweizer Straße and Wallstraße are home to the traditional Apfelwein taverns, with long wooden tables and grey stoneware jugs. Free to wander, budget an evening. Our tip: aim for the established houses on Schweizer Straße rather than the neon bar strip on Kleine Rittergasse (see the overrated section).

## Things to do in Frankfurt

Once you have covered the main sights, these are the things that make Frankfurt feel like a place rather than a stopover.

- **Drink apple wine properly.** Order a "Schoppen" (0.3 litre in the ribbed Geripptes glass) or share a Bembel jug at a big table. "Sauergespritzt" means topped up with sparkling water, which makes it easier going.

- **Take the Ebbelwei-Express.** A painted vintage tram that loops the city on weekends with apple wine and pretzels included. It is touristy but cheerful, around €10.

- **Join the Friday evening scene at Friedberger Platz.** The Nordend farmers' market turns into an open-air drinking session after work, mostly locals in their 30s with a glass of wine.

- **Walk Berger Straße in Bornheim.** The long shopping street of the most neighbourly part of town, with independent shops, bakeries and old taverns at the northern end.

- **Go to a Saturday flea market.** The Museumsufer flea market runs along Schaumainkai on alternate Saturdays. Check the dates before you go.

- **Catch an Eintracht Frankfurt match.** The Deutsche Bank Park stadium atmosphere is excellent and tickets are hard but not impossible to get for smaller games.

- **Climb the Goetheturm.** A wooden lookout tower in the Sachsenhausen city forest, rebuilt after an arson attack, with a free view back to the skyline and a beer garden at the bottom.

- **Visit the Christmas market** on Römerberg and around Paulsplatz from late November to just before Christmas. It is one of the biggest in Germany and crowded at weekends.

## Frankfurt for free (or almost)

The best things here cost little. The old town, the cathedral interior, the Paulskirche and both riverbanks are free. Several museums are free on the last Saturday of the month under the city's "Satourday" scheme, aimed at families, so check which ones are taking part.

- Walk the Eiserner Steg at dusk for the skyline view.

- Browse the Kleinmarkthalle without buying (you will buy).

- Climb the Goetheturm for a free panorama.

- Sit with a takeaway beer on the Mainkai steps in summer.

- Wander the Westend's villa streets and the Grüneburgpark.

- Look at the Alte Oper, the reconstructed opera house, from Opernplatz, which has fountains and terraces.

## What is overrated (and what to do instead)

- **Restaurants on Römerberg.** You pay for the view with mediocre schnitzel and inflated prices. Eat in Sachsenhausen or the Kleinmarkthalle instead.

- **The Zeil.** Frankfurt's main shopping street is the same chain stores as any German city. Walk it once for the MyZeil mall's odd glass facade, then go to Berger Straße.

- **Kleine Rittergasse.** Sachsenhausen's bar strip near the river is mostly cheap shots and stag parties. The proper Apfelwein taverns are a 10-minute walk south.

- **The Main Tower on a cloudy day.** Wait for a clear evening, or climb the cathedral tower for a cheaper view with more character.

## When to visit Frankfurt

The best months are **May to September**, when the riverbanks come alive and apple wine gardens open outdoors. July and August reach 25-30°C and are pleasant, though occasional heatwaves hit harder in a city of concrete. Late spring and early autumn are the sweet spot for weather and prices.

Winter is grey and cold, around 0-5°C, but late November to December brings the Christmas market. January and February are the cheapest time to visit, outside of trade fair weeks.

That last point matters more than the weather. Frankfurt is a trade fair city, and during big events at the Messe (the Book Fair in October is the most famous) hotel prices can double or triple. Check the Messe calendar before booking anything.

## Where to stay in Frankfurt: best areas

- **Altstadt and Innenstadt:** best for a short first trip, walking distance to almost everything. Expect €120-220 a night for a mid-range double, more during fairs.

- **Sachsenhausen:** across the river, close to the museums and the taverns, a bit calmer at night once you are away from the bar strip. Around €100-180.

- **Westend:** leafy, upmarket and quiet, near the Palmengarten and the Alte Oper. Good for couples and business travellers, €130-250.

- **Nordend and Bornheim:** the most liveable areas, full of cafes and local restaurants, with fewer hotels and more apartments. Around €90-160.

- **Bahnhofsviertel:** the cheapest beds (€70-130) and the best transport links, right by the main station. It also has visible drug use and street dealing, especially around Taunusstraße and Niddastraße. Fine for many travellers, not ideal for families or anyone arriving late alone.

## What to eat in Frankfurt

Frankfurt food is hearty, sour and built to go with apple wine. The signature dish is **Grüne Soße**, a cold sauce of seven herbs (borage, chervil, cress, parsley, burnet, sorrel and chives) with sour cream, served with boiled eggs and potatoes. It is better than it sounds and is the one thing you should order.

- **Handkäs mit Musik:** a sharp, translucent sour-milk cheese marinated with onions in vinegar and oil. The "music" is a joke about what the onions do to you later. An acquired taste; order one to share.

- **Frankfurter Würstchen:** the original thin pork sausages, boiled, with mustard and bread. Try them at the Kleinmarkthalle.

- **Rippchen mit Kraut:** cured pork chop with sauerkraut, the classic tavern main.

- **Frankfurter Kranz:** a ring cake with buttercream and caramelised nut crunch.

- **Bethmännchen:** marzipan and almond biscuits, mostly at Christmas.

A tavern lunch with green sauce and a Schoppen costs about €15-20. A sit-down dinner with a main, apple wine and dessert runs €30-45 per person in Sachsenhausen. A sausage and bread at the market is around €5-6. For cheap and excellent, the Bahnhofsviertel has Vietnamese, Ethiopian, Turkish and Indian places from €10-15.

## Airport to city centre and getting around

The fastest way from Frankfurt Airport to the centre is the **S-Bahn S8 or S9** from the regional station under Terminal 1, which reaches the Hauptbahnhof in about 12-15 minutes for roughly €6-7. Buy a ticket from the machine before boarding and check you are not getting the zone-wide "Frankfurt" ticket by mistake; the airport ticket is a specific fare. A taxi costs about €45-60 and takes 20-30 minutes without traffic.

Flying Ryanair to "Frankfurt Hahn"? That airport is about 120 km away, in a different state. The coach to Frankfurt takes around 1h45 and costs about €15-20, so factor that into the "cheap" flight.

The city runs on U-Bahn, trams, S-Bahn and buses under the RMV network. Useful tickets:

- **Single ticket** in the city: about €3.50-4.

- **Day ticket (Tageskarte)** for Frankfurt: about €7, worth it from the second ride.

- **Frankfurt Card:** about €13 for one day or €20 for two, with transport including the airport and up to 50% off many museums.

- **Deutschlandticket:** about €63 a month for unlimited local and regional transport across Germany. It is a subscription you have to cancel, but great value if you add day trips.

The centre itself is very walkable: Römerberg to the Städel is 15 minutes on foot.

## Day trips from Frankfurt

- **Heidelberg:** the castle ruin, the old bridge and the university town feel. About 1 hour by train, direct or with one change. A full day.

- **Rhine Valley (Rüdesheim and the Loreley):** vineyards, castles and river cruises. About 1-1.5 hours by regional train to Rüdesheim; combine with a boat to St. Goar. A full day, best May to October.

- **Mainz:** the cathedral, the Gutenberg Museum and wine bars. About 35-40 minutes on the S8. Half a day is enough.

- **Würzburg:** the baroque Residenz palace and Franconian wine. About 1h10 by ICE. A full day.

- **Marburg:** a hilly university town with a castle and Germany's first Gothic church. About 1 hour by regional train.

## Common mistakes and scams

Frankfurt is safe for a big city, and outright scams are rare. Most problems are self-inflicted.

- **Riding without a valid ticket.** There are no gates, but plain-clothes inspectors check often, and the fine is €60. Buy before boarding.

- **Booking blind during trade fairs.** The same room can cost €90 one week and €350 the next.

- **Assuming cards work everywhere.** Many traditional taverns and market stalls are still cash only or have a minimum. Carry €40-50.

- **Wandering the Bahnhofsviertel side streets late at night unprepared.** It is rarely dangerous for tourists, but open drug use is real. Stick to the main streets and keep an eye on your phone.

- **Arriving on a Sunday expecting to shop.** Almost all shops close, apart from those in the main station and the airport.

- **Petitions and "deaf charity" clipboards** around Hauptwache and the Zeil. Walk on; they are a distraction for pickpockets.

## Frankfurt FAQ

### How many days do you need in Frankfurt?

Two days covers the old town, the main museums, the skyline and an evening of apple wine. Add a third for a day trip to Heidelberg or the Rhine.

### Is Frankfurt worth visiting?

Yes, if you like museums, food and contrast between old and new. It is not Germany's prettiest city, but it is better than its business reputation, and it makes an easy base for the Rhine.

### What is Frankfurt famous for?

Banking and the European Central Bank, its skyline, the Book Fair, apple wine and green sauce. It is also Goethe's birthplace.

### Is Frankfurt safe for tourists?

Generally yes. The one area to be aware of is the Bahnhofsviertel near the main station, which has visible drug use; it is busy and mostly fine in the daytime but best approached with normal big-city caution at night.

### Is the Frankfurt Card worth it?

It is worth it if you arrive by air and visit two or more museums, since it covers the airport train and gives up to half price off entry. If you only walk the old town, a day ticket is cheaper.

### Can you do Frankfurt on a layover?

With six hours or more, yes. The S-Bahn reaches the centre in about 15 minutes, so you can see Römerberg, the cathedral and the river and be back with time for security.

### What is the best area to stay in Frankfurt for tourists?

The Altstadt and Innenstadt for convenience, Sachsenhausen for museums and taverns. Choose the Bahnhofsviertel only if price and transport matter more than atmosphere.

### What to see in Frankfurt in one day?

If you only have one day, follow day 1 of this itinerary: Old town, museums and apple wine. Morning: start at Römerberg early, walk through the Neue Altstadt to the Kaiserdom and climb the tower.

### Is Frankfurt expensive?

It depends on how you travel. This guide puts the "shoestring" level at about €70-100 per person per day and the "mid-range" level at about €170-250, sharing a room. Flights are extra: this page shows real fares checked by Happy Low Cost.
