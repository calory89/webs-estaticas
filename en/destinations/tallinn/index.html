Travel guide

Tallinn travel guide: what to see, eat and do in three days

🏨 Hotels in Tallinn

Why go to Tallinn

Tallinn has one of the best-preserved medieval centres in northern Europe, and it is small enough to walk end to end in twenty minutes. Beyond the walls, the city is surprisingly modern. Estonia runs much of its public life online, the startup scene is real, and the old industrial districts have turned into the places locals actually go.

It also suits a short break. Flights are cheap from much of Europe, everything uses the euro, and the Old Town, a seaside park and a hipster quarter all sit within a short tram ride of each other. I'd say it's best for three days, a little rushed in two.

What to see in Tallinn

  • Town Hall Square (Raekoja plats): the old heart of the city. Come early, before the tour groups arrive, and look up at the Gothic town hall. In December it hosts the Christmas market.
  • Toompea Hill: the upper town, where the Estonian parliament sits in a pink baroque building. Walk up Pikk Jalg, the "Long Leg" street.
  • Alexander Nevsky Cathedral: an onion-domed Orthodox church on Toompea, built under the Russian Empire. Dress modestly and keep your voice low inside.
  • Viewing platforms at Kohtuotsa and Patkuli: two terraces on the edge of Toompea. Kohtuotsa gives the classic postcard of red roofs and church spires. Go near sunset.
  • The city walls and towers: a good stretch survives, including Fat Margaret and Kiek in de Kök, which has a museum inside. You can also walk along part of the wall for a small fee.
  • St Olaf's Church (Oleviste): once among the tallest buildings in the world. The climb up the tower is narrow and steep but rewards you with a wide view over the harbour.
  • Kadriorg Park and Palace: a baroque palace and gardens built by Peter the Great. Next door, the KUMU art museum is the best place to understand Estonian art.
  • Telliskivi Creative City and Kalamaja: former factories now full of street art, cafés, vintage shops and weekend markets. Kalamaja next door has wooden houses and a calmer, residential feel.
  • Seaplane Harbour (Lennusadam): a maritime museum in a concrete hangar, with a full-size submarine, the Lembit. Good for a wet afternoon.
  • Patarei Sea Fortress: a former prison on the waterfront. It is bleak and sobering, and it gives you a very different view of the 20th century here.

Things to do

Take a free walking tour of the Old Town on your first morning. The guides tend to be honest about the Soviet years, which you won't get from a plaque. Afterwards, wander the side streets: Katariina Käik, a lane with craft studios, is lovely when empty.

For something different, book a sauna. Estonians take it seriously, and several places in the city let you rent a private one with a view or a plunge pool. The Noblessner quarter, with old submarine shipyards turned into restaurants, makes a good evening walk by the water. In summer, the beach at Pirita is a short bus ride away. If you have time, the Estonian Open Air Museum at Rocca al Mare shows rural life in old farmhouses and windmills, and it is a pleasant, quiet half day.

The Song Festival Grounds are worth knowing about. Estonia's "Singing Revolution" is part of the national story, and the amphitheatre is where huge choirs gather every few years.

How many days and suggested route

Day 1, Old Town. Start at Town Hall Square, climb Toompea, see the cathedral and the two viewpoints, then come back down for lunch. After that, St Olaf's, the walls and a slow evening in the lanes.

Day 2, beyond the walls. Take the tram to Kadriorg for the palace and park, then KUMU. In the afternoon, head to Telliskivi and Kalamaja and stay for dinner.

Day 3, your choice. Seaplane Harbour and Patarei, or the Open Air Museum, or a ferry across to Helsinki (about two hours each way). Lahemaa National Park, with its manor houses and coast, is also doable as a long day trip with a car or a tour.

When to go

June to August is the easiest time. Days are long, and around midsummer it barely gets dark. Temperatures usually sit around 18 to 22°C, with the odd rainy day. Midsummer (23 and 24 June) is a real national holiday, so expect some places to shut and many locals to head to the countryside.

May and September are quieter and cheaper, with cooler air and fewer cruise passengers. Winter is cold, often just around or below freezing, and the sun sets around mid-afternoon in December. But snow on the red roofs, mulled wine and the Christmas market make it very atmospheric. Pack proper boots, because the cobbles get slippery.

Where to stay

Old Town: the most atmospheric option and the most expensive. It is also noisier at weekends. Choose it if this is a first, short trip.

Rotermann Quarter and the city centre: modern hotels a few minutes outside the walls, with easy transport and often better value for the price.

Kalamaja and Telliskivi: my pick for a slightly longer stay. Cafés, bars and guesthouses, about a fifteen-minute walk from the Old Town.

Kadriorg: leafy, quiet and a bit residential. Good for couples who want calm.

On a tight budget, there are plenty of hostels in and around the Old Town, with dorm beds from roughly 15 to 30 euros. Mid-range hotels commonly run 70 to 130 euros a night, and boutique places in the medieval centre go well beyond that.

What to eat and where

Estonian food is hearty and Baltic: rye bread (leib), smoked fish, pork, potatoes, mushrooms and berries. Try kama, a mix of roasted grain flours stirred into buttermilk or yoghurt, and marinated herring on dark bread. In winter, look for blood sausage (verivorst) with lingonberry jam. Try the sweet curd snack kohuke as well; it is sold in supermarkets across the country.

For a proper sit-down dinner, Rataskaevu 16 is a well-known Estonian restaurant in the Old Town, so book ahead. Olde Hansa is the medieval theme restaurant, with candlelight and waiters in costume. It's touristy, but fun once. For something more local, the Balti Jaama Turg market near the train station has food stalls, and Telliskivi has plenty of casual places. Vana Tallinn is the sweet, rum-based liqueur you'll see everywhere. A small glass is enough.

Lunch deals in the centre often give you a soup, a main and a drink for about 10 to 14 euros, which is far better value than dinner.

Getting from the airport and getting around

Lennart Meri Tallinn Airport is only about four kilometres from the centre. Tram line 4 runs from the terminal to the city centre in roughly fifteen minutes, and it's the simplest option. A taxi or ride-hailing app (Bolt is popular) costs around 8 to 12 euros to the Old Town, depending on traffic.

The Old Town is entirely walkable. For Kadriorg, Telliskivi and Pirita, use the trams and buses; you can pay by contactless card or buy a ticket on board, and a single fare is a couple of euros. Ferries to Helsinki leave from the harbour terminals, a short walk or tram ride from the centre. Book early in summer, and note that some operators are cheaper on weekdays.

Rough daily budget

  • Budget: about 50 to 70 euros, with a hostel, market lunches and free sights.
  • Mid-range: about 110 to 160 euros, with a decent hotel, restaurant dinner and a museum or two.
  • Comfortable: 200 euros and up, with a boutique hotel, tasting menus and private extras such as a sauna.

These are only rough figures, and prices climb in summer and around Christmas.

FAQs

Is Tallinn expensive?

It is cheaper than the Nordic capitals, but no longer a bargain. Food and drink are still reasonable, while Old Town hotels are the biggest cost.

Do I need Estonian or Russian?

No. English is widely spoken, especially among younger people and in tourist areas. A simple "aitäh" (thank you) is appreciated.

Is Tallinn safe?

Yes, it is generally very safe. Normal city care applies in crowded areas, and the main hazard in winter is the icy cobbles.

Can I pay by card everywhere?

Almost. Contactless cards and phones work in nearly all shops, restaurants and public transport, so you won't need much cash.

Is a day trip to Helsinki worth it?

It can be, if you have never been, and the ferry is easy. But with only three days I'd stay in Tallinn and see more of the city.
