Why go to Kraków

Kraków came through the Second World War with its medieval centre largely intact, and you feel it the moment you step into the Main Square. Gothic churches, merchants' houses and a Renaissance cloth hall sit in a compact grid that you can cross on foot in fifteen minutes. Then there is the price: beer, dinner and a decent bed cost far less than in Western Europe, so a long weekend feels generous.

It is also a city with weight. Students from the Jagiellonian University, one of Europe's oldest, keep the cellar bars full. The Jewish quarter, Kazimierz, is lively again, and the history of the 20th century is close enough to visit in a day. Few cities in Europe mix good nights out with that much seriousness.

What to see in Kraków

  • Main Market Square (Rynek Główny): one of the largest medieval squares in Europe. Go early, around 7:30, when pigeons outnumber people, and come back after dark when the facades are lit.
  • St. Mary's Basilica: look for the two mismatched towers. A trumpeter plays the hejnał from the taller one every hour, stopping mid-note in memory of a legendary sentry. Inside, Veit Stoss's carved wooden altarpiece is the reason to pay the small entry fee.
  • Cloth Hall (Sukiennice): the long arcade in the middle of the square. The ground floor is mostly amber and souvenirs, but upstairs there is a gallery of 19th-century Polish painting, and the café-lined arcades are good for a quick coffee.
  • Wawel Hill: the royal castle and cathedral on a rock above the Vistula. The cathedral, where Polish kings were crowned and buried, is the highlight. Book the castle interiors ahead in summer, and walk the courtyard for free.
  • Kazimierz: the old Jewish quarter, with synagogues on Szeroka Street, courtyards, vintage shops and some of the best bars in town. It is quietest on weekday mornings.
  • Oskar Schindler's Factory: in Podgórze, across the river. It is not really about Schindler. It is a thorough, sobering museum on Kraków under Nazi occupation. Allow two to three hours and book a slot.
  • Czartoryski Museum: home to Leonardo's Lady with an Ermine. The painting is smaller than you expect and more arresting than you expect.
  • The Planty and the Barbican: a green ring of park follows the line of the old city walls. Walk it to reach the Barbican and St. Florian's Gate, the best-preserved remains of the medieval defences.

Things to do

Walk the Vistula boulevards at sunset, when locals jog, cycle and sit on the steps with a beer. Have a drink in Kazimierz, in a candlelit bar where the chairs do not match, and move on to a "piwnica", a cellar pub, in the Old Town. For something different, take a tram out to Nowa Huta, the socialist-realist district built in the 1950s, and see a completely different Kraków.

Two day trips are worth the time. The Wieliczka Salt Mine, about 14 km southeast, has chapels carved from salt hundreds of metres underground. Auschwitz-Birkenau lies roughly 70 km west, a 90-minute ride by bus or organised tour. It is a heavy day. Entry is free but needs a timed pass or guided tour, so reserve weeks ahead for summer.

Krakow itinerary: 3 days, day by day

Three days covers the city well. Two is enough if you skip a day trip. Four lets you add Auschwitz or the salt mine without rushing.

3-day itinerary for Krakow
DayPlan
Day 1Main Square, St. Mary's, Cloth Hall, a lunch of pierogi, then Wawel in the afternoon. Finish with dinner on the Old Town side streets.
Day 2Kazimierz in the morning, a late lunch on Plac Nowy, then cross the river to Schindler's Factory. Return for drinks in Kazimierz.
Day 3Wieliczka in the morning, back by mid-afternoon, then the Czartoryski Museum or a slow walk along the Planty. If you want Auschwitz, swap it for this day.

When to go

May, June and September are the sweet spot, with daytime temperatures around 18 to 24°C and long evenings. July and August are warmer, often 25 to 30°C, and busier. Spring can be rainy, so pack a light jacket.

Winters are cold, often around 0°C or below, and the Old Town can be hazy with smog on still days. The compensation is the Christmas market on the Main Square, from late November through December, with mulled wine and grilled cheese with cranberry. October is calm and golden if you are happy with some grey days.

Where to stay

  • Old Town (Stare Miasto): central and walkable, but pricey and noisy on weekends. Pick a side street, not the square itself.
  • Kazimierz: my choice for most visitors. Great food and bars, a short walk to Wawel, and a better balance of price and atmosphere. Hostels from around €15 to €25 a night, mid-range hotels from about €70.
  • Podgórze: across the river, quieter and cheaper, near Schindler's Factory and with good views back at Wawel.
  • Near the main station (Kraków Główny): practical for early trains and day trips, and usually good value. It lacks charm, but the Old Town is a five-minute walk away.

What to eat and where

Start with pierogi, dumplings filled with potato and cheese, meat, or sauerkraut and mushrooms. Add żurek, a sour rye soup served in a bread bowl, and bigos, a slow-cooked hunter's stew of cabbage and meat. Street-side, buy an obwarzanek, a ring-shaped bread covered in sesame or poppy seeds that you will see on carts across the centre.

The late-night classic is the zapiekanka, a halved baguette topped with mushrooms, cheese and ketchup and grilled. The best-known stands are in the round market building on Plac Nowy in Kazimierz. For a cheap, honest lunch, look for a bar mleczny, a communist-era milk bar where a full meal costs a few euros. The queue at lunchtime is a good sign. Wash it down with a Polish craft beer, or a shot of flavoured vodka if you are feeling brave.

Getting from the airport and getting around

Kraków John Paul II International Airport (Balice) is about 11 km west of the centre. A direct train to Kraków Główny takes roughly 17 to 20 minutes and costs a couple of euros. Buses also run to the centre, and ride-hailing apps or a taxi usually cost around €10 to €20 depending on the hour.

The centre is compact enough to walk. For farther trips, use trams and buses, which share one ticketing system based on time (20, 60 or 90 minutes). Buy from machines or the official app, and validate paper tickets on board. Taxi apps are cheap for late-night rides back from Kazimierz.

Rough daily budget

  • Budget: about €40 to €55 a day, with a hostel bed, milk bar meals, street food and one museum.
  • Mid-range: about €80 to €120, with a comfortable hotel, restaurant dinners, a couple of paid sights and a few drinks.
  • Comfortable: €150 and up, for a boutique hotel, good restaurants and private tours.

Poland uses the złoty (PLN), not the euro. Cards are accepted almost everywhere, but carry a little cash for markets and small cafés.

FAQs

Is Kraków safe for tourists?

Yes, it is generally very safe. Watch for pickpockets in crowded areas and trams, and be careful with taxis hailed on the street. Use apps instead.

Do I need to speak Polish?

No. English is widely spoken in the centre, especially by younger people. A simple "dziękuję" (thank you) is appreciated, though.

Is three days enough?

Yes, for the city and one day trip. With Auschwitz and Wieliczka both on the list, make it four.

Should I book Auschwitz in advance?

Yes. Entry is free but needs a reserved timed pass or a guided tour, and spots go fast from spring to autumn. Book as soon as you know your dates.

What is the cheapest time to visit?

January, February and early November have the lowest prices for flights and hotels. Expect cold, short days, but the museums and cellar bars are warm and uncrowded.
