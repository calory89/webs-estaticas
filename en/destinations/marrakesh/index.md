## Why go to Marrakech

Marrakech hits you in the first ten minutes: motorbikes squeezing through alleys barely wider than a doorway, the smell of cedar and grilled meat, a donkey cart, a call to prayer bouncing off pink walls. It is intense, and it is also one of the easiest places to reach on a cheap flight from Europe. You can be drinking mint tea in a courtyard within a few hours of leaving home.

It rewards people who slow down. The big sights are good, but the real pleasure is in the old city's side streets, in a rooftop at sunset, in a long lunch while the midday heat passes. Expect to be hassled a bit. Expect also to be charmed more often than you thought.

## What to see in Marrakech

- **Jemaa el-Fna.** The main square is quiet by day, with orange-juice carts and a few snake charmers you should avoid photographing unless you want to pay. From late afternoon it fills with food stalls, musicians and storytellers. Watch from a café terrace first, then dive in.

- **Koutoubia Mosque.** The 12th-century minaret is the city's landmark and guide. Non-Muslims can't go inside, but the gardens around it are pleasant, and it looks best near sunset.

- **Bahia Palace.** A 19th-century palace of painted cedar ceilings, zellige tilework and quiet courtyards. Go when it opens to beat the tour groups.

- **Saadian Tombs.** A small, ornate walled garden near the Kasbah. The queue can be long, so aim for early morning.

- **El Badi Palace.** Mostly ruins, but huge ones, with storks nesting on the walls. Climb to the terraces for a view over the medina rooftops.

- **Ben Youssef Madrasa.** A former Islamic college with an exquisite carved courtyard. It's one of the most photogenic spots in the city, so avoid midday.

- **Majorelle Garden and the Yves Saint Laurent Museum.** The cobalt-blue villa and cactus garden are popular, so book a timed ticket ahead. The YSL museum next door is small and well designed.

- **Le Jardin Secret.** A restored Islamic garden in the medina, calmer than most big sights. It's a good place to sit for twenty minutes.

- **The Mellah.** The old Jewish quarter has a cemetery, a synagogue and a more local feel, with spice shops nearby.

- **The souks.** Leather, lamps, babouche slippers, rugs, spices. Everything is negotiable, and getting lost is part of the experience.

## Things to do

Book a hammam. A traditional one with black soap and a vigorous scrub costs little, while the spa versions in riads and hotels cost more and are gentler. Take a cooking class, which often starts with a trip to the market and ends with you eating your own tagine. A hot-air balloon flight over the Palmeraie at dawn is touristy but beautiful.

For a break from the medina, try a day trip. The Ourika Valley and the Atlas foothills are cooler in summer, and the Agafay Desert is a stony landscape about 40 minutes out, good for camel rides and sunset dinners. Essaouira on the Atlantic coast is a long day trip of about three hours each way, so better with an overnight stay.

## When to go

March to May and September to November are the best months, with warm days and comfortable evenings. Summer is brutal: July and August often pass 40°C, and you will spend the afternoons indoors. Winter days are sunny and pleasant, but nights can drop to around 5°C and many riads are poorly heated, so pack a jumper.

Ramadan shifts through the calendar. Sights stay open, though some restaurants close during the day and things run more slowly. The evenings, when everyone breaks the fast, are memorable.

## Where to stay

**The medina** is the obvious choice. Stay in a riad, a traditional house around a courtyard, ideally near Jemaa el-Fna or in the Kasbah for easy walking. The trade-off is noise and the maze of lanes. Send your riad your arrival time, since many will send someone to meet you.

**Gueliz** is the modern, French-built district with cafés, bars, shops and wider streets. It suits travellers who want an easier base and don't mind a short taxi to the old city.

**Hivernage** has the larger hotels, pools and nightlife, and the **Palmeraie** has luxury resorts, but both are far from the action.

Budget guide: hostels and simple guesthouses run roughly 10 to 25 euros a night, good mid-range riads 60 to 120, and luxury places start at around 150 and climb steeply.

## What to eat and where

Start with **tagine**, slow-cooked meat or vegetables in a conical clay pot, and **couscous**, traditionally served on Fridays. Try **pastilla**, a sweet-savoury pie of pigeon or chicken dusted with sugar and cinnamon, and **tanjia**, a Marrakech speciality of beef or lamb slow-cooked in an urn. **Mechoui**, tender roast lamb, is served at stalls in the Mechoui Alley near the Jemaa el-Fna end of the souks. Breakfast on **msemen** pancakes, olives and mint tea.

The square's night stalls are a show and a meal. Pick one that's busy, check prices on the menu, and ignore the people who tug your sleeve. For quieter food, look to the small family places in the Mellah, the cafés of Gueliz, and rooftop restaurants for sunset. Fresh orange juice from the carts costs next to nothing and is excellent.

## Getting from the airport and getting around

Marrakech Menara Airport (RAK) is about 15 minutes from the centre. Bus 19 runs to Jemaa el-Fna and on to Gueliz for a few euros. Taxis wait outside, so agree a price before you get in. Expect roughly 100 dirhams to the medina, more at night. Many riads can arrange a pickup, which is worth it on arrival.

Inside the medina, walk. Cars can't enter most lanes, and porters with carts or motorbike taxis can carry luggage. Outside it, use **petit taxis**, the small beige ones, and insist on the meter. Day trips are easily booked through your riad or a licensed agency. Download an offline map, as GPS signal is patchy in the lanes.

## Rough daily budget

- **Budget:** around 35 to 50 euros a day, with a hostel bed, street food, and walking everywhere.

- **Mid-range:** around 80 to 130 euros, with a decent riad, sit-down meals, and a hammam or class.

- **Comfortable:** 200 euros and up, for a boutique riad or hotel, nicer restaurants, and private drivers.

Museum entries are inexpensive. Day tours and spa treatments are where the budget grows. Carry small notes in dirhams, as change can be hard to come by.

## FAQ

### Is Marrakech safe for tourists?

Generally yes. Petty theft and persistent touts are the main nuisances. Keep valuables close, avoid unofficial guides, and be polite but firm.

### Do I need a visa?

Many nationalities, including most EU, UK, US and Canadian passport holders, can enter visa-free for short stays. Rules change, so check with your government's travel advice before you book.

### What should I wear?

Light, loose clothing that covers shoulders and knees is comfortable and respectful, especially in the medina. Bring a layer for winter nights and comfortable shoes for uneven streets.

### Can I drink the tap water?

Most visitors stick to bottled water, which is cheap and everywhere. Be careful with ice and raw salads at the cheapest stalls if you have a sensitive stomach.

### Is it worth bargaining in the souks?

Yes, it's expected. Start at around half the first price, stay good-humoured, and walk away if it isn't right. Check the price in two or three shops first.
