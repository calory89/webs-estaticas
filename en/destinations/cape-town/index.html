Why go to Cape Town

Few cities put a flat-topped mountain, two oceans, a wine region and a penguin colony within an hour's drive of each other. Cape Town does, and it also has a complicated, deeply felt history that you can't ignore and shouldn't want to. The food is excellent and often cheap by European or North American standards. The light around six in the evening, when Table Mountain turns orange, is worth the flight alone.

It's also a long way from most places, so flight deals matter. Give it at least five days, because the wind and cloud will rearrange your plans at least once.

What to see: the places worth your time

  • Table Mountain. Take the cableway or hike up Platteklip Gorge (steep, two to three hours). Go first thing, before the cloud "tablecloth" rolls in. The cable car shuts in strong wind, so keep a spare morning.
  • Cape Point and the Cape of Good Hope. The southern tip of the peninsula is windswept and dramatic. Walk to the old lighthouse, then look out for baboons and ostriches along the road. Keep your sandwiches zipped away.
  • Boulders Beach, Simon's Town. A colony of African penguins waddles around granite boulders and shares the sand with swimmers. Go early or late to avoid the tour buses.
  • Robben Island. The ferry leaves from the V&A Waterfront, and former political prisoners often lead the tours. It's sobering and well run. Book several days ahead, since boats get cancelled when the sea is rough.
  • Bo-Kaap. The brightly painted houses on cobbled streets are the postcard shot, but it's also a living Cape Malay neighbourhood with mosques and a small museum. Be respectful when taking photos of residents.
  • Kirstenbosch National Botanical Garden. On the eastern slopes of Table Mountain, with protea gardens and a treetop walkway. Summer concerts on the lawn on Sunday evenings are lovely, with a picnic and a bottle of local wine.
  • District Six Museum. A small, powerful museum about the forced removals of the apartheid era. It gives context to everything else you see.
  • Chapman's Peak Drive. A coastal road cut into the cliffs between Hout Bay and Noordhoek. It's a toll road and occasionally closes for rockfall or wind. Pull over at the viewpoints.
  • Signal Hill and Lion's Head. Drive up Signal Hill for sunset, or hike Lion's Head at dawn or on a full moon, when it gets sociable. The last section has chains and ladders, so don't go in high wind.
  • Zeitz MOCAA. Contemporary African art in a converted grain silo at the Waterfront. Good for a rainy afternoon.

Things to do

Stand-up paddle or surf lessons at Muizenberg are easy and fun, with the colourful Victorian beach huts as backdrop. The water is cold on the False Bay side only slightly less than on the Atlantic side. Camps Bay is for sunsets and people-watching, not swimming.

Spend a day in the Constantia wine valley, the oldest in the country, or go further to Stellenbosch and Franschhoek, about an hour east. Chenin Blanc and Pinotage are the grapes to try. Tastings are usually cheap, and you should arrange a driver or a tour so nobody drives afterwards.

On Saturdays, the Neighbourhood Goods Market in Woodstock is a good place for lunch, and the surrounding streets have street art and design shops. In winter and spring (roughly June to November), whale watching from Hermanus or the coast road is superb, with southern right whales close to shore.

Cape Town itinerary: 5 days, day by day

Three days covers the highlights, five lets you breathe, and seven adds the winelands properly. A workable five-day plan:

5-day itinerary for Cape Town
DayPlan
Day 1Table Mountain in the morning, Company's Garden and Bo-Kaap in the afternoon, sunset on Signal Hill.
Day 2Robben Island in the morning, then the Waterfront and Zeitz MOCAA. Dinner on Kloof Street or in Woodstock.
Day 3The peninsula loop: Muizenberg, Kalk Bay, Boulders Beach, Cape Point, and back via Chapman's Peak.
Day 4Kirstenbosch in the morning, then Constantia wine estates.
Day 5Stellenbosch or Franschhoek, or a buffer day if weather wrecked an earlier plan.

When to go: weather and seasons

Summer (November to March) is dry, warm and long-lit, with beach weather and the strong southeaster wind that locals call the Cape Doctor. It's also busiest and priciest, especially December and January. Autumn (March to May) and spring (September to November) are my favourites: mild, fewer crowds, better prices. Winter (June to August) brings cool temperatures and real rain, with grey spells, but also green hillsides, whales and cheap hotels. Pack a windproof layer whatever the month.

Where to stay

  • City Bowl and Gardens: central, walkable restaurants, close to the mountain. A good first-time base for mid-range hotels and guesthouses.
  • V&A Waterfront: safe, polished and convenient, with higher prices and a touristy feel.
  • Green Point and Sea Point: promenade runs, sea views, lots of apartments. Excellent value and well connected by bus and Uber.
  • Camps Bay and Clifton: beach glamour and top prices. Good for a short splurge.
  • Woodstock and Observatory: creative, cheaper, with hostels and boutique stays. Check the specific street at night.
  • Kalk Bay and Muizenberg: relaxed seaside towns, ideal if you have a car and want quiet.

Budget travellers will find good hostels in the City Bowl. Mid-range guesthouses cost far less than similar places in Europe, and luxury is comparatively cheap too.

What to eat and where

Start with a Cape Malay curry or bobotie, a spiced minced-meat dish with an egg topping, in Bo-Kaap. Try a gatsby, a huge sub sandwich stuffed with chips and meat, which is cheap and filling. Fresh fish and chips at Kalk Bay harbour, with a seal watching from the water, is a favourite lunch. Look for snoek (a local fish) grilled with apricot glaze, and finish with koeksisters, sticky syrupy doughnuts. A proper braai (barbecue) is a social ritual, and boerewors sausage is the classic. Biltong, dried cured meat, is the snack to take home.

For restaurants, Kloof Street, Bree Street and Woodstock have the best concentration, and Constantia and Stellenbosch estates offer superb long lunches. Tipping around 10 to 15 percent is normal.

Getting from the airport and getting around

Cape Town International Airport (CPT) is about 20 to 30 minutes from the centre without traffic. The simplest option is Uber or Bolt, which are cheap and reliable. The MyCiTi bus connects the airport to the city centre, and you buy a rechargeable card at the station. Regular taxis work too, but agree the fare first.

In the city, use Uber or Bolt, especially after dark. MyCiTi serves the Waterfront, Sea Point and the City Bowl. The Red Bus hop-on, hop-off is fine for a first orientation. For the peninsula and winelands, hire a car (driving is on the left) or join a day tour. Trains to Simon's Town are scenic but have had reliability problems, so check current conditions locally first.

Safety, briefly: stay aware in the city centre after dark, don't leave valuables in parked cars, don't hike remote trails alone, and avoid walking between neighbourhoods at night. Use Uber and you'll be fine.

Rough daily budget

  • Budget: roughly 50 to 70 US dollars, covering a hostel dorm, cheap meals, public transport and Uber, and one activity.
  • Mid-range: roughly 120 to 200 dollars, with a comfortable guesthouse or hotel, restaurant dinners and tours.
  • Comfortable: 300 dollars and up, for a luxury hotel, wine estates and private drivers.

Car hire, Robben Island and big-name wine lunches are the biggest extras.

FAQs

Is Cape Town safe for tourists?

Yes, if you use common sense. Stick to busy areas, use Uber at night and keep valuables out of sight. Tourist areas are generally well patrolled.

Do I need a visa?

Many nationalities, including most EU, UK, US and Australian passport holders, can enter visa-free for short stays. Rules change, so check with official sources before booking.

What is the best month to visit?

February to April and October to November give the best mix of weather, fewer crowds and fair prices. December and January are hot but busy and expensive.

Do I need a car?

Not for the city. For Cape Point, Chapman's Peak and the winelands, a car or tour gives much more freedom.

Is tap water safe to drink?

Yes, Cape Town's tap water is generally safe and good quality. Carry a refillable bottle, especially for hikes.

How many days do you need in Cape Town?

5 days are enough to see the essentials of Cape Town without rushing. The route in this guide is organised day by day.

Is Cape Town worth visiting?

For the traveller who wrote this guide, yes: Few cities put a flat-topped mountain, two oceans, a wine region and a penguin colony within an hour's drive of each other. 5 days are enough for the essentials.

What to see in Cape Town in one day?

If you only have one day, follow day 1 of this itinerary: Table Mountain in the morning, Company's Garden and Bo-Kaap in the afternoon, sunset on Signal Hill.

What to see in Cape Town in two days?

With two days, follow days 1 and 2 of this itinerary. Day 1: Table Mountain in the morning, Company's Garden and Bo-Kaap in the afternoon, sunset on Signal Hill. Day 2: Robben Island in the morning, then the Waterfront and Zeitz MOCAA.
