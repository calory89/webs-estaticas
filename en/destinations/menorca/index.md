# Menorca in 5 Days: Calas, Ruins and Honest Local Tips

> Menorca travel guide by Happy Low Cost, 5-day itinerary. Original page: https://www.happylowcost.com/en/destinations/menorca/
> Updated: 2026-11-21

Menorca is the quiet Balearic island. It's flatter, greener and far less built-up than Mallorca or Ibiza. The whole island has been a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve since 1993, and its prehistoric Talayotic sites joined the World Heritage list in 2023. You come here for turquoise coves backed by pine trees, two very different towns (Ciutadella in the west, Mahón/Maó in the east), cheese, gin and long lazy lunches. If you want superclubs, go to Ibiza.

It suits couples, families, hikers and anyone who likes swimming more than partying. We'd give it **5 days**. That's enough to see both towns, swim at the best southern and northern calas and visit a couple of ancient sites without rushing. Three days feels like a teaser. A week lets you add a proper stretch of the Camí de Cavalls coastal path.

## What to see in Menorca: the places worth your time

### Ciutadella old town

The former capital is the island's prettiest town. It has honey-coloured stone, the Gothic cathedral and arcaded Ses Voltes street. The old harbour is lined with restaurants that sit below the town walls. Give it half a day. Walking around is free, and the cathedral charges a few euros. **Our tip:** come back at sunset. Walk the harbour, then have a drink on Plaça des Born, which has the obelisk and the town hall.

### Mahón (Maó) and its harbour

Maó sits on one of the longest natural harbours in the Mediterranean. The British ran the island for much of the 18th century, and you can still see it in the Georgian sash windows and the gin. Plan on half a day for the upper town, the Mercat del Claustre del Carme (a market inside an old cloister) and the steps down to the port. **Our tip:** take a one-hour glass-bottom boat trip around the harbour (about €15-20). It's the easiest way to understand the place.

### Cala Macarella and Macarelleta

These are the postcard coves: white sand, limestone cliffs and water the colour of mouthwash. Macarella is the bigger one with a beach bar. Macarelleta is a 10-minute clifftop walk away and is smaller and prettier. The beaches are free, but in summer the car park fills early and the road then closes to cars. Take the bus from Ciutadella instead. **Our tip:** arrive before 9:30 or after 17:00.

### Cala Turqueta

Turqueta is a southern cove with pines down to the sand and calm, clear water. It's about 25 minutes from Ciutadella, and the paid car park is a 10-minute walk from the beach. It's slightly less mobbed than Macarella. Bring water and shade, because facilities are minimal.

### Cala Mitjana and Cala Galdana

Galdana is a big resort beach with hotels and every service. Cala Mitjana is a 20-minute walk east through the woods and far more beautiful. You get cliffs, rock-jumping spots and no buildings. Both are free. **Our tip:** park or take the bus to Galdana, walk to Mitjana, and return to Galdana for lunch.

### Cala Pregonda

The north coast looks completely different: red sand, dark rock and rugged islets. Pregonda is the best of it. You park near Binimel·là and walk about 20 minutes. It's free. Go on a day with southerly wind. When the Tramuntana blows from the north, the water gets rough and fills with seaweed.

### Fornells

Fornells is a whitewashed fishing village on a calm bay in the north. It's famous for caldereta de langosta (lobster stew) and is good for kayaking or paddleboarding. Two or three hours is enough unless you're eating. **Our tip:** walk out to the Torre de Fornells watchtower for the view over the bay.

### Naveta des Tudons

This is a 3,000-year-old stone tomb shaped like an upturned boat, near Ciutadella. It's the best-known Talayotic monument and one of the oldest roofed buildings in Europe. Allow 30-45 minutes. Entry is a few euros. Go at the end of the day, when the light is warm.

### Torre d'en Galmés and Talatí de Dalt

Torre d'en Galmés is the largest Talayotic settlement, a hilltop of houses, cisterns and towers near Alaior, with a small paid entry. Talatí de Dalt near Maó is smaller but has a fine taula, a T-shaped stone monument. Allow an hour each. The info panels are decent, but a guide or audio guide adds a lot.

### Monte Toro

At 358 m, this is the island's highest point, above Es Mercadal. A sanctuary sits on top, and on clear days you can see both coasts and sometimes Mallorca. It's free, and you need 30 minutes. Drive up on a clear morning. Skip it if it's hazy.

### Binibeca Vell

This whitewashed "fishermen's village" near Sant Lluís was actually built in the 1970s as a holiday complex. It's cute and very photogenic. People live there, though, so keep your voice down. Thirty minutes is plenty. It's free.

### Fortaleza de La Mola and Illa del Rei

La Mola is a huge 19th-century fortress guarding the mouth of Maó harbour, with tunnels and gun batteries. Entry is around €10-15, and you'll need 2 hours. Illa del Rei, in the harbour, has a restored British naval hospital and a contemporary art gallery run by Hauser & Wirth. The gallery is free, and you pay a few euros for the boat over. **Our tip:** do La Mola in the morning. The fortress is very exposed and gets hot.

### Cap de Cavalleria and Favàritx lighthouses

These are two wild northern lighthouses. Cavalleria sits on high cliffs. Favàritx stands in a lunar landscape of black slate. Both are free, and each needs about an hour including the drive. Sunset at Cavalleria is the best free show on the island.

## Things to do in Menorca

Once you've seen the main sights, these are the activities we'd actually spend time on.

- **Walk a stretch of the Camí de Cavalls.** This old coastal bridleway (GR 223) loops the whole island for about 185 km. The Cala Galdana to Cala Turqueta section takes about 2-3 hours and links several of the best southern coves.

- **Kayak or snorkel.** Fornells bay is ideal for beginners. Guided sea kayak trips to sea caves on the south coast cost about €40-60.

- **Tour the Xoriguer gin distillery** on Maó harbour. It's free to wander and taste. Try a **pomada**, which is gin and lemonade.

- **Visit a cheese farm** around Alaior or Es Migjorn for Mahón-Menorca DOP cheese, and buy some aged (curado) to take home.

- **Have a sunset drink at Cova d'en Xoroi** in Cala en Porter. It's a bar built into cliff caves. Entry with a drink is around €20-25, and it's very busy at sunset. Touristy, yes, but the setting is genuinely unique.

- **Take a boat trip along the south coast.** This lets you see the coves without the car park fight. Half-day trips run about €40-70.

- **Buy avarques.** These are traditional Menorcan sandals made with tyre-rubber soles, about €30-60. They're sold all over Ciutadella.

- **Join the Sant Joan festival** in Ciutadella (around 23-24 June). It features horses rearing through the crowds. It's wild, crowded, and accommodation triples in price.

## Menorca for free (or almost)

Menorca's best things cost nothing. Every beach is public and free, though some have paid parking in summer. The Camí de Cavalls is free, and so are the lighthouses, Monte Toro and the old towns of Ciutadella and Maó.

- Several Talayotic sites (including Talatí de Dalt in some seasons) are free or nearly free. Check before you go.

- The Hauser & Wirth gallery on Illa del Rei is free. You only pay for the short boat ride.

- Gin tastings at Xoriguer are free.

- Markets are free to browse: Mercat des Peix in Maó and Ciutadella's market square. The Saturday morning market on Plaça de s'Esplanada in Maó is also worth a look.

- Sunsets from Cap de Cavalleria, Ciutadella harbour or Punta Nati cost nothing.

## What is overrated (and what to do instead)

- **Driving to Macarella at midday in August.** You'll queue, the road will close, and the beach will be packed. Take the bus or arrive early. Even better, go to Cala Mitjana, Pregonda or Cala Escorxada (a long walk, but nearly empty).

- **Caldereta de langosta at any price.** In Fornells it costs €70-90+ per person. It's delicious, but share it or order fish of the day instead if you're on a budget.

- **Binibeca Vell as a destination.** It's a 30-minute stop, not a half day. Combine it with Cala Binibeca or Binisafúller beach.

- **Staying in Cala Galdana or Son Bou expecting charm.** They're convenient resort strips. For atmosphere, sleep in Ciutadella, Maó or Es Castell.

## When to visit Menorca

The best time to visit Menorca is **June or September**. The sea is warm (around 22-25°C), the days are long, and prices sit below the August peak. July and August are hot (around 30°C), crowded, and the most expensive time for flights, cars and rooms. Sea temperatures peak then, though.

May and October are great for hiking and quieter beaches. The water is cool but swimmable for most. From November to March, many hotels and restaurants close, flight links shrink, and the Tramuntana wind can be fierce. It's cheap and peaceful, but it's a walking trip, not a beach trip.

## Where to stay in Menorca: best areas

- **Ciutadella:** Best for first-timers who want atmosphere, restaurants and quick access to the southwest coves. Doubles run about €90-200 a night in summer.

- **Maó:** Closest to the airport and ferries, with a real-town feel and good food. It's better for the east and La Mola. Doubles cost about €80-180.

- **Es Castell:** A small, pretty town at the mouth of Maó harbour with waterfront dinners at Cales Fonts. It's quiet and good for couples, at €90-180.

- **Fornells:** Best for the north coast, water sports and seafood. Supply is limited, so book early. Expect €100-220.

- **Sant Lluís and the southeast:** Villas and rural hotels suit families with a car. Rural agroturismos run €120-300+.

## What to eat in Menorca

Menorca's cooking is simple and local: cheese, cured pork, seafood and vegetables. Look for these dishes:

- **Queso Mahón-Menorca:** Cow's milk cheese with an orange rind. Order it semicurado or curado with olive oil.

- **Caldereta de langosta:** Spiny lobster stew. Fornells is the place to try it.

- **Sobrassada and carn i xulla:** Soft paprika sausage and a local cured sausage. Try them on toast or with honey.

- **Oliaigua:** A peasant soup of tomato, onion and peppers, poured over bread.

- **Berenjenas rellenas:** Stuffed aubergines, a homely classic.

- **Ensaïmada and pastissets:** Spiral pastry and flower-shaped butter biscuits, for breakfast or souvenirs.

- **Gin de Menorca and pomada:** The island's British-era drink.

A menú del día (set lunch) in town costs about €15-20, mostly on weekdays. A beach-bar lunch runs €25-35 per person and often isn't great value. Dinner with wine at a decent harbour restaurant is about €35-55 per person. Harbour-front tables in Ciutadella and Maó charge for the view, so eat one street back for better value.

## Airport to city centre and getting around

Menorca Airport (MAH) is about 5 km from Maó. **Bus line 10** runs from the airport to Maó bus station in about 15-20 minutes for roughly €3, and runs often in summer. A **taxi** to Maó costs about €15-20, and one to Ciutadella about €60-70 (around 45 minutes). In summer there's also a direct bus to Ciutadella. Check the current timetable.

Line 1 links Maó and Ciutadella along the main road (Me-1) in about an hour for €5-6. In summer, extra beach buses run from Ciutadella to Macarella, Turqueta and Cala Galdana. Buses are cheap but don't reach many coves, so a **rental car** is the realistic choice. Expect €30-70 a day in high season and less in May or October. Book early for July and August. There's no tourist travel pass worth buying. A rechargeable bus card gives small discounts if you ride a lot.

## Day trips from Menorca

- **Mallorca:** Ferries from Ciutadella reach Alcúdia in about 1.5-2 hours. Return foot-passenger fares run about €50-90. Alcúdia old town and Formentor are doable in a long day.

- **S'Albufera des Grau Natural Park:** A wetland reserve 20 minutes from Maó, with birdwatching trails and boats to Illa d'en Colom in summer.

- **A full-day boat trip around the south coast** from Ciutadella or Cala Galdana: 6-7 hours with swimming stops, about €60-90 with lunch.

- **Cap de Favàritx and Cala Presili:** 30 minutes from Maó. Pair the lighthouse with a near-empty sandy beach.

## Common mistakes and scams

Menorca is safe, and real scams are rare. Most problems are self-inflicted:

- **Arriving at Macarella or Turqueta at 11:00 in summer.** The car park will be full, and you'll lose an hour.

- **Ignoring the wind forecast.** If the Tramuntana (north wind) blows, swim in the south. If the wind is from the south, go north.

- **Underestimating the walks to the coves.** Many need 15-30 minutes on rocky paths. Bring shoes, water and shade, because some calas have no bar at all.

- **Leaving valuables in rental cars** at beach car parks. Break-ins happen.

- **Not booking car hire and the Sant Joan week early.** Prices jump fast.

- **Paying for "lobster" without checking the price per kilo.** Ask before you order.

## Menorca FAQ

### How many days do you need in Menorca?

Five days is the sweet spot for both towns, the best calas and a couple of ancient sites. With three days you'll mostly see the southwest and one town.

### Is Menorca better than Mallorca?

Menorca is quieter, smaller and less developed, with better wild coves. Mallorca has more variety, mountains, nightlife and Palma. For a relaxed beach trip, Menorca usually wins.

### Do you need a car in Menorca?

Not strictly, since summer buses reach the main towns and several coves. A car makes things much easier and is the only practical way to reach the north coast and lighthouses.

### Is Ciutadella or Mahón better to stay in?

Ciutadella is prettier and closer to the famous southwest coves. Maó is handier for the airport, the east and the harbour sights. Many people split their stay between the two.

### What is the most beautiful beach in Menorca?

Macarelleta and Cala Mitjana top most lists. Cala Pregonda is the most striking on the north coast.

### Is Menorca expensive?

It's mid-priced by Spanish standards but pricey in August. Shoulder months and supermarket picnics keep costs down.

### Is Menorca good for families?

Very good. Shallow sandy bays like Son Bou, Cala Galdana and Binibeca suit small kids, and the island is calm and safe.

### Is Menorca worth visiting?

For the traveller who wrote this guide, yes: Menorca is the quiet Balearic island. It's flatter, greener and far less built-up than Mallorca or Ibiza. 5 days are enough for the essentials. Budget: Shoestring (about €70-100 per person per day): Shared room or hostal (€40-60 each), buses, supermarket picnics, one menú del día, free beaches.
