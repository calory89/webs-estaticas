Cappadocia is not a city. It's a stretch of central Anatolia where volcanic ash eroded into cones, columns and canyons, and people carved homes, churches and entire underground towns into the rock. Most visitors base themselves in Göreme, Uçhisar or Ürgüp, all within 15 minutes of each other. The landscape is the main event, and the best parts are free if you're willing to walk. The balloons are as spectacular as the photos suggest, and they cost a lot more than most people expect.

It suits hikers, photographers, couples who want a cave hotel with a view, and anyone happy to wake up before sunrise. It doesn't suit people who want nightlife or beaches. Plan 4 days. Two days covers the headline sights in a rush, three is comfortable, and four gives you a spare morning in case your balloon flight is cancelled by wind, which happens more than operators admit.

What to see in Cappadocia: the places worth your time

Göreme Open Air Museum

This is the essential stop: a cluster of rock-cut monasteries and churches with Byzantine frescoes, 1 km uphill from Göreme village. Plan 1.5-2 hours. Entry is about €25 (around 1,250 TRY), and the Dark Church costs extra, about €5-8. Our tip: pay for the Dark Church. Its frescoes are the best preserved in the region because so little light reached them. Arrive at opening, before the tour buses come in at around 10am.

Rose Valley and Red Valley

These two connected valleys are the best hike in Cappadocia, with pink-streaked rock and cave churches you can wander into, including the Column Church and the Church of the Crosses. Allow 2-4 hours depending on your route. It's free, though tea stalls along the way sometimes ask a small fee to pass. Walk it in the late afternoon and finish at the sunset viewpoint above the valley near Çavuşin, which is less crowded than the spots in Göreme.

Uçhisar Castle

Uçhisar Castle is a giant rock pinnacle riddled with old rooms, and it's the highest point in the area. The climb takes about 45 minutes and entry costs about €6. The 360-degree view over Pigeon Valley and Göreme is worth it. Go late afternoon for softer light. The steps at the top are uneven, so wear proper shoes.

Pigeon Valley

This easy, mostly downhill walk runs from Uçhisar to Göreme, about 4 km and 1.5 hours. The cliffs are dotted with dovecotes carved to collect pigeon droppings for fertiliser. It's free. Start in Uçhisar so gravity is on your side, and skip the "wishing tree" souvenir stalls at the top.

Love Valley

Yes, the rock formations look exactly like what you think. Love Valley is also one of the classic balloon landing and viewing spots. The walk takes 1-2 hours and is free. The top viewpoint is the easy photo, but walking down into the valley floor shows you the scale properly.

Derinkuyu Underground City

Derinkuyu is the deepest of the underground cities, with around eight levels open to visitors, ventilation shafts, stables, chapels and rolling stone doors. It once sheltered thousands of people. It's about 30 km south of Göreme. Plan 1 hour on site, and expect to pay about €13-15. Skip it if you're claustrophobic: some passages force you to crouch. Go first thing or at lunchtime to dodge the tour groups.

Kaymaklı Underground City

Kaymaklı is wider and shallower than Derinkuyu, and it sits 10 km closer to Göreme. Expect a similar price and about 45 minutes inside. Do one underground city, not both, because they're more alike than the brochures suggest. Kaymaklı is better if you're tall or nervous about tight spaces.

Paşabağ (Monks Valley)

Paşabağ has the most photographed mushroom-topped fairy chimneys, some with hermit cells carved inside, near Çavuşin. You need 30-45 minutes, and there's a small entry fee of a few euros. It's very compact and very busy midday, so go early or late.

Zelve Open Air Museum

Zelve is a valley village that was inhabited until the 1950s, when erosion made it unsafe. It's less polished and less crowded than Göreme. Allow 1-1.5 hours and expect to pay about €12. Combine it with Paşabağ, which is 1 km away. Many visitors skip Zelve, and that's precisely its appeal.

Ihlara Valley

Ihlara is a 14 km canyon with a river and dozens of frescoed churches in the walls, about 80 km southwest of Göreme. Most people walk the 3-4 km stretch from the main stairs to Belisırma village, which takes 2-3 hours. Entry is about €8-10. Bring water for the 360-odd steps back up, or arrange to be picked up in Belisırma, where the riverside restaurants serve trout.

Selime Monastery

Selime is a huge rock-cut monastery complex at the northern end of Ihlara Valley, with a cathedral-sized hall carved from the cliff. Allow 1 hour. Entry is a few euros, or included with some passes. It's often tacked onto the end of the Green Tour when everyone is tired, so get there first if you can.

Ortahisar

Ortahisar is a quieter village with its own rock castle and old citrus-storage caves still in use. It's a good 1-2 hour stop, and the village is free to wander. Have tea in the main square and watch real village life, something that barely exists in Göreme anymore.

Things to do in Cappadocia

The sights are only half the reason to come. These are the experiences we'd prioritise.

  • Take a hot air balloon flight. It costs €200-350 per person in high season for a standard flight of about an hour. Book with a licensed operator and give yourself two possible mornings in case of cancellation.
  • Watch the balloons from the ground. It's free and arguably better for photos. Good spots are the Sunset Point hill above Göreme, the hotel terraces, or the road toward Love Valley.
  • Join a pottery workshop in Avanos. The town sits on the Kızılırmak river and has a centuries-old pottery tradition. A try-it session at a workshop costs €10-25.
  • Ride a horse at sunset. Ranches around Göreme offer 1-2 hour rides through the valleys for about €30-50. Pick one that looks after its animals.
  • Go on an ATV tour. It's loud, dusty and fun for an hour (€30-50). It's also a big reason the valley tracks are eroding, so do it once at most.
  • Do a wine tasting in Ürgüp. The area has grown wine since antiquity. Turasan and Kocabağ have tasting rooms, and local reds from Öküzgözü grapes are good value.
  • Spend a night in a cave hotel. Even if you're on a budget, one night in a carved room with a terrace view is the Cappadocia thing to do.
  • See a Turkish night with whirling dervishes. The genuine Sema ceremony at the Saruhan caravanserai near Avanos is quiet and serious. The touristy dinner-show version is a different, louder product.

Cappadocia for free (or almost)

You can spend whole days here without paying a lira for entry. Walk Rose, Red, Pigeon, Love and Meskendir valleys, all free and all connected by trails. Watch the balloons go up at dawn from the hill above Göreme. Catch sunset from Sunset Point in Göreme, which has a small or no fee depending on the season, or from the Aktepe area.

Wander Çavuşin's old village on the cliff, where the crumbling Church of St John the Baptist sits above the new town. The local dolmuş minibuses cost about €1 between villages. A picnic of gözleme, dried apricots and ayran from a village market keeps lunch under €5.

What is overrated (and what to do instead)

  • The "Instagram rooftops" in Göreme. Some hotels charge non-guests to pose with carpets and balloons behind them, and there are queues for a photo. Instead, walk to any ridge above the village at dawn. You'll get the same sky for free.
  • Doing both underground cities. After one, the second feels like a rerun. Pick Derinkuyu for depth or Kaymaklı for comfort, and spend the saved hour in Ihlara.
  • The Red Tour bus. It covers sights you can easily walk or reach by dolmuş from Göreme. The Green Tour is the one worth paying for, because its sights are spread out.
  • Turkish night dinner shows. At €50-70 you get mediocre set food, belly dancing and unlimited drinks of questionable origin. Go to the real Sema ceremony instead, or just have a good dinner.

When to visit Cappadocia

The best time to visit Cappadocia is April to June or September to October. Days are warm (around 20-28°C), hiking is pleasant and balloons fly most mornings. July and August are hot and dry, often 30°C+, so hike early. These are also the most expensive and crowded months.

Winter (December to February) is cold, with nights below freezing. Snow on the fairy chimneys is gorgeous, though, and hotel prices can drop by 30-50%. Balloon cancellations are more frequent in winter. November and March are the quietest compromise. Book well ahead for Turkish holidays and Bayram periods, when domestic tourism spikes.

Where to stay in Cappadocia: best areas

  • Göreme: best for first-timers and budget travellers. It's the closest base to the museum and trailheads, with the most restaurants and the bus station. It's also the busiest. Hostels cost €15-25 a night, and cave hotels €60-200+.
  • Uçhisar: best for couples and views. It's higher and quieter, with upmarket cave hotels and beautiful sunsets. You'll mostly need taxis or the dolmuş. Expect €80-300.
  • Ürgüp: a real town with more local life, wineries, good restaurants and boutique hotels. It's about 10 minutes from Göreme. Budget €50-200.
  • Ortahisar: a quiet village for people who want calm and authenticity. It has a few lovely boutique cave hotels and very few crowds. Rooms cost €60-180.
  • Avanos: on the river, cheaper and less touristy. Good for those with a car. Expect €40-120.

What to eat in Cappadocia

The local signature is testi kebab, a meat-and-vegetable stew slow-cooked in a sealed clay pot that's cracked open at your table. It's theatrical and sometimes overpriced, but good when done well. Order it once. Mantı, tiny dumplings with yoghurt and chilli butter, comes from nearby Kayseri and is better value. Try Kayseri's pastırma (spiced cured beef), especially with eggs at breakfast.

Look out for gözleme (thin stuffed flatbread), pottery kebab variations, trout in Ihlara, and local dried apricots and pekmez (grape molasses) at breakfast. Drink local wine or ayran. A Turkish breakfast spread at your hotel is usually generous enough to keep you going until mid-afternoon.

  • Lunch: €7-12 (350-600 TRY) for pide, gözleme or a lokanta meal.
  • Dinner: €15-30 per person with a drink at a mid-range place, and €40+ at terrace restaurants in Uçhisar.

Avoid restaurants on Göreme's main drag with photo menus and touts outside. Walk a street back.

Airport to city centre and getting around

You have two airports to choose from.

  • Nevşehir Kapadokya (NAV): closest, about 40 minutes to Göreme. It has fewer flights, mostly from Istanbul.
  • Kayseri (ASR): more flights, about 1 hour 15 minutes to Göreme.

From either airport, shared shuttle buses timed with flights cost about €10-15 per person. Book in advance through your hotel or online. A private transfer costs €40-70, and taxis are similar or more.

Overnight buses from Istanbul take around 10-11 hours and drop you at Nevşehir or Göreme. Check whether the ticket includes the free connecting minibus.

Getting around once you're there:

  • Dolmuş: local minibuses connect Nevşehir, Uçhisar, Göreme, Çavuşin and Avanos, plus Ürgüp-Göreme. They cost about €1 a ride and run roughly every 30-60 minutes during the day.
  • Car rental: €30-50 a day. It's worth it for Ihlara, the underground cities and Soğanlı.
  • Scooters: €20-30 a day, but roads have fast traffic.
  • Museum Pass Cappadocia: covers the main state-run sites (Göreme, Zelve, the underground cities, Ihlara and more). It's worth it if you plan to visit three or more paid sites, but check the current price, since fees rise often.

How much a trip to Cappadocia costs

  • Shoestring: about €45-60 a day. That covers a hostel dorm or simple pension (€15-25), lokanta meals and street food (€15), dolmuş rides, and one paid site a day.
  • Mid-range: about €120-180 a day. That covers a cave hotel double split between two (€50-90 each), restaurant dinners with wine, a Green Tour or car rental, and the museum pass.
  • Balloon flight: add €200-350 per person on top of either budget.

Day trips from Cappadocia

  • Soğanlı Valley: rock churches with almost no visitors, about 1 hour south of Ürgüp. You need a car or taxi. Plan half a day.
  • Kayseri: a big, conservative city with a Seljuk old core, the covered bazaar, and the best mantı and pastırma. It's about 1 hour by bus from Nevşehir or Ürgüp.
  • Mount Erciyes: an extinct volcano near Kayseri with a ski resort in winter, about 1.5 hours away.
  • Konya: home of the Mevlana Museum and Rumi's tomb, about 3 hours by bus. It's a long day, so it's better as a stop on your way west.
  • Tuz Gölü (Salt Lake): a vast white salt flat about 1.5-2 hours west, often on the way to Konya. Go at sunset.

Common mistakes and scams

  • Booking the cheapest balloon you can find. Cheap flights often mean 20-28 people in a basket and shorter flights. Ask about basket size, flight duration and insurance.
  • Not leaving a spare morning. Flights are cancelled by the civil aviation authority when winds are too strong. Book on your first morning so you can roll over.
  • Fake "official" balloon sellers on the street. Book through your hotel or a known agency. Don't pay cash deposits to touts.
  • Carpet and onyx shop stops on tours. Many cheap tours include a long "demonstration" at a shop that pays commission. Ask beforehand, and you're never obliged to buy.
  • Taxis without a meter. Agree a price first or insist on the meter. Your hotel can quote fair fares.
  • Underestimating the sun. The valleys have almost no shade. Carry 1.5 litres of water and sunscreen even in spring.
  • Climbing on fairy chimneys. It's fragile, sometimes fenced off, and you can be fined.

Cappadocia FAQ

How many days do you need in Cappadocia?

Four days is ideal. It covers the valleys, an underground city, Ihlara and a backup morning for the balloon. Two days is the bare minimum.

How much is a hot air balloon ride in Cappadocia?

Expect about €200-350 per person in high season for a standard flight, and less in winter. Prices change with demand and the euro-lira exchange rate.

Is Göreme or Uçhisar better to stay in?

Göreme is better for budgets, walking access and restaurants. Uçhisar is quieter, more upmarket and has better views.

Can you visit Cappadocia without a tour?

Yes. Göreme's sights and the valleys are walkable, and dolmuş minibuses link the villages. You only really need a tour or car for Ihlara and the underground cities.

Which underground city is best, Derinkuyu or Kaymaklı?

Derinkuyu is deeper and more dramatic. Kaymaklı is closer to Göreme and less cramped. One is enough.

What is the best month to visit Cappadocia?

May and late September to October are best, with warm days, cool nights and frequent balloon flights. Winter is cheaper and beautiful in snow, but colder and less reliable for flying.

What is the nearest airport to Cappadocia?

Nevşehir Kapadokya (NAV) is the closest, about 40 minutes from Göreme. Kayseri (ASR) has more flights and is about 1 hour 15 minutes away.
