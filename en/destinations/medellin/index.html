Medellin in 4 Days: Metrocables, Markets and Mountains

🏨 Hotels in Medellin

Medellin is a big, hilly, working city of around 2.5 million people squeezed into the Aburrá Valley. It has a properly good metro and cable car system, mild weather all year and a famous turnaround from the violence of the 1980s and 90s. It is not a postcard colonial town like Cartagena. The centre is chaotic and gritty, the nice neighbourhoods are modern, and much of the appeal is the people, the food, the views from the hillsides and how easy it is to live well on not much money.

It suits travellers who like cities with energy, food lovers, digital nomads, hikers and anyone flying into Colombia on a cheap fare and wanting a soft landing. We think 4 days is right: three for the city itself and one for the day trip to Guatapé, which almost everyone does and which is worth it. With only two days you will see the highlights but spend too much time in traffic.

What to see in Medellin: the places worth your time

Comuna 13

Comuna 13 is the hillside barrio that went from one of the most dangerous places in the city to its most visited, thanks to outdoor escalators, graffiti and community-led tourism. Go for the street art, the hip-hop dancers and the view back over the valley. Allow 2-3 hours. Walking around is free, and a guided tour with a local guide costs about €10-20 (45,000-90,000 COP). Our tip: go first thing in the morning, because by midday it is wall-to-wall tour groups and selfie sticks. Take the metro to San Javier, then a short bus or taxi up.

Plaza Botero and the Museo de Antioquia

Plaza Botero holds 23 bronze sculptures by Fernando Botero, the city's most famous son, and the plaza itself is free. Next door, the Museo de Antioquia has a large collection of his paintings plus works he donated by other artists, for about €5-6 (around 25,000 COP). Give it 2 hours together. The striking black-and-white Palacio de la Cultura Rafael Uribe Uribe stands on the same square. Go in the morning on a weekday, keep your phone in your pocket and leave before dusk, as the area gets sketchy.

Metrocable Line K to Santo Domingo

The cheapest scenic ride in South America is a normal metro ticket, about €0.85 (around 3,800 COP). From Acevedo station, Line K glides over the brick houses of the northeastern comunas up to Santo Domingo. It takes 15-20 minutes each way and you can stay in the system without paying again. Avoid rush hour, when the cabins are packed with commuters and you will not see much.

Parque Arví

Parque Arví is a big ecological reserve of pine and native forest on the mountain above the city, reached by continuing from Santo Domingo on Metrocable Line L. Line L charges a separate fare of about €3 (around 13,000 COP), and the park itself is free to wander, with paid guided trails. Allow half a day. Bring a jacket, as it is noticeably colder up there, and go on a weekday because the Line L queue on Sundays can run past an hour. Check the line is running before you set off, since it closes for maintenance.

Museo Casa de la Memoria

This museum covers Colombia's armed conflict through the stories of victims, and it is the most important visit in the city if you want to understand where Medellin has come from. Entry is free. Plan on 1.5-2 hours, and read up a little beforehand because some displays are in Spanish only. It sits next to Parque Bicentenario, a short walk from the San José tram stop.

Jardín Botánico and Parque Explora

The botanical garden is free, shady and calm, with an orchid house and a striking wooden canopy called the Orquideorama. Spend an hour or two there. Parque Explora across the road is a science museum with a large freshwater aquarium, worth it with kids, at about €8-10. Both are right by the Universidad metro station on Line A, and the Planetario and Parque de los Deseos are next door too.

Pueblito Paisa on Cerro Nutibara

Pueblito Paisa is a replica of a traditional Antioquian village on a small hill in the middle of the city. The village is a bit twee, but the 360-degree view from the top is the best easy viewpoint in town. It is free and takes about an hour. Take a taxi up, around €3, and come late afternoon for the light.

Museo de Arte Moderno de Medellín (MAMM)

MAMM sits in a converted industrial building in Ciudad del Río and puts on solid contemporary exhibitions, with a good bookshop and café. Entry costs about €4-5, and an hour and a half is enough. Afterwards, walk to Provenza or eat nearby.

El Poblado and Provenza

El Poblado is the leafy, upmarket area in the south-east where most visitors stay, and Provenza is its densest grid of cafés, bars and restaurants. Parque Lleras is the nightlife hub. It is pleasant and safe by Medellin standards, but it is also the most expensive and most gringo part of the city. Wander Provenza in the afternoon, then judge for yourself at night.

Laureles and La 70

Laureles is a flat, residential neighbourhood west of the river with tree-lined circulars and a more local feel than Poblado. La 70, the main strip, fills up with bars, salsa and fried food at weekends. It is free to wander, and you can eat well there for half Poblado prices. Its main circulars are walkable from Estadio station.

Cerro de las Tres Cruces

This is a steep hike locals do for exercise, topped by three crosses and a broad view over the valley. It takes about 45-60 minutes up and costs nothing. Go at dawn on a weekend when it is crowded with joggers and families (safer that way), take water and leave valuables at home.

Plaza Minorista

Medellin's big wholesale market for fruit, vegetables, meat and flowers sits just north of the centre. Come for exotic fruits like lulo, guanábana, granadilla and maracuyá, and for cheap juices. It is free to walk around, takes an hour, and you can taste fruit for a couple of euros. Go in the morning, ask prices before tasting and go with someone if you can, as the surrounding streets are rough.

Things to do in Medellin

The best part of Medellin is not on a museum list. Here is what we would add.

  • Take a salsa class. Plenty of schools in Poblado and Laureles offer group classes for about €5-10 or private lessons around €15-20. Then go dancing at a salsa bar on La 70 or in the centre.
  • Watch a football match. Atlético Nacional and Independiente Medellín share the Estadio Atanasio Girardot. The atmosphere is intense. Go with a group tour or local friends, never wear the rival colours, and expect tickets from €10-30.
  • Paraglide over the valley. Tandem flights from San Félix, about an hour north, cost roughly €40-60 for 15-20 minutes. Go in the morning for calmer conditions.
  • Do a coffee farm tour. Several farms on the edge of the city and in nearby hills offer half-day tours for €30-50 with transport. You get better coffee country further south, but this is the easy version.
  • Ride the whole metro network. Combine Metrocable lines, the Ayacucho tram and the escalators for a cheap afternoon of urban sightseeing.
  • Eat your way through a food hall. Mercado del Río in Ciudad del Río has dozens of stalls under one roof. It is convenient rather than cheap, at €8-15 a plate.
  • Visit Envigado. This separate municipality just south of Poblado has a lively main park and a more local evening scene. The metro gets you there in minutes.

Medellin for free (or almost)

Many of the best things here cost nothing or close to it:

  • Plaza Botero's sculptures and the Palacio de la Cultura exterior.
  • Museo Casa de la Memoria, free.
  • Jardín Botánico, free.
  • Pueblito Paisa and its viewpoint, free (a taxi up is about €3).
  • Walking Comuna 13 on your own in daylight, free, though tipping the performers is expected.
  • Metrocable Line K for the price of a metro ticket, under €1.
  • Cerro de las Tres Cruces at dawn, free.
  • Free walking tours of the centre run daily. They are tip-based, and €8-10 is fair. Real Tours has the best reputation and books up.

Medellin itinerary: 4 days

Day 1: Centre and Metrocable

Morning: Do a free walking tour of the centre or go to Plaza Botero and the Museo de Antioquia on your own. Afternoon: Ride the metro to Acevedo and up Metrocable Line K to Santo Domingo, then back down and on to Universidad for the Jardín Botánico. Evening: Return to Poblado for dinner in Provenza.

Day 2: Comuna 13 and Laureles

Morning: Get to Comuna 13 early. Afternoon: Head to the Museo Casa de la Memoria, then take a taxi up to Pueblito Paisa for late-afternoon views. Evening: Eat a cheap dinner in Laureles, then catch some salsa on La 70 if it is the weekend.

Day 3: Guatapé

Full day: Take an early bus from Terminal Norte to El Peñol rock, climb the 700-odd steps, then go into Guatapé town for its colourful zócalos and lunch by the reservoir. Be back in Medellin by evening, tired.

Day 4: Parque Arví and Ciudad del Río

Morning: Take Metrocable K then L to Parque Arví for a forest walk. Afternoon: Head down to MAMM and eat at Mercado del Río. Evening: Finish with a farewell night in Envigado or a rooftop bar in Poblado.

What is overrated (and what to do instead)

Pablo Escobar tours. Many Colombians find the narco tourism grim, and some tours glamorise a man who killed thousands. Go to the Casa de la Memoria instead, which tells the story from the victims' side.

Parque Lleras at night. It is loud, overpriced and full of hustlers, and it has a seedy undertone. You will have a better night on La 70, in Envigado or at a proper salsa bar.

Comuna 13 at midday. Arriving at noon on a Saturday means shuffling in a queue past souvenir stalls. Go early on a weekday, or skip the hard sell of the most crowded stretches and take a walk with a resident guide.

Living entirely in Poblado. Plenty of visitors never leave it and come away thinking Medellin is all brunch spots and English menus. Spend at least a day in Laureles, the centre and the north.

When to visit Medellin

Medellin is good any month: it sits at about 1,500 metres, so days are around 22-28°C and nights are cooler all year. The rainiest stretches are roughly April-May and October-November, with sudden afternoon downpours, so plan outdoor things for the morning. December to February and July to August are drier.

The Feria de las Flores in early August and the Christmas lights (alumbrados) in December are the busiest and priciest times, so book accommodation well ahead. February-March and September are quieter and usually cheaper for flights and rooms.

Where to stay in Medellin: best areas

  • El Poblado: safest and easiest for a first visit, with restaurants and nightlife everywhere. Hostel dorms cost €10-18, mid-range hotels €45-90.
  • Laureles: flat, walkable, more local and better value. It suits second-timers and longer stays. Doubles cost about €30-60.
  • Envigado: quieter, residential, cheaper, and near the metro. It is good for nomads. Apartments run about €30-50.
  • Manila (in Poblado): a smaller pocket with good cafés and hostels. It is less frantic than Lleras. Dorms cost €10-15, private rooms €30-50.

We would avoid staying in the centre. It is fine by day, but empties out and feels unsafe at night.

What to eat in Medellin

Paisa food is hearty, meaty and built for farm workers. Order the bandeja paisa once: beans, rice, ground beef, chicharrón, chorizo, fried egg, plantain, avocado and arepa on one plate. Hatoviejo is a reliable classic, though we would share one. Mondongo, a tripe soup, is better than it sounds, and Mondongo's is the famous place for it. Other things to try:

  • Arepas with everything, including the plain, slightly bland paisa version.
  • Buñuelos and pandebono for breakfast.
  • Empanadas with ají sauce from any street stand.
  • Fresh juices made with lulo, maracuyá or mora, in water or milk.
  • Obleas, thin wafers with arequipe, as a street snack.
  • Tinto, the small black street coffee, for pennies (although the specialty coffee shops in Poblado are better).

Look for a menú del día or "corrientazo" at lunch: soup, a main, rice and juice for about €3.50-6 (15,000-25,000 COP). Dinner in a good Provenza restaurant costs €15-30 with a drink. A beer is about €1.50 in a local bar and €3-4 in Poblado.

Airport to city centre and getting around

José María Córdova airport (MDE) is in Rionegro, over the mountains and about 45-60 minutes from the city depending on traffic.

  • Airport bus: Buses run to the San Diego area near the centre for about €4-5 (around 20,000 COP). From there, take a short taxi to Poblado or Laureles.
  • Official taxi: A fixed fare of roughly €22-28 (100,000-120,000 COP) to Poblado. Use the official rank, not the touts.
  • Ride-hailing apps: Uber, DiDi and inDrive operate, often slightly cheaper than taxis. Pickups at the airport can be awkward, though.

In town, the metro is clean, safe and cheap at under €1 a ride. Get a rechargeable Cívica card at a station to skip ticket queues. Tourists can usually get one with a passport, and topping up works across metro, Metrocable, tram and integrated buses. Taxis are inexpensive (€3-6 for most cross-town rides), but use an app or make sure the meter is on.

How much a trip to Medellin costs

  • Shoestring: €30-45 a day. This covers a hostel dorm, menú del día lunches, street food, metro transport and free sights.
  • Mid-range: €70-120 a day. This covers a private room or decent hotel in Poblado or Laureles, sit-down dinners, taxis, a paid tour or two and some nights out.
  • Guatapé day trip: €15-25 on your own by bus, €40-60 on an organised tour.

Day trips from Medellin

Guatapé and El Peñol: The giant rock with a zigzag staircase overlooks a reservoir full of islands. Buses leave from Terminal Norte (Caribe metro station), taking about 2 hours and costing around €5. The climb costs about €6. Leave early and go on a weekday if you can.

Santa Fe de Antioquia: A whitewashed colonial town that is hot, slow and photogenic. Buses from Terminal Norte take about 1.5-2 hours, and the Puente de Occidente, an old suspension bridge, is nearby.

Jardín: A lovely coffee-country town with a colourful main square. It is 3-4 hours by bus from Terminal Sur, so we would stay at least one night rather than do it as a day trip.

San Félix: This is the place for paragliding and mountain views, about an hour north by bus or taxi.

Common mistakes and scams

  • "Dar papaya": This local phrase means making yourself an easy target. Do not walk around with your phone out on the street, especially in the centre.
  • Dating app and drink spiking: There have been serious cases of foreigners being drugged (with scopolamine and similar) and robbed after meeting people through apps or in bars. Meet in public, never leave your drink and do not bring strangers back to your room.
  • Taxis without meters: Use an app or insist on the meter, and avoid flagging cabs outside Parque Lleras late at night.
  • Fake notes and change tricks: Check bills, especially 50,000 COP notes, and count your change.
  • Underestimating traffic: Cross-town rides at rush hour can take an hour. Use the metro where possible.
  • Getting your Guatapé timing wrong: Arriving at El Peñol at noon on a Sunday means queues and heat.

Medellin FAQ

How many days do you need in Medellin?

Four days is ideal: three for the city and one for Guatapé. Two days covers Comuna 13, the centre and the Metrocable, but feels rushed.

Is Medellin safe for tourists?

It is much safer than its reputation, and Poblado, Laureles and Envigado are fine with normal precautions. Petty theft and drugging scams are the real risks, so keep valuables hidden and be careful with nightlife.

Is Comuna 13 worth visiting?

Yes, especially early in the morning or with a local guide who explains its history. It is crowded and commercial at peak times, but still worth the trip.

What is the best area to stay in Medellin?

El Poblado is best for a first trip and for nightlife. Laureles is better value and feels more local.

How do I get from Medellin airport to El Poblado?

A fixed-fare taxi costs about €22-28 and takes 45-60 minutes. The airport bus to San Diego is about €4-5, plus a short taxi ride.

Do I need cash in Medellin?

Yes, for street food, markets, small shops and buses, though cards work in most restaurants and hotels. Use ATMs inside banks or malls.

What is the weather like in Medellin?

It is spring-like all year, about 22-28°C by day. Expect afternoon rain, especially in April-May and October-November.

Is Guatapé worth a day trip from Medellin?

Yes, it is the best day trip from the city. Go early on a weekday to beat the queues at El Peñol.
