# Things to Do in Seville: 3 Days of Tapas and Palaces

> Seville travel guide by Happy Low Cost, 3-day itinerary. Original page: https://www.happylowcost.com/en/destinations/seville/
> Updated: 2026-11-21

Seville is the Spain people picture before they've been: orange trees, tiled courtyards, flamenco, and tapas bars where locals eat standing up at 10pm. The postcard version is real. So is the less romantic side: the centre fills with tour groups by 11am, summer afternoons get dangerously hot, and the streets around the cathedral serve some of the worst paella in Andalusia. Get the timing right and it's one of the most rewarding cities in Europe, and still cheaper than Madrid or Barcelona.

It suits couples, food lovers, architecture fans and anyone flying in on a budget, since flights from across Europe are often cheap. Families do fine too, with lots of parks and pedestrian streets. **Three days is the right amount**: two for the big sights and a third for Triana, a museum or two and a slower evening. Add a fourth if you want a day trip to Córdoba or Cádiz.

## What to see in Seville: the places worth your time

### Real Alcázar

The Alcázar is the one sight you can't skip. It's a royal palace still used by the Spanish monarchy, built mostly in the Mudéjar style, with carved plaster, tiled halls and gardens big enough to wander for an hour. Allow 2-3 hours and budget about €16-20 for general entry, more if you add the upper royal rooms.

**Our tip:** book a timed ticket online days ahead (weeks ahead around Easter) and take the first slot of the day. Once the tour groups arrive the Courtyard of the Maidens turns into a photo queue. Save the gardens for the end.

### Seville Cathedral and the Giralda

This is the largest Gothic cathedral in the world, and it holds what is said to be Christopher Columbus's tomb. The real reward is the Giralda, the old minaret turned bell tower. You climb ramps rather than stairs (built so a man on horseback could ride up), and the view over the rooftops is excellent.

Expect about €13-15 online and 1.5-2 hours. Buy online to skip the ticket line, and climb the Giralda before the Patio de los Naranjos fills up.

### Plaza de España

Built for the 1929 Ibero-American Exhibition, it's a huge half-circle of brick and painted tiles with a tiled alcove for each Spanish province. It's free and open, so you can come any time. One hour is enough unless you rent a rowing boat on the canal (around €6-8 for a short session).

Go at sunset or early morning. At midday in summer the plaza is a frying pan with no shade, and the hawkers selling fans and castanets are out in force.

### Parque de María Luisa

Right next to Plaza de España, this is the city's best park: shaded paths, ponds, tiled benches and ducks. It's free, and it's the place to go when the heat gets too much. At the far end, Plaza de América has the Archaeological Museum and the Museum of Popular Arts and Customs.

### Barrio Santa Cruz

The old Jewish quarter is a maze of narrow lanes, whitewashed houses and tiny squares beside the Alcázar walls. It's pretty and completely touristy. Walk it early, before 9am, or late in the evening, when Plaza de Santa Cruz and Callejón del Agua are quiet and you can hear your own footsteps.

### Metropol Parasol (Las Setas)

Locals call this giant wooden waffle structure in Plaza de la Encarnación "the mushrooms". You walk a rooftop path with views over the old town, and Roman ruins sit in the basement (the Antiquarium). The rooftop now costs about €15, which is steep for what it is. Go at sunset, or skip it if you've already climbed the Giralda.

### Triana

Cross the Puente de Isabel II and you're in Triana, the old neighbourhood of sailors, potters and flamenco singers. Start at the Mercado de Triana, then walk Calle Betis along the river and browse the ceramic shops on Calle Alfarería and Calle Antillano Campos. Give it half a day, including lunch.

Under the market is the Castillo de San Jorge, a small, free and sobering museum about the Inquisition, which had its headquarters here.

### Casa de Pilatos

This 16th-century ducal palace mixes Mudéjar, Gothic and Renaissance styles. Many people think its tiles beat the Alcázar's, and it gets a fraction of the crowds. Allow an hour and about €12-18, depending on whether you add the upper floor.

### Palacio de las Dueñas

This was the home of the Duchess of Alba, with a courtyard full of bougainvillea and a garden straight out of a painting. It's smaller and calmer than the Alcázar, costs about €12 and takes 45 minutes. It's good in the afternoon when the big sights are rammed.

### Museo de Bellas Artes

Spain's second-best collection of old masters after the Prado, with Murillo, Zurbarán and Valdés Leal, housed in a former convent with lovely cloisters. Entry is about €1.50 and free for EU citizens. Give it 1.5 hours. On Sunday mornings the Plaza del Museo outside hosts a small art market.

### Real Maestranza bullring

Whatever you think of bullfighting, this is one of the oldest and most elegant bullrings in Spain. The guided tour (about €10-12, 40 minutes) covers the arena and a small museum. You can visit without attending a fight.

### Torre del Oro and the river

This 13th-century watchtower on the Guadalquivir now holds a small naval museum (a few euros). The tower itself is fine but not a big deal. The real value is the riverside walk from here to Triana, especially at dusk.

### Hospital de los Venerables

This former home for retired priests in Santa Cruz has a sunken courtyard and a chapel with stunning frescoes, plus a few Velázquez works. Most visitors walk past it. It costs about €10 and takes 45 minutes.

## Things to do in Seville

The best Seville moments usually happen between the sights, not inside them.

- **See flamenco in an intimate venue.** Skip the dinner-and-show tablaos. Casa de la Memoria and the Museo del Baile Flamenco run small, serious shows for about €25-30. Book a day or two ahead.

- **Do a tapas crawl the local way.** Order one or two dishes per bar, then move on. Calle Feria, Alfalfa and Triana are the best areas for it.

- **Ride a bike.** Seville is flat and has good bike lanes. The public Sevici scheme has a weekly pass, or rent a bike for about €12-15 a day.

- **Kayak or paddle the Guadalquivir.** Several outfits run sunset trips, which is a smarter way to see the river than the packed tour boats.

- **Spend an evening on the Alameda de Hércules.** This long plaza north of the centre is where younger locals drink outdoors. It's less polished and more real.

- **Visit the Thursday market on Calle Feria.** Known as "El Jueves", it's one of the oldest flea markets in Spain: junk, antiques and odd old records.

- **Take a hammam.** Aire de Sevilla in an old palace near Santa Cruz is pricey (about €40-60) but a good rest after a day on hot cobblestones.

## Seville for free (or almost)

A lot of Seville costs nothing. Plaza de España, Parque de María Luisa, Barrio Santa Cruz and the river walk are all free and among the best things in the city.

- **Archivo de Indias:** free, next to the cathedral, with documents from Spain's colonial empire in a handsome Renaissance building.

- **Castillo de San Jorge:** free, under the Triana market.

- **Museo de Bellas Artes:** €1.50, or free for EU citizens.

- **Real Alcázar:** it has historically offered free slots on Monday evenings, but these are limited and often reserved for residents. Check before you plan around it.

- **Cathedral:** attending a mass is free, though you won't get the tourist route or the Giralda.

- **Churches:** the Basílica de la Macarena and the Iglesia del Salvador are cheap or free at certain times and well worth seeing.

- **Sunset:** watching the sun go down from Calle Betis or the Puente de Triana costs nothing.

## What is overrated (and what to do instead)

- **Horse-drawn carriages.** They're pricey, slow in traffic and controversial in summer heat. Rent a bike instead.

- **The restaurants on Calle Mateos Gago and right around the cathedral.** The menus come in six languages, with frozen paella and inflated prices. Walk ten minutes to Calle Feria or Triana and you'll eat better for less.

- **Las Setas at full price.** If you've climbed the Giralda, you've already had the better view. Go only at sunset, or not at all.

- **Big dinner-and-flamenco shows.** They're long, overpriced and the food is mediocre. Book a small venue and eat elsewhere.

## When to visit Seville

**The best months are March to May and late September to November.** Expect 20-28°C, oranges in blossom in spring, and pleasant evenings.

July and August are brutal. Temperatures of 40°C and above are normal, sightseeing after noon is a struggle, and some locals leave town. Prices for hotels drop, though, so it works if you're happy to live at night and nap through the afternoon.

Semana Santa (Holy Week) and the Feria de Abril are spectacular, but hotel prices can triple and the centre gets gridlocked. Book months ahead or avoid those weeks. Winter (December to February) is the cheapest and quietest time, with mild days around 15-18°C and cold nights in poorly heated buildings.

## Where to stay in Seville: best areas

- **Santa Cruz / Cathedral area:** ideal for first-timers who want to walk everywhere. It's touristy and the most expensive, at about €110-200 a night for a mid-range double.

- **Alfalfa and El Centro:** central, livelier and with better bars. It's our pick for most visitors, at roughly €90-160.

- **Triana:** local feel, great food, and a 10-15 minute walk to the cathedral. Expect €80-140.

- **Alameda / Macarena:** younger and artsier, with good nightlife and cheaper rooms, though it's a bit of a walk to the main sights. Around €60-120.

- **Hostels around the centre:** dorm beds cost about €20-35 most of the year, more during the Feria.

## What to eat in Seville

Seville is a tapas city: small plates, shared, eaten at the bar. Here's what to order:

- **Salmorejo:** a thick, cold tomato and bread soup topped with ham and egg.

- **Espinacas con garbanzos:** spinach and chickpeas with cumin, a Moorish-influenced classic.

- **Carrillada:** slow-cooked pork cheeks that fall apart with a fork.

- **Pringá:** shredded stewed meats in a small roll (a montadito).

- **Cazón en adobo:** marinated fried dogfish.

- **Solomillo al whisky:** pork loin in a garlic and whisky sauce, a local favourite.

- **Tortillitas de camarones:** crispy shrimp fritters, more of a Cádiz thing but common here.

- **Drinks:** a cold Cruzcampo, a glass of Manzanilla sherry, or tinto de verano when it's hot.

Some reliable names: El Rinconcillo (open since 1670) for the history, Bodeguita Romero for pringá, and Las Golondrinas in Triana for grilled pork.

Tapas usually cost €3-5 each. A weekday lunch menu (menú del día) runs €13-18 with drink. A full tapas dinner for two with wine comes to about €40-60. Locals eat lunch around 2-3pm and dinner from 9pm, and kitchens often close in between.

## Airport to city centre and getting around

**The EA airport bus is the cheapest way into town.** It costs about €4-5, runs every 20-30 minutes and takes 35-40 minutes to Plaza de Armas, with stops at Santa Justa and Prado de San Sebastián. Taxis charge a fixed fare of about €25-30 to the centre, depending on time and day, and take around 20 minutes. Uber and Cabify also operate.

Once you're in town you'll mostly walk. The old centre is compact, and many streets are too narrow for buses. For longer hops:

- **Tussam buses:** about €1.40 a single. A rechargeable card brings the cost down, and the tourist card covers 1 or 3 days.

- **Metrocentro tram:** a short line from Plaza Nueva to San Bernardo, past the cathedral.

- **Metro Line 1:** useful mainly for outer areas.

- **Sevici bikes:** a weekly subscription is cheap, and the bike lanes are excellent.

## Day trips from Seville

- **Córdoba:** the high-speed AVE train takes about 45 minutes, and the Mezquita alone is worth the trip. It's a full day.

- **Cádiz:** about 1h45 by train. You get an old port city, the sea, beaches and great fried fish.

- **Itálica:** Roman ruins and a huge amphitheatre in Santiponce, about 20-30 minutes by bus from Plaza de Armas. Entry is cheap or free for EU citizens. Half a day is enough.

- **Ronda:** about 2 hours by bus. The gorge and bridge are dramatic. It's a long day, better with a car or a tour.

- **Carmona:** about 45 minutes by bus. A quiet hilltop town with a Roman necropolis and a fine parador for a drink with a view.

## Common mistakes and scams

- **The rosemary women.** Around the cathedral and Alcázar, women press a sprig of rosemary into your hand, "read your fortune" and then demand money. Keep your hands to yourself and keep walking.

- **Not booking the Alcázar.** Queues and sold-out slots are common in high season, so book online.

- **Sightseeing at 3pm in July.** Heatstroke is a genuine risk. Do outdoor things early and late, and siesta in between.

- **Eating at 7pm.** You'll find empty dining rooms or tourist traps. Have a snack and eat after 9pm.

- **Pickpockets.** Watch your bag on crowded buses and during processions.

- **Unofficial ticket resellers.** Buy from the monuments' own websites.

## Seville FAQ

### How many days do you need in Seville?

Three days covers the main sights at a comfortable pace. Add a fourth for a day trip to Córdoba or Cádiz.

### Is Seville expensive?

Not by Western European standards. Tapas at €3-5 and cheap lunch menus keep costs down, though hotels jump during Easter and the Feria.

### Do I need to book the Alcázar in advance?

Yes, especially from March to October. Book on the official website and take the earliest slot you can.

### What is the best month to visit Seville?

April, May, October and early November offer warm weather without the extreme summer heat. Avoid July and August unless you handle heat well.

### Is Seville walkable?

Very. Almost every major sight sits within a 30-minute walk of the cathedral, and the city is flat.

### Is Seville safe for tourists?

Yes. It's generally safe. The main issues are pickpockets in crowds and the rosemary scam near the cathedral.

### Can you do Seville and Córdoba in one trip?

Easily. The AVE train takes about 45 minutes, so Córdoba works as a day trip from Seville.
