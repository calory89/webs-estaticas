# Los Cabos in 5 Days: Beaches, Whales and Real Tacos

> Los Cabos travel guide by Happy Low Cost, 5-day itinerary. Original page: https://www.happylowcost.com/en/destinations/los-cabos/
> Updated: 2026-11-21

Los Cabos is two very different towns joined by a 30 km strip of resorts. Cabo San Lucas is the loud one: marina, party boats, El Arco and bars playing the same playlist until 2am. San José del Cabo, closer to the airport, is quieter, prettier and better for dinner. Between them runs the Tourist Corridor, where the best snorkelling bays and the most expensive hotels sit side by side. It is not cheap Mexico. Prices are pegged to American visitors, and many places quote in dollars before pesos.

It suits couples, groups of friends, divers, anglers and anyone who wants sun and good seafood without a long-haul connection through Mexico City. Families do fine if they stay away from the Pacific-facing beaches, where the surf kills people every year. We would give it **5 days**: two in Cabo San Lucas and the bays, one in San José, and two for day trips to Todos Santos and either Cabo Pulmo or La Paz. Three days covers the highlights but leaves you living in the resort bubble.

## What to see in Los Cabos: the places worth your time

### El Arco (Land's End)

The rock arch where the Sea of Cortez meets the Pacific is the postcard shot of Los Cabos, and it does deserve the boat ride. You can only see it properly from the water: glass-bottom pangas leave from the marina and Médano Beach, and a 45-60 minute loop costs about €15-20 (MXN 300-400) per person. Our tip: go first thing in the morning, before the party catamarans crowd the sea lion colony, and agree the price before you step aboard.

### Playa del Amor (Lover's Beach)

Most panga tours will drop you here and collect you later, which is the best way to do it. The sheltered side faces the Sea of Cortez and is calm enough to swim; walk through the rocks and you reach Playa del Divorcio on the Pacific side, which is stunning and absolutely not for swimming. Allow 1-2 hours. There is no shade, no shop and no toilets, so take water and something to sit on.

### Playa El Médano

Cabo San Lucas's main beach is long, swimmable and lined with beach clubs, jet-ski renters and vendors selling silver, hats and sarongs every four minutes. It is fun for an afternoon with a beer, less so if you want peace. Free to use; a lounger with drinks minimum runs about €15-30 (MXN 300-600). Walk towards the eastern end, near the hotels, for more space.

### Cabo San Lucas Marina

The boardwalk around the marina is where fishing charters, yachts and sunset cruises depart, and where most touts will try to sell you a timeshare dressed up as a free breakfast. It is worth a 1-hour evening stroll for the fishing boats coming in and the sea lions lounging near the fish-cleaning stations. Free. Skip the restaurants facing the water, where you pay double for the view.

### Bahía Chileno

The best public snorkelling beach on the Corridor, around 15 km from Cabo San Lucas, with a reef close enough to swim to from the sand. Access is free, there are public toilets and some shade palapas, and you can rent snorkel gear on site for about €10-15. Arrive before 10am: the car park fills, and tour boats arrive late morning. Half a day is plenty.

### Bahía Santa María

Chileno's neighbour is a horseshoe cove with calmer water and more fish, though with less facilities. Free entry and parking (a small tip to the parking attendant is normal). Bring your own gear and water. If you only have time for one bay, choose Santa María for snorkelling and Chileno for a lazy beach day.

### San José del Cabo historic centre

The old town around Plaza Mijares, with its colonial church and pastel streets, is the nicest place in Los Cabos to wander and eat. You need 2-3 hours by day, longer if you stay for dinner. Free. Come in the late afternoon when the heat drops and the galleries along Calle Obregón open their doors.

### San José Art Walk

On Thursday evenings from roughly November to June, the galleries of the Distrito del Arte stay open late, often with a glass of wine, and the streets fill with stalls and musicians. It is the closest Los Cabos gets to a local evening out, even if half the crowd is visitors. Free to wander. Have dinner afterwards rather than before, because restaurants are packed between 7 and 9pm.

### Misión de San José del Cabo

The current church on Plaza Mijares is a 20th-century rebuild of the 1730 Jesuit mission, so manage expectations. Still, it is a 15-minute stop if you are already in the plaza, with a mural over the entrance recalling the mission's violent early history. Free; dress modestly if a service is on.

### Estero San José

This freshwater estuary, where the San José river meets the sea, is a bird-watching spot with palm-lined paths right next to the hotel zone. Allow an hour or two for a walk, or kayak it if a local operator is running trips. Free. Go early morning for birds and before the humidity kicks in.

### La Playita and Puerto Los Cabos

East of San José, La Playita is a fishing beach where pangas still launch from the sand, and the adjacent marina at Puerto Los Cabos has a sculpture walk worth a look. The open beach is not great for swimming, but the area is good for a quiet afternoon and cheap fishing charters. Free.

### Flora Farms

An organic farm in the foothills above San José, with a restaurant, bar and shop, and a reputation that sometimes outruns the reality. The setting is gorgeous and the produce excellent, but you will pay resort prices (dinner €50-80 per person). Book ahead, and go for brunch or a cocktail at sunset if you want the atmosphere without the full bill.

## Things to do in Los Cabos

The main reason to come is the water, and most of the best experiences happen on it rather than in town.

- **Whale watching (December to April):** humpbacks pass right along the coast. Small-boat tours cost about €50-80 for 2-3 hours. Pick operators with smaller boats over the big catamarans.

- **Snorkelling and diving:** Chileno and Santa María are easy from the beach; for serious diving, Cabo Pulmo is the one.

- **Sport fishing:** Los Cabos is one of the world's big marlin destinations. A shared or small panga charter from La Playita is far cheaper than a marina yacht, from about €200-350 for half a day.

- **Sunset sail:** pick a small sailboat over a party boat if you want to actually see the sunset. Around €60-100.

- **Surfing:** Costa Azul near San José has breaks for intermediates; beginners should head to Cerritos near Todos Santos.

- **ATV and camel rides:** sold everywhere. Fun if you like it, but they are pricey (€80-150) and the dust is real.

- **Sea turtle releases:** run by conservation groups between roughly August and December. Book through reputable outfits.

## Los Cabos for free (or almost)

Free and almost free things exist, though you will need to plan around them. Every beach in Mexico is public by law, even if a hotel acts otherwise; use official access points.

- Snorkel at Chileno or Santa María with your own gear.

- Walk the San José historic centre and catch the Thursday Art Walk.

- Stroll the Cabo San Lucas marina at sunset to see the sea lions.

- Look for whales from shore at Playa Palmilla or the Corridor viewpoints in season.

- Browse the Saturday organic market in San José for cheap breakfasts.

- Bird-watching at Estero San José.

## Los Cabos itinerary: 5 days

### Day 1: Cabo San Lucas and El Arco

**Morning:** early panga to El Arco and Lover's Beach, then a couple of hours on the sand. **Afternoon:** lunch on tacos in town, then Médano Beach with a cold drink. **Evening:** walk the marina, eat tacos at a stand like Gardenias, and decide whether you have the energy for the bars.

### Day 2: The Corridor bays

**Morning:** head to Santa María by 9am to snorkel. **Afternoon:** move to Chileno to relax under a palapa. **Evening:** sunset sail from Cabo San Lucas, or an early night if you plan to go far tomorrow.

### Day 3: San José del Cabo

**Morning:** bird walk at Estero San José, then coffee near Plaza Mijares. **Afternoon:** La Playita and Puerto Los Cabos, maybe a fishing trip. **Evening:** the Art Walk if it is Thursday, then dinner in the old town.

### Day 4: Todos Santos and Cerritos

**Morning:** drive about 1 hour north to Todos Santos and wander the galleries and old town. **Afternoon:** surf lesson or beach time at Cerritos. **Evening:** back in Cabo for a cheap dinner.

### Day 5: Cabo Pulmo or La Paz

**Morning:** leave early for Cabo Pulmo to dive or snorkel, or for La Paz to see whale sharks in season. **Afternoon:** lunch near the water. **Evening:** final dinner in San José, which is closer to the airport.

## What is overrated (and what to do instead)

- **Party boats:** loud, crowded, and you barely see El Arco. Take a small panga in the morning instead.

- **Marina restaurants:** mediocre food at high prices. Walk a few blocks inland.

- **Swimming with dolphins:** expensive and ethically questionable. Do a whale-watching trip or snorkel instead.

- **All-inclusive dinners every night:** you miss the best food in the area. Eat out in San José at least twice.

## When to visit Los Cabos

The best time to visit is November to April: dry, sunny, around 25-28°C, with whales from December. That is also high season, and Christmas and US spring break (March) push prices way up. May and June are hotter but calmer and good value. July to October is hot and humid, with hurricane risk from August to October, though you get the biggest discounts. Sea temperatures are warmest in late summer.

## Where to stay in Los Cabos: best areas

- **Cabo San Lucas centre and Médano:** best for nightlife and walking to the beach and marina. About €80-250 per night.

- **San José del Cabo old town:** best for food, couples and a calmer pace, and close to the airport. About €70-200.

- **Tourist Corridor:** resorts and quiet beaches, but you need a car. €250-800+.

- **Pedregal:** luxury hillside villas above Cabo San Lucas, with views to match. €300+.

- **Todos Santos:** boutique stays, an artsy feel, and far from the crowds. €80-200.

## What to eat in Los Cabos

Seafood rules here, and the best food comes from taco stands rather than hotel restaurants. Look out for these:

- **Fish and shrimp tacos:** battered and fried, served with cabbage, crema and salsas. Expect €2-3 each.

- **Chocolate clams (almejas chocolatas):** local clams, often grilled with butter and cheese.

- **Aguachile:** raw shrimp in lime, chilli and cucumber.

- **Ceviche and tostadas:** fresh, cheap, perfect for lunch.

- **Damiana:** a local herbal liqueur, best in a margarita.

A taco lunch with a drink costs about €8-12 (MXN 160-240). A mid-range dinner in San José runs €30-50 per person, while marina or resort restaurants easily hit €60+.

## Airport to city centre and getting around

Los Cabos International Airport (SJD) sits about 13 km from San José and 45-50 km from Cabo San Lucas. Official taxis are expensive: about €40 to San José and €80-100 to Cabo. Shared shuttles cost about €20-25 per person, and private transfers booked in advance often beat the taxi rank. Águila buses have operated from the airport; check current schedules.

Between towns, public buses run along the Corridor for about €2-3 (MXN 40-60). Renting a car (from €30-50 per day, plus mandatory Mexican insurance) makes the most sense for bays and day trips. Rideshare apps work in town, but airport pickups have been contentious, so check before relying on them. There are no travel passes.

## How much a trip to Los Cabos costs

- **Shoestring:** €60-90 per day for a basic room or hostel, tacos, buses and free beaches.

- **Mid-range:** €150-250 per day for a decent hotel, a rental car, one tour and restaurant dinners.

- **Comfort:** €400+ per day for a resort, boat trips and fine dining.

## Day trips from Los Cabos

- **Todos Santos:** 1 hour by car on Highway 19, with an arty town and surf at Cerritos.

- **Cabo Pulmo:** about 2 hours, partly on a rough road. It is a protected reef with excellent diving.

- **La Paz:** about 2 hours away, with a malecón, Balandra beach and whale sharks (roughly October to April).

- **El Triunfo:** an old mining village on the way to La Paz, good for a lunch stop.

## Common mistakes and scams

- **The airport "shark tank":** people in uniforms offering free transport or tours are selling timeshares. Walk straight outside.

- **Swimming on the Pacific side:** beaches like Playa del Divorcio have deadly currents. Obey the red flags.

- **Paying in dollars:** the exchange rate is usually poor. Pay in pesos.

- **Unagreed prices:** confirm the taxi or panga fare before you start.

- **Skipping car insurance:** online rental quotes often exclude mandatory Mexican liability insurance.

## Los Cabos FAQ

### How many days do you need in Los Cabos?

Five days lets you see both towns, the bays and two day trips. Three days covers the basics.

### Is Los Cabos expensive?

Yes, by Mexican standards. Eating tacos and using buses keeps costs down.

### Is it better to stay in Cabo San Lucas or San José del Cabo?

Cabo San Lucas suits nightlife and beach access. San José is better for food and a quieter stay.

### Can you swim at the beaches in Los Cabos?

On the Sea of Cortez side, yes: Médano, Chileno and Santa María. On the Pacific side, no.

### When can you see whales in Los Cabos?

From December to April, with the peak in January to March.

### Do you need a car in Los Cabos?

Not if you stay in town, but it makes bays and day trips much easier.

### Is Los Cabos safe for tourists?

The tourist areas are generally safe. The biggest risks are the ocean currents and scams.
