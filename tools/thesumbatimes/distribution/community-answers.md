## Rules of thumb (read first)

1. **Answer first.** The answer must be complete in the text. Someone who never clicks should still get what they asked for. The link is a footnote.
2. **One link at most, and only when it adds something** the answer cannot hold: a table of dates, a month-by-month chart, a map. If the answer is complete without it, post no link.
3. **Disclose every time.** End with a line such as: "Disclosure: I publish The Sumba Times, the site linked above." On Quora, put it in your credential as well. Never post from a second account, never ask friends to upvote.
4. **Read the rules of each community before posting.**
   - Reddit: every subreddit has its own self-promotion rule in the sidebar or wiki. Many travel subreddits remove links to your own site or allow them only in a weekly thread; some forbid them entirely. If the rule says no, post the answer without the link, or do not post. A common yardstick across Reddit is that links to your own work should be a small minority of what you post (often described as about one in ten).
   - TripAdvisor forums: links to your own site and anything that looks like advertising are normally removed. Post the answer with no link and no site name.
   - Quora: links are tolerated when the answer stands on its own and the affiliation is disclosed.
   - Facebook groups: ask the admins first.
5. **Answer the question that was asked**, for that person's dates, budget and route. Adapt the draft; do not paste it unchanged twice in the same community.
6. **Only reply to questions that are recent** (days, not years) and where you can add something the existing answers lack.
7. **Never recommend a hotel**, including Villa Eco Beach, and never run down a competitor. If someone asks where to stay, describe areas, not properties.
8. **Be a member first.** Comment and help on threads where you have nothing to link. Accounts that only appear to drop links get banned, and deserve it.
9. **If you are corrected, thank the person** and fix the guide if they are right.
10. **No phone numbers, no private messages** offering services.

## English (15)

### 1. When is the best time to visit Sumba?

For most people, the dry season: roughly May to October, with June to August the sunniest and most comfortable. August and September are the driest (Waingapu gets about 5 mm of rain in a month). The landscape changes with it: the hills are green from late January to April and turn gold from July to October, so decide which Sumba you want in your photos. February and March are wet and the roads get muddy, but that is when the Pasola happens. April and early May are a sweet spot: still green, rains ending, few visitors. Waterfalls are fullest just after the rains, but the pools are clearer for swimming in the dry season. Surf is most consistent from about April to October.

Month-by-month detail: https://thesumbatimes.com/en/things-to-do/best-time-to-visit-sumba/?utm_source=reddit&utm_medium=community

### 2. How do I get to Sumba from Bali?

Fly. There are no international flights to Sumba, so nearly everyone comes on a short domestic flight from Denpasar. The island has two airports: Tambolaka (TMC) in the west, for the lagoons, Kodi and the traditional villages, and Waingapu (WGP) in the east, for the savanna hills. Wings Air has been flying Bali to Tambolaka several times a day, about 1 h 30. Since 15 July 2026 TransNusa has flown Bali to Waingapu non-stop four times a week (Tuesday, Wednesday, Friday, Saturday). The trick that saves a full day: fly into one airport and out of the other, and cross the island by car once, instead of driving back about 170 km. Small planes mean small baggage allowances, and schedules on these routes change often, so check with the airline before you lock anything in.

(No link needed. If asked for more: https://thesumbatimes.com/en/getting-here/how-to-get-to-sumba-from-bali/?utm_source=reddit&utm_medium=community)

### 3. Is Sumba safe? Solo female traveller here

Generally yes. Sumba is calm and friendly and serious crime against visitors is rare. The risks are practical ones. Malaria: the CDC lists all of East Nusa Tenggara as a risk area, so talk to a travel clinic and tell them you are going to Sumba specifically, not just Bali. The sea: strong currents on the south and west coasts and no lifeguards. Roads: avoid driving after dark, there are no street lights and animals sleep on the tarmac. Medical care is basic, serious cases are flown to Bali, so get insurance that covers evacuation. For a woman travelling alone: dress modestly away from the beach, do not travel alone at night on rural roads, and use a driver or guide someone trustworthy has recommended for remote areas such as Kodi. Tap water is not drinkable. Telkomsel has the best coverage.

Health and safety detail: https://thesumbatimes.com/en/getting-here/sumba-travel-safety-health/?utm_source=reddit&utm_medium=community

### 4. Do I need a driver, or can I rent a scooter in Sumba?

Most visitors hire a car with a local driver, roughly IDR 600,000 to 1,000,000 a day. Ask whether fuel and the driver's meals and overnight stays are included. A scooter is about IDR 150,000 to 200,000 a day and fine for confident riders, but roads switch from asphalt to gravel or mud without warning, distances are long, and legally you need your motorcycle licence plus an International Driving Permit with category A, or your insurance may not pay out. There is no Grab or Gojek and no metered taxis. Buses link the main towns but are slow and irregular. Tambolaka to Waingapu is about 170 km and takes 4 to 5 hours by car. Fill up whenever you pass a petrol station, queues and shortages happen, and download offline maps because the signal drops in the hills.

Driving times between the main stops: https://thesumbatimes.com/en/getting-here/getting-around-sumba/?utm_source=reddit&utm_medium=community

### 5. How much does a week in Sumba cost?

Rough totals for two people for 7 days, not counting the international flight: about IDR 8.5 to 12.5 million on a tight budget (scooter, guesthouses, warungs), IDR 15 to 29 million mid-range with a car and driver, and from around IDR 45 million in the high-end resorts. The big items are the return flight from Bali (often IDR 2.3 to 2.8 million per person), the car with driver (IDR 600,000 to 1,000,000 a day) and accommodation, because mid-range choice is thin. Day-to-day costs are low: a warung meal is IDR 20,000 to 50,000 and village donations are usually IDR 10,000 to 40,000 per person. Bring cash: ATMs are only in Tambolaka, Waikabubak and Waingapu, and the airports have no money exchange.

Full breakdown by travel style: https://thesumbatimes.com/en/getting-here/sumba-travel-costs/?utm_source=reddit&utm_medium=community

### 6. How many days do I need in Sumba, and what route?

Seven days is the sweet spot. Five works at a fast pace, ten lets you add the south coast and a jungle waterfall trek. The route matters more than the length: fly into Tambolaka and out of Waingapu (or the reverse) and cross the island once. A typical week: two days in the west for Weekuri Lagoon, Mandorak and Ratenggaro in Kodi; two days around Waikabubak for Praijing village and Lapopu waterfall; then the long drive east (Waikabubak to Waingapu is about 130 km, 3.5 to 4.5 hours) with Wairinding hill on the way; and two days around Waingapu for Walakiri beach at sunset, Tanggedu or Waimarang waterfall and an ikat village. Plan on 30 to 40 km per hour once you leave the main road, and check which days your flight out actually runs before fixing the plan.

Day-by-day versions for 5, 7 and 10 days: https://thesumbatimes.com/en/things-to-do/sumba-itinerary-5-7-10-days/?utm_source=reddit&utm_medium=community

### 7. Sumba or Flores (or just stay in Bali)?

They are three different trips. Bali: easy logistics, food, nightlife, temples, crowds. Flores: Komodo dragons, diving out of Labuan Bajo, volcano scenery such as Kelimutu. Sumba: empty savanna, living megalithic villages, long beaches with nobody on them, and simpler infrastructure, which means fewer restaurants, slower roads and more planning. If you want comfort and choice, Sumba will frustrate you. If you want space and culture that is not staged for visitors, it is the one. The easiest combination is Bali plus Sumba, about 1 h 30 by air. Sumba also links to Labuan Bajo with a Wings Air flight of about 45 to 50 minutes to Tambolaka, a few times a week and seasonal, so check it for your dates.

(No link needed. Comparison table: https://thesumbatimes.com/en/things-to-do/sumba-vs-bali-flores/?utm_source=reddit&utm_medium=community)

### 8. When is Pasola 2027?

Nobody knows yet, and that is normal. Pasola is always in February and March, in Kodi (Southwest Sumba) and in Lamboya, Wanokaka and Gaura (West Sumba), but the exact days are set by the Rato, the Marapu elders, from the moon and the arrival of the nyale sea worms, and they are confirmed only a few weeks ahead. As a guide, in 2026 it was 9-11 February and 9-11 March in Kodi, 10 February in Lamboya, 9 March in Wanokaka and 14 March in Gaura. The first Kodi date was 3 February in 2024 and 18 February in 2025, so it can move by three weeks. The Kodi schedule has been announced between 21 and 24 January in the last three years. If you want to see it, book changeable flights, keep a week free in February or March, and check again from late January.

We keep the calendar updated here: https://thesumbatimes.com/en/culture/pasola-2027-dates/?utm_source=reddit&utm_medium=community

### 9. Can I get from Labuan Bajo (Komodo) to Sumba without going back to Bali?

Sometimes. Wings Air has flown Labuan Bajo to Tambolaka non-stop in about 50 minutes, around three times a week, but flight databases list it as seasonal (November to March), so check your exact dates with the airline. If it is not running, the backup is Labuan Bajo to Bali (about 1 h 10, many flights a day) and Bali to Tambolaka the same day, roughly 4 to 7 hours airport to airport. By sea there is no direct ferry: you would cross Labuan Bajo to Sape on Sumbawa (about 6.5 hours) and then take the Sape to Waikelo ferry, which reopened in May 2026 without a published timetable. That takes two to three days and only makes sense if the journey is the point. Leave a spare day between a Komodo boat trip and your flight.

### 10. Getting to Sumba from Europe: how long does it really take?

Plan on a day and a half to two days door to door. There are no direct flights from Europe to Sumba, or to anywhere near it. You fly to Bali (Denpasar) with one stop, usually Doha, Dubai, Istanbul or Singapore, which is roughly 16 to 18 hours from London. From Bali it is a domestic flight of about 1 h 30 to Tambolaka or Waingapu. Most people sleep one night in Bali, which also gives you a buffer if the long-haul flight is late. Book the domestic leg separately and with some slack, schedules on the Sumba routes change. Indonesian entry rules depend on your passport, so check the official e-visa site before you go.

### 11. Is Sumba good for surfing? Beginner-friendly?

Good, but mostly not for beginners. The surf is on the south and west coasts, which face the open Indian Ocean: the Kodi coast around Pero, the bays of Lamboya and Wanukaka in the west (Marosi and Kerewe are in that area), and Tarimbang in the east. The season is the dry season, roughly April to October, when south-west groundswells arrive. Water is around 27 C. Nearly everything breaks over shallow reef and is sensitive to the tide, so it suits intermediate and advanced surfers. Beginners should stick to small days and sandy sections, and there are no lifeguards anywhere. There is no public transport to the breaks, so you need a car or a scooter with a rack, and you should bring whatever spares you need with you.

Spot-by-spot notes: https://thesumbatimes.com/en/things-to-do/surfing-in-sumba/?utm_source=reddit&utm_medium=community

### 12. Is Weekuri Lagoon worth it, and how do I get there?

Yes, if you are in the west anyway. It is a salt-water lagoon in Kodi Utara, filled by the sea seeping through the coral rock, so the water is clear and you float easily. It is 40 to 60 km from Tambolaka airport depending on the route, 1.5 to 2 hours by car, and there is no public transport. In 2025 and 2026 visitors were usually asked for around IDR 15,000 to 20,000 per person, so bring small notes. Depth goes from about 30 cm at the edges to around 2.5 m, more at high tide, so check before jumping. Go in the morning on a weekday in the dry season, and wear water shoes, the rocks are sharp and slippery. Mandorak beach is about a kilometre away and Ratenggaro village is a short drive south, so the three make an easy day.

### 13. Visiting traditional villages in Sumba: what is the etiquette?

These are homes and sacred sites, not open-air museums, so behave like a guest. On arrival say "permisi", greet whoever comes to meet you and sign the guest book. A donation is expected: usually IDR 10,000 to 40,000 per person, and around IDR 20,000 to 50,000 has been reported at Ratenggaro. A small bundle of betel nut (sirih pinang) is a welcome gift, and if it is offered to you, accept it or politely take coffee instead. Ask before every photo ("boleh foto?"), especially of people and inside houses. Never touch or sit on the stone tombs or ritual objects. Dress modestly. A local driver or guide who can introduce you makes a real difference, and some villages, such as Praijing near Waikabubak, now run their own guided visits. If a ceremony or a funeral is under way, ask whether visitors are welcome that day.

How to visit Ratenggaro in particular: https://thesumbatimes.com/en/places/ratenggaro-village/?utm_source=reddit&utm_medium=community

### 14. Where can I buy real Sumba ikat, and what should it cost?

Buy from the weavers. The easiest place is Kampung Raja Prailiu on the edge of Waingapu, where you can watch the process. Further south-east are the villages near Melolo (Pau, Umabara), then Rende and Kaliuda. Sumba ikat is warp ikat: the pattern is tied and dyed into the threads before weaving. Traditional dyes are indigo for blue and morinda root for red, and a large natural-dye cloth takes around 4 to 12 months. That is why prices vary so much: roughly IDR 250,000 for a small piece with chemical dyes, and from about IDR 1.5 million to well over IDR 10 million for a large hand-spun, natural-dye hinggi (2025 to 2026). Ask which dyes were used and how long it took, pay in cash, and do not bargain a year of work down to a souvenir price.

How to read the motifs and tell natural dye from chemical: https://thesumbatimes.com/en/culture/sumba-ikat-weaving-where-to-buy/?utm_source=reddit&utm_medium=community

### 15. Do people speak English in Sumba? Is there mobile signal and cash?

A little English, mostly in towns, hotels and among guides. In villages you need some Indonesian or a local guide. Indonesian is understood almost everywhere, even though people speak one of about nine local languages at home (Kambera in the east, Weyewa and Kodi in the west). Learn four things: terima kasih (thank you), permisi (excuse me), berapa harganya? (how much is it?) and boleh foto? (may I take a photo?). For signal, Telkomsel has the widest coverage, buy and register the SIM or eSIM before you arrive, and download offline maps because data disappears in the hills. For money, there are ATMs only in Tambolaka, Waikabubak and Waingapu, cards are rarely accepted outside hotels, and the airports have no exchange, so carry rupiah in small notes.

## Español (8)

Para foros y grupos en español (Los Viajeros, grupos de Facebook de viajes a Indonesia, Quora en español, r/viajes). Las mismas reglas: responder primero, un enlace como máximo y avisar siempre de la relación con la web.

### 1. ¿Cuál es la mejor época para viajar a Sumba?

Para la mayoría, la estación seca: más o menos de mayo a octubre, con junio, julio y agosto como los meses más soleados y cómodos. Agosto y septiembre son los más secos (en Waingapu caen unos 5 mm en todo el mes). El paisaje cambia mucho: las colinas están verdes de finales de enero a abril y doradas de julio a octubre, así que conviene decidir qué Sumba quieres ver. Febrero y marzo son lluviosos y las carreteras se embarran, pero es cuando se celebra la Pasola. Abril y principios de mayo son un buen término medio: todo sigue verde, las lluvias se retiran y hay poca gente. Las cascadas bajan con más agua justo después de las lluvias, aunque las pozas están más limpias para bañarse en la estación seca.

El detalle mes a mes: https://thesumbatimes.com/es/things-to-do/mejor-epoca-para-viajar-a-sumba/?utm_source=foro&utm_medium=community

### 2. ¿Cómo se llega a Sumba desde España?

No hay vuelos directos a Sumba desde Europa. Se vuela a Bali (Denpasar) con una escala, normalmente en Doha, Dubái, Estambul o Singapur, y desde allí se toma un vuelo nacional de alrededor de una hora y media a Tambolaka (oeste de la isla) o a Waingapu (este). Lo habitual es dormir una noche en Bali; cuenta con un día y medio o dos de puerta a puerta. Un consejo que ahorra una jornada entera: entra por un aeropuerto y sal por el otro, y cruza la isla en coche una sola vez. Los horarios de las rutas a Sumba cambian con frecuencia, así que confirma con la aerolínea antes de comprar y deja margen entre el vuelo largo y el nacional.

(No hace falta enlace. Si piden más: https://thesumbatimes.com/es/getting-here/como-llegar-a-sumba-desde-europa/?utm_source=foro&utm_medium=community)

### 3. ¿Es seguro viajar a Sumba? ¿Hay malaria?

En general, sí: es una isla tranquila y acogedora, con poca delincuencia grave. Los riesgos son prácticos. Malaria: los CDC estadounidenses consideran zona de riesgo toda Nusa Tenggara Oriental, de modo que conviene pasar por un centro de vacunación internacional y decir que vas a Sumba, no solo a Bali. El mar: corrientes fuertes en las costas sur y oeste, y ningún socorrista. La carretera: mejor no conducir de noche, porque no hay alumbrado y los animales duermen sobre el asfalto. La atención médica es básica y los casos graves se trasladan a Bali, así que contrata un seguro que cubra la evacuación. El agua del grifo no es potable. Y para el móvil, Telkomsel es la operadora con mejor cobertura.

Lo explicamos con más detalle aquí: https://thesumbatimes.com/es/getting-here/es-seguro-viajar-a-sumba-salud-malaria/?utm_source=foro&utm_medium=community

### 4. ¿Cuánto cuesta una semana en Sumba?

Para dos personas y siete días, sin contar el vuelo internacional: entre 8,5 y 12,5 millones de rupias con presupuesto ajustado (unos 420-620 euros), entre 15 y 29 millones en gama media con coche y conductor (unos 750-1.430 euros) y a partir de unos 45 millones en los resorts de lujo. Lo que más pesa es el vuelo de ida y vuelta desde Bali (a menudo 2,3-2,8 millones por persona), el coche con conductor (600.000-1.000.000 de rupias al día) y el alojamiento, porque hay poca oferta intermedia. El día a día es barato: una comida en un warung cuesta entre 20.000 y 50.000 rupias y los donativos en las aldeas, entre 10.000 y 40.000 por persona. Lleva efectivo: solo hay cajeros en Tambolaka, Waikabubak y Waingapu.

El desglose por tipo de viaje: https://thesumbatimes.com/es/getting-here/cuanto-cuesta-viajar-a-sumba/?utm_source=foro&utm_medium=community

### 5. ¿Alquilo moto o contrato conductor?

Casi todo el mundo contrata un coche con conductor local, por unas 600.000-1.000.000 de rupias al día; pregunta si incluye la gasolina y las comidas y noches del conductor. La moto cuesta unas 150.000-200.000 rupias al día y es buena opción si conduces con soltura, pero el asfalto se convierte en grava o barro sin avisar, las distancias son largas y, legalmente, necesitas tu carné de moto y el permiso internacional con categoría A; sin ellos, el seguro puede no cubrirte. No hay Grab ni Gojek, ni taxis con taxímetro. Los autobuses unen las ciudades principales, pero son lentos e irregulares. De Tambolaka a Waingapu hay unos 170 km, entre cuatro y cinco horas en coche. Llena el depósito en cada gasolinera, porque hay colas y a veces escasez, y descarga los mapas sin conexión.

### 6. ¿Cuándo es la Pasola en 2027?

Todavía no se sabe, y es lo normal. La Pasola se celebra siempre en febrero y marzo, en Kodi (Sumba Suroccidental) y en Lamboya, Wanokaka y Gaura (Sumba Occidental), pero los días exactos los fijan los rato, los ancianos de la fe marapu, según la luna y la llegada de los gusanos marinos nyale, y solo se confirman unas semanas antes. Como referencia, en 2026 fue del 9 al 11 de febrero y del 9 al 11 de marzo en Kodi, el 10 de febrero en Lamboya, el 9 de marzo en Wanokaka y el 14 de marzo en Gaura. En los tres últimos años, el programa de Kodi se anunció entre el 21 y el 24 de enero. Si quieres verla, compra vuelos que admitan cambios, resérvate una semana en febrero o en marzo y vuelve a mirar a finales de enero.

Mantenemos el calendario actualizado aquí: https://thesumbatimes.com/es/culture/pasola-2027-fechas/?utm_source=foro&utm_medium=community

### 7. ¿Sumba o Flores? ¿Merece la pena salir de Bali?

Son tres viajes distintos. Bali: logística fácil, buena comida, vida nocturna, templos y mucha gente. Flores: dragones de Komodo, buceo desde Labuan Bajo y paisajes volcánicos como el Kelimutu. Sumba: sabanas vacías, aldeas megalíticas habitadas y playas larguísimas sin nadie, a cambio de una infraestructura más sencilla: pocos restaurantes, carreteras lentas y más planificación. Si buscas comodidad y variedad, Sumba puede desesperarte; si buscas espacio y una cultura que no está montada para el visitante, es tu isla. La combinación más práctica es Bali y Sumba, a una hora y media de avión. Sumba también conecta con Labuan Bajo mediante un vuelo corto de Wings Air a Tambolaka, que opera algunos días por semana y solo en temporada, así que compruébalo para tus fechas.

### 8. ¿Cuántos días hacen falta para ver Sumba?

Siete días es lo ideal. Con cinco se ven los imprescindibles a buen ritmo, y con diez puedes añadir la costa sur y una caminata por la selva hasta una cascada. Más que la duración importa el recorrido: entra por Tambolaka y sal por Waingapu (o al revés) y cruza la isla una sola vez. Una semana tipo: dos días en el oeste para la laguna de Weekuri, Mandorak y la aldea de Ratenggaro, en Kodi; dos días en torno a Waikabubak, con la aldea de Praijing y la cascada de Lapopu; el trayecto largo hacia el este (de Waikabubak a Waingapu hay unos 130 km, entre tres horas y media y cuatro y media), con parada en la colina de Wairinding; y dos días cerca de Waingapu para el atardecer en Walakiri, una cascada (Tanggedu o Waimarang) y una aldea de tejedoras de ikat. Fuera de la carretera principal, calcula entre 30 y 40 km por hora.
