# The Sumba Times: press release template, two drafts and target lists

Prepared on 10 November 2026. Nothing has been sent. Every fact in the two drafts is already published on thesumbatimes.com (the Pasola 2027 calendar page and the facts and figures page); if those pages change, update the release before sending. No phone number is given anywhere: the only contact is info@ecobeachcity.com.

## 1. Rules before sending

- Send a release only when there is something a journalist can use today: a date, a figure, a new data page. "We exist" is not news.
- One language per email: Indonesian for Indonesian media, Spanish for Spanish media, English for travel trade and international desks.
- Paste the text in the body of the email. No attachments on first contact; link the press kit instead: https://thesumbatimes.com/en/press/ (es: /es/press/, id: /id/press/), once press.py is wired.
- Say who publishes The Sumba Times (Eco Beach City) in every release. It is on every page of the site and it must be in the release too.
- Do not promote any hotel, including the publisher's. The release is about information on Sumba.
- Use each outlet's public contact form or newsroom address found on its own contact page. Do not buy lists and do not guess addresses.
- One follow-up after five working days, then stop.

## 3. Draft A: the Pasola calendar

Source page: https://thesumbatimes.com/en/culture/pasola-2027-dates/

### English

**PRESS RELEASE · For immediate release**

**Pasola 2027: no dates yet, and a three-year calendar showing when to expect them**

*The Sumba Times publishes every Pasola date since 2024 and will update the page as soon as the Rato elders announce the 2027 season.*

[CITY], [DD Month YYYY]. The dates of Pasola 2027, the ritual mounted battle of western Sumba, have not been announced. As of 7 November 2026 no official schedule exists, because the days are set each year by the Rato, the customary elders of the Marapu faith, who follow the moon and the arrival of the nyale sea worms. The Sumba Times has published a calendar with every date from 2024 to 2026 so that travellers and the travel trade can plan around the pattern.

The Pasola is held every February and March in four areas: Kodi, in Southwest Sumba, and Lamboya, Wanokaka and Gaura, in West Sumba. In 2026 it fell on 9-11 February and 9-11 March in Kodi, on 10 February in Lamboya, on 9 March in Wanokaka and on 14 March in Gaura. The first Kodi Pasola fell on 3 February in 2024, 18 February in 2025 and 9 February in 2026: the calendar moves by up to three weeks from one year to the next.

In 2024 and 2025 the Kodi schedule was agreed on 24 January, and in 2026 the Rato announced it on 21 January. The 2027 Kodi dates can therefore be expected in the second half of January 2027. Visitors who want to see the Pasola should keep a flexible window of at least a week in February and another in March.

The page, in English, Spanish and Indonesian, lists the fields where each Pasola is held, explains how the dates are decided and how to watch safely and respectfully, and links the local sources for every date. It will be updated when the elders and the regency governments publish the 2027 schedule.

## 4. Draft B: Sumba in facts and figures

Source page: https://thesumbatimes.com/en/getting-here/sumba-facts-figures/

### English

**PRESS RELEASE · For immediate release**

**Sumba in 47 figures: a sourced data page for an island short of reliable numbers**

*Area, population by regency, airports, climate and visitors, each figure with its source and year, free to cite.*

[CITY], [DD Month YYYY]. The Sumba Times has published "Sumba in facts and figures", a reference page that gathers 47 key data points about the island of Sumba, in East Nusa Tenggara, each with its source and year. It is meant for travellers, journalists and AI assistants that need a figure they can cite.

Among the figures: Sumba covers about 10,900 to 11,150 km², roughly twice the size of Bali, and had an estimated 852,832 inhabitants in mid-2025 according to BPS, against 779,049 at the 2020 census. The island has four regencies. Southwest Sumba is the most populous, with 335,500 people, and East Sumba the largest, with 7,000.5 km². The highest point is Mount Wanggameti, at 1,225 m.

Sumba has two airports, Tambolaka (TMC) and Waingapu (WGP), about 450 km east of Bali. Tambolaka handled 165,416 passengers in 2024, below its 2018 peak of 294,984, and Waingapu 58,819. East Sumba recorded 41,524 tourist visits in 2025, according to the regency's tourism and culture office.

The page is available in English, Spanish and Indonesian. Figures may be quoted with a link to the page; for formal work, the original source named beside each figure should be cited as well.
