The beach-proof checklist: 12 specs to ask before buying a cabin

Salt, sun and storms destroy cheap prefab in two seasons. These are the specs that separate a 20-year unit from a 2-year one.

Updated October 2026

Why coastal sites are different

Within 1 km of the sea, salt spray corrodes ordinary steel in months. Add UV, humidity and cyclone-season winds and most budget cabins start leaking and rusting by year two. Ask for these in writing.

Structure

  • ✓Hot-dip galvanized steel frame (not only powder coated), with zinc thickness stated
  • ✓316 stainless bolts and fixings, not 304 or zinc-plated
  • ✓A marine-grade or C4/C5 paint system on exposed steel
  • ✓A written wind rating (km/h or Beaufort) and how the unit is anchored

Envelope

  • ✓Rock wool or PU sandwich panels of 75 mm or more
  • ✓Aluminium or uPVC windows with laminated or tempered glass
  • ✓Roof slope and gutters for tropical rain, plus a sealed roof membrane
  • ✓Termite-resistant or composite deck, not untreated pine

Paper

  • ✓Warranty years for structure, corrosion and waterproofing, separately
  • ✓Form E or the right certificate of origin for your country
  • ✓Electrical to your local voltage and plug standard
  • ✓Photos or video of the exact unit before the balance payment

Questions

Is powder coating enough?+

Not by the sea. Powder coat over hot-dip galvanizing is good; powder coat alone chips and rusts underneath.

What wind rating do I need?+

Ask your local building rules. In cyclone areas look for a tested rating of at least 150 km/h and proper ground anchors.
